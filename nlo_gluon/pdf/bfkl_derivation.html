Derivation of the BFKL Term in NLO Gluon Production

How the $\log(\vee/k^+)$ term with the $K_{ww'}(z)\,K_{xx'}(z)$ structure follows from the $(1-\xi)/\xi$ term of the splitting function

$d_\perp=2-\epsilon$, $\overline{\rm MS}$ scale $\mu\equiv\mu_{\overline{MS}}$, $c_0\equiv2e^{-\gamma_E}$, transverse measure $\int_x\equiv\int d^2x$.

1. Goal

Start from the first line of the NLO spectrum,

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N^{(\rm I)}}{d^{3}k}&=\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',y'}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}} \Big[-\frac{d_{\perp}}{\epsilon}-d_{\perp}\gamma_{E}-\frac{d_{\perp}}{2}\log\frac{(y'-y)^{2}\mu^{2}}{4}-1\Big]\\ &\qquad\times\int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{1}\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}\,e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi} \Big[\xi(1-\xi)+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_{+}}\Big] \big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x), \end{aligned} $$

and show that its $\log(\vee/k^+)$-enhanced part is exactly

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm BFKL}}{d^{3}k}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}} \int_{x,x',z,w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot (w'-w)}\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}}\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^{2}(x'-z)^{2}}\\ &\qquad\times\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x). \end{aligned} $$
The last Wilson line is written $U^{ce}(x)$ in both of the original expressions. It is taken here to be $U^{ec}(x)$, the same in both, so it does not affect the derivation.

2. Notation

$$ C\equiv\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}},\qquad a\equiv\frac{k^+}{\vee},\qquad r\equiv y'-y, $$ $$ \mathcal A(x,y,x',y')\equiv\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}},\qquad \mathcal O(y,y')\equiv\big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x), $$ $$ K_{ww'}(z)\equiv\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}} . $$

The dependence of $\mathcal O$ on $x,x'$ is kept implicit. In this notation the target result reads

$$ \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm BFKL}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}} \int_{x,x',z,w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot (w'-w)}\,K_{ww'}(z)\,\mathcal A(x,z,x',z)\,\mathcal O(z,z). $$

3. Step 1: expand the bracket

Call the square bracket in the first line $L(r)$. Pull out $d_\perp/2$ and use $\gamma_E$ to define $c_0$:

$$ L=-\frac{d_\perp}{2}\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+2\gamma_E+\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{4}\Big]-1 =-\frac{d_\perp}{2}\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]-1, \qquad \log\frac{r^2\mu^2e^{2\gamma_E}}{4}=\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}. $$

Insert $d_\perp=2-\epsilon$:

$$ L=-\Big(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Big)\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]-1 =-\frac{2}{\epsilon}+1-\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}-1+O(\epsilon) =-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}+O(\epsilon). $$

For what follows, split $L$ into a part that does not depend on $r$ and a part that does:

$$ L(r)=\underbrace{\Big[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]}_{\equiv\,L_0\ (\text{no } r)}\;\underbrace{-\;\log r^2}_{r\text{-dependent}} . $$

Each of the two logarithms on the right carries a compensating unit; only their sum is meaningful, and nothing below depends on that unit.

4. Step 2: which term of the splitting function can give $\log(\vee/k^+)$

A $\log(\vee/k^+)=\log(1/a)$ can only come from the lower limit $\xi\to a\to0$. Define

$$ \Phi_{W}(q)\equiv\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-iq\cdot(y'-y)}\,W\,\mathcal O(y,y'). $$

The first line is then

$$ \frac{d^{3}N^{(\rm I)}}{d^{3}k}=C\int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}\Big[\xi(1-\xi)+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_{+}}\Big]\, \Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\Big(\frac{k}{\xi}\Big). $$

As $\xi\to0$, the momentum $k/\xi\to\infty$, so what decides whether a log appears is the large-$q$ behaviour of $\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(q)$. Section 5 shows

$$ \Phi_{\mathcal AL}(q)\;\xrightarrow{\;q\to\infty\;}\;\frac{\rm const}{q^2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k/\xi)\sim\xi^2 . $$

The three terms of the splitting function therefore behave as follows:

TermWeight $\frac{1}{\xi^2}\times$ term$\times\,\xi^2$ from $\Phi$$\int_a d\xi$ as $a\to0$
$\xi(1-\xi)$$\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}$$\xi(1-\xi)$finite, no log
$\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}$$\frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}$$\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}$$\log(1/a)=\log(\vee/k^+)$

The subtraction in the plus distribution acts only at $\xi=1$, so it cannot produce a log at $\xi\to0$. Only the $(1-\xi)/\xi$ term is needed:

$$ T\equiv C\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)/\xi}\,\mathcal A(x,y,x',y')\,L(y'-y)\,\mathcal O(y,y'). $$

5. Step 3: the $r$-independent part $L_0$ gives no log

The $L_0$ part of $T$ is

$$ T_0=C\,L_0\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}\,\Phi_{\mathcal A}\!\Big(\frac{k}{\xi}\Big). $$

$\Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)$ is the LO structure at transverse momentum $q$ (amplitude times conjugate):

$$ \Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)=a_i(q)\,a_i^\dagger(q),\qquad a_i(q)=\int_{x,y}e^{iq\cdot y}\frac{(x-y)_i}{(x-y)^2}\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]\rho(x). $$

At large $q$, $a_i$ is controlled by $y\to x$. There $[U(y)-U(x)]\simeq(y-x)_j\partial_jU(x)$ vanishes linearly and cancels the $1/|x-y|$ of the kernel. What is left, $(x-y)_i(y-x)_j/(x-y)^2$, is bounded, so its Fourier transform falls like $1/q^2$. Hence

$$ a_i\sim\frac1{q^2},\qquad \Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)\sim\frac{1}{q^4}, $$

which is the familiar $1/k^4$ tail of the LO spectrum. Therefore

$$ \frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}\,\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k/\xi)\sim\xi \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T_0\ \text{has a finite limit as } a\to0 . $$

So the collinear pole $-2/\epsilon$ and the $\mu$-dependence do not enter the BFKL term, as the target result requires. Only the $-\log r^2$ part remains:

$$ T\big|_{\log}=C\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)/\xi}\,\big[-\log(y'-y)^2\big]\, \mathcal A(x,y,x',y')\,\mathcal O(y,y'). $$

6. Step 4: rescale the dipole

At fixed $x,x',y$, change variables from $y'$ to $s$:

$$ y'=y+\xi s,\qquad d^2y'=\xi^2\,d^2s,\qquad \frac{k\cdot(y'-y)}{\xi}=k\cdot s,\qquad (y'-y)^2=\xi^2s^2 . $$

Then

$$ T\big|_{\log}=C\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\int_{x,y,x',s}e^{-ik\cdot s}\,\big[-\log s^2-\log\xi^2\big]\, \mathcal A(x,y,x',y+\xi s)\,\mathcal O(y,y+\xi s). $$

The $\xi^2$ from the Jacobian has turned $1/\xi^3$ into $1/\xi$.

The $-\log\xi^2$ piece gives no log. It has no $s$-dependence, so undoing the rescaling gives $-\log\xi^2\cdot\xi^{-2}\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k/\xi)\sim\xi^2\log\xi$. Its integrand behaves as $\xi\log\xi$, which is integrable at $\xi=0$.

What remains is

$$ T\big|_{\log}=C\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\,F(\xi)+(\text{finite as }a\to0), \qquad F(\xi)\equiv\int_{x,y,x',s}e^{-ik\cdot s}\big(-\log s^2\big)\,\mathcal A(x,y,x',y+\xi s)\,\mathcal O(y,y+\xi s). $$

7. Step 5: take $\xi\to0$ inside $F$

Write $F(\xi)=F(0)+[F(\xi)-F(0)]$. For fixed $x,x',y$, the product $\mathcal A\,\mathcal O$ is smooth in $y'$ near $y'=y$, so $F(\xi)-F(0)=O(\xi)$. Then $\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}[F(\xi)-F(0)]$ has a finite limit as $a\to0$ and gives no log. At $\xi=0$, $y'=y$; rename $y\equiv z$:

$$ F(0)=\int_{x,x',z}\mathcal A(x,z,x',z)\,\mathcal O(z,z)\;\times\;J(k), \qquad J(k)\equiv\int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\big(-\log s^2\big), $$ $$ \mathcal A(x,z,x',z)=\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^2(x'-z)^2},\qquad \mathcal O(z,z)=\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x). $$
Physical picture. $\xi=k^+/p^+\to0$ means the parent gluon is much harder than the measured one. The rescaling shows that its transverse size $|y'-y|=\xi|s|$ shrinks to zero. The hard gluon therefore crosses the target at one point $z$ in both the amplitude and the conjugate, while the soft measured gluon carries the momentum $k$. This is exactly the structure of the target result.

8. Step 6: Fourier transform of the logarithm

In two dimensions, $\log|s|$ is the Green's function of the Laplacian:

$$ \nabla^2_s\log s^2=2\,\nabla^2_s\log|s|=4\pi\,\delta^2(s). $$

Multiply by $e^{-ik\cdot s}$ and integrate over $s$. On the left, integrate by parts twice ($\nabla^2e^{-ik\cdot s}=-k^2e^{-ik\cdot s}$):

$$ -k^2\int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\log s^2=4\pi \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\log s^2=-\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\quad(k\neq0). $$

This holds in the distributional sense; any additive constant in $\log s^2$ contributes only $\delta^2(k)$, which vanishes for $k\neq0$. Therefore

$$ J(k)=\int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\big(-\log s^2\big)=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}. $$

The same holds with $e^{+ik\cdot s}$, since $\log s^2$ is even in $s$.

9. Step 7: write $4\pi/k^2$ as the soft-gluon kernel

(a) One leg. Use $\frac{u_i}{u^2}=\frac12\partial_i\log u^2$ and integrate by parts:

$$ I_i(k)\equiv\int d^2u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\frac{u_i}{u^2} =\frac12\int d^2u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\,\partial_i\log u^2 =-\frac{i k_i}{2}\int d^2u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\log u^2 =-\frac{ik_i}{2}\Big(-\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\Big)=2\pi i\,\frac{k_i}{k^2}. $$

Since $u_i/u^2$ is odd, $I_i(-k)=-I_i(k)=-2\pi i\,k_i/k^2$.

(b) Both legs. For any fixed $z$, put $u=w-z$, $u'=w'-z$. Then $w'-w=u'-u$, and the integral factorizes:

$$ \int_{w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}K_{ww'}(z) =\int_{u}e^{ik\cdot u}\frac{u_i}{u^2}\int_{u'}e^{-ik\cdot u'}\frac{u'_i}{u'^2} =I_i(k)\,I_i(-k) =\Big(2\pi i\frac{k_i}{k^2}\Big)\Big(-2\pi i\frac{k_i}{k^2}\Big)=\frac{4\pi^2}{k^2}. $$

Comparing with Step 6:

$$ J(k)=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}\,\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}} \qquad\text{for any }z. $$

The point $z$ can be taken to be the position of the hard gluon, which is what turns $J(k)$ into the emission kernel of the soft measured gluon at $w,w'$.

10. Step 8: the $\xi$ integral

$$ \int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}=\Big[\log\xi-\xi\Big]_a^1=\log\frac1a-1+a=\log\frac{\vee}{k^+}-1+\frac{k^+}{\vee}. $$

Keep $\log(\vee/k^+)$. The $-1+k^+/\vee$ is a non-logarithmic constant and belongs in the finite remainder.

11. Step 9: assemble

Collecting Steps 2–8,

$$ T\big|_{\log(\vee/k^+)}=C\,\log\frac{\vee}{k^+}\,F(0) =C\,\log\frac{\vee}{k^+}\int_{x,x',z}\mathcal A(x,z,x',z)\,\mathcal O(z,z)\;\frac1\pi\int_{w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}K_{ww'}(z). $$

The prefactor is

$$ \frac{C}{\pi}=\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\cdot\frac{N_c}{(2\pi)^3}\cdot\frac{g^2}{2\pi^2}\cdot\frac1{k^+}\cdot\frac1\pi =\frac{g^4N_c}{(4\pi^2)(2\pi^2)\,\pi}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{k^+} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\,\frac{1}{k^{+}} . $$

Therefore

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm BFKL}}{d^{3}k}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}} \int_{x,x',z,w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot (w'-w)}\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}}\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^{2}(x'-z)^{2}}\\ &\qquad\times\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x), \end{aligned} $$

which is the target result, including the prefactor. Everything dropped along the way has a finite limit as $\vee\to\infty$.

13. The other two lines of the NLO spectrum

13.1 The third line (the $d_\perp$-weighted $\xi$ integral) gives no $\log(\vee/k^+)$

Its weight is $d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}$, with no $1/\xi^2$, and its integrand is $\mathcal A-2\mathcal B$, where $\mathcal B=\frac{(x-y)\cdot r\,(x'-y')\cdot r}{(x-y)^2(x'-y')^2r^2}$. As $r\to0$, $(\mathcal A-2\mathcal B)\propto(\delta_{ij}-2\hat r_i\hat r_j)$, which is traceless. Its Fourier transform is $-\frac{4\pi}{q^2}(\delta_{ij}-2\hat q_i\hat q_j)$, so $\Phi_{\mathcal A-2\mathcal B}(k/\xi)\sim\xi^2$ and the integrand goes like $\xi$. There is no log.

13.2 The second line: the explicit $\log(\vee/k^+-1)$

With $b\equiv k^+/(k^++\Lambda)$, $b/(1-b)=k^+/\Lambda$ and $a/(1-a)=k^+/(\vee-k^+)$ hold exactly, the blue bracket of the second line is exactly

$$ \log\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^+}-1\Big)=\int_{k^+/\vee}^{k^+/(k^++\Lambda)}d\xi\Big[\frac{1}{1-\xi}+\frac1\xi\Big]. $$

Its $\int d\xi/\xi$ part multiplies $\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k)$: the phase is frozen at its $\xi=1$ value, the Wilson lines sit at $y\neq y'$, and the collinear pole $-2/\epsilon$ is still present. That is not the $\xi\to0$ limit of the $(1-\xi)/\xi$ term, which is the structure derived above.

Resolution. This identity shows that the blue bracket is the subtraction term of the plus prescription in line 1, taken in the form $\int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_+}e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}=\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}-e^{-ik\cdot r}}{\xi(1-\xi)}$. Converting this to the standard plus distribution generates $-e^{-ik\cdot r}\log(\vee/k^+-1)$, which cancels the blue $\log(\vee/k^+-1)$ exactly. The BFKL term derived above is then the only $\log(\vee/k^+)$ term. See Step 3 of nlo_gluon_decomposition.pdf for the full derivation.
