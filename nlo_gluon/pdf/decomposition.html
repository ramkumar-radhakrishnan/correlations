NLO Gluon Production: BFKL, JIMWLK, Running-Coupling, DGLAP and Finite Terms

A step-by-step derivation of every term from the NLO single-gluon spectrum

$d_\perp=2-\epsilon$ (so $2/\epsilon=1/\hat\epsilon$ for $D=4-2\hat\epsilon$), $\overline{\rm MS}$ scale $\mu\equiv\mu_{\overline{MS}}$, $c_0\equiv2e^{-\gamma_E}$, $\int_x\equiv\int d^2x$, pure glue ($\beta_0=\tfrac{11}{3}N_c$).

0. Starting expression

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k} &= \underbrace{\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\int_{x,y,x',y'}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}\Big[-\frac{d_{\perp}}{\epsilon}-d_{\perp}\gamma_{E}-\frac{d_{\perp}}{2}\log\frac{(y'-y)^{2}\mu^{2}}{4}-1\Big] \int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{1}\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}\,e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi}\Big[\xi(1-\xi)+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_{+}}\Big]\mathcal O}_{\rm line\ (I)} \\ &+\underbrace{\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}}\,{\color{blue}\Big[\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-1\Big)\Big]}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}\Big[\cdots\Big]\mathcal O}_{\rm line\ (II)} \\ &+\underbrace{\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}}\int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{k^{+}/(k^{+}+\Lambda)}\!d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi}\Big[\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}-d_\perp\frac{(x-y)\cdot (y'-y)\,(x'-y')\cdot (y'-y)}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}(y'-y)^{2}}\Big]\mathcal O}_{\rm line\ (III)} \end{aligned} $$

Here $[\cdots]$ in line (II) is the same bracket as in line (I), and $\mathcal O=[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')][U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$.

The last Wilson line is written $U^{ce}(x)$ in the original. It is taken to be $U^{ec}(x)$ throughout; it multiplies every term identically, so this does not affect the separation.

1. Notation

$$ C\equiv\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{g^4N_c}{8\pi^4}\frac{1}{k^+}, \qquad a\equiv\frac{k^+}{\vee},\qquad b\equiv\frac{k^+}{k^++\Lambda},\qquad r\equiv y'-y, $$ $$ \mathcal A\equiv\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}},\qquad \mathcal B\equiv\frac{(x-y)\cdot r\,(x'-y')\cdot r}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}\,r^{2}},\qquad K_{ww'}(z)\equiv\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}}, $$ $$ \Phi_W(q)\equiv\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-iq\cdot r}\,W\,\mathcal O . $$

All three lines carry the same prefactor $C$ (check: $N_c$ simply moves between factors). $\Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)$ is the LO structure at transverse momentum $q$:

$$ \Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)=a_i(q)\,a_i^\dagger(q),\qquad a_i(q)=\int_{x,y}e^{iq\cdot y}\frac{(x-y)_i}{(x-y)^2}\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]\rho(x). $$

In this notation the expression is

$$ \frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=C\Big\{\int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,p(\xi)\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big) +\Big[\log\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^+}-1\Big)\Big]\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k) +\int_a^b d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\,\Phi_{\mathcal A-d_\perp\mathcal B}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big)\Big\}, $$ $$ p(\xi)\equiv\xi(1-\xi)+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_+}, $$

where $L$ is the bracket, treated in Step 1.

2. Step 1: expand the bracket $L$

Pull out $d_\perp/2$ and combine $\gamma_E$ with the log, using $\log\frac{r^2\mu^2e^{2\gamma_E}}{4}=\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}$:

$$ L=-\frac{d_\perp}{\epsilon}-d_\perp\gamma_E-\frac{d_\perp}{2}\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{4}-1 =-\frac{d_\perp}{2}\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]-1 . $$

Insert $d_\perp/2=1-\epsilon/2$. The $O(\epsilon)\times\frac{2}{\epsilon}$ cross term gives $+1$, which cancels the $-1$:

$$ L=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}+1-1+O(\epsilon) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\,L(r)=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}+O(\epsilon)\,} $$

The remaining $O(\epsilon)$ terms can be dropped: every quantity $L$ multiplies is finite. These are the $\xi$-integrals at fixed $\vee,\Lambda$, the rapidity logs, and an $\epsilon$-independent $g\to gg$ splitting function.

Meaning of $L$. With $\int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{(q^2)^{\alpha}}=\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha)}{4^\alpha\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}(r^2)^{\alpha-d/2}$ at $\alpha=1$, $d=2-\epsilon$, and the $\overline{\rm MS}$ rescaling $\mu^2\to\mu^2e^{\gamma_E}/4\pi$,

$$ 4\pi\mu^{\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{q^2} =\Gamma\!\big(-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\big)\Big(\frac{r^2\mu^2e^{\gamma_E}}{4}\Big)^{\epsilon/2} =-\frac2\epsilon-\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}+O(\epsilon)=L(r). $$

In two dimensions, $\int d^2z\,K_{yy'}(z)=\int d^2q\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{q^2}$. So $L$ is the integral over the position $z$ of the unobserved gluon (with no Wilson line at $z$), and $1/\epsilon$ is its final-state collinear divergence: relative transverse momentum $q\to0$, i.e. large separation.

3. Step 2: the blue bracket is a $\xi$-integral

From the definitions of $a$ and $b$:

$$ \frac{b}{1-b}=\frac{k^+}{\Lambda},\qquad \frac{a}{1-a}=\frac{k^+}{\vee-k^+}. $$

With $\int\frac{d\xi}{\xi(1-\xi)}=\log\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}$:

$$ \int_a^b\frac{d\xi}{\xi(1-\xi)}=\log\frac{b}{1-b}-\log\frac{a}{1-a} =\log\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^+}-1\Big)\qquad\text{exactly.} $$

So line (II) is $C\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k)\int_a^b\frac{d\xi}{\xi(1-\xi)}$: an integrand $\frac{1}{\xi(1-\xi)}$ multiplied by the phase at its $\xi=1$ value.

4. Step 3: identify the plus prescription and combine lines (I) and (II)

The $\xi/(1-\xi)$ term of line (I), before regularization, is

$$ \int_a^b\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}\,e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}=\int_a^b d\xi\,\frac{e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}}{\xi(1-\xi)} . $$

Adding and subtracting the integrand at its $\xi=1$ value $e^{-ik\cdot r}$ gives

$$ \int_a^b d\xi\,\frac{e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}}{\xi(1-\xi)} =\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}-e^{-ik\cdot r}}{\xi(1-\xi)} +e^{-ik\cdot r}\int_a^b\frac{d\xi}{\xi(1-\xi)}+O\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{k^+}\Big). $$

The first integral is regular at $\xi=1$, so its upper limit $b$ can be replaced by 1; the error is $O(1-b)=O(\Lambda/k^+)$. By Step 2, the second term is exactly line (II). So line (I) + line (II) is the decomposition above, and the plus prescription of line (I) means

$$ \int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_+}\,e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}\equiv\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}-e^{-ik\cdot r}}{\xi(1-\xi)} . \qquad\text{(U)} $$

Convert (U) to the standard plus distribution. Let $f(\xi)\equiv e^{-ik\cdot r/\xi}/\xi$, so $f(1)=e^{-ik\cdot r}$. The standard $[0,1]$ distribution restricted to $[a,1]$ is

$$ \text{(S)}\equiv\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{f(\xi)}{(1-\xi)_+}=\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{f(\xi)-f(1)}{1-\xi}+f(1)\log(1-a). $$

Then

$$ \text{(U)}=\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{f(\xi)-f(1)/\xi}{1-\xi} =\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{f(\xi)-f(1)}{1-\xi}+f(1)\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-1/\xi}{1-\xi} =\int_a^1 d\xi\,\frac{f(\xi)-f(1)}{1-\xi}-f(1)\log\frac1a, $$

since $\frac{1-1/\xi}{1-\xi}=-\frac1\xi$. Therefore

$$ \text{(U)}=\text{(S)}-f(1)\Big[\log(1-a)+\log\frac1a\Big]=\text{(S)}-e^{-ik\cdot r}\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^+}-1\Big). $$

Combine. Insert this into line (I) and add line (II). The $\log(\vee/k^+-1)$ cancels exactly:

$$ \text{(I)}+\text{(II)}=C\Big\{\int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,p_{\rm S}(\xi)\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big) +\log\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k)\Big\}, $$

where $p_{\rm S}$ is $p$ with the standard plus distribution. The explicit $\log(\vee/k^+-1)$ of line (II) is the price of the subtraction (U); it is not a separate rapidity log. All $\log(\vee/k^+)$ dependence now sits in the lower limit of the $(1-\xi)/\xi$ term (Step 5).

This identification follows from the exact identity of Step 2. If your $(1-\xi)_+$ was instead already the standard one, the $\log(\vee/k^+-1)$ of line (II) would not cancel, and it would double count the BFKL term of Step 5.

5. Step 4: complete the splitting function and isolate the running coupling

The full pure-glue $g\to g$ splitting function is

$$ P_{gg}(\xi)=2N_c\Big[\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_+}+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\xi(1-\xi)\Big]+\frac{\beta_0}{2}\,\delta(1-\xi),\qquad\beta_0=\frac{11}{3}N_c . $$

The momentum sum rule fixes the $\delta$-coefficient:

$$ \int_0^1 d\xi\,\xi\,p_{\rm S}(\xi)=\int_0^1\frac{\xi^2-1}{1-\xi}\,d\xi+\int_0^1(1-\xi)\,d\xi+\int_0^1\xi^2(1-\xi)\,d\xi =-\frac32+\frac12+\frac1{12}=-\frac{11}{12}, $$ $$ \int_0^1 d\xi\,\xi\,P_{gg}=2N_c\Big(-\frac{11}{12}\Big)+\frac{11N_c}{6}=0. $$

Hence $p_{\rm S}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}-\frac{11}{12}\delta(1-\xi)$. The $\delta$-term evaluates at $\xi=1$ (with $\int_a^1\delta(1-\xi)\,d\xi=1$), so

$$ \int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,p_{\rm S}\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big) =\int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big)-\frac{11}{12}\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k). $$

The last term is the running-coupling term (Section 10). It carries the $\beta_0$ coefficient that the plus distribution did not produce.

6. Step 5: the $\log(\vee/k^+)$ hidden in the $P_{gg}$ convolution, which is the BFKL term

6.1 Only the $(1-\xi)/\xi$ term and the $-\log r^2$ part contribute

A $\log(1/a)=\log(\vee/k^+)$ can only come from $\xi\to a\to0$, where $\Phi$ is probed at $k/\xi\to\infty$. Split

$$ L=L_0-\log r^2,\qquad L_0\equiv-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{\mu^2}{c_0^2}\quad(\text{no }r). $$
  • The $L_0$ part. It multiplies $\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k/\xi)$. As $y\to x$, $[U(y)-U(x)]\simeq(y-x)_j\partial_jU$ cancels the $1/|x-y|$ of the kernel. The remaining bounded function has $a_i\sim q^{-2}$, so $\Phi_{\mathcal A}(q)\sim q^{-4}$ (the LO $1/k^4$ tail). Even the most singular weight $\frac{1-\xi}{\xi^3}$ then gives $\sim\xi$: no log. So the pole and $\log\mu^2$ do not enter the BFKL term.
  • The $-\log r^2$ part. Section 6.2 shows $\xi^{-2}\Phi_{\mathcal A(-\log r^2)}(k/\xi)\to\mathcal S$, a constant. Multiplied by the weights:
    • $\frac{1}{\xi^2}\,\xi(1-\xi)\cdot\xi^2\mathcal S$ goes like $\xi$: no log.
    • $\frac{1}{\xi^2}\,\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)_+}\cdot\xi^2\mathcal S$ goes like $\xi$: no log. The plus subtraction acts at $\xi=1$ only.
    • $\frac{1}{\xi^2}\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\cdot\xi^2\mathcal S\to\mathcal S/\xi$: this gives $\log(1/a)$.

6.2 The limit $\mathcal S$

(a) Rescale the dipole. At fixed $x,x',y$, put $y'=y+\xi s$:

$$ d^2y'=\xi^2d^2s,\qquad \frac{k\cdot(y'-y)}{\xi}=k\cdot s,\qquad -\log(y'-y)^2=-\log s^2-\log\xi^2 . $$

This gives

$$ \xi^{-2}\,\Phi_{\mathcal A(-\log r^2)}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big)=\int_{x,y,x',s}e^{-ik\cdot s}\big[-\log s^2-\log\xi^2\big]\, \mathcal A(x,y,x',y+\xi s)\,\mathcal O(y,y+\xi s). $$

The $-\log\xi^2$ piece is $-\log\xi^2\,\xi^{-2}\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k/\xi)\sim\xi^2\log\xi\to0$.

(b) Take $\xi\to0$. For fixed $x,x',y$, $\mathcal A\,\mathcal O$ is smooth in $y'$ at $y'=y$, so the limit is reached with $O(\xi)$ corrections. Rename $y\to z$; the hard parent then sits at a single point $z$ in the amplitude and the conjugate:

$$ \mathcal S=J(k)\int_{x,x',z}\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^2(x'-z)^2}\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^e(x), $$ $$ J(k)\equiv\int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\big(-\log s^2\big). $$

(c) Fourier transform of the log. From $\nabla^2\log s^2=4\pi\delta^2(s)$, multiplying by $e^{-ik\cdot s}$ and integrating by parts twice:

$$ -k^2\int d^2s\,e^{-ik\cdot s}\log s^2=4\pi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J(k)=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\qquad(k\neq0). $$

Any constant added to $\log s^2$ only gives $\delta^2(k)$, which vanishes for $k\neq0$.

(d) Write it as the soft-gluon kernel. Using $u_i/u^2=\frac12\partial_i\log u^2$:

$$ I_i(k)\equiv\int d^2u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\frac{u_i}{u^2}=-\frac{ik_i}{2}\int d^2u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\log u^2=2\pi i\frac{k_i}{k^2}, \qquad I_i(-k)=-I_i(k). $$

With $u=w-z$ and $u'=w'-z$, for any $z$:

$$ \int_{w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}K_{ww'}(z)=I_i(k)\,I_i(-k)=\frac{4\pi^2}{k^2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad J(k)=\frac1\pi\int_{w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}K_{ww'}(z). $$
$$ \mathcal S=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x,x',z,w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}}\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^{2}(x'-z)^{2}}\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^e(x) $$

6.3 Exact subtraction

Since $\int_a^1 d\xi/\xi=\log(\vee/k^+)$ exactly, write

$$ \int_a^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi^2}\,\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big) =\mathcal S\log\frac{\vee}{k^+} +\int_a^1 d\xi\Big[\frac{1}{\xi^2}\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}\Phi_{\mathcal AL}\!\big(\tfrac{k}{\xi}\big)-\frac{\mathcal S}{\xi}\Big]. $$

By Sections 6.1–6.2, the bracket on the right is integrable at $\xi=0$, so its lower limit can be set to 0, up to $O(k^+/\vee)$.

7. Step 6: split the subtracted convolution into DGLAP and finite parts

The convolution probes the parent momentum $q=k/\xi$. Split $L$ at that scale:

$$ L=\underbrace{\Big[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{\xi^2\mu^2}{k^2}\Big]}_{\text{pole}+\log(\mu^2/q^2)} \;\underbrace{-\;\log\frac{k^2r^2}{\xi^2c_0^2}}_{-\log(q^2r^2/c_0^2)} . $$

The first bracket has no $r$-dependence, so it multiplies $\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k/\xi)$. This is the standard DGLAP structure $-[1/\hat\epsilon+\log(\mu^2/q^2)]\,P\otimes{\rm LO}$. By Section 6.1 it is integrable at $\xi\to0$ with no subtraction. The $\mathcal S/\xi$ subtraction therefore belongs entirely to the second piece. Only $-\log r^2$ feeds $\mathcal S$; the extra $\log(\xi^2/k^2)$ multiplies $\Phi_{\mathcal A}$ and vanishes as $\xi^2\log\xi$.

8. Step 7: line (III) is finite

  • $d_\perp\to2$. Line (III) has no pole, so $(d_\perp-2)\mathcal B=-\epsilon\,\mathcal B\to0$.
  • Upper limit, no $\log(k^+/\Lambda)$. The weight $(1-\xi)/\xi$ vanishes at $\xi=1$, so $\int_b^1(1-\xi)\,d\xi=O(\Lambda^2/k^{+2})$ and $b\to1$.
  • Lower limit, no $\log(\vee/k^+)$. As $r\to0$, $(\mathcal A-2\mathcal B)\,\mathcal O\to\frac{(x-y)_i(x'-y)_j}{(x-y)^2(x'-y)^2}(\delta_{ij}-2\hat r_i\hat r_j)\,\mathcal O(y,y)$. The matrix $\delta_{ij}-2\hat r_i\hat r_j=-\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\phi&\sin2\phi\\ \sin2\phi&-\cos2\phi\end{pmatrix}$ is traceless and has angular average 0. Using $\int d\phi\,e^{\pm2i\phi}e^{-iqr\cos(\phi-\phi_q)}=-2\pi J_2(qr)\,e^{\pm2i\phi_q}$ and $\int_0^\infty r\,J_2(qr)\,dr=2/q^2$: $$\int d^2r\,e^{-iq\cdot r}\big(\delta_{ij}-2\hat r_i\hat r_j\big)=-\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\big(\delta_{ij}-2\hat q_i\hat q_j\big).$$ So $\Phi_{\mathcal A-2\mathcal B}(k/\xi)\sim\xi^2$, and $\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\,\xi^2\sim\xi$: no log. The limit $a\to0$ costs $O(k^{+2}/\vee^2)$.

9. Result: all terms

Collecting Steps 1–7, with $\mathcal S$ from Section 6.2:

$$ \frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm BFKL}}{d^{3}k}+\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm JIMWLK}}{d^{3}k}+\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm RC}}{d^{3}k} +\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm DGLAP}}{d^{3}k}+\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm finite}}{d^{3}k} +O\Big(\frac{k^+}{\vee},\frac{\Lambda}{k^+},\epsilon\Big). $$

9.1 BFKL, $\propto\log(\vee/k^+)$

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm BFKL}}{d^{3}k}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}} \int_{x,x',z,w,w'}e^{-ik\cdot (w'-w)}\frac{(w-z)\cdot(w'-z)}{(w-z)^{2}(w'-z)^{2}}\frac{(x-z)\cdot(x'-z)}{(x-z)^{2}(x'-z)^{2}}\\ &\qquad\times\big[U^{e'c}(z)-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(z)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x) \end{aligned} $$

This is $C\,\mathcal S\log(\vee/k^+)$; the prefactor $C/\pi=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{g^4N_c}{8\pi^5}\frac{1}{k^+}$ includes the $1/\pi$ of $\mathcal S$.

9.2 JIMWLK, $\propto\log(k^+/\Lambda)$

$$ \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm JIMWLK}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda} \int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}} \Big[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{(y'-y)^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]\mathcal O $$

Using $L=\frac1\pi\int_zK_{yy'}(z)$ (dimensionally regularized $z$-integral, Step 1), this is

$$ \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}\int_{x,y,x',y',z}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}K_{yy'}(z)\,\mathcal A\,\mathcal O . $$

It has the same prefactor as BFKL. The roles are reversed: here the soft, unobserved gluon $z$ is emitted by the measured gluon $(y,y')$; in BFKL the soft measured gluon $(w,w')$ is emitted by the hard gluon $z$.

9.3 Running coupling

$$ \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm RC}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\;\frac{11N_c}{6} \int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{(y'-y)^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\Big]\mathcal O $$

This is $-\frac{11}{12}C\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k)$, with $\frac{11N_c}{6}=\frac{\beta_0}{2}$; in terms of $\alpha_s$ it is $\frac{\alpha_s\beta_0}{2\pi}\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{k^+}\big[\frac2\epsilon\Phi_{\mathcal A}+\Phi_{\mathcal A\log(r^2\mu^2/c_0^2)}\big](k)$.

  • In pure dimensional regularization the on-shell self-energy is scaleless ($1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}-1/\epsilon_{\rm IR}=0$). So the pole can be read as the UV pole removed by $\overline{\rm MS}$ charge renormalization of the LO.
  • The log then makes the coupling run to the dipole scale: $\alpha_s(\mu^2)\to\alpha_s(c_0^2/r^2)=\alpha_s(\mu^2)\big[1+\frac{\alpha_s\beta_0}{4\pi}\log\frac{r^2\mu^2}{c_0^2}\big]$.
  • With quarks, $\beta_0\to\frac{11N_c-2n_f}{3}$, and the $g\to q\bar q$ real channel must also be added.

9.4 DGLAP

$$ \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm DGLAP}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}\,P_{gg}(\xi)\Big[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{\xi^2\mu^2}{k^2}\Big] \int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi}\frac{(x-y)\cdot (x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}\,\mathcal O $$

Here $P_{gg}$ is the full function of Step 4 with the standard plus distribution, and $C/(2N_c)$ has been used. This is the timelike $g\to g$ fragmentation convolution: $\frac{d\xi}{\xi^3}\times\frac{dN_{\rm LO}}{d^3p}$ with $\frac{dN_{\rm LO}}{d^3p}\propto\frac{1}{p^+}$, at the parent momentum $p^+=k^+/\xi$, $p=k/\xi$. The $-2/\epsilon=-1/\hat\epsilon$ is removed by the $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterm of $D_g(\xi,\mu^2)$. The $\log(\xi^2\mu^2/k^2)=\log(\mu^2/p^2)$ is the factorization-scale log, natural choice $\mu=|k|/\xi$.

9.5 Finite terms

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm finite}}{d^{3}k}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\Bigg\{ \int_0^1 d\xi\Bigg[\frac{1}{\xi^2}\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi}\,\mathcal A\Big(-\log\frac{k^2(y'-y)^2}{\xi^2c_0^2}\Big)\mathcal O -\frac{\mathcal S}{\xi}\Bigg]\\ &\qquad\qquad+\int_0^1 d\xi\,\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\int_{x,y,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot (y'-y)/\xi}\Big[\mathcal A-2\mathcal B\Big]\mathcal O\Bigg\} \end{aligned} $$
  • First integral. This is the non-log remainder of the $P_{gg}$ convolution. The $-\mathcal S/\xi$ subtraction is exactly what was moved into BFKL, so the integrand is regular at $\xi=0$.
  • Second integral. This is line (III) at $d_\perp=2$, with the limits extended to $0,1$.
  • Both are free of poles, of $\log\mu$, and of rapidity logs.

11. Remarks and checks

  1. Plus prescription. The separation of Step 3 is the one forced by the exact identity of Step 2. If your $(1-\xi)_+$ is the standard one, line (II) keeps an extra $C\log(\vee/k^+-1)\,\Phi_{\mathcal AL}(k)$. At large $k$ that extra term tends to the BFKL term, so it would double count.
  2. The JIMWLK term carries the collinear pole. It is a soft–collinear double log, $\log(k^+/\Lambda)$ times $1/\epsilon$. In the complete NLO the $\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}$ must cancel against virtual graphs with the same cutoff $\Lambda$ and against the $U(z)$ terms of the JIMWLK Hamiltonian, which are not in the expression shown. Do not also absorb it into DGLAP. The BFKL term, by contrast, comes out pole-free (Section 6.1).
  3. Normalization. Relative to $\mathcal N\Phi_{\mathcal A}$ with $\mathcal N=\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{k^+}$, the DGLAP coefficient is $(\alpha_s/\pi)P_{gg}$ and the RC coefficient is $\alpha_s\beta_0/2\pi$. Both are the textbook values, $(\alpha_s/2\pi)P_{gg}$ and $\alpha_s\beta_0/4\pi$, if the LO is $dN_{\rm LO}/d^3k=2\mathcal N\Phi_{\mathcal A}(k)$. The two checks are consistent with each other. If the LO is $\mathcal N\Phi_{\mathcal A}$, look for a stray factor of 2, for example the identical-gluon factor $\tfrac12$.
  4. Assumptions. Two are used: smoothness of $\mathcal A\,\mathcal O$ in $y'$ at $y'=y$ for fixed $x,x',y$ (Step 5), and the LO $q^{-4}$ tail. Both are standard; a target field that is rough at short distances would need a separate check.
