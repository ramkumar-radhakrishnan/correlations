# Part I — The formalism you actually need to compute observables

The paper builds `Ω`. It does not build the machinery that turns `Ω` into a multiplicity
distribution. That machinery is the subject of this file, and it is the real content of the
proposed follow-up: **the hard part of the multiplicity project is not the `O(g^4)` diagrams —
those are already encoded in `Ω` — it is knowing which structural reduction makes the Fock-space
algebra finite.** There is one, and it is the reason this project is tractable at all.

---

## 1.1 The state

Born–Oppenheimer separation splits the hadron's light-cone wave function into a fast valence
sector (`k⁺ > Λ`) and a soft sector (`k⁺ < Λ`). The soft modes are created on top of the valence
state by a unitary dressing operator:

```
|Ψ⟩  =  Ω[ρ; a, a†]  |v⟩ ⊗ |0⟩_soft
```

Two facts drive everything:

1. **`Ω` is unitary in the soft Fock space** — `Ω† Ω = 1`. This is what fixes the coefficient
   relations the paper uses.
2. **`Ω`'s coefficients are operators, not numbers.** They are functionals of `ρ^a(x)`, which does
   not commute with itself. So the coefficients must be kept in order throughout.

## 1.2 The normal-ordered expansion (as actually written in the paper)

Eq. (4.1) of arXiv:2607.18373 organizes `Ω` by **operator length**, not by order in `g`:

```
Ω  =  N
    + ∫_p           [ A₁ a  +  A₂ a† ]
    + ∫_{p,q}       [ B₁ aa  +  B₂ a†a†  +  B₃ a†a ]
    + ∫_{p,q,r}     [ C₁ a†a†a  +  C₂ a†aa ]
    + ∫_{p,q,r,s}   [ D₁ a†a†a†a , D₂ a†a†aa , D₃ a†aaa ]
    + ∫_{p…v}       [ E₁,₂,₃  six-operator structures ]
```
(colour, polarization and momentum labels suppressed; the paper writes these in blackboard bold
`N, A, B, C, D, E`.)

**Order counting** — the paper's assignment, which is *not* what a naive Fock-structure argument
would give:

| Coefficient | Fock structure | Order | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `N` | `1` | `N − 1 = O(g²)` | normalization = the virtual correction |
| `A₁, A₂` | `a`, `a†` | `O(g)` | soft emission off the valence charge; `A₂ ∝ ρ`, Eq. (5.2) = the WW field |
| `B₁, B₂, B₃` | `aa`, `a†a†`, `a†a` | `O(g²)` | pair absorption, **pair emission (squeezing)**, rescattering |
| `C₁, C₂` | `a†a†a`, `a†aa` | **`O(g)`** | **soft→soft splitting from the three-gluon vertex; carries no `ρ`**, Eq. (5.5) |
| `D₁,₂,₃` | four operators | `O(g²)` | partly expressible via `A` and `C`, Eqs. (5.6)–(5.7) |
| `E₁,₂,₃` | six operators | `O(g²)` | built from `C·C`, Eqs. (5.8)–(5.9) |

**The `O(g)` trilinear `C` is the structurally important feature.** It exists because the
three-gluon vertex connects one soft gluon to two soft gluons with no valence insertion at all —
a transition that costs one power of `g` and zero powers of `ρ`. Its presence means **`Ω` is not
a Gaussian unitary at any order**, and it is what makes the `O(g)` diagonalization in the paper's
§6 nontrivial: the coherent operator alone does not diagonalize `H₁`; `C` is needed too.

**The two-piece split of `B₂` is the physics of the fluctuation program.** Eq. (5.3) contains a
`ρρ` term — "two independent emissions correlated only through the source" — and an `f^{acb}ρ`
term from the three-gluon vertex, the genuine `1→2` splitting off a single charge, plus an
instantaneous piece from `H_gg-inst`. Only the second is irreducibly quantum. **Any claim of new
physics in the fluctuations should be traced to which piece is responsible.**

**Completeness of the basis.** The structures omitted from (4.1) are the ones forbidden by `k⁺`
conservation (`a†a†a†`, `aaa`, `a†a†a†a†`, …) or first arising at `O(g³)` (`a†a†a†a†a`, …). A
genuinely irreducible `2→3` transition would need a five-operator coefficient at `O(g)`; it does
not exist, because `2→3` is already generated by `C₁` acting with one spectator gluon. The basis
is complete at `O(g²)`.

## 1.3 Unitarity constraints

`Ω†Ω = 1` at each order in `g` relates the coefficients. The paper's version is Eqs. (4.3)–(4.5):
`Ω†⁽¹⁾ = −Ω⁽¹⁾` at `O(g)`, and `Ω⁽²⁾ + Ω†⁽²⁾ = −Ω†⁽¹⁾Ω⁽¹⁾` at `O(g²)`. Concretely:

* `A₁ = −A₂*` and `B₁ = −B₂*` (emission/absorption pairs),
* `B₃` and `D₃`, `E₃` are *fixed by the constraint equation* rather than by matching — the paper
  states this explicitly below Eq. (5.12),
* `N` is fixed by `⟨Ψ|Ψ⟩ = 1`; Eq. (5.1) gives `N = 1 − g²∫ ρᵇ(−p)ρᵇ(p)/(p⁺p²)`. **The
  normalization *is* the virtual correction**, and it must be kept — it is exactly what makes
  `⟨N⟩` finite mode-by-mode.
* The paper also writes `Ω = exp(iG)` with `G` Hermitian by construction, Eq. (5.11)–(5.12).

**Practical consequence for the observable:** although `Ω` has six coefficient families, the
soft-vacuum sector sees only three of them. See §1.4.

## 1.5 The key structural reduction

The claim in the first draft — "`Ω` is a Gaussian unitary at fixed `ρ`" — is **false**. `Ω`
contains the `O(g)` trilinear `C` and the `O(g²)` quartic and sextic structures `D`, `E`; it is
not the exponential of a quadratic form.

The conclusion nevertheless holds for the observable, for a sharper reason. **`Ω` is written
fully normal-ordered**, so every `C`, `D`, `E` structure carries at least one annihilation
operator on the right and therefore annihilates the soft vacuum. Acting on `|0⟩`:

```
Ω|0⟩  =  N|0⟩  +  ∫ A₂ a†|0⟩  +  ∫∫ B₂ a†a†|0⟩            exact through O(g²)
```

which has 0-, 1- and 2-gluon components only — i.e. it agrees with a **displaced squeezed vacuum**
through the order retained. So the Gaussian machinery below is not an approximation to `Ω`; it is
an exact parameterization of `Ω|0⟩` at this order, and it fails at `O(g³)`, where a three-gluon
component appears.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **The wave-function multiplicity program needs only Eqs. (5.1), (5.2), (5.3).** The paper's
   `C`, `D`, `E` play no role in `Ω|0⟩`.
2. **They become essential the moment the target acts.** After eikonal scattering `a → U a`, the
   annihilation operators no longer kill the state, and `C`, `D`, `E` all contribute to produced-
   gluon observables. That is Project E of Part VIII, and it is the natural consumer of the rest
   of the paper's results.

With that established: the soft state is Gaussian through `O(g²)`, so

```
Ω† a^a_i(k) Ω  =  ∫_q [ u(k,q) a(q)  +  v(k,q) a†(q) ]  +  α(k)
```

an *inhomogeneous Bogoliubov transformation*, with (schematically, suppressing colour and
polarization indices which ride along)

```
α  =  A₂  +  O(g³)                         [displacement, O(g)]
v  =  B₂  +  O(g⁴)                         [pair amplitude, O(g²)]
u  =  1 + O(g²)                            [O(1) + O(g²)]
```

and unitarity becomes the standard Bogoliubov relations `u u† − v v† = 1`, `u v^T = v u^T`.

**Everything about `N` follows from three objects**, evaluated at fixed `ρ`:

```
α_k         =  ⟨Ψ| a_k |Ψ⟩                     displacement            O(g)
n_{kl}      =  ⟨Ψ| a†_k a_l |Ψ⟩ − α*_k α_l     connected occupation    O(g⁴)   = (v v†)*_{kl}
m_{kl}      =  ⟨Ψ| a_k a_l |Ψ⟩ − α_k α_l       anomalous / pair        O(g²)   = (u v†)_{kl}
```

(indices `k` collapse momentum + colour + polarization.)

**The order assignment `m = O(g²)` while `n = O(g⁴)` is the pivot of this entire research
program.** The anomalous correlator is *one* power of the pair amplitude; the occupation
correction is *two*. Consequently the leading non-Poissonian effect is an **interference between
the classical Weizsäcker–Williams displacement and the quantum pair-emission amplitude**, of
order `α*α*m ~ g²·g² = g⁴` — precisely the order the paper's Introduction promises. It is *not*
the `|B₂|²` "two real gluons" term, which is `g⁸`-suppressed relative to `⟨N⟩²`. Anyone attacking
this problem by squaring the two-gluon emission amplitude is computing a subleading effect.

## 1.6 How `Ω` acts on Fock states

For completeness, since the moments can also be organized as a Fock-space sum:

```
Ω|0⟩  =  N [ |0⟩  +  ∫ A₂ a†|0⟩  +  ½∫∫ (A₂ A₂ + B₂) a†a†|0⟩  +  … ]
```

so the `n`-gluon component of the dressed state is

```
Ψ_n(k₁…k_n)  =  N · Sym [ ∏ A₂(k_i)  +  Σ_{pairs} B₂(k_i,k_j) ∏_{rest} A₂  +  … ]
```

i.e. a **coherent (product) part plus pairwise-correlated insertions**. This is the Fock-space
statement of "displaced squeezed state" and is the form in which the CGC literature usually
writes things. It is *much* worse for computing high moments than the Bogoliubov route — the
combinatorics of `Sym` explodes — which is why §1.5 is the recommended organizing principle. Use
the Fock form only for cross-checks at `n ≤ 3`.

## 1.7 Where the valence charge operators bite

Two distinct roles, often conflated:

**(a) `ρ` as a source (classical).** `A₂ ∝ b ∝ gρ/k`. This is what survives when `[ρ,ρ] → 0` and
gives the classical CGC picture.

**(b) `ρ` as a non-commuting operator.** `[ρ^a(x), ρ^b(y)] = ig f^{abc} δ²(x−y) ρ^c(x)`. Because
`f^{abc}` carries an explicit `g`, a single commutator costs `g` and converts `ρρ → ρ`. So:

* In `⟨N⟩` at `O(g⁴)`, reordering products produces `g·⟨ρρρ⟩ → ⟨ρρ⟩` terms that are the *same
  order* as genuine two-loop-like contributions. **They cannot be dropped.**
* This is where the "non-commuting valence colour charge operators" emphasized in the abstract
  actually do work. It is also the single most likely source of an algebra error, and the most
  likely target of a referee's technical objection. (The paper's own Eq. (4.28)–(4.29)
  "decomposition procedure" is exactly this step, done correctly.)

**Ordering prescription.** You must adopt and state one — e.g. all `ρ`'s to the right of all
`a†`'s, symmetrized in the valence indices — and show that observables are independent of it
(they must be, since `Ω` is a fixed operator; the ordering only redistributes content between
coefficients). A short appendix demonstrating ordering-independence of `⟨N⟩` at `O(g⁴)` would
pre-empt an obvious referee complaint.

## 1.8 How expectation values are computed — the two-stage structure

```
⟨⟨ f(N) ⟩⟩   =   ∫ Dρ  W_Y[ρ]   ⟨ f(N) ⟩_ρ
                 └──── stage 2 ────┘ └─ stage 1 ─┘
```

* **Stage 1 (Fock).** At fixed `ρ`, the state is Gaussian through `O(g²)` ⇒ Wick's theorem in
  `(α, n, m)` gives every moment in closed form. This stage is *exactly solvable*.
* **Stage 2 (colour).** `W_Y[ρ]` is the CGC weight: MV (Gaussian) at the initial condition,
  JIMWLK-evolved thereafter. This stage produces `⟨ρ^{2p}⟩` correlators.

The correct way to combine them is the **law of total cumulance**, which cleanly separates
"quantum Fock-space fluctuation" from "classical colour-charge fluctuation":

```
κ₁ = E_ρ[ μ(ρ) ]
κ₂ = E_ρ[ κ₂^Fock(ρ) ]  +  Var_ρ[ μ(ρ) ]
κ₃ = E_ρ[ κ₃^Fock(ρ) ]  +  3 Cov_ρ[ μ(ρ), κ₂^Fock(ρ) ]  +  κ₃^ρ[ μ(ρ) ]
κ₄ = E_ρ[ κ₄^Fock ] + 4Cov_ρ[μ, κ₃^Fock] + 3Var_ρ[κ₂^Fock]
     + 6 κ₃^ρ-type mixed terms + κ₄^ρ[ μ(ρ) ]
```
with `μ(ρ) ≡ ⟨N⟩_ρ`.

This decomposition is, in my judgement, **the single most publishable organizational idea in the
whole program**, because it makes the following statement provable rather than rhetorical:

> Every term containing `κ_p^ρ[μ]` is classical CGC colour-charge fluctuation — known physics,
> parametrically enhanced by the number of sources. Every term containing `κ_p^Fock` is genuinely
> quantum, generated by the `O(g²)` pair-emission coefficient `B₂` of `Ω`, and is *not* enhanced
> by the source density. The two are distinguishable by their `N_c`, `S_⊥`, and `k_⊥` dependence.

## 1.9 Which correlators of `ρ` you will need

| Observable | Highest `ρ` correlator | MV (Gaussian) status |
|---|---|---|
| `⟨N⟩` at `O(g²)` | `⟨ρρ⟩` | `= g²μ² δ^{ab} δ²(x−y)`, trivial |
| `⟨N⟩` at `O(g³)` | `⟨ρρρ⟩` | **vanishes** in MV (C-even weight); nonzero only with odderon / `d^{abc}` odd terms |
| `⟨N⟩` at `O(g⁴)` | `⟨ρρρρ⟩` | Wick → 3 pairings |
| `⟨N²⟩` | `⟨ρ⁴⟩` | 3 pairings |
| `⟨N³⟩` | `⟨ρ⁶⟩` | 15 pairings |
| `⟨N⁴⟩` | `⟨ρ⁸⟩` | 105 pairings |

The `(2p−1)!!` growth looks alarming but is not the real cost: in cumulants all disconnected
pairings cancel by construction, and the surviving connected topologies number `O(p!)` at worst.
The genuine cost is that each connected topology carries a distinct transverse-momentum
convolution (see Part V).

**The `⟨N⟩` at `O(g³)` entry is worth pausing on.** It is the first place a C-odd (odderon)
correlator enters the gluon multiplicity. In MV it is zero; in a model with a nonzero
`⟨ρ^aρ^bρ^c⟩ d^{abc}` it is not. That is a small, self-contained, genuinely novel calculation —
see Part VIII, Project D.

## Summary of Part I

1. `Ω` is *not* Gaussian — the `O(g)` trilinear `C` and the `O(g²)` `D`, `E` see to that. But it
   is written fully normal-ordered, so `Ω|0⟩` is exactly a truncated displaced squeezed state
   through `O(g²)`, parameterized by `N`, `A₂`, `B₂` alone.
2. At fixed classical `ρ`, all moments follow from `(α, n, m)` by Wick's theorem. Exactly solvable.
3. `m = O(g²)` but `n = O(g⁴)`. The leading non-Poissonian effect is the **displacement–squeezing
   interference** `α*α*m ~ O(g⁴)`, not `|B₂|²`.
4. `ρ` non-commutativity contributes at the *same* order as the NLO kernels and must be retained;
   an ordering-independence check is mandatory.
5. The law of total cumulance separates quantum (Fock) from classical (colour) fluctuations. Use
   it as the paper's organizing principle.
