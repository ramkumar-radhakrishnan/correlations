# Part VI — Proposed numerical studies

For each: why it matters, whether it exists in the literature, and what behaviour to expect.
"Novel?" is judged against the survey in Part VII.

---

### P1. Multiplicity distribution `P(N)`, fixed single configuration

**Why.** The most direct display of the `O(g⁴)` effect with nothing averaged away.
**In the literature?** `P(N)` from CGC exists (Gelis–Lappi–McLerran, Glasma flux tubes) but
**always after colour averaging and always at classical order**. A fixed-configuration `P(N)`
from an NLO wave function does not exist. **Novel.**
**Expect.** Nearly Poisson; deviation at the `1/N_dof` level, i.e. a few percent for realistic
`Q_s`. Plot `P(N)/P_Poisson(N)` — a mild `S`-shape (excess in the tails if `C₂ > 0`, depletion if
`C₂ < 0`). Do **not** plot `P(N)` raw; the deviation is invisible.

### P2. `c₂ = C₂/⟨N⟩²` versus `Q_s`

**Why.** `1/c₂` is the negative-binomial `k`. This is the observable with the most direct
experimental contact.
**In the literature?** `k_NBD(Q_s)` exists at classical order (`k ∝ (N_c²−1)Q_s²S_⊥/2π`,
Gelis–Lappi–McLerran). The `O(g⁴)` quantum correction to it does **not** exist. **Novel as a
correction.**
**Expect.** `c₂ ≈ a/(N_c²−1)S_⊥Q_s² · [1 + b·α_s(Q_s) + …]`. The interesting question is the sign
and size of `b`. Plot `c₂ · (N_c²−1)S_⊥Q_s²` versus `Q_s` — should be flat at LO, and its slope
*is* the new physics. **This is the money plot of the project.**

### P3. `κ₂/⟨N⟩ − 1` versus `Q_s`, decomposed

**Why.** Shows quantum vs classical contributions side by side (law of total cumulance).
**In the literature?** The decomposition itself is not in the CGC literature. **Novel.**
**Expect.** Classical part positive and dominant at large `S_⊥Q_s²`; quantum part smaller and
possibly of either sign. **If the quantum part is everywhere `< 1%` of the classical part, the
project's physics case collapses — this plot is the go/no-go test.** Run it early.

### P4. Cumulants versus rapidity `Y`

**Why.** Tests whether the `Y`-dependence is generated by JIMWLK (the consistency check of
Part III §3.5b).
**In the literature?** `Y`-dependence of `⟨N⟩` is standard. `Y`-dependence of `c₂` from an NLO
operator is **novel**.
**Expect.** `⟨N⟩` grows like `e^{ω Y}`; `Q_s²(Y) ∝ e^{λY}` with `λ ≈ 0.2–0.3`; hence
`c₂ ∝ e^{−λY}` — **cumulant ratios decrease with rapidity/energy**, i.e. multiplicity
distributions narrow (relative to the mean) as energy grows. This is a clean, falsifiable
qualitative prediction and it can be confronted with the `√s`-dependence of NBD `k` in `pp` data
(where `k` is observed to *decrease* with `√s`, i.e. `c₂` *increases* — **note the tension**, and
address it: the data are for produced hadrons in a rapidity window, not for the projectile wave
function, and the discrepancy is likely a fragmentation/geometry effect. Discuss it; do not hide
it).

### P5. Cumulants versus infrared cutoff `Λ_IR`

**Why.** Establishes which observables are IR-safe. Non-optional.
**In the literature?** Routine in CGC, but not for cumulants. **Novel in this context, low
impact.**
**Expect.** `⟨N⟩ ~ ln(Q_s/Λ_IR)`; `C₂` a different power. Plot `c₂` vs `Λ_IR` — if it plateaus,
you have an IR-safe observable and should say so loudly; if it does not, quote everything
`k_⊥`-differentially instead. **Run this before writing any abstract**, because it determines
what you are allowed to claim.

### P6. Cumulants versus UV cutoff / `k⁺` (factorization scale)

**Why.** The soft/valence separation is a scheme choice — the paper's `Λ` and `∨`; scheme
dependence must be shown to be JIMWLK-compensated.
**In the literature?** Standard for `⟨N⟩`. **Novel for `c₂`.**
**Expect.** Residual `ln ∨` dependence at `O(g⁴)` that cancels against the LO term evolved by
one step of JIMWLK. Demonstrating that cancellation numerically is a strong technical result and
answers the "your number is scheme-dependent" objection directly. (Note the paper itself raises
`∨`-independence in §2.3 and never returns to it, so this would also close a gap there.)

### P7. `c₂` versus transverse momentum `k_⊥`

**Why.** IR-safe alternative to the integrated cumulant; and the shape carries the discriminating
information.
**In the literature?** `k_⊥`-differential multiplicity correlations exist in the glasma-graph
literature; `k_⊥`-differential *factorial cumulants of an NLO wave function* do not. **Novel.**
**Expect.** `c₂(k_⊥)` roughly flat below `Q_s`, rising above it, because the pair-emission kernel
`B₂` is harder than the WW field `A₂`. A rise of the correlation strength with `k_⊥` above `Q_s`
is a distinctive signature.

### P8. Two-gluon correlation versus `Δφ` — **the discriminator plot**

**Why.** Separates the new pair-emission mechanism (away side) from Bose enhancement (near side).
See Part IV §4.3.
**In the literature?** Near-side ridge from Bose enhancement: Altinoluk–Armesto–Beuf–Kovner–
Lublinsky and successors. **An away-side component from the NLO squeezing kernel of the wave
function is not in the literature.** **Most novel item in the list.**
**Expect.** `Γ(Δφ)` with a near-side peak (`O(g⁶)`, Bose) and an away-side peak (`O(g⁴)`, pair
emission) — so at `O(g⁴)` the away-side one should actually **dominate**. That is a surprising and
highly quotable statement, and it is the result most likely to get the paper cited outside the
small-`x` formalism community. **Verify it carefully; if true it is the abstract.**

### P9. Dependence on source density `μ²` / `N_s`

**Why.** Verifies the `1/N_dof` scaling and separates "more sources" from "stronger sources".
**In the literature?** The scaling law is folklore; an explicit NLO check is **novel but minor**.
**Expect.** `c₂ · N_s → const`. A deviation signals an error in the calculation. **Use as a code
validation plot, put it in an appendix.**

### P10. KNO function `Ψ(z) = ⟨N⟩P(N)`, `z = N/⟨N⟩`, at several `Y`

**Why.** Tests KNO scaling and its violation.
**In the literature?** KNO from CGC/dipole models: yes (nearly-Gaussian action; Mueller–Kharzeev–
Levin; Le–Mueller–Munier). **KNO from an NLO wave function: no.** **Novel.**
**Expect.** Approximate KNO with violations of relative size `α_s(Q_s)`. Plot `Ψ(z)` at three `Y`
values; the spread between curves *is* the violation.

### P11. `c₃`, `c₄` (translationally invariant model only)

**Why.** Completeness; contact with the fluctuation-observable community.
**In the literature?** Not for CGC wave functions. **Novel but incomplete** (Part II §2.6).
**Expect.** `c_p ~ N_dof^{1−p}`, alternating signs plausible. **Must be labelled "Gaussian
contribution only; the `O(g⁶)` three-gluon amplitude is not included."** If you cannot bring
yourself to write that sentence, do not make the plot.

### P12. Sign map of `C₂` in the `(k_⊥, Q_s)` plane

**Why.** Locates any sub-Poissonian region — the genuinely non-classical regime.
**In the literature?** Nothing comparable. **Novel.**
**Expect.** Most of the plane super-Poissonian; possibly a sub-Poissonian island at large `k_⊥`
where the interference term dominates over `|B₂|²`. If such a region exists, this becomes the
paper's most striking figure — "the CGC wave function is locally sub-Poissonian", with a direct
quantum-optics reading (non-classical light).
