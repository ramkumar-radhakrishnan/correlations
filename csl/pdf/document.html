Digitized Chiral Soliton Lattice Hamiltonian

Generalization to arbitrary qubits per site and arbitrary lattice size

Extends the one-qubit, two-site derivation of CSL — quantum computing (eqs. 1.1–1.21) to $n_q$ qubits per site ($Q=2^{n_q}$ field values) and $N$ sites, with explicit results for $(n_q,N)=(1,4)$, $(2,2)$ and $(2,4)$.
All formulae verified numerically — 57/57 checks, including entry-by-entry reproduction of eq. (1.21).

1. Setup and the generalized Hamiltonian

The classical effective Hamiltonian density in one spatial dimension, with the magnetic shorthand $H\equiv\mu B/(4\pi^2 f_\pi^2)$, promotes to the quantum energy

$$\hat E=f_\pi^2\int dx\left[\frac{\Pi^2}{2f_\pi^4}+\tfrac12(\partial_x\phi)^2 +m_\pi^2(1-\cos\phi)-H\,\partial_x\phi\right].$$

Discretizing on $N$ sites of spacing $a$, $x=na$ with $n=1,\dots,N$, and compactifying the two $\phi$-derivative terms exactly as in eq. (1.6),

$$\tfrac12(\Delta\phi_n)^2\;\longrightarrow\;1-\cos\Delta\phi_n, \qquad \Delta\phi_n\;\longrightarrow\;\sin\Delta\phi_n, \qquad \Delta\phi_n\equiv\phi_{n+1}-\phi_n,$$

gives the general lattice Hamiltonian, valid for any number of sites and any field digitization:

$$\hat H=t\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat\Pi_n^{2} \;+\;J\sum_{\langle n,n+1\rangle}\bigl[\mathbb 1-\cos\Delta\phi_n\bigr] \;+\;h\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigl[\mathbb 1-\cos\phi_n\bigr] \;-\;\kappa\sum_{\langle n,n+1\rangle}\sin\Delta\phi_n$$ $$t=\frac{1}{2f_\pi^2a},\qquad J=\frac{f_\pi^2}{a},\qquad h=f_\pi^2m_\pi^2a,\qquad \kappa=f_\pi^2H=\frac{\mu B}{4\pi^2}.$$

The bond sum runs over $N-1$ bonds for open boundary conditions — the case used in eq. (1.20), where the single $N=2$ bond appears once — or over $N$ bonds for periodic boundaries. Note that $\kappa$ carries no factor of $a$: the magnetic term is topological in origin, which is why it is the one coupling that does not scale with the lattice spacing.

2. Digitization: clock and shift operators

The compact angle $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ is replaced by $Q$ equally spaced points $\phi_k=2\pi k/Q$, $k=0,\dots,Q-1$, and the integer $k$ is stored in binary on the $n_q$-qubit site register, $k=\sum_{j=0}^{n_q-1}2^jb_j$. Define the clock and shift operators

$$\hat Z_Q\,|k\rangle=\omega^k|k\rangle\;\;\bigl(=e^{i\hat\phi}\bigr), \qquad \hat X_Q\,|k\rangle=|k+1 \bmod Q\rangle, \qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/Q}.$$

Every term of the Hamiltonian is then exact for any $Q$:

termoperator form
$\cos\phi_n$$\tfrac12\bigl(\hat Z_n+\hat Z_n^\dagger\bigr)=\mathrm{Re}\,\hat Z_n$
$\sin\phi_n$$\tfrac{1}{2i}\bigl(\hat Z_n-\hat Z_n^\dagger\bigr)=\mathrm{Im}\,\hat Z_n$
$\cos\Delta\phi_n$$\tfrac12\bigl(\hat Z_{n+1}\hat Z_n^\dagger+\hat Z_{n+1}^\dagger\hat Z_n\bigr)$
$\sin\Delta\phi_n$$\tfrac{1}{2i}\bigl(\hat Z_{n+1}\hat Z_n^\dagger-\hat Z_{n+1}^\dagger\hat Z_n\bigr)$

For $Q=2$ these collapse to $\cos\phi=Z$, $\cos\Delta\phi=Z_nZ_{n+1}$ and $\sin\Delta\phi=0$, i.e. eqs. (1.10), (1.17) and (1.18) of the original note. The angle-addition step used there, $\cos(\phi_{n+1}-\phi_n)=\cos\phi_{n+1}\cos\phi_n +\sin\phi_{n+1}\sin\phi_n$, is simply the real part of $\hat Z_{n+1}\hat Z_n^\dagger$.

3. The kinetic term at arbitrary $Q$

This is the piece that requires genuine generalization: eq. (1.15) was obtained by a $2\times2$ similarity transformation, which does not extend. The conjugate momentum $\hat\Pi=-i\partial_\phi$ on the compact rotor has integer eigenvalues $l$, taken in the symmetric window

$$l\in\Bigl\{-\tfrac{Q}{2},\,\dots,\,\tfrac{Q}{2}-1\Bigr\}, \qquad\text{which for }Q=2\text{ is }\{0,-1\}\text{ — eq.\ (1.11)},$$

with position and momentum bases related by the discrete Fourier transform $|l\rangle=Q^{-1/2}\sum_k\omega^{kl}|k\rangle$, eq. (1.12). Since $\hat X_Q|l\rangle=\omega^{-l}|l\rangle$, any function of $l$ is a polynomial in the shift operator. Writing $\hat\Pi^2=\sum_l l^2|l\rangle\langle l|$ in the position basis gives $\hat\Pi^2=\sum_{m=0}^{Q-1}c_m\hat X_Q^{\,m}$ with $c_m=\tfrac1Q\sum_l l^2\omega^{lm}$. Evaluating that sum with the generating function $f(x)=\sum_{l=-Q/2}^{Q/2-1}x^l$, which vanishes at $x=\omega^m$ for $m\not\equiv0$,

$$c_m=\frac1Q\,(x\partial_x)^2f\Big|_{x=\omega^m} =\frac{-2(-1)^m\,\omega^m}{(\omega^m-1)^2} =\frac{(-1)^m}{2\sin^2(\pi m/Q)},$$

while $\sum_l l^2=\tfrac{1}{12}Q(Q-1)(Q-2)+\tfrac14 Q^2$ fixes the diagonal:

$$\hat\Pi^2=\frac{Q^2+2}{12}\,\mathbb 1 \;+\;\sum_{m=1}^{Q-1}\frac{(-1)^m}{2\sin^2(\pi m/Q)}\;\hat X_Q^{\,m}.$$

Hermiticity is automatic because $c_m=c_{Q-m}$ and $\hat X_Q^{\,Q-m}=(\hat X_Q^{\,m})^\dagger$; the identity coefficient also satisfies $c_0=-\sum_{m\neq0}c_m$, which is just the statement that the $l=0$ eigenvalue vanishes. Two independent checks:

  • At $Q=2$: $\hat\Pi^2=\tfrac12(\mathbb 1-X)$, so $t\hat\Pi^2=\tfrac{1}{4f_\pi^2a}(\mathbb 1-X)$ — exactly eq. (1.15).
  • The diagonal element is $\langle k|\hat\Pi^2|k\rangle=Q^2/12+1/6$, which is the Fornberg pseudo-spectral second-derivative matrix on a periodic grid — as it must be, since $\hat\Pi^2=-\partial_\phi^2$.

At $Q=4$ the expansion terminates after three terms, $\hat\Pi^2=\tfrac32\mathbb 1-(\hat X+\hat X^\dagger)+\tfrac12\hat X^2$, with spectrum $\{0,1,1,4\}=l^2$ for $l\in\{0,\pm1,-2\}$.

4. Qubit encoding

Under the binary encoding the clock operator factorizes into single-qubit phase gates, and the top qubit contributes a bare $Z$:

$$\hat Z_Q=\bigotimes_{j=0}^{n_q-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\omega^{2^j}\end{pmatrix}_{\!j} = i\,e^{-i\pi/Q}\;Z_{n_q-1}\prod_{j=0}^{n_q-2} \Bigl[\cos\tfrac{\pi 2^j}{Q}\,\mathbb 1-i\sin\tfrac{\pi 2^j}{Q}\,Z_j\Bigr].$$

Expanding the product over subsets $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n_q-2\}$ gives closed-form Pauli series for both trigonometric functions,

$$\cos\hat\phi=\sum_{S}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{Q}+|S|\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)A_S\; Z_{n_q-1}\!\!\prod_{j\in S}\!Z_j, \qquad \sin\hat\phi=\sum_{S}\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{Q}+|S|\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)A_S\; Z_{n_q-1}\!\!\prod_{j\in S}\!Z_j,$$ $$A_S=\prod_{j\in S}\sin\tfrac{\pi 2^j}{Q}\;\prod_{j\notin S}\cos\tfrac{\pi 2^j}{Q}.$$

Each carries exactly $Q/2$ Pauli-$Z$ strings, every one of them containing $Z_{n_q-1}$ — the shift $\phi\to\phi+\pi$ flips the most significant bit and flips the sign of both functions, so neither can contain a string that is even under it.

The shift $\hat X_Q$ is the mod-$Q$ increment, a cascade of multi-controlled NOTs. Its Pauli expansion has $3^{\,n_q-1}+1$ strings, so for $n_q\gtrsim3$ one should not expand it at all: implement $e^{-i\tau t\hat\Pi^2}$ as $\mathrm{QFT}\to$ diagonal $e^{-i\tau t\,l^2}$ phases $\to\mathrm{QFT}^\dagger$, costing $O(n_q^2)$ gates.

The one-qubit case, $n_q=1$ ($Q=2$)

$$\cos\phi_n=Z_n,\qquad \sin\phi_n=0,\qquad \hat\Pi_n^2=\tfrac12(\mathbb 1-X_n).$$

The two-qubit case, $n_q=2$ ($Q=4$)

Writing $Z_{n,1}$ and $Z_{n,0}$ for the most and least significant qubit of site $n$ (so $\phi=0,\tfrac\pi2,\pi,\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ for $|b_1b_0\rangle=|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$):

$$\cos\phi_n=\tfrac12\bigl(Z_{n,1}+Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}\bigr),\qquad \sin\phi_n=\tfrac12\bigl(Z_{n,1}-Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}\bigr),$$ $$\hat\Pi_n^2=\tfrac32\mathbb 1-X_{n,0}-X_{n,1}X_{n,0}+\tfrac12X_{n,1},$$ $$\cos\Delta\phi_n=\tfrac12\bigl(Z_{n,1}Z_{n+1,1}+Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}Z_{n+1,1}Z_{n+1,0}\bigr),$$ $$\sin\Delta\phi_n=\tfrac12\,Z_{n,1}Z_{n+1,1}\bigl(Z_{n,0}-Z_{n+1,0}\bigr).$$

The mass term reads $m_\pi^2a\,\mathrm{diag}(0,1,2,1)$, the $Q=4$ analogue of eq. (1.9), and $\hat\Pi_n^2$ above has the required spectrum $\{0,1,1,4\}$.

Physics remark. At $Q=2$ one has $\Delta\phi\in\{0,\pm\pi\}$, so $\sin\Delta\phi\equiv0$ and the coupling $\kappa$ drops out of the Hamiltonian identically, at any $N$. Eq. (1.18) is therefore not an artifact of the two-site truncation: a one-qubit register cannot see the magnetic field at all, and no chiral soliton lattice can form. The CSL first becomes accessible at $n_q=2$. Relatedly, the linear term $-\kappa\sum_n\Delta\phi_n$ telescopes to a boundary term, whereas the compactified $\sin\Delta\phi_n$ does not — and it is exactly that failure to telescope which allows the soliton lattice to form.

5. Results

Sites are labelled $n=1,\dots,N$ with open boundary conditions. All coefficients are exact rationals times $t$, $J$, $h$, $\kappa$.

Reference: $n_q=1$, $N=2$ — the original case

$$\hat H=t\sum_{n=1}^{2}\tfrac12\bigl(\mathbb 1-X_n\bigr)+J\bigl(\mathbb 1-Z_1Z_2\bigr) +h\sum_{n=1}^{2}\bigl(\mathbb 1-Z_n\bigr)$$

With $t=1/(2f_\pi^2a)$ this reproduces eq. (1.20), and its matrix reproduces eq. (1.21) entry by entry.

A.  One qubit, four sites: $n_q=1$, $N=4$

4 qubits, $16\times16$, 12 Pauli strings, $\kappa$ absent.

$$\hat H=(2t+3J+4h)\,\mathbb 1 -\frac{t}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}X_n -J\sum_{n=1}^{3}Z_nZ_{n+1} -h\sum_{n=1}^{4}Z_n$$

This is an open transverse-field Ising chain with a longitudinal field. In the computational basis the diagonal element of the configuration $(k_1\ldots k_4)$, $k_n\in\{0,1\}$, is $2t+2J\,\#\{n:k_n\neq k_{n+1}\}+2h\,\#\{n:k_n=1\}$, and the only off-diagonal elements are $-t/2$ between configurations differing at a single site.

B.  Two qubits, two sites: $n_q=2$, $N=2$

4 qubits, $16\times16$, 15 Pauli strings. This is the smallest system in which the magnetic term is non-trivial.

$$\hat H=(3t+J+2h)\,\mathbb 1 +t\sum_{n=1}^{2}\Bigl[-X_{n,0}+\tfrac12X_{n,1}-X_{n,1}X_{n,0}\Bigr]$$ $$-\frac{J}{2}\Bigl[Z_{1,1}Z_{2,1}+Z_{1,1}Z_{1,0}Z_{2,1}Z_{2,0}\Bigr] -\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{2}\Bigl[Z_{n,1}+Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}\Bigr]$$ $$+\frac{\kappa}{2}\Bigl[Z_{1,1}Z_{2,1}Z_{2,0}-Z_{1,1}Z_{1,0}Z_{2,1}\Bigr]$$

Equivalently, in the basis $|k_1k_2\rangle$ the diagonal is

$$3t+J\Bigl[1-\cos\tfrac{\pi(k_2-k_1)}{2}\Bigr] +h\sum_{n=1}^{2}\Bigl[1-\cos\tfrac{\pi k_n}{2}\Bigr] -\kappa\sin\tfrac{\pi(k_2-k_1)}{2},$$

with off-diagonal elements $-t$ for $\Delta k=\pm1$ and $+t/2$ for $\Delta k=\pm2$ on a single site.

C.  Two qubits, four sites: $n_q=2$, $N=4$

8 qubits, $256\times256$, 33 Pauli strings, maximum Pauli weight 4.

$$\hat H=(6t+3J+4h)\,\mathbb 1 +t\sum_{n=1}^{4}\Bigl[-X_{n,0}+\tfrac12X_{n,1}-X_{n,1}X_{n,0}\Bigr]$$ $$-\frac{J}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{3}\Bigl[Z_{n,1}Z_{n+1,1}+Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}Z_{n+1,1}Z_{n+1,0}\Bigr] -\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}\Bigl[Z_{n,1}+Z_{n,1}Z_{n,0}\Bigr]$$ $$-\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{3}Z_{n,1}Z_{n+1,1}\Bigl[Z_{n,0}-Z_{n+1,0}\Bigr]$$

The 32 non-identity strings split as 12 kinetic (one- and two-local $X$), 6 gradient (two- and four-local $Z$), 8 mass (one- and two-local $Z$) and 6 magnetic (three-local $Z$). The whole kinetic sector mutually commutes and the whole potential sector is diagonal, so a Trotter step needs only two layers.

The bond counts follow because $\cos\phi$ and $\sin\phi$ each carry $Q/2$ strings, so $\cos\Delta\phi_n=C_{n+1}C_n+S_{n+1}S_n$ starts from $2(Q/2)^2$ products of which exactly half cancel. The $n_q=1$ column is the degenerate case: $\sin\phi\equiv0$ collapses the gradient term to one string per bond and kills the magnetic term entirely. Total qubits $Nn_q$; Hilbert dimension $2^{Nn_q}=Q^N$.

8. Verification

The accompanying code runs 57 independent checks, all passing:

  • The $n_q=1$, $N=2$ Hamiltonian matrix matches eq. (1.21) entry by entry.
  • The $Q=2$ limits of every general formula reproduce eqs. (1.10), (1.15), (1.17), (1.18).
  • $\hat\Pi^2=\sum_m c_m\hat X^m$ has spectrum exactly $\{l^2\}$ for $Q$ up to 32, and diagonal $Q^2/12+1/6$.
  • Both product forms of $\hat Z_Q$, and the closed-form Pauli series for $\cos\phi$ and $\sin\phi$, verified up to $n_q=5$.
  • The $Q=4$ operator identities of §4, and Hermiticity plus reality of all Pauli coefficients for the three cases of §5.
