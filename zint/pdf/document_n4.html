2. The one thing to check: the overall $1/k^{+}$

In your first line the denominator is $i\,k\cdot(y'-z)$ — not $i\,k\cdot(y'-z)/k^{+}$. That missing $k^{+}$ is the fingerprint: it was already spent by $dp^{+}=k^{+}d\xi$ (equivalently, you integrated $d\xi$ directly). So the $\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ that you factored out of $\frac{1}{p^{+}+k^{+}}=\frac{1}{k^{+}(1+\xi)}$ has already been cancelled by the measure.

Concretely: $\frac{1}{k^{+}}\int dp^{+}\big[\cdots\big]=\int d\xi\big[\cdots\big]=\{\text{your braces}\}$, with no $1/k^{+}$ left over.

So the $\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ standing in front of your braces is legitimate only if it was in the prefactor before the bracket — e.g. from the $\frac{1}{2k^{+}}$ of the gluon phase space. If it is the one you pulled out of the bracket, the answer is too small by $k^{+}$. Trace it back to the pre–integration expression; that is a one–line check.

3. Extracting the rapidity logarithm

Two expansions do all the work. For $\varepsilon\to0^{+}$,

$$\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\varepsilon)=\gamma_{E}+\log\big(i\kappa\varepsilon\big)+O(\kappa\varepsilon), \qquad \mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa X)\xrightarrow[X\to\infty]{}-i\pi\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\kappa .$$

The second is worth pausing on: $\mathrm{Ei}$ at large imaginary argument does not go to zero, it goes to the constant $\mp i\pi$ ($|{\rm Ei}(-i\,10^{6})|=3.141592$). Dropping the upper limit is therefore wrong by $i\pi$.

Line by line, as $\Lambda\to0$ and $\vee\to\infty$

Third line, $A/(1+\xi)$: no rapidity log. The denominator $1+\xi$ is regular at $\xi=0$, so $\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa(1+\lambda))\to\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa)$ smoothly:

$$\longrightarrow\;e^{i\kappa}\big[-i\pi\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\kappa-\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa)\big].$$

First line, $-C$: no rapidity log. $e^{-i\kappa\lambda}\to1$, and the $\Xi$ piece is bounded and oscillatory (Riemann–Lebesgue kills it under the transverse integration):

$$\longrightarrow\;\frac{i}{\kappa}\;+\;\text{(oscillatory)}.$$

This also retires the worry I raised last time that this term looked linear in $\vee$. It is not — the phase saves it.

Second line, $-B/\xi$: the sole rapidity divergence. In the double limit the bracket collapses to a single logarithm,

$$-\big[\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\Xi)-\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\lambda)\big] \;\longrightarrow\;\gamma_{E}+\log\big(i\kappa\lambda\big) \;=\;\boxed{\;-\,\ell_{k}\;+\;\gamma_{E}+\log\!\big(i\,k\cdot(y'-z)\big)\;}$$

verified to $10^{-9}$ for both signs of $\kappa$ (the $\log$ is the principal branch, and it carries the $\pm i\pi/2$ automatically). The coefficient of $\ell_{k}$ is $-1$, and it sits on the structure $B$ alone.

4. What multiplies $\ell_{k}$ is the BK kernel

Strip the $\ell_{k}$ off and look at what it multiplies:

$$-\,\ell_{k}\;\underbrace{\frac{(x-z)\cdot(y'-z)}{(x-z)^{2}\,(y'-z)^{2}}}_{\textstyle \mathcal K(x,y';z)}\;\times\; \underbrace{\frac{(x'-y')\cdot(y-w)}{(x'-y')^{2}\,(y-w)^{2}}}_{\text{no }z\text{: LO structure}}$$

The first factor is exactly the real–emission piece of the Balitsky–Kovchegov / JIMWLK dipole kernel, with $z$ the emission point and $x,y'$ the two legs. That is the strongest available check that the $p^{+}$ integration is right: the rapidity divergence had to come out proportional to the evolution kernel, and it does.

And this is where last note's transverse IR log goes

In the previous note I found a logarithmic divergence of the $z$–integration at large $|z|$, of coefficient $-\pi\delta^{mj}$, and observed that it is tied to $\xi\to0$. The full BK kernel is

$$\mathcal M(x,y';z)=\frac{(x-y')^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}} =\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(y'-z)^{2}}-2\,\mathcal K(x,y';z),$$

and the two $1/(\cdot)^{2}$ terms are the virtual (self–energy / same–side) contributions of the same order, which are not in the row you wrote. Their large–$|z|$ angular averages are what cancel the divergence:

5. What to do next

  1. Settle the $1/k^{+}$ (§2) against the pre–integration expression.
  2. Take $\vee\to\infty$ honestly. Keep the $-i\pi\,\mathrm{sgn}\kappa$ from $\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\Xi)$ — it is a finite constant, not zero, and it contributes to the imaginary (absorptive) part.
  3. Write the three brackets in their limiting forms (§3). You then have a clean split: one term $\propto\ell_{k}$, everything else $\ell_{k}$–independent.
  4. Collect the virtual partners so that $-2\mathcal K\to\mathcal M$. Until you do, the $z$–integral is IR divergent and no separation into “finite” pieces is meaningful.
  5. Reduce the colour structure. $f^{c'd'a}$ contracted with $[UU\rho-UU\rho]\,[UUU\rho\rho-U\rho\rho]$ has to be brought to dipole / quadrupole operators before the $\ell_{k}$ coefficient can be compared with $\mathcal H_{\rm JIMWLK}$. The check to aim for is $$\frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\ell_{k}}=-\,\ell_{k}\; \mathcal H_{\rm JIMWLK}\otimes\frac{d^{3}N_{\rm LO}}{d^{3}k}.$$
  6. Absorb, don't cancel. Once (5) holds, define the target weight functional at rapidity $Y=\ell_{k}$ and subtract; what remains is the genuine NLO impact factor.
  7. Then do the transverse integrals with the master $B^{mj}(Q,c)$ of the previous note — but note the phase is now gone (you integrated $p^{+}$ first), so the roles of $\Lambda$ and $Q$ are swapped: $\Lambda$ is what cuts the large–separation region, and the leftover $\log(i\,k\cdot(y'-z))$ from §3 is what turns the single $\ell_{k}$ into the double log $\ell_{k}^{2}$ found earlier.
