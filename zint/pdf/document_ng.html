The row with two $\mathfrak C$ vertices

Assembly check — where the transverse divergence really is — dimensional regularisation of the $z$ integral — the $p^{+}$ integral with a $+$ prescription

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $r\equiv y'-y$,  $\kappa\equiv\dfrac{k\cdot r}{k^{+}}$,  $P_{\vee}\equiv\vee-k^{+}$,  $d=2-2\epsilon$

Verdict in one paragraph. The prefactor, the two $\delta^{(2)}$'s, the $w,w'$ integrations and the full tensor contraction of the two $\mathfrak C$ brackets are all correct — every step reproduces your quoted four–term bracket exactly (§1). Four bookkeeping corrections are needed: a missing $\boldsymbol{N_{c}}$ from the colour reduction, two transposed adjoint indices, $\int_{z,w,w'}\!\to\!\int_{z}$ in the last two lines, and a missing $x'$ in the measure. On the divergences: there is no ultraviolet divergence anywhere in the transverse integrand — and in particular none at $y\to y'$ (§2). What is divergent is the infrared end of the $z$ integral, $|z|\to\infty$, because the colour reduction has removed every $U(z)$ and the phase never touches $z$. Dimensional regularisation in $d=2-2\epsilon$ with $\epsilon>0$ handles it, and — this is the point — the same $\frac{1}{\epsilon}(r^{2})^{-\epsilon}$ that carries the pole is the $\log\frac{1}{(y-y')^{2}}$ you were worried about (§4). The $2/k^{+}$ term then cancels identically (§5), and the $+$ prescription goes through cleanly on what is left (§6). Final result boxed in §7.

1. The assembly is correct

1.1 Prefactor

With $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}=-\frac{ig}{\sqrt2\pi\sqrt S}$, $\mathfrak A=+\frac{ig}{\sqrt2\pi\sqrt S}$ and two $\mathfrak C$'s each carrying $-\frac{g}{2\pi\sqrt{2S}}\frac{\sqrt{p^{+}(S-p^{+})}}{S}$:

$$(-i)(-1)(-1)(+i)=+1\quad\text{(real, as your }+\tfrac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\text{ requires)},$$ $$\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\sqrt2\pi)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt2)^{2}} =\frac{1}{32\pi^{7}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}\ \checkmark,$$ $$\frac{1}{S}\cdot\frac{p^{+}(S-p^{+})}{S^{3}}=\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{4}}\ \checkmark \qquad(S-p^{+}=k^{+}).$$

1.2 The two deltas and the $w,w'$ integrations

$$\delta^{(2)}\!\Big[y-w+\tfrac{p^{+}}{S}(w-z)\Big]\ \Longrightarrow\ w=\frac{S\,y-p^{+}z}{k^{+}},\qquad w-z=\frac{S}{k^{+}}\,(y-z),$$ so each WW kernel $\frac{(w-z)^{m}}{(w-z)^{2}}\to\frac{k^{+}}{S}\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}}$ (two of them) and each delta contributes a Jacobian $(S/k^{+})^{2}$ (two of them): $$\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{4}}\cdot\Big(\frac{k^{+}}{S}\Big)^{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{S}{k^{+}}\Big)^{4} =\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}\ \checkmark$$ which is exactly your overall $\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ with a $\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}$ left inside the bracket. And $w'-w=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y'-y)$ gives the phase $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)S/k^{+}}=e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)p^{+}/k^{+}}$ — your third line. $\checkmark$

1.3 The tensor contraction

Relabelling the slots of $\mathfrak C_{1jik}$ gives precisely your bracket $\big[\delta_{km}\delta_{ij}-\frac{S}{p^{+}}\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik}-\frac{S}{k^{+}}\delta_{im}\delta_{kj}\big]$. Contracting the bra and ket brackets over the internal $i,j$:

$$T^{mm'kk'}=\Big[d_{\perp}-\frac{2S^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+}}\Big]\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'} +\Big[\frac{S^{2}}{p^{+2}}+\frac{S^{2}}{k^{+2}}\Big]\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'} +\frac{2S^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+}}\,\delta_{km'}\delta_{k'm},$$ and multiplying by the $\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}$ of §1.2 reproduces your four terms exactly (symbolic difference $=0$ for all three structures):

$$\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'} +\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\delta_{mk'}\delta_{m'k}-\delta_{m'k'}\delta_{km}\big) +\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big)\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}.$$

1.4 Four corrections

  1. A factor $N_{c}$ is missing. The reduction uses $U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$ and then $f^{c'da}f^{cda}=N_{c}\delta^{c'c}$. Checked numerically with explicit $SU(3)$ $f^{abc}$ and random adjoint Wilson lines: the full colour contraction equals exactly $3\times$ your reduced form (max deviation $2.7\times10^{-15}$), and $3=N_{c}$.
  2. Two transposed indices. As literally written, the ket has $U^{db}(z)$ in the first term but $U^{bd}(z)$ in the second, so the cross terms give $\big(U(z)U(z)\big)^{d'd}$, not $\delta^{d'd}$ (numerically off by $1.67$). The same mismatch appears as $U^{ce}(x)$ against $U^{ec}(y)$. With the orderings made uniform the reduction is exact. So the last line should read $\big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]$, not $\dots-U^{ce}(x)$.
  3. $\int_{z,w,w'}\to\int_{z}$ in the last two displayed equations — $w$ and $w'$ have already been eaten by the deltas.
  4. $\int_{x,y,y'}\to\int_{x,x',y,y'}$ — $x'$ appears in the integrand.

2. Where the transverse singularities are — and there is no UV

Power counting: if $n$ points collapse onto each other at scale $\rho$ the measure supplies $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$, against an integrand $\rho^{-\nu}$ with $\nu$ the number of kernels that go singular. A logarithm needs the product to be $\rho^{0}$. The four kernels here live on the separations $\{y,z\}$, $\{x,y\}$, $\{y',z\}$, $\{x',y'\}$ — no kernel connects $y$ to $y'$, and the phase $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)S/k^{+}}$ is analytic there. 3. The $z$ integral in closed form (cutoff)

All of the $z$ dependence sits in the rank–2 tensor

$$T^{mm'}(y,y')\;=\;\int d^{2}z\;\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}}\,\frac{(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y'-z)^{2}}.$$

Using the complex–coordinate masters $\int_{|z|<R}\frac{d^{2}z}{(\bar z-\bar a)(z-b)}=\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{|a-b|^{2}}$ for the trace and $\int_{|z|<R}\frac{d^{2}z}{z-y}=-\pi\bar y$ for the traceless part:

$$\boxed{\;T^{mm'}=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\Big)\delta^{mm'}-\pi\,\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\;, \qquad r=y'-y.\;}$$

Verified by 2D quadrature with $R=3000$ at a generic non–aligned configuration:

Two features to read off. First, the $\log R^{2}$ is the IR divergence of §2. Second, $-\log r^{2}$: this is the $y\to y'$ singularity you sensed. But it is a logarithm of the result, not a divergence of an integral — and $\int d^{2}y'\,\log\frac{1}{(y-y')^{2}}$ is perfectly convergent. It is the same object as the IR pole, as the next section makes manifest.

4. Dimensional regularisation, $d=2-2\epsilon$

Take $\epsilon>0$, i.e. $d<2$, which is what damps the large–$|z|$ region. Feynman–parametrise, shift $z\to z'+xy+\bar x y'$ with $\Delta=x\bar x\,r^{2}$, drop the odd term and use $z'^{m}z'^{m'}\to\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{d}z'^{2}$:

$$T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}=\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int\! d^{d}z'\, \frac{\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{d}z'^{2}-x\bar x\,r^{m}r^{m'}}{[z'^{2}+\Delta]^{2}} =\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!dx\Big[\tfrac{\delta^{mm'}}{2}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\epsilon)\Delta^{-\epsilon} -x\bar x\,r^{m}r^{m'}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Delta^{-1-\epsilon}\Big].$$

Both $x$ integrals give the same Euler beta $\int_{0}^{1}(x\bar x)^{-\epsilon}dx=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)}$, so

$$\boxed{\;T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}=C(\epsilon)\,\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon} \Big[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\Big],\qquad C(\epsilon)=\pi^{1-\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)}.\;}$$

Cross–check on the trace. Contracting with $\delta^{mm}=d=2-2\epsilon$ gives $C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}\frac{1-2\epsilon}{\epsilon}$, which is identical (symbolically, difference $=0$) to the independent evaluation through the massless bubble $\int\frac{d^{d}z}{(z-y)^{2}(z-y')^{2}}$ with $u\cdot v=\frac12(u^{2}+v^{2}-r^{2})$ and the two scaleless pieces set to zero. $\checkmark$

Expanding, $C(\epsilon)=\pi\,e^{-\epsilon(\log\pi+\gamma_{E}-2)}$, so with $\bar\mu^{2}\equiv\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}$

$$\frac{C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}}{2\epsilon} =\frac{\pi}{2}\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\big(r^{2}\bar\mu^{2}\big)+2\Big],$$

to be compared with $\frac{\pi}{2}\big(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\big)$ from §3: the $-\log r^{2}$ matches, $-\pi\hat r\hat r$ matches, and $$\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \log\big(R^{2}\bar\mu^{2}\big)-1 .$$

This is the answer to your question. The pole and the $y\to y'$ logarithm are one and the same object, $\frac{1}{\epsilon}(r^{2})^{-\epsilon}$. Dim reg does not "cure a UV divergence at $y\to y'$" — there isn't one — it resums the $\log(y-y')^{2}$ into a power and exposes the divergence as an infrared $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ pole of the $z$ integral. Note the sign convention: $\epsilon>0$ here, opposite to a UV subtraction.

5. Contracting $T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}$ with the momentum bracket

Write $X^{k}=\frac{(x-y)^{k}}{(x-y)^{2}}$, $X'^{k'}=\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$ and

$$\mathcal X\equiv X\!\cdot\!X'=\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}},\qquad \mathcal R\equiv(\hat r\!\cdot\!X)(\hat r\!\cdot\!X') =\frac{\big[r\cdot(x-y)\big]\big[r\cdot(x'-y')\big]}{r^{2}(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}.$$
The $2/k^{+}$ term dies. $T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}$ is symmetric (§3, §4), so $\delta_{mk'}\delta_{m'k}$ and $\delta_{mk}\delta_{m'k'}$ give the same contraction $T^{kk'}X^{k}X'^{k'}$ and

$$\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\delta_{mk'}\delta_{m'k}-\delta_{m'k'}\delta_{km}\big)T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}X^{k}X'^{k'}=0$$

identically — confirmed numerically to $8\times10^{-3}$ against a $\mathcal O(24)$ integrand (quadrature noise).

For the other two, with $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ used consistently (it came from $\delta_{ii}$):

$$\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}\,\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}\;\to\; \frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}\Big[\big(\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}-1\big)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\Big],$$ $$\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big)\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}\;\to\; \Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big)C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon} \Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-2\Big)\mathcal X.$$

Adding them, with $\bar L\equiv\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\big(r^{2}\bar\mu^{2}\big)$, the scheme constants $+2$ and $-2$ cancel and everything collapses to

$$\boxed{\;\pi\left\{\ \frac{\bar L\,\mathcal X}{p^{+}} \;+\;\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}\,\bar L\,\mathcal X \;+\;\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}+p^{+})^{2}}\Big[(\bar L+1)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\Big]\right\}\;}$$

(symbolic difference from the direct $\epsilon$–expansion: exactly $0$). The residual $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ pole therefore has coefficient $\mathcal X\big[\frac{1}{p^{+}}+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}+p^{+})^{2}}\big]$ and multiplies exactly the leading–order transverse structure $\mathcal X\,[U(y')-U(x')][U(y)-U(x)]$.

6. The $p^{+}$ integral, and the $+$ prescription

All the remaining $p^{+}$ dependence is the bracket above times the phase $e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}$, $\kappa=\frac{k\cdot(y'-y)}{k^{+}}$. Three integrals, $P_{\vee}=\vee-k^{+}$:

6.1 The $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ term — here the $+$ prescription is legitimate

Because the $z$ integral was already made finite by dim reg, the coefficient of $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ is a $p^{+}$–independent, transversally finite number; and the $p^{+}\to0$ limit of the phase does not strip the leading–order oscillation, since $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}$ factored out earlier. That is precisely the condition that failed in the earlier rows. So, exactly (up to $O(\kappa\Lambda)$):

$$\mathcal I_{1}=\int_{\Lambda}^{P_{\vee}}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}\,e^{-i\kappa p^{+}} =\underbrace{\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}}_{\text{rapidity log}} +\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\vee-k^{+}}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}\Big[e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}-1\Big]}_{\big[1/p^{+}\big]_{+}},$$ $$\mathcal I_{1}=\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\mathrm{Cin}\big(|\kappa|P_{\vee}\big) -i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\kappa)\,\mathrm{Si}\big(|\kappa|P_{\vee}\big),\qquad \mathrm{Cin}(x)=\gamma_{E}+\log x-\mathrm{Ci}(x).$$

7. The result

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\mathfrak C\mathfrak C} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{4\pi^{4}}\,\frac{1}{2k^{+}} \int_{x,x',y,y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}\;\Big\{\; \bar L\,\mathcal X\Big[\mathcal I_{1}+\frac{\mathcal I_{2}}{k^{+2}}\Big] +\Big[(\bar L+1)\,\mathcal X-2\,\mathcal R\Big]\mathcal I_{3}\;\Big\}$$ $$\times\;\big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\,\rho^{e}(x)$$

with $\ \bar L=\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\big(r^{2}\bar\mu^{2}\big)$, $\ \bar\mu^{2}=\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}$, $\ r=y'-y$, $\ \kappa=\dfrac{k\cdot r}{k^{+}}$, and $\mathcal X,\mathcal R,\mathcal I_{1,2,3}$ as defined in §5–6. The $\frac{\pi}{2\pi}=\frac12$ is the $C(0)=\pi$ of the $z$ integral against the $\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}$.

7.1 The rapidity logarithm

Only $\mathcal I_{1}$ carries $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, and it rides on $\bar L\,\mathcal X\,[U(y')-U(x')][U(y)-U(x)]$ — the leading–order structure. Writing $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}=\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda}+\log\big(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\big)$ the first piece is absorbed into JIMWLK exactly as in the previous rows; the second is a finite $\vee$–dependent remainder.

7.2 The $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is infrared — track it

The pole is not renormalised away by a coupling or a DGLAP splitting: it came from $|z|\to\infty$ and it sits on the LO colour structure. It exists in this row alone only because $U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$ wiped out every $U(z)$. In rows where a $U(z)$ survives, $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ at large $|z|$ cuts the same integral off. So the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ here must cancel against the companion rows in the full NLO sum — worth checking explicitly before using this row on its own. Note also the mixed double logarithm $\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ hiding in $\bar L\,\mathcal I_{1}$.
