The fourth row

No ultraviolet divergence — and the $p^{+}$ integral in closed form

$A\equiv(x-z)^{2}$,  $B\equiv(x-w)^{2}$,   $D\equiv(\vee-k^{+})A+k^{+}B$

0. Verdict

The expression is correct — prefactor, phases and the way the residual $\frac{1}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$ is absorbed into $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s bracket all check out exactly (§1).

No transverse ultraviolet divergence. Every coincidence is regular — in fact more regular than merely integrable (§2). The reason is that there is no $\mathfrak C$ in this row: last time the UV came from $\mathfrak C$'s kernel meeting $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s squared denominator on the same separation, and here that pairing does not exist.

There is an infrared divergence at large $|z|$ (§3) — $z$ is the one variable carrying no phase.

The $p^{+}$ integral is elementary (no $\mathfrak C$ means no $\delta$–function and hence no $p^{+}$–dependent phase) and comes out as Weizsäcker–Williams kernels times logarithms (§4).

That leftover $\frac{1}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$ divides $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s bracket, turning

$$\Big[\tfrac{\delta^{ij}(A-B)}{2(z-w)^{2}}+\tfrac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{k^{+}}(\cdots)+\tfrac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{p^{+}}(\cdots)\Big] \;\longrightarrow\; \Big[\tfrac{\delta^{ij}(A-B)}{2(p^{+}+k^{+})(z-w)^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{k^{+}}(\cdots)+\tfrac{1}{p^{+}}(\cdots)\Big],$$

which is exactly the bracket you wrote, with no overall $k^{+}$ power left over. Difference from the quoted prefactor: identically zero.

2. No ultraviolet divergence

Measured $\langle{\rm integrand}\rangle_{\theta}$ at each coincidence. A finite constant means the integrand is $O(1)$ there — better than the $O(1/\rho)$ that would already suffice:

The triple collapse $x\sim z\sim w$, where $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s denominator $p^{+}A+k^{+}B$ vanishes, does give $\langle F\rangle\sim1/\rho^{2}$ ($\rho^{2}\langle F\rangle=0.088647$, stable over four decades). But three collapsing points mean two relative two–vectors, measure $\rho^{4}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$, so the net is $\rho^{2}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$: convergent.

Why this row differs from the last one. There the ultraviolet divergence came from $\mathfrak C$'s kernel $\frac{(z-x)^{m}}{(z-x)^{2}}$ contracting with $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s $\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}$ — the same separation twice, hence $1/\rho^{2}$. This row has no $\mathfrak C$ at all: the two $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}$'s supply kernels on $(x'-w')$ and $(y-z)$, which are different separations from $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s $(z-w)$, $(x-z)$, $(x-w)$. Nothing pairs up.

3. But there is an infrared divergence, at large $|z|$

$\langle F\rangle$ falls as $1/R^{2}$, and $z$ is the one integration variable carrying no phase:

   R=1e+02:  R^2<F> = -1.06404661
   R=1e+03:  R^2<F> = -1.06418776
   R=1e+04:  R^2<F> = -1.06418917
   R=1e+05:  R^2<F> = -1.06418919

so $\int\rho\,d\rho\,(c/\rho^{2})=c\log R$: logarithmically divergent. Large $|w|$ has the same $1/R^{2}$ ($-0.11792$) but $w$ carries $e^{+ik\cdot w}$, whose oscillation regulates it.

4. The $p^{+}$ integral

With no $\mathfrak C$ there is no $\delta$–function and hence no $p^{+}$–dependent phase: the three integrals are rational and elementary.

$$\mathcal I_{1}=\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{(p^{+}+k^{+})(p^{+}A+k^{+}B)} =\frac{1}{k^{+}(B-A)}\log\frac{\vee\,B}{D}$$ $$\mathcal I_{2}=\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}(p^{+}A+k^{+}B)} =\frac{1}{k^{+}B}\left[\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}+\log\frac{k^{+}B}{D}\right]$$ $$\mathcal I_{3}=\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}A+k^{+}B}=\frac{1}{A}\log\frac{D}{k^{+}B}$$

(all at $\Lambda\to0$; the exact finite–$\Lambda$ forms are in the results file).

Three things fall out. In $\mathcal I_{1}$ the coefficient $\frac{A-B}{2(z-w)^{2}}$ cancels the $\frac{1}{B-A}$, so the apparent $A=B$ singularity disappears. In $\mathcal I_{2}$ the $\frac{1}{B}$ combines with the $(x-w)^{i}$ in the numerator into a WW kernel $\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{(x-w)^{2}}$, and in $\mathcal I_{3}$ the $\frac{1}{A}$ does the same with $(x-z)^{j}$.

5. Final answer

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{ig^{4}}{8\pi^{5}}\,\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{z,w,w',x,y,x'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}\; \frac{(x'-w')^{i}}{(x'-w')^{2}}\,\frac{(y-z)^{j}}{(y-z)^{2}}\;\mathcal T^{ij}\;\times\;[\text{colour}]$$ $$\mathcal T^{ij}=-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{2(z-w)^{2}}\,\log\frac{\vee\,(x-w)^{2}}{D} \;+\;\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{(x-w)^{2}}\left[\frac{(z-w)^{j}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{2(x-z)^{2}}\right] \left[\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}+\log\frac{k^{+}(x-w)^{2}}{D}\right]$$ $$+\;\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{(x-z)^{2}}\left[\frac{(z-w)^{i}}{(z-w)^{2}}-\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{2(x-w)^{2}}\right] \log\frac{D}{k^{+}(x-w)^{2}},\qquad D=(\vee-k^{+})(x-z)^{2}+k^{+}(x-w)^{2},$$

with the colour bracket $\big[U^{ab'}(x')U^{c'd'}(y)U^{c'b}(z)\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{d'}(y)-U^{bd'}(z)U^{d'e'}(y)U^{ac'}(w')\rho^{c'}(x')\rho^{e'}(y)\big] \big[f^{cbd}U^{ac}(w)\rho^{d}(x)-f^{acd}U^{bc}(z)U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)\big]$ unchanged. The prefactor picked up the $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ from $\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}$: $\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{5}}$.

Checked against direct $p^{+}$ quadrature on four random geometries and three index pairs, to $10^{-7}$–$10^{-9}$ relative (quadrature–limited).

The two longitudinal endpoint logarithms

  • $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, from $\mathcal I_{2}$ — the rapidity log, from $p^{+}\to0$. It rides on $\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{(x-w)^{2}}\big[\frac{(z-w)^{j}}{(z-w)^{2}} +\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{2(x-z)^{2}}\big]$, a product of WW kernels.
  • A $\log\vee$ from $\mathcal I_{3}$ — as $\vee\to\infty$, $\log\frac{D}{k^{+}B}\to\log\frac{\vee(x-z)^{2}}{k^{+}(x-w)^{2}}$. This is a genuine large–$p^{+}$ logarithm: the $\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ bracket term has no $p^{+}$ in its numerator, so against $\frac{1}{p^{+}A+k^{+}B}$ it gives $\int^{\vee}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}$. Worth checking against the kinematic origin of $\vee$ — it is not a rapidity divergence and will not be absorbed by evolution. In $\mathcal I_{2}$ by contrast the $\vee$'s cancel: $\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda}+\log\frac{k^{+}B}{\vee A}=\log\frac{k^{+}B}{\Lambda A}$.
