The rapidity term

Is $2b$ the only one — and does it build the evolution kernel?

$K_{ab}\equiv\dfrac{a-b}{(a-b)^{2}}$ the Weizsäcker–Williams kernel; $A=(x-z)^{2}$, $B=(x-w)^{2}$, $A'=(x'-z)^{2}$, $B'=(x'-w')^{2}$

Your rewriting is exact, and within this row the answer is yes — the blue piece is the only source of $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$; even the other two pieces of $2b$ are $\Lambda$–finite (§2). But it is not the only term in the cross section, and the proof is inside this very expression. Your prefactor collapses to $\frac{1}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}$ and your four–term bracket factorises (§3), leaving the $z$–kernel $$\mathcal V_{z}=K_{zw}-\tfrac{1}{2}K_{zx}\,,$$ which is not the dipole kernel $K_{zw}-K_{zx}$. That factor of $\frac12$ is exactly why this row has a $|z|\to\infty$ logarithm: the half–weighted kernel squared has a $\frac{1}{4|z|^{2}}$ tail, while the full one falls as $|z|^{-4}$ (§4). The row's IR divergence and the incompleteness of its evolution kernel are the same fact, and they close together when the partner rows are added.

3. Two simplifications you are leaving on the table

3.1 The prefactor is just $\frac{1}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}$

$$\frac{1}{B'AB-B^{2}A'}\times\Big[A-\frac{BA'}{B'}\Big]\;=\;\frac{1}{BB'}\qquad\text{identically.}$$

(Symbolic; numerically $0.065412525179$ both ways.) The $\Delta=AB'-A'B$ cancels between the two factors — which also means this piece has no removable pole at all, unlike every other piece in the six parts. It is the one term you can integrate numerically without any special care.

3.2 The four–term bracket factorises

$$\Big[\cdots\text{your four terms}\cdots\Big] =\big[(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')\big]\;\big[\mathcal V_{z}\cdot\mathcal V_{z}'\big],$$ $$\mathcal V_{z}=\frac{z-w}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{x-z}{2(x-z)^{2}}=K_{zw}-\tfrac12 K_{zx},\qquad \mathcal V_{z}'=K_{zw'}-\tfrac12 K_{zx'}.$$

(Checked: $0.036521945575$ both ways.) Putting the two together, your whole rapidity term is

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\,\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda} \int_{z,w,w',x,x'} e^{-ik(w'-w)}\; \underbrace{\frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}}_{\text{LO structure}}\; \underbrace{\Big[K_{zw}-\tfrac12K_{zx}\Big]\!\cdot\!\Big[K_{zw'}-\tfrac12K_{zx'}\Big]}_{\text{$z$-gluon kernel}} \;\times\;\big[\text{colour}\big]$$

which is the shape a rapidity term must have: the LO single–gluon–production structure times a kernel for the emitted soft gluon, times the rapidity span. Worth writing it this way — it is much easier to compare against the JIMWLK kernel.

4. No, it is not the whole evolution kernel — and the $\frac12$ proves it

The BK/JIMWLK dipole kernel is $K_{zw}-K_{zx}$: the difference of two full WW kernels, one to each end of the dipole. What you have is $K_{zw}-\frac12K_{zx}$. The missing half has to come from somewhere, and the place it shows up is the large–$|z|$ behaviour:

Yours tends to $\frac14$ — a flat $\frac{1}{4|z|^{2}}$ tail, hence $\int\frac{d^{2}z}{|z|^{2}}$, a logarithm. The full kernel tends to zero like $|z|^{-2}$, i.e. the product falls as $|z|^{-4}$: no large–$|z|$ log at all. The reason is the dipole structure itself — at large $|z|$, $K_{zw}$ and $K_{zx}$ both approach $\hat z/|z|$ and cancel to leading order, leaving only the $O(|z|^{-2})$ difference. Colour neutrality in position space.

So the row's IR divergence and its incomplete kernel are one and the same fact. Multiplying this term by $\frac{\mathcal K_{ww'}}{k^{+}}$ and the $\frac14$ reproduces exactly the $|z|\to\infty$ coefficient measured earlier, $\frac{\mathcal K_{ww'}}{4k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ — the same number, arrived at two ways. When the partner rows supply the other halves of $K_{zx}$ and $K_{zx'}$, the kernel becomes $K_{zw}-K_{zx}$ and the $z$ integral converges at the same moment. You should not try to regulate that log on its own; it is telling you the sum is incomplete.

5. What still has to contribute

To finish the kernel and close the $z$ integral you need, at minimum:

  • the $(1\rho)\times(2\rho)$ cross terms, your $N_{1}$ — whose $|z|\to\infty$ plateau I measured earlier as exactly $-2\times$ this row's;
  • the $(2\rho)\times(2\rho)$ piece of $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$, still missing from your expression, coefficient $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}$ — note its $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$, which is a rapidity log of its own;
  • the $\mathfrak B_{3}$ rows, which also carry $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$;
  • the virtual rows — these supply the opposite–sign half of the real–virtual cancellation and are what turn the kernel into a difference in the first place.

Only once all of those are in should you read off the coefficient of $\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda}$ and match it to the JIMWLK Hamiltonian acting on the LO cross section.
