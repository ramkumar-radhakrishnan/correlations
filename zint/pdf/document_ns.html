The $(1\rho)\times(1\rho)$ piece of $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$

Transverse divergences, the DGLAP question, and the $p^{+}$ integration

$A\equiv(x-z)^{2}$,  $B\equiv(x-w)^{2}$,  $C\equiv(z-w)^{2}$,  $A'\equiv(x'-z)^{2}$,  $B'\equiv(x'-w')^{2}$,  $C'\equiv(z-w')^{2}$;  $S\equiv p^{+}\!+k^{+}$,  $\zeta\equiv k^{+}/S$,  $\bar\zeta\equiv p^{+}/S$;  $\vee\equiv q^{+}$ the total $+$–momentum, $\Lambda$ the rapidity cutoff on $p^{+}$.

Summary. The prefactor and the colour structure are right (three bookkeeping corrections in §1). There is no transverse UV divergence anywhere — all 26 collapsing subsets of $\{x,x',z,w,w'\}$ are convergent (§3), including the "loop closing" region $z\to w$. There is exactly one transverse IR divergence, from $|z|\to\infty$, and after the $p^{+}$ integration its coefficient is remarkably clean (§6): $$\frac{1}{4k^{+}}\,\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}\; \frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}\;\int^{R}\!\frac{d^{2}z}{|z|^{2}}\,,$$ i.e. (LO transverse structure) $\times$ (full rapidity span), with the $z$–gluon completely decoupled. That is a soft / rapidity log, not a collinear one: this row gives no DGLAP (§7) — the would–be collinear region $z\to w$ is only $\rho^{-1}$ singular, and the $\zeta$–weight of the log is $1/(\zeta\bar\zeta)$, which is part of $P_{gg}$ but not $P_{gg}$. The complete $p^{+}$–integrated answer is boxed in §5.

1. The expression as written

1.1 Prefactor — correct

With $\mathfrak B^{(1)}=\dfrac{ig^{2}\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}}{4\pi^{2}S}$ and the standard $\dfrac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}$,

$$\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathfrak B^{(1)}\mathfrak B^{(1)\dagger} =\frac{g^{4}}{32\pi^{7}}\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{2}} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{2}}\,,$$

exactly your quoted coefficient (ratio $=1$ symbolically). The vertex's own $1/S$ is kept inside the brackets, which is why they carry $S/k^{+}$ and $S/p^{+}$ — consistent.

1.2 Colour — correct as written

Relabelling the source index $a\to d$, the ket is $U^{ac}(w)f^{cbd}\rho^{d}(x)-U^{bc}(z)f^{acd}U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)$, exactly as you have it. The two terms carry different structure constants ($f^{cbd}$ vs $f^{acd}$), so $f$ is correctly not pulled out front.

Three bookkeeping items.
  1. The bra's second term reads $f^{ac'd'}U^{bc'}(z)\,U^{e'd'}(x')\rho^{e'}(x')$ while the ket's reads $f^{acd}U^{bc}(z)\,U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)$: the two Wilson–line indices are transposed relative to each other. Numerically $\max|U(x)-U(x)^{T}|=1.03$ on a random configuration, so this is not a harmless relabelling — fix the ordering to be uniform.
  2. The measure should be $\displaystyle\int_{x,x',z,w,w'}$, not $\int_{z,w,w'}$: both $x$ and $x'$ appear, in the kernels and in $\rho^{d}(x)$, $\rho^{d'}(x')$.
  3. $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ has three pieces. This row is $(1\rho)\times(1\rho)$; the cross terms $(1\rho)\times(2\rho)$ are your $N_{1}$; the $(2\rho)\times(2\rho)$ piece, coefficient $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}$, is still missing. It matters for §6, because it carries the same $1/|z|^{2}$ tail.

2. The vertex factorises — this is what makes everything below tractable

Write the bracket as $\mathcal W^{ji}(x,z,w)$. It is not a generic $2\times2$ tensor: each of the two non–$\delta$ structures is an outer product of one fixed vector with one composite vector,

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal W^{ji}=\delta^{ji}\,\frac{A-B}{2C}\;+\;\frac{S}{k^{+}}\,(x-z)^{j}\,\mathcal V_{w}^{\,i} \;+\;\frac{S}{p^{+}}\,\mathcal V_{z}^{\,j}\,(x-w)^{i}\;}$$ $$\mathcal V_{w}^{\,i}\equiv\frac{(z-w)^{i}}{(z-w)^{2}}-\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{2(x-w)^{2}},\qquad \mathcal V_{z}^{\,j}\equiv\frac{(z-w)^{j}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{2(x-z)^{2}}\,.$$

(Reconstruction agrees with the coded vertex to $7\times10^{-15}$.) Call the three structures $\mathcal T_{1},\mathcal T_{2},\mathcal T_{3}$ in that order; the primed ones use $x',w'$ and the same $z$. 3. Transverse UV: there is none

A logarithmic short–distance divergence needs the integrand to go as $\rho^{-2}$ when $n$ points collapse, because $n$ collapsing points supply $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$. Scanning all $\binom{5}{2}+\binom{5}{3}+\binom{5}{4}+\binom{5}{5}=26$ subsets of $\{x,x',z,w,w'\}$ at $\rho=10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6}$ (angle–averaged, with the phase in place):

no UV in any of the 26 regions.

The interesting one is the "loop closing" region $z\to w$ — the two final–state gluons at the same transverse point. Put $\rho=|z-w|$:

$\langle|F|\rangle\rho$ is flat, so the integrand is $\rho^{-1}$, not $\rho^{-2}$: against $d^{2}\rho\sim\rho\,d\rho$ this is $\int d\rho$, linearly convergent, no log.

Why, structurally. Two things conspire.
  • Each $\mathcal W^{ji}(x,z,w)$ is only $\rho^{-1}$ singular at $z\to w$, not $\rho^{-2}$: the $\delta^{ji}$ numerator $A-B=(x-z)^{2}-(x-w)^{2}=-2(x-w)\!\cdot\!(z-w)+(z-w)^{2}$ vanishes like $\rho$, cancelling one power of $C=\rho^{2}$; and $\mathcal V_{w},\mathcal V_{z}$ are $\rho^{-1}$.
  • Only one of the two vertices is singular there. The conjugate vertex is $\mathcal W^{ji}(x',z,w')$ — regular at $z=w$ because $w'\neq w$. And $w'\neq w$ is enforced by the measurement itself: the phase $e^{-ik_{\perp}\cdot(w'-w)}$ is what separates the amplitude and conjugate emission points of the observed gluon.
Forcing both to be singular, $z\to w$ and $w'\to w$, makes $|F|\sim\rho^{-2}$ (checked: $\langle|F|\rangle\rho^{2}=1.067324\cdot10^{-1}$, constant over $\rho=10^{-2}\ldots10^{-5}$), but the three–point measure is $\rho^{3}d\rho$, so it goes as $\int\rho\,d\rho$: convergent again, and in any case that corner is oscillation–killed once $k_{\perp}\neq0$.
Answer to "a loop at the final state must have a UV". It would, if the loop momentum flowed through a single propagator pair. Here the unobserved gluon's transverse position $z$ is shared between amplitude and conjugate (its $\mathbf p_{\perp}$ was integrated, setting $z=z'$), and its two kernels attach to different points, $w$ on one side and $w'$ on the other. The measurement phase keeps $w$ and $w'$ apart, so the two singular kernels never sit on the same separation. That is the same mechanism that made every other real (cut) row UV–free; the only UV in this calculation lives in the purely virtual row $\mathfrak A^{(1)\dagger}\times\mathfrak A^{(3)}$, where it comes out as $-2b_{0}/\epsilon$.

4. Transverse IR: the one genuine divergence, at $|z|\to\infty$

At large $|z|$ the vertex degenerates. Using $A\to|z|^{2}$, $C\to|z|^{2}$ in §2,

$$\frac{\mathcal W^{ji}(x,z,w)}{S\,D}\;\longrightarrow\;-\frac{1}{2p^{+}k^{+}}\, \frac{(x-z)^{j}(x-w)^{i}}{(x-z)^{2}(x-w)^{2}},\qquad D\equiv p^{+}A+k^{+}B\,,$$

which is the $\mathcal T_{2}$ (i.e. $S/k^{+}$) structure: $\mathcal V_{w}\to-\frac{(x-w)}{2(x-w)^{2}}$ while $(x-z)^{j}$ grows like $|z|$. The $\mathcal T_{1}$ and $\mathcal T_{3}$ structures fall faster. Squaring, $(x-z)\!\cdot\!(x'-z)/(AA')\to1/|z|^{2}$ with no angular cancellation, so the integrand has a flat $1/|z|^{2}$ tail: a logarithmic IR divergence in $z$. Directly, at fixed $p^{+}$,

$$F\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{4p^{+}k^{+}}\,\frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}\, \frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\,,$$

confirmed to 9 decimals against the prediction at six values of $p^{+}$. This is a real IR divergence of this row: the colour average does not cut it off, because $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ as $z$ leaves the target and $\langle\mathcal C\rangle$ tends to a finite non–zero limit.

The other large–distance ends are harmless. $|x|,|x'|\to\infty$ also look log–divergent in a bare scan, but $x,x'$ are the arguments of $\rho$: the colour–charge correlator has the transverse support of the target, so those ends are cut at the target radius in the standard way. $|w|,|w'|\to\infty$ are Fresnel–oscillatory — the scan does not converge there, and it should not: $\int d^{2}w\,\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{(x-w)^{2}}e^{ik\cdot w}$ is a conditionally convergent Fourier transform, finite distributionally.

5. The $p^{+}$ integration, exactly

Only six independent longitudinal weights occur, because the $\mathcal T_{a}$ carry $1,\,S/k^{+},\,S/p^{+}$ against the overall $p^{+}k^{+}/S^{2}$:

$$h_{11}=\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{2}},\quad h_{12}=\frac{p^{+}}{S},\quad h_{13}=\frac{k^{+}}{S},\quad h_{22}=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}},\quad h_{23}=1,\quad h_{33}=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\,.$$

The two energy denominators factorise, $D\,D'=AA'\,(p^{+}+\alpha)(p^{+}+\alpha')$ with

$$\alpha\equiv\frac{k^{+}B}{A}=\frac{k^{+}(x-w)^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}},\qquad \alpha'\equiv\frac{k^{+}B'}{A'}=\frac{k^{+}(x'-w')^{2}}{(x'-z)^{2}}\,,$$

both strictly positive, so no pole sits on the contour — no principal value, no $i\pi$, this row is clean. Define

$$\mathcal M_{ab}\;=\;\frac{1}{AA'}\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\!dp^{+}\; \frac{h_{ab}(p^{+})}{(p^{+}+\alpha)(p^{+}+\alpha')}\;=\;\frac{1}{AA'}\Big[F_{ab}(\vee-k^{+})-F_{ab}(\Lambda)\Big].$$

5.1 Where the endpoint logs live

Expanding the primitives at both ends:

  • $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ occurs only in $\mathcal M_{33}$, with residue $\dfrac{1}{k^{+}BB'}$. This is the rapidity divergence.
  • $\log\vee$ occurs only in $\mathcal M_{22}$, with residue $\dfrac{1}{k^{+}AA'}$. It is not a divergence: $\vee=q^{+}$ is fixed by kinematics.
  • $\mathcal M_{11},\mathcal M_{12},\mathcal M_{13},\mathcal M_{23}$ are finite at both ends.

Taking $\Lambda\to0$ and $\vee\to\infty$ in the finite parts, and writing everything in $A,B,A',B'$ (all four verified against the exact integrals to $10^{-9}$):

$$\mathcal M_{23}=\frac{1}{k^{+}\Delta}\,\log\frac{AB'}{A'B},\qquad \Delta\equiv AB'-A'B,$$ $$\mathcal M_{22}=\frac{1}{k^{+}AA'}\left[\log(\vee-k^{+}) -\frac{AB'\log\frac{k^{+}B'}{A'}-A'B\log\frac{k^{+}B}{A}}{\Delta}\right],$$ $$\mathcal M_{33}=\frac{1}{k^{+}}\left[\frac{1}{BB'}\log\frac{1}{\Lambda} +\frac{1}{\Delta}\left(\frac{A}{B}\log\frac{k^{+}B}{A}-\frac{A'}{B'}\log\frac{k^{+}B'}{A'}\right)\right],$$ $$\mathcal M_{13}=-\frac{k^{+}}{AA'}\left[\frac{\log k^{+}}{(\alpha-k^{+})(\alpha'-k^{+})} +\frac{\log\alpha}{(k^{+}-\alpha)(\alpha'-\alpha)} +\frac{\log\alpha'}{(k^{+}-\alpha')(\alpha-\alpha')}\right],$$ $$\mathcal M_{12}=\frac{1}{AA'}\left[\frac{k^{+}\log k^{+}}{(\alpha-k^{+})(\alpha'-k^{+})} +\frac{\alpha\log\alpha}{(k^{+}-\alpha)(\alpha'-\alpha)} +\frac{\alpha'\log\alpha'}{(k^{+}-\alpha')(\alpha-\alpha')}\right],$$ $$\mathcal M_{11}=-\frac{k^{+}}{AA'}\left[\frac{1}{(\alpha-k^{+})(\alpha'-k^{+})} +\frac{(\alpha\alpha'-k^{+2})\log k^{+}}{(\alpha-k^{+})^{2}(\alpha'-k^{+})^{2}} -\frac{\alpha\log\alpha}{(k^{+}-\alpha)^{2}(\alpha'-\alpha)} -\frac{\alpha'\log\alpha'}{(k^{+}-\alpha')^{2}(\alpha-\alpha')}\right].$$

The apparent poles at $\alpha=k^{+}$, $\alpha'=k^{+}$, $\alpha=\alpha'$ (and $\Delta=0$) are all removable — each bracket is regular there, as the partial–fraction construction guarantees.

5.2 The assembled result

$$N_{2}=\frac{g^{4}}{32\pi^{7}}\int d^{2}x\,d^{2}x'\,d^{2}z\,d^{2}w\,d^{2}w'\; e^{-ik_{\perp}\cdot(w'-w)}\;\Big\langle\,\mathcal C\,\Big\rangle\;\mathcal I(x,x',z,w,w')$$ $$\mathcal I=\frac{(A-B)(A'-B')}{2CC'}\,\mathcal M_{11} +\left[\frac{A-B}{2C}(x'-z)\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{w'}+\frac{A'-B'}{2C'}(x-z)\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{w}\right]\mathcal M_{12}$$ $$+\left[\frac{A-B}{2C}\mathcal V_{z}'\!\cdot\!(x'-w')+\frac{A'-B'}{2C'}\mathcal V_{z}\!\cdot\!(x-w)\right]\mathcal M_{13} +\big[(x-z)\!\cdot\!(x'-z)\big]\big[\mathcal V_{w}\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{w'}\big]\,\mathcal M_{22}$$ $$+\Big[\big[(x-z)\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{z}'\big]\big[\mathcal V_{w}\!\cdot\!(x'-w')\big] +\big[(x'-z)\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{z}\big]\big[\mathcal V_{w'}\!\cdot\!(x-w)\big]\Big]\mathcal M_{23} +\big[\mathcal V_{z}\!\cdot\!\mathcal V_{z}'\big]\big[(x-w)\!\cdot\!(x'-w')\big]\,\mathcal M_{33}$$

with $\langle\mathcal C\rangle=\big\langle[U^{ac}(w)f^{cbd}\rho^{d}(x)-U^{bc}(z)f^{acd}U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)] [\,\cdot\,]^{\dagger}_{x'\!,w'}\big\rangle$ and the $\mathcal M_{ab}$ above.

6. The transverse log after the $p^{+}$ integration

This is the prettiest part. Take $|z|\to\infty$ in $\mathcal I$ after integrating $p^{+}$. Four pieces survive at order $|z|^{-2}$ — $\mathcal M_{22},\mathcal M_{33},\mathcal M_{23}$ (twice) — and they conspire:

  • $\mathcal M_{22}$ carries $+\log|z|^{2}$ from its $\Lambda$–end, $\mathcal M_{33}$ carries $-\log|z|^{2}$: the $\log^{2}$ cancels, leaving a single log.
  • The leftovers $\mp\frac{B+B'}{B'-B}\log\frac{B'}{B}$ of $\mathcal M_{22}+\mathcal M_{33}$ cancel exactly against $\mathcal M_{23}+\mathcal M_{32}$.

What survives is

$$\mathcal I\;\xrightarrow[\;|z|\to\infty\;]{}\; \frac{1}{4k^{+}}\;\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}\; \frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}\;\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\;+\;O(|z|^{-3}).$$

Read it off. The transverse factor $\frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-w')}{(x-w)^{2}(x'-w')^{2}}$ is precisely the LO single–gluon–production structure: the Weizsäcker–Williams kernel for the observed gluon, emitted from $x$ at $w$ in the amplitude and from $x'$ at $w'$ in the conjugate. The unobserved gluon has completely decoupled — all that is left of it is $\int d^{2}z/|z|^{2}$ and the rapidity span $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$. That is the definition of the eikonal/soft region. The $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ is the JIMWLK rapidity divergence, to be absorbed into the small–$x$ evolution of the target; the accompanying $\int d^{2}z/|z|^{2}$ is cut off only once the partner terms are included — the missing $(2\rho)\times(2\rho)$ piece, the $N_{1}$ cross terms (whose $|z|\to\infty$ plateau I checked is exactly $-2\times$ this one), and the virtual rows, where the same eikonal kernel appears with the opposite sign. Taken by itself, $N_{2}$ is log–IR divergent in $|z|$, and that is expected, not an error.

7. DGLAP: no — and here is why, three independent ways

7.1 There is no collinear region

A final–state (fragmentation) collinear log has to come from the two produced gluons approaching each other in transverse space, i.e. $|z-w|\to0$. §3 shows that region is $\rho^{-1}$, giving $\int d\rho$: convergent, no log. The reason is the measurement itself: the conjugate vertex sits at $w'\neq w$ and is regular there, and $e^{-ik_{\perp}\cdot(w'-w)}$ is exactly what prevents $w'$ from following $w$. No collinear region, no collinear log, no fragmentation function.

7.2 The splitting function is not there

A DGLAP log must carry the complete splitting function; a purely soft $1/(\zeta\bar\zeta)$ weight is the signature of eikonal emission.

7.3 The kinematics are wrong for fragmentation

In the row with two $\mathfrak C$ vertices — the one that does fragment — the $\mathfrak C$ delta function ties the emission points together as $w'-w=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y'-y)=\frac{1}{\zeta}(y'-y)$, so the phase becomes $e^{-i(k_{\perp}/\zeta)\cdot(y'-y)}$: the hard factor is evaluated at the parent's momentum $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, and the longitudinal measure comes out as $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$. Those are the two hallmarks of a fragmentation function.

Here nothing of the sort happens: $w$ and $w'$ are free integration variables, and the phase is $e^{-ik_{\perp}\cdot(w'-w)}$ with the observed $k_{\perp}$, unrescaled. There is no $\xi$–integral left against the log either — after §5 the log's coefficient is the pure number $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, i.e. $\log\frac{1}{x_{g}}$, not $\int\frac{d\xi}{\xi}P_{gg}(\xi)\log\mu^{2}$.

Verdict on DGLAP. $N_{2}$ produces a small–$x$ (JIMWLK/BK) rapidity log multiplying the LO structure, not a collinear DGLAP log. Two final–state gluons with one integrated out is a necessary condition for fragmentation, not a sufficient one — you also need the two kernels to become singular on the same separation, and the measurement phase forbids that here. The DGLAP/fragmentation logs in this calculation live in the rows where a $\mathfrak C$ vertex ties the observed gluon's amplitude and conjugate positions together.
