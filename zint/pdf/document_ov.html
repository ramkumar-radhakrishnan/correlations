3. Part (b): the UV terms

The UV region is $r\to0$ ($z\to x$). The exact singular part of $T(r)$ is

$$T_{\rm UV}(r)=d_{\perp}\,\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{P^{2}r^{4}} +\Big(\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\Big)\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}r^{2}},$$

and $\frac{1}{\bar\xi D}\to\frac{1}{\bar\xi D_{0}}=\frac{1}{s^{2}}$. Block by block: $T_{1}\to\xi\bar\xi$, $T_{2}\to\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}$, $T_{4}\to\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$ — one term of $C_{\rm UV}$ each — while the $T_{3},T_{5},T_{6}$ pieces cancel pointwise because their coefficient is $2-2\xi-2\bar\xi=0$. Checked: $\rho^{2}\times\langle T/(\bar\xi D)\rangle\to C_{\rm UV}\,\mathcal W$ to 8 digits at five values of $\xi$, and the subtracted integrand falls as $\rho^{2}$.

The two masters (in $d=2-2\epsilon$, your $\overline{\rm MS}$ convention)

$$\int d^{d}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}}=\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{q^{2}}\Big], \qquad \int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r^{i}r^{m}}{r^{4}} =\frac{\pi}{2}\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{q^{2}}\Big]\delta^{im}-\pi\,\hat q^{i}\hat q^{m}.$$

The first is your (1.19)–(1.20). The second is where (1.15) loses something: the $-\pi\hat q^{i}\hat q^{m}$ is finite, and contracting with $d_{\perp}\xi\bar\xi P^{i}s^{m}$ (the $\epsilon\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ from $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ included) leaves

$$\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T_{\rm UV}(r)}{s^{2}} =\pi\,\mathcal W\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}}\Big] \;+\;\pi\,\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{P\cdot s-2(P\cdot\hat k)(s\cdot\hat k)}{P^{2}s^{2}},$$

with $\hat q=\hat k$. Cutoff cross-check: the $\hat q\hat q$ coefficient comes out $-3.14159265359=-\pi$.

4. Part (b): the answer

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}_{b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\!\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\, \bigg\{\mathcal W\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}}\Big] +\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{P\cdot s-2(P\cdot\hat k)(s\cdot\hat k)}{P^{2}s^{2}} +\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathcal I(\xi)\bigg\}\,\mathcal U_{b}$$ $$\mathcal I(\xi)=\int d^{2}r\;e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot r}\Big[\frac{T(r)}{\bar\xi D}-\frac{T_{\rm UV}(r)}{s^{2}}\Big]_{d_{\perp}=2}, \qquad \mathcal U_{b}=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]N_{c}\big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)$$

with $T(r)$ the six blocks, (1.8) and (1.9) corrected. $\mathcal I$ has no pole and needs no regulator.

The first two terms integrate in closed form ($\lambda=\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$; each confirmed by quadrature):

Why there is a $\log$ at $\xi\to1$ — three scales

As $\bar\xi\to0$ the $r$ integral has three scales: $|s|$, the mass $M=|s|\sqrt{\xi/\bar\xi}\to\infty$, and the oscillation length $\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\bar\xi k}\gg M$. The true integrand has a $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ tail in $|s|<|r|<M$ (from $T_{2}$, whose weight $\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}$ is large) and another in $M<|r|<\frac{1}{q}$ (from the $T_{3},T_{4},T_{6}$ pieces that grow like $|r|$). Each gives $\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{2\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$; together $+\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$. The UV counterterm $\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}}$, however, keeps its $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ all the way from $0$ to $\frac{1}{q}$ and so carries $\frac{2\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ — that is the $\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi^{2}}$ in (1.21). The remainder $\mathcal I=\text{true}-\text{counterterm}$ therefore has $-\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$.

The correct prescription at $\xi\to1$

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}=\Big[\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\Big]_{+}+\delta(\bar\xi)\,L,\qquad \frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}=\Big[\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big]_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta(\bar\xi)\,L^{2}.$$

Applied to $\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal I$ it contributes $-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal W L^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\pi}L$ to the braces, so the double logarithm of part (b) is

$$\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\,\mathcal W\,L^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal W\log^{2}\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}, \qquad\text{not }\ \mathcal W\log^{2}\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}\ \text{ as (1.22) alone suggests.}$$

At $\xi\to0$

$[\frac{1}{\xi}]_{+}$ alone is legitimate at fixed $x,y$ and gives $\delta(\xi)\,\frac{b_{0}(x,y)}{\pi}L$. But see §7 before using it.

No UV divergence. $\mathcal C_{a}=-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]=O(|r|)$, and that linear piece is odd in $\hat r$ while the UV residue is even, so two powers are gained: with a smooth $U(z)$ field, $\rho^{2}\langle\cdot\rangle$ is $2.1\times10^{-5},\,10^{-7},\,10^{-9},\,10^{-11}$ at $|r|=10^{-2}\dots10^{-5}$ against the colour-stripped constant $0.0134814$. 7. Where I stopped, and why

The $\delta(\xi)$ term from $\xi\to0$ is divergent in the remaining $\int_{x}$. At $\xi=0$ the total phase $\exp[-ik\cdot(w'-\bar\xi z-\xi x)]$ loses all dependence on $x$, and the residue of the $\frac{1}{\xi}$ pole has a $\frac{1}{x^{2}}$ tail at large $|x|$ (fixed $y,z$). Checked: $|x|^{2}\langle\text{residue}\rangle\to-0.0457352$ at $|x|=10^{1\dots4}$, matching $\frac{P\cdot(y-z)}{2P^{2}(y-z)^{2}}=-0.0457352$. So $\int d^{2}x$ of the $\delta(\xi)$ coefficient diverges logarithmically. Physically: the gluon with $p^{+}\to0$ sitting far from the others — the mirror image, under $(x\leftrightarrow z,\ \xi\leftrightarrow\bar\xi)$, of the $\xi\to1$ log in §5.

Its colour factor at large $|x|$ is $N_{c}\big[U(y)-\mathbb 1\big]$ in part (b) and $-f^{adc}f^{fde}\big[U^{ce}(z)-\delta^{ce}\big]$ in part (a). Neither is the LO colour structure, so this piece cannot be absorbed inside this row. There are two consistent ways forward, and they lead to different-looking final answers for this row:

what it meanswhat the answer looks like
(A)Regulate the soft-$x$ region within this row: keep $\xi$ finite through the $\int_{x}$, so its own oscillation $e^{i\xi k\cdot x}$ cuts it off at $|x|\sim\frac{1}{\xi k}$. A $\frac{\log(1/\xi)}{\xi}$ at $\xi\to0$ as well; two distributions at each end; an extra $\frac{1}{2}L^{2}$ with colour $N_{c}[U(y)-\mathbb 1]$ (b) and $-f f[U(z)-\mathbb 1]$ (a).
(B)Leave it for the cross-row soft subtraction: combine the $U(x)\to\mathbb 1$ pieces of this row with those of the other Group I rows (e.g. Row IV) before any $\xi$ integral, where they should cancel. This row keeps only the $\xi\to1$ double log; the $\delta(\xi)$ coefficient is carried unintegrated in $x$ until the rows are combined.

Both are legitimate; which one fits depends on how you and your advisor are organising the soft/rapidity divergences across the whole NLO calculation. I did not want to pick one and hand you a “final answer” built on the wrong choice.
