The UV–projected row, integrated

$\int d^2r$ and $\int d\xi$ done — the UV term and the finite terms

$\displaystyle\int d^{2}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{1}{\bar\xi D}\,\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}r^{2}} \Big[\xi\bar\xi+\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\tfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\Big]$, then $\int d\xi$

0. The answer

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{2\,\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{\alpha_s^{2}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',w'}e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\; \underbrace{\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}}_{\rm WW\times WW}\; \Big[\;\mathcal U+\mathcal F\;\Big]\;\Big[\cdots\Big]\rho^{b'}\!(x')\rho^{f}\!(y),$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal U=\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\,\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{m^{2}}\;} \qquad \boxed{\;\mathcal F=\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,\ell_k^{2}\;+\;c_0\,\ell_k\;+\;\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}+c_1\;}$$

with $\ell_k=\log(k^+/\Lambda)$, $\alpha_s=g^2/4\pi$, and $c_0,c_1$ the two finite functions of $k$ and $y-x$ given in §4. The whole $m$–dependence — equivalently the whole $1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}$ — is the single term $\mathcal U$, and $-\tfrac{11}{6}\times2N_c=-b$ at $N_f=0$.

1. What has to be integrated

With $r=z-x$, $s=y-x$, $P=x'-w'$, $\bar\xi=1-\xi$, $q=\bar\xi k$,

$$D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2},\qquad M^{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2},\qquad D_0\equiv D\big|_{r=0}=s^{2}+M^{2}=\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi},\qquad C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}.$$

Nothing in the bracket depends on $r$ except $1/r^{2}$ and $1/D$, and $C_{\rm UV}$ does not depend on $r$ at all. So the entire transverse integration is one master,

$$\mathcal G(\xi)\;\equiv\;\int d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{(r^{2}+m^{2})\,\big[(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}\big]},$$

$m$ being the ultraviolet regulator ($z\to x$); nothing else needs one.

2. The $z$ integral, exactly

Feynman–parametrise, $\tfrac1{AB}=\int_0^1\tfrac{d\alpha}{[\alpha A+\bar\alpha B]^2}$ with $A=r^2+m^2$, $B=D$:

$$\alpha A+\bar\alpha B=(r-b)^{2}+\Delta,\qquad b=\bar\alpha s,\qquad \Delta=\bar\alpha\big(\alpha s^{2}+M^{2}\big)+\alpha m^{2}.$$

Shift $r=b+u$; the phase factorises, $e^{iq\cdot r}=e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s}e^{iq\cdot u}$, and the $u$ integral is the two–dimensional Yukawa transform differentiated once,

$$\int\!\frac{d^{2}u\,e^{iq\cdot u}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}} =-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta}\,2\pi K_0\big(q\sqrt\Delta\big) =\frac{\pi q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1\big(q\sqrt\Delta\big),\qquad q\equiv|q| .$$

Writing $t=\bar\alpha$ (so $\Delta\to m^{2}$ at $t=0$, which is where the divergence sits),

$$\mathcal G=\pi\int_0^1\!dt\;e^{itq\cdot s}\,\frac{q\,K_1\big(q\sqrt\Delta\big)}{\sqrt\Delta}, \qquad \Delta=t\big[(1-t)s^{2}+M^{2}\big]+(1-t)m^{2}.$$

As $t\to0$: $\Delta\to tD_0+m^{2}$, $xK_1(x)\to1$, $e^{itq\cdot s}\to1$, so the integrand tends to $1/(tD_0+m^{2})$. Add and subtract it; $\pi\int_0^1\frac{dt}{tD_0+m^{2}} =\frac{\pi}{D_0}\log\frac{D_0+m^{2}}{m^{2}}$, and the remainder is finite at $m=0$:

$$\mathcal G=\frac{\pi}{D_0}\Big[\log\frac{D_0}{m^{2}}+\hat I(\xi)\Big]+O(m^{2}),$$ $$\hat I(\xi)=D_0\int_0^1\!dt\left[\,e^{itq\cdot s}\,\frac{q\,K_1\big(q\sqrt{\Delta_0}\big)}{\sqrt{\Delta_0}} -\frac{1}{t\,D_0}\right],\qquad \Delta_0=t\big[(1-t)s^{2}+M^{2}\big].$$

The integrand is integrable at $t\to0$ (the bracket tends to $i\,q\cdot s/D_0$) and regular at $t\to1$ for $M>0$. Equivalently, in closed form up to one $\alpha$–integral,

$$\hat I=\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{q^{2}D_0}+\frac{2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi}{\pi},\qquad \Phi=2\pi e^{iq\cdot s}K_0(Mq),\quad \Psi^i=\int\!d^2r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}r^i}{r^{2}D},$$

which agrees with the $t$–representation to $10^{-14}$ (§6A) — the two were derived independently.

3. Putting it back in the row

Multiply by $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}}$ and by the $\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ of the longitudinal denominator. Because $\frac1{D_0}=\frac{\bar\xi}{s^{2}}$, the $\bar\xi$ cancels exactly:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\,\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}}\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\mathcal G =\pi\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\;\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\; \left[\underbrace{\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi\,m^{2}}}_{\textbf{UV}} +\underbrace{\hat I(\xi)}_{\textbf{finite}}\right].$$

So after the $z$ integration the row is a product of two Weizsäcker–Williams kernels times $P_{gg}/2N_c$ times a logarithm, plus the same kernels times $P_{gg}/2N_c$ times the finite function $\hat I$. The choice of $D_0$ as the argument of the logarithm is a scheme choice; it is the one that makes the UV logarithm read $\log\big[(y-x)^{2}/\bar\xi m^{2}\big]$, i.e. the running coupling at the dipole size. Any other choice moves a constant (and a $\log\bar\xi$) between the two brackets.

4. The $p^+$ integral

$\int\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}$ over $[\lambda,1-\lambda]$, $\lambda=\Lambda/k^+$, $\ell_k=\log(1/\lambda)$. The two pieces behave quite differently.

4.1 The UV term — elementary

$\log\frac{(y-x)^2}{\bar\xi m^2}=\log\frac{(y-x)^2}{m^2}+\log\frac{1}{1-\xi}$, and both moments of $C_{\rm UV}=\xi(1-\xi)-2+\frac{1}{\xi(1-\xi)}$ are elementary:

piece of $C_{\rm UV}$from$\int d\xi$$\int d\xi\,\log\frac{1}{1-\xi}$
$\xi(1-\xi)$$\Theta_1$, instantaneous$\tfrac16$$\tfrac{5}{36}$
$\dfrac{\xi}{1-\xi}$$\Theta_2$$\ell_k-1$$\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}-1$
$\dfrac{1-\xi}{\xi}$$\Theta_4$$\ell_k-1$$\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1$
total$2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}$$\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}+\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\tfrac{67}{36}$
$$\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi m^{2}} =\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{m^{2}} +\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}.$$

The double logarithm comes only from $\Theta_2$, the $\pi^{2}/6$ only from $\Theta_4$, and it is the instantaneous $\Theta_1$ that turns $-2$ into $-\tfrac{11}{6}$ and $-2$ into $-\tfrac{67}{36}$. Note that $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}=-2\big(\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\tfrac{67}{36}\big)$ is the constant of Eq. (C.10) of arXiv:1610.03453.

4.2 The finite term

$\hat I$ depends on $\xi$ through $M^{2}=\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$ and through $q=\bar\xi k$, so $\int d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\hat I$ is not elementary. Its structure is fixed by the two endpoints.

$\xi\to0$. Then $M^{2}\to0$ and $\Delta_0\to t s^{2}(1-t+\xi)$ develops a logarithm at $t\to1$: $\hat I=e^{ik\cdot s}\log\tfrac1\xi+c_0+O(\xi\log\xi)$, with

$$c_0(k,s)=s^{2}\!\int_0^1\!dt\left[e^{itk\cdot s}\frac{k\,K_1\big(k s\sqrt{t(1-t)}\big)}{s\sqrt{t(1-t)}} -\frac{1}{t\,s^{2}}-\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}}{s^{2}(1-t)}\right],$$

a convergent integral (the second subtraction handles $t\to1$, the first $t\to0$). Since $C_{\rm UV}\to1/\xi$ there, $\int d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\hat I$ picks up $e^{ik\cdot s}\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}+c_0\ell_k$.

$\xi\to1$. Then $M\to\infty$ and $q\to0$ together, $\hat I\sim\bar\xi\log\tfrac1{\bar\xi}\to0$, so $C_{\rm UV}\hat I\sim\log\tfrac1{\bar\xi}$ — integrable. This endpoint contributes no rapidity logarithm.

$$\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\hat I(\xi) =e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\,\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}\;+\;c_0\,\ell_k\;+\;c_1 ,$$

$c_1$ being the remaining convergent double integral. For the test kinematics $s=(0.7,-0.4)$, $k=(0.9,0.6)$ the fit over $\lambda=10^{-3}\ldots10^{-6}$ gives (§6D)

$$\text{coefficient of }\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}:\ 0.925384+0.380329i \quad\text{vs}\quad e^{ik\cdot s}=0.924909+0.380188i ,$$ $$c_0=-0.588515-0.116237i\ \ (\text{closed form}),\qquad c_1=0.350743+0.149676i .$$

5. Assembling

Collecting the prefactor, $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}k^{+}}\times\frac{\pi}{2\pi} =\frac{g^{4}}{128\pi^{7}k^{+}}=\frac{\alpha_s^{2}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}}$, and adding §4.1 and §4.2:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{2\,\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{\alpha_s^{2}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',w'}e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\; \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\;\Big[\mathcal U+\mathcal F\Big]\; \Big[\cdots\Big]\rho^{b'}\!(x')\rho^{f}\!(y)$$ $$\mathcal U=\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{m^{2}} \;\longleftrightarrow\;\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}} +\log\big(\pi e^{\gamma_E}\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)\Big],$$ $$\mathcal F=\underbrace{\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}}_{\text{from the UV log's }\bar\xi} +\underbrace{e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+c_0\,\ell_k+c_1}_{\text{from }\hat I} =\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\ell_k^{2}+c_0\ell_k+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}+c_1 .$$

Three things to read off.

  • The UV term. Its coefficient is $\int d\xi\,P_{gg}/2N_c=2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}$, and $-\tfrac{11}{6}\cdot2N_c=-\tfrac{11N_c}{3}=-b$ at $N_f=0$: a product of two WW kernels times $b\log$, i.e. the running coupling at the dipole size $(y-x)^{2}$. This is the piece that cancels against the $-2/\epsilon$ of the self–energy counterterm.
  • The rapidity logarithms. $\ell_k^{2}$ appears twice, once from each endpoint: the $\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}$ of §4.1 comes from $\xi\to1$ (through $\log\tfrac1{\bar\xi}$ against the $\tfrac{\xi}{1-\xi}$ pole), the $e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}$ from $\xi\to0$ (through the $z\to y$ logarithm $\log\tfrac1{M^{2}}$ against the $\tfrac{1-\xi}{\xi}$ pole). They are the $(\delta Y)^{2}$/LO$^{2}$ content of the row and must be subtracted before the NLO kernel is read off.
  • Scheme. Only $\mathcal U+\mathcal F$ is fixed; the boundary between them is the argument chosen for the logarithm in §3. Keeping $2\pi K_0(m|q|)$ whole instead of splitting at $D_0$ would move $\log\tfrac{1}{\bar\xi^{2}}$ across, changing $\tfrac12\ell_k^2+\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}-\tfrac{67}{36}$ into $\ell_k^2+\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}-\tfrac{67}{18}$ and $c_0,c_1$ correspondingly. The coefficient of $\log(1/m^{2})$, $2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}$, is the same in every scheme.
