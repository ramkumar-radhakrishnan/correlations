0. The short version

Your row is the vertex of Eq. (4.16) of the paper. Under the identification

$$\boxed{\;z_{\rm paper}\;\leftrightarrow\;x,\qquad z'_{\rm paper}\;\leftrightarrow\;z,\qquad y\;\leftrightarrow\;y\;}$$

one has $Y=y-z_{\rm paper}=y-x=s$, $Y'=y-z'_{\rm paper}=y-z=s-r$, $Z=z_{\rm paper}-z'_{\rm paper}=x-z=-r$, and therefore

$$\bar\xi\,D\;=\;\bar\xi\,(s-r)^2+\xi\,s^2\;=\;(1-\xi)(Y')^2+\xi\,Y^2 ,$$

which is exactly the second energy denominator of (4.16). Better still, the six $\xi$–weights of your bracket are exactly the six products that the paper's vertex $\Lambda,\Theta$ decomposition produces. So the whole of §4.2 / Appendix H.2 applies, and the $p^+$ integral is elementary: it needs only the paper's (C.2), (C.5), (C.6). Doing it gives

$$\int_{\Lambda/k^+}^{1-\Lambda/k^+}\!\!d\xi\;\frac{\text{bracket}}{\bar\xi D} \;=\;\underbrace{\ell_k\Big[\frac{V_2}{Y^2}+\frac{V_4}{(Y')^2}\Big]}_{\text{(a) rapidity }\to\;(\delta Y)^2\text{, Eq.\,(H.7)}} \;+\;\underbrace{\ln\!\frac{Y^2}{(Y')^2}\;\mathcal G_{\rm log}}_{\text{(b) the NLO logs, Eq.\,(H.6)}} \;+\;\underbrace{\mathcal G_{\rm rat}}_{\text{(c) rational, Eq.\,(H.6)}},$$

with $\ell_k\equiv\ln(k^+/\Lambda)$, and the ultraviolet ($z\to x$) content of the whole thing sitting in one place:

$$\Big[\int d\xi\;\frac{\text{bracket}}{\bar\xi D}\Big]_{r\to0} =\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\frac{(s\cdot P)}{P^2s^2}\,\frac{1}{r^2}+\text{(integrable)}, \qquad 2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}=\int d\xi\,\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}.$$

The $2\ell_k$ comes entirely from block (a) and the $-\tfrac{11}{6}$ entirely from blocks (b)+(c) — verified numerically in §10E. And $-\tfrac{11}{6}\times2N_c=-\tfrac{11N_c}{3}=-b$ at $N_f=0$: this row is a running–coupling contribution. §9 shows that its $\xi$ integral is literally the paper's (C.10), which is where $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}$ comes from.

1. Three things in the expression as typed

These have to be settled before any integral, because two of them change the answer.

(i) $\Theta_6$ carries a minus sign twice. In (1.1) the last structure already appears with an overall minus, and your (1.10) gives the result of contracting it:

$$-\Big[\tfrac{(y-x)^m(x-z)^i}{(x-z)^2}-\tfrac{(y-x)^m(y-z)^i}{2(y-z)^2} +\tfrac{(y-z)^m(x-z)^i}{(x-z)^2}+\tfrac{(y-x)^i(y-z)^m}{2(y-x)^2}\Big]\frac{P^ir^m}{P^2r^2} =\;+\frac{2(P\cdot r)(r\cdot s)}{P^2r^4}-\frac{P\cdot r}{P^2r^2}+\dots$$

In the expression you typed this same right–hand side appears again behind a minus sign, $-\big[\tfrac{2(P\cdot r)(r\cdot s)}{P^2r^4}-\dots\big]$. That flips $\Theta_6$. It matters: with the sign of (1.10) the coefficient of $\tfrac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^4}$ is $d_\perp\xi\bar\xi-2\bar\xi-2\xi+2=d_\perp\xi\bar\xi$ and the UV coefficient comes out as $P_{gg}/2N_c$; with the extra minus it is $d_\perp\xi\bar\xi-4$ and $P_{gg}$ never appears. The sign of (1.10) is used below.

(ii) $\Theta_4$: the factor 2, again. Contracting the $\Theta_4$ structure of (1.1), $-\frac{k^+-p^+}{p^+}\big[\frac{(y-z)^i(x-z)^m}{(x-z)^2}+\frac{(y-x)^m(y-z)^i}{2(y-x)^2}\big]$, with $\frac{P^ir^m}{P^2r^2}$ gives $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\frac{(s\cdot P)-(P\cdot r)}{P^2r^2} \big[1-\frac{r\cdot s}{2s^2}\big]$ — the $2s^2$ that is missing in (1.8) and in the expression you typed. Since you have now written it twice without the 2, everything below is carried with a parameter

$$V_4=\frac{(s\cdot P)-(P\cdot r)}{P^2r^2}\Big[1-\kappa\,\frac{r\cdot s}{s^2}\Big], \qquad \kappa=\tfrac12\ \text{(from (1.1))},\qquad \kappa=1\ \text{(as printed)} .$$

Every result is given for both. Nothing that matters here depends on it: $\kappa$ does not enter the UV coefficient, nor the rapidity block, nor the $\xi$ integrals — only the finite remainder (§10D shows the UV coefficient is $\kappa$–independent to 7 digits).

(iii) The phase. Written out, $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)}=e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}$ has no $r$–dependence at all, so (1.3) is consistent only if $q$ is treated as $\xi$–independent, which is how (1.11) is written ($\int d\xi$ outside, $e^{iq\cdot r}$ inside). That is what is done below, and it is also what the paper needs: §4.2 has one power of $\ln(\Lambda/k^+)$ and hence $(\delta Y)^2$. If instead $q=\bar\xi k$ (the second exponential being $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(z-x)}$), the $\xi$ integrals below acquire a factor $e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot r}$: the $\ell_k$ terms are unchanged (they come from the endpoints, where the phase is $e^{ik\cdot r}$ and $1$), but the finite ones stop being elementary and an extra $\ln(1/\bar\xi)$ appears, generating $\ell_k^2$. See §9.

4. The $p^+$ integration: six elementary integrals

Everything $\xi$–dependent is now in the weights and in $\Delta(\xi)=A+\xi C$. Define, with $\lambda=\Lambda/k^+$, $\ell_k=\ln(1/\lambda)=\ln(k^+/\Lambda)$,

$$J_i\;\equiv\;\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;\frac{w_i(\xi)}{\Delta(\xi)},\qquad w=\Big(\xi\bar\xi,\ \frac{\xi}{\bar\xi},\ \bar\xi,\ \frac{\bar\xi}{\xi},\ \xi,\ 1\Big),$$

so that $\displaystyle\int d\xi\;\frac{\rm bracket}{\bar\xi D}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}J_i\,V_i$. Derive them in the order in which they build on one another. Everything follows from

$$\frac{1}{\Delta}=\frac{1}{A+\xi C},\qquad \frac{1}{\xi\Delta}=\frac1A\Big[\frac1\xi-\frac{C}{\Delta}\Big],\qquad \frac{1}{\bar\xi\Delta}=\frac1B\Big[\frac1{\bar\xi}+\frac{C}{\Delta}\Big],\qquad \xi=\frac{\Delta-A}{C}.$$

$J_6$ (no weight). $\int_0^1 d\xi/\Delta=\frac1C\ln\frac{B}{A}$ — this is the paper's (C.2). Write $L\equiv\ln(B/A)=\ln\!\big[Y^2/(Y')^2\big]$: $$J_6=\frac{L}{C}.$$

$J_5$ ($w=\xi$). Using $\xi=(\Delta-A)/C$: $\int\frac{\xi}{\Delta}=\frac1C\int\big(1-\frac{A}{\Delta}\big)=\frac1C-\frac{A}{C}J_6$, i.e. $$J_5=\frac1C-\frac{A}{C^2}L .$$

$J_3$ ($w=\bar\xi$). Simply $J_3=J_6-J_5$, and $1+\frac{A}{C}=\frac BC$: $$J_3=\frac{B}{C^2}L-\frac1C .$$

$J_1$ ($w=\xi\bar\xi$). $J_1=J_5-\int\frac{\xi^2}{\Delta}$, and with $\xi^2=\frac{(\Delta-A)^2}{C^2}$, $\int_0^1\Delta\,d\xi=A+\frac C2$: $\int\frac{\xi^2}{\Delta}=\frac1{C^2}\big[\frac C2-A+\frac{A^2}{C}L\big]$, so $$J_1=\frac{1}{2C}+\frac{A}{C^2}-\frac{AB}{C^3}\,L .$$

$J_4$ ($w=\bar\xi/\xi$) — the first place a rapidity logarithm appears. From $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac1\xi-1$ and the partial fraction above, $\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\frac{d\xi}{\xi\Delta}=\frac1A\big[\ell_k-L\big]$ — this is the paper's (C.5). Subtracting $J_6$ and using $\frac1A+\frac1C=\frac{B}{AC}$: $$J_4=\frac{\ell_k}{A}-\frac{B}{AC}\,L .$$

$J_2$ ($w=\xi/\bar\xi$) — the second rapidity logarithm. With $t=\bar\xi$, $\Delta=B-tC$, $\frac{1}{t(B-tC)}=\frac1B\big[\frac1t+\frac{C}{B-tC}\big]$, giving $\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\frac{d\xi}{\bar\xi\Delta}=\frac1B\big[\ell_k+L\big]$. Subtracting $J_6$ and using $\frac1B-\frac1C=-\frac{A}{BC}$: $$J_2=\frac{\ell_k}{B}-\frac{A}{BC}\,L .$$

A sign in the paper. $\int\frac{d\xi}{\bar\xi\Delta}=\frac1B(\ell_k+L)$ is the paper's (C.6), which is printed as $-\frac{1}{W^2}\big(\ln\frac{\Lambda}{k^+}+\ln\frac{W^2}{(W')^2}\big) =\frac1B(\ell_k-L)$. The $L$ should come with the opposite sign: (C.6) is (C.5) with $\xi\to1-\xi$, which swaps $A\leftrightarrow B$ and hence $L\to-L$, turning $\frac1A(\ell_k-L)$ into $\frac1B(\ell_k+L)$. Direct quadrature confirms it (§10B): for $A=1,B=4$ the integral is $5.52739$, not $4.83424$. (C.5) itself is right.

5. The result of the $p^+$ integration

$$\mathcal G(r)\;\equiv\;\int_{\Lambda/k^+}^{1-\Lambda/k^+}\!\!d\xi\;\frac{\rm bracket}{\bar\xi D} \;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{6}J_i\,V_i ,$$ $$\begin{array}{llll} J_1=\dfrac{1}{2C}+\dfrac{A}{C^2}-\dfrac{AB}{C^3}L, & J_2=\dfrac{\ell_k}{B}-\dfrac{A}{BC}L, & J_3=\dfrac{B}{C^2}L-\dfrac{1}{C}, \\[6pt] J_4=\dfrac{\ell_k}{A}-\dfrac{B}{AC}L, & J_5=\dfrac{1}{C}-\dfrac{A}{C^2}L, & J_6=\dfrac{L}{C}, \end{array}$$ $$A=(s-r)^2=(Y')^2,\qquad B=s^2=Y^2,\qquad C=B-A=2s\cdot r-r^2,\qquad L=\ln\frac{B}{A}, \qquad \ell_k=\ln\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}.$$

Regrouped by the object that multiplies each piece — which is how the paper presents it —

$$\mathcal G=\ell_k\,\mathcal G_{\rm rap}+L\,\mathcal G_{\rm log}+\mathcal G_{\rm rat},$$ $$\mathcal G_{\rm rap}=\frac{V_2}{B}+\frac{V_4}{A} =\frac{V_2}{Y^2}+\frac{V_4}{(Y')^2},$$ $$\mathcal G_{\rm log}=-\frac{AB}{C^3}V_1-\frac{A}{BC}V_2+\frac{B}{C^2}V_3-\frac{B}{AC}V_4 -\frac{A}{C^2}V_5+\frac{1}{C}V_6 ,$$ $$\mathcal G_{\rm rat}=\Big(\frac{1}{2C}+\frac{A}{C^2}\Big)V_1-\frac{1}{C}V_3+\frac{1}{C}V_5 .$$

5.1 What each block is, in the paper's language

$\ell_k\mathcal G_{\rm rap}$ is Eq. (H.7). The paper's $(\delta Y)^2$ piece is

$$\Sigma^{(\delta Y)^2}_{JSSJ}\;\propto\; \left(\frac{{\rm tr}\big(\Lambda(x,z,z')\Lambda^T(y,z,z')\big)}{(X')^2(Y')^2} +\frac{{\rm tr}\big(\Lambda(x,z',z)\Lambda^T(y,z',z)\big)}{X^2Y^2}\right)\ln\frac{\Lambda}{k^+},$$

i.e. the $1/\xi$ vertex divided by $(Y')^2$ plus the $1/(1-\xi)$ vertex divided by $Y^2$. Ours is the same thing with one vertex instead of two:

$$\mathcal G_{\rm rap}=\underbrace{\frac{V_4}{(Y')^2}}_{1/\xi\ \text{vertex}} +\underbrace{\frac{V_2}{Y^2}}_{1/(1-\xi)\ \text{vertex}} =\frac{(s\cdot P)-(P\cdot r)}{P^2r^2(s-r)^2}\Big[1-\kappa\frac{r\cdot s}{s^2}\Big] +\frac{P\cdot s}{P^2r^2s^2}+\frac{(P\cdot s)(r\cdot s-r^2)}{2P^2r^2(s-r)^2s^2}.$$

The correspondence is exact, term for term: it is the $1/\xi$ and $1/(1-\xi)$ poles of the vertex that produce $\ell_k$, and each is divided by the value of $\Delta(\xi)$ at the endpoint where its pole sits — $\Delta(0)=A=(Y')^2$ for the $1/\xi$ pole, $\Delta(1)=B=Y^2$ for the $1/(1-\xi)$ pole.

$L\,\mathcal G_{\rm log}+\mathcal G_{\rm rat}$ is Eq. (H.6). (H.6) has the same shape: a rational term ($-\tfrac{1}{2Z^4}$) plus terms carrying $\ln\frac{X^2}{(X')^2}$ and $\ln\frac{Y^2}{(Y')^2}$. Ours has one such logarithm, $L=\ln\frac{Y^2}{(Y')^2}$, because there is one energy denominator.

Use $\sum_iJ_iV_i$, not the regrouping, for numbers. $\mathcal G_{\rm log}$ and $\mathcal G_{\rm rat}$ have spurious $1/C^3$ poles that cancel between them ($C\to0$ is the circle $|r-s|=|s|$, where nothing happens physically). Each $J_i$ separately is regular there, with

$$J_i=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{n\ge0}\Big(\frac CB\Big)^{n}\!\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,w_i(\xi)\bar\xi^{\,n} \;\Longrightarrow\; \begin{array}{lll} J_1B=\tfrac16+\tfrac{\epsilon}{12}+\tfrac{\epsilon^2}{20}+\dots & J_2B=(\ell_k-1)+\tfrac\epsilon2+\dots & J_3B=\tfrac12+\tfrac\epsilon3+\dots\\ J_4B=(\ell_k-1)+\epsilon(\ell_k-\tfrac32)+\dots & J_5B=\tfrac12+\tfrac\epsilon6+\dots & J_6B=1+\tfrac\epsilon2+\dots \end{array}$$

with $\epsilon=C/B$. Evaluating $J_1$ from the closed form at $\epsilon\lesssim10^{-5}$ loses all significance (§10G); use the series there.

6. The $k^+$ integration — $\delta Y$ and $(\delta Y)^2$

Your expression is differential in $k^+$. To land on the form of §4.2, integrate over the rapidity slice as the paper does, $\int_\Lambda^{e^{\delta Y}\Lambda}\frac{dk^+}{k^+}$:

$$\int_\Lambda^{e^{\delta Y}\Lambda}\frac{dk^+}{k^+}=\delta Y,\qquad \int_\Lambda^{e^{\delta Y}\Lambda}\frac{dk^+}{k^+}\,\ell_k =\int_\Lambda^{e^{\delta Y}\Lambda}\frac{dk^+}{k^+}\ln\frac{k^+}{\Lambda}=\frac{(\delta Y)^2}{2},$$

so that, exactly as in (4.18),

$$\int\frac{dk^+}{k^+}\,\mathcal G\;=\;\underbrace{\delta Y\Big[L\,\mathcal G_{\rm log}+\mathcal G_{\rm rat}\Big]}_{\text{the NLO kernel, cf. (4.19), (H.6)}} \;+\;\underbrace{\frac{(\delta Y)^2}{2}\,\mathcal G_{\rm rap}}_{\text{cf. (4.20), (H.7)}} .$$

The $(\delta Y)^2$ piece is the one that must reassemble into $\tfrac12(\delta Y\,H^{\rm LO}_{\rm JIMWLK})^2$, Eq. (4.11) — and indeed $\mathcal G_{\rm rap}$ is built only out of products of LO Weizsäcker–Williams kernels $\frac{r^i}{r^2}$, $\frac{s^i}{s^2}$, $\frac{(s-r)^i}{(s-r)^2}$, $\frac{P^i}{P^2}$, which is what (4.20) and (4.38) are made of.

7. UV, finite, and rapidity terms

Only the first block needs a regulator. Doing its $z$ integral, $\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}/(r^2+m^2)=2\pi K_0(m|q|)=\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{m^2q^2}$, gives

a product of two Weizsäcker–Williams kernels times $b\log$ — the running–coupling structure of $K_{JSJ}$, Eq. (2.64)/(2.66).

8. The other order, and where $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}$ comes from

If instead the $z$ integral is done first (the companion note), the UV logarithm comes out carrying a $\bar\xi$ inside its argument, because $D_0\equiv D|_{r=0}=s^2/\bar\xi$:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\frac{\mathcal W_{\rm UV}}{P^2} =\pi\,\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\,\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^2(y-x)^2}\; \log\frac{(y-x)^2}{\bar\xi\,m^2},$$

and the $\xi$ integral then needs the logarithmic moments of $P_{gg}$. These are exactly the paper's (C.10). Writing $C_{\rm UV}=\xi\bar\xi+\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\tfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi} =\xi(1-\xi)-2+\tfrac{1}{\xi(1-\xi)}$ — which is literally the weight of (C.10) —

$$\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}=2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6},\qquad \int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\,\ln\big[\xi(1-\xi)\big] =-\ell_k^2+\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^2}{3},$$

the second being (C.10) with the $\ln(k^2/\mu^2)$ piece removed. Since $C_{\rm UV}$ is symmetric under $\xi\leftrightarrow\bar\xi$, $\int C_{\rm UV}\ln(\xi\bar\xi)=2\int C_{\rm UV}\ln\bar\xi$, so the moment this row actually needs is half of it:

$$\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\log\frac{1}{1-\xi} =\frac{\ell_k^2}{2}+\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{67}{36} =-\frac12\left(-\ell_k^2+\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^2}{3}\right),$$

giving, for the whole UV block of this row,

$$\int\frac{dp^+}{2\pi}\;\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\,\log\frac{k^+(y-x)^2}{(k^+-p^+)m^2} =\frac{k^+}{2\pi}\left[\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\log\frac{(y-x)^2}{m^2} +\frac{\ell_k^2}{2}+\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{67}{36}\right].$$

So: $-\tfrac{11}{6}\times2N_c=-b$ at $N_f=0$, and the constant is exactly the $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}$ of (C.10) — the same combination that ends up as the $\big(\tfrac{67}{9}-\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}\big)N_c$ of $K_{JSJ}$, Eqs. (2.64), (2.66), (4.36). (The remaining factor–of–two bookkeeping between $\tfrac{67}{18}$ and $\tfrac{67}{9}$ is the $x\leftrightarrow y$ symmetrisation of (4.31); the $\pi^2$ term additionally receives the self–energy/normalisation pieces of (4.31), which are not in this row, so this is an identification of the source of the constant, not a complete assembly of $K_{JSJ}$.)

Why the two orders look different. Doing $\xi$ first (§5) the UV logarithm is $\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{m^2q^2}$ with coefficient $2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}$ and there is no $\ell_k^2$; doing $z$ first the logarithm is $\log\frac{(y-x)^2}{\bar\xi m^2}$ and the extra $\log\frac1{\bar\xi}$ generates $\tfrac12\ell_k^2$. The totals agree; the $\bar\xi$ simply sits in a different block. The coefficient of $\log(1/m^2)$ — the only regulator–dependent number — is $2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}$ in both.

9. What this reproduces, and what it cannot

Reproduced. The vertex and its six $\xi$–weights; the energy denominator $(1-\xi)(Y')^2+\xi Y^2$; the $p^+$ integration using nothing but (C.2), (C.5), (C.6); the resulting three–block structure (rapidity $\times\ln\frac{\Lambda}{k^+}$, a $\ln\frac{Y^2}{(Y')^2}$ block, a rational block) which is the shape of (H.6)–(H.7); the $\ell_k\to\tfrac12(\delta Y)^2$ mechanism of (4.18)–(4.20); the fact that the $(\delta Y)^2$ block is built only from products of LO WW kernels, as (4.20) and (4.38) are; $b$ and $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^2}{3}$.

Not reproducible from this row, and worth knowing before you spend more time on it. $\Sigma_{JSSJ}$, Eq. (4.16), has two energy denominators, because both sides of the overlap are the two–gluon wave function $|\psi_{gg\rho}\rangle$. That is why H.2 needs (C.3) and (C.4) as well, why both $\ln\frac{X^2}{(X')^2}$ and $\ln\frac{Y^2}{(Y')^2}$ appear, and where the $1/Z^4$ of $K_{JSSJ}$ comes from. Your row has one energy denominator, one LO Weizsäcker–Williams kernel $\frac{P^ir^m}{P^2r^2}$ in place of the second vertex, a Fourier phase, and a fifth point $w'$ — it is a production object, $d^3N/d^3k$, not an $S$–matrix overlap. It therefore cannot give $K_{JSSJ}$ directly.

The row it actually resembles is §4.3, $\Sigma_{JJSSJ}$, Eq. (4.21): one energy denominator, the same vertex, contracted with products of LO kernels. Its $\xi$ integration (4.22) is done with exactly (C.2), (C.5), (C.6) and yields exactly the structure obtained in §5 — a $\ln\frac{W^2}{(W')^2}$ block plus a $\ln\frac{\Lambda}{k^+}$ block whose coefficient is the $1/\xi$ vertex piece divided by $(W')^2$ plus the $1/(1-\xi)$ piece divided by $W^2$, which is our $\mathcal G_{\rm rap}=V_4/(Y')^2+V_2/Y^2$. If the target is a $\Sigma_{JSSJ}$–type kernel, the missing ingredient is the second energy denominator, i.e. the second $|\psi_{gg\rho}\rangle$ — not anything in the $\xi$ or $z$ integration.
