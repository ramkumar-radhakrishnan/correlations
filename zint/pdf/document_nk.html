The $|\mathfrak B_2|^{2}$ row

$N_{1}$ (three $\rho$) and $N_{2}$ (two $\rho$) — transverse divergences, then the $p^{+}$ integral

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $A\equiv(x-z)^{2}$,  $B\equiv(x-w)^{2}$,  $C\equiv(z-w)^{2}$,  $A'\equiv(x'-z)^{2}$,  $B'\equiv(x'-w')^{2}$,  $D=p^{+}A+k^{+}B$

Summary. Both prefactors are correct (§1), but one piece of $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ is missing and two measures are wrong. On divergences: there is no ultraviolet divergence in either $N_{1}$ or $N_{2}$ — all $26$ collapsing regions of $N_{2}$ and all $57$ of $N_{1}$ converge (§2). The transverse divergence that is there is infrared, at large $|z|$, and it has a clean mechanism: the one–$\rho$ (gluon–exchange) part of $\mathfrak B_{2}$ degenerates at large $|z|$ into exactly the two–$\rho$ part's kernel, with the same prefactor up to a factor $i$ (§3). That ties the $z$–divergences of $N_{1}$ and $N_{2}$ to the $4\rho$ term you left out — they are not independent. The $p^{+}$ integrals then go through in closed form: three masters for $N_{1}$ (§4), six for $N_{2}$ (§5), all verified against quadrature.

In $N_{1}$ the $\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}$ and $1/\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}$ cancel, and the vertex's overall $\frac{1}{S}$ distributes as

$$\frac{1}{S}\Big[\tfrac{\delta^{ij}(A-B)}{2C}+\tfrac{S}{k^{+}}(\cdots)+\tfrac{S}{p^{+}}(\cdots)\Big] =\frac{\delta^{ij}(A-B)}{2S\,C}+\frac{1}{k^{+}}(\cdots)+\frac{1}{p^{+}}(\cdots),$$

which is your $N_{1}$ bracket exactly. $\checkmark$

Three things to fix.
  1. The $4\rho$ term is absent. It is a pure product of four WW kernels, $\frac{(y-w)^{i}(x-z)^{j}(y'-w')^{i}(x'-z)^{j}}{(y-w)^{2}(x-z)^{2}(y'-w')^{2}(x'-z)^{2}}$, and it carries $\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}$ — a double soft factor. §3 shows it is not optional: it is exactly what the $z$–divergences of $N_{1}$ and $N_{2}$ run into.
  2. $N_{2}$'s measure reads $\int_{z,w,w'}$ but $x$ and $x'$ appear in the integrand — it should be $\int_{x,x',z,w,w'}$.
  3. $N_{2}$'s two brackets both use $i,j$ contracted; keep the slot order $W_{ji}$ consistent ($j$ with $(x-z)$, $i$ with $(x-w)$) or the cross terms transpose.

2. No ultraviolet divergence, in either piece

Write the bracket as a matrix $W_{ji}(x,z,w)$ (the $S$–multiplied form), so

$$N_{1}\ \propto\ \frac{(x'-w')^{i}}{(x'-w')^{2}}\frac{(y-z)^{j}}{(y-z)^{2}}\frac{W_{ji}(x,z,w)}{S\,D}, \qquad N_{2}\ \propto\ \frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{2}}\frac{W_{ji}(x,z,w)\,W_{ji}(x',z,w')}{D\,D'} .$$

Every subset of the integration points was collapsed at scale $\rho$ against the measure $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$. Representative rows (full tables in the results file):

3. The real transverse divergence: large $|z|$, and what it means

Send $|z|\to\infty$ with everything else fixed. In $W_{ji}$ the $\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ structure grows like $|z|$ (through $-\frac{(x-z)^{j}(x-w)^{i}}{2(x-w)^{2}}$) while $D\sim p^{+}|z|^{2}$, and the limit is clean:

$$\frac{W_{ji}(x,z,w)}{S\,D(x,z,w)}\;\xrightarrow[\;|z|\to\infty\;]{}\; -\frac{1}{2p^{+}k^{+}}\,\frac{(x-w)^{i}}{(x-w)^{2}}\,\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{(x-z)^{2}}\,. $$

3.1 The one–$\rho$ vertex becomes the two–$\rho$ vertex

Feed that limit back into $\mathfrak B^{(1)}$ and compare with $\mathfrak B^{(2)}$:

$$\mathfrak B^{(1)}\Big|_{|z|\to\infty}=-\frac{i g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}}\int_{x} f^{cba}\rho^{a}(x)\,\frac{(x-w)^{i}(x-z)^{j}}{(x-w)^{2}(x-z)^{2}},$$ $$\mathfrak B^{(2)}=-\frac{g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}}\int_{x,y} \big\{\rho^{c}(y),\rho^{b}(x)\big\}\,\frac{(y-w)^{i}(x-z)^{j}}{(y-w)^{2}(x-z)^{2}} .$$

Same kernel, same $\frac{g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}}$ — the ratio of prefactors is exactly $i$. They differ only in the colour/source content: $-if^{cba}\rho^{a}(x)$ with both kernels at the same point, against $-\{\rho^{c}(y),\rho^{b}(x)\}$ with the kernels at independent points. Physically: at large $|z|$ the exchanged–gluon graph stops resolving the exchange and becomes a product of two independent Weizsäcker–Williams emissions.

How to handle it. It is not UV, so no short–distance counterterm applies. Two statements follow, and the second is the important one.
  • Regulate it the same way as the previous row: $\int d^{2-2\epsilon}z$ with $\epsilon>0$ damps the large–$|z|$ end and turns the log into a $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}$ pole. Nothing new is needed.
  • Do not treat $N_{1}$ and $N_{2}$ separately at that end. Their $z$–tails carry the same transverse structure and the same $\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}$ as the $4\rho$ term you dropped — which is the term built from precisely that kernel squared. The three pieces have to be combined before the $z$ integral is regulated, or the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$'s will not come out right. Restore the $4\rho$ term first.

4. The $p^{+}$ integral for $N_{1}$

All the $p^{+}$ dependence is $\frac{1}{D}$ times the three bracket weights $\frac{1}{2S},\frac{1}{p^{+}},\frac{1}{k^{+}}$. Three masters, $P_{\vee}=\vee-k^{+}$:

$$\mathcal J_{1}=\int_{\Lambda}^{P_{\vee}}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}D} =\frac{1}{k^{+}B}\log\frac{P_{\vee}\,k^{+}B}{\Lambda\,(P_{\vee}A+k^{+}B)} \ \xrightarrow[\vee\to\infty]{}\ \frac{1}{k^{+}B}\log\frac{k^{+}B}{\Lambda A},$$ $$\mathcal J_{2}=\int_{\Lambda}^{P_{\vee}}\frac{dp^{+}}{D} =\frac{1}{A}\log\frac{P_{\vee}A+k^{+}B}{\Lambda A+k^{+}B} \ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{A}\log\frac{\vee A}{k^{+}B},$$ $$\mathcal J_{3}=\int_{\Lambda}^{P_{\vee}}\frac{dp^{+}}{(p^{+}+k^{+})D} =\frac{1}{k^{+}(B-A)}\log\frac{\vee B}{P_{\vee}A+k^{+}B} \ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{k^{+}(B-A)}\log\frac{B}{A}.$$

The $\delta^{ij}$ term collapses exactly. Its weight is $\frac{A-B}{2C}\,\mathcal J_{3}$, and the $(A-B)$ cancels the $(B-A)$: $$\frac{A-B}{2C}\cdot\frac{\log(B/A)}{k^{+}(B-A)} =-\frac{1}{2k^{+}C}\log\frac{B}{A} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -\,\frac{\delta^{ij}}{2k^{+}(z-w)^{2}}\,\log\frac{(x-w)^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}} .$$

So, with $\mathcal W^{ij}_{p}$ and $\mathcal W^{ij}_{k}$ the two $p^{+}$–independent tensors:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{1}}{d^{3}k}=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{ig^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{x,y,z,w,x',w'}e^{-ik(w'-w)}\frac{(x'-w')^{i}(y-z)^{j}}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-z)^{2}} \left\{-\frac{\delta^{ij}\log\frac{B}{A}}{2k^{+}C} +\mathcal W^{ij}_{p}\,\mathcal J_{1}+\frac{\mathcal W^{ij}_{k}}{k^{+}}\,\mathcal J_{2}\right\}$$

plus the second, $w\leftrightarrow w'$ term with the opposite sign. The $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ sits only in $\mathcal J_{1}$; the $\log\vee$ only in $\mathcal J_{2}$.

6. Where this leaves you

  • No UV in either piece, so no transverse counterterm and no $\overline{\rm MS}$ subtraction is needed before the $p^{+}$ integral.
  • The $z$ integral is IR log divergent in both, with the coefficients of §3.2. Regulate in $d=2-2\epsilon$, $\epsilon>0$, as in the previous row — but restore the $4\rho$ term first, because the tails of $N_{1}$, $N_{2}$ and $N_{4}$ are all built from the same $\frac{(x-w)^{i}(x-z)^{j}}{(x-w)^{2}(x-z)^{2}}$ kernel.
  • The $p^{+}$ integrals are done: closed forms in §4 and §5, all verified. The rapidity logarithm is isolated in one structure in each piece ($\mathcal J_{1}$ for $N_{1}$, $T_{3}T_{3}'$ for $N_{2}$), so the $\big[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\big]_{+}$ prescription applies to that structure alone — exactly as in the previous rows.
  • The $\log\vee$ is real and not a rapidity log ($\mathcal J_{2}$, $T_{2}T_{2}'$). It comes from the large–$p^{+}$ end where the eikonal treatment breaks down; it will not be absorbed by evolution and should be checked against the kinematic bound.
