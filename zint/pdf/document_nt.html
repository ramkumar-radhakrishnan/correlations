The $w\to z$ region of $N_{2}$

Where $f\,f\,U(w)U(z)=N_{c}U$ actually fires — and whether it can make a divergence

$U$ adjoint, real orthogonal; $f^{abc}$ totally antisymmetric; $N_{c}=3$ throughout. Ket $=f^{cbd}U^{ac}(w)\rho^{d}(x)-f^{acd}U^{bc}(z)U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)$, free indices $a$ (observed gluon, at $w$) and $b$ (unobserved gluon, at $z$).

Short answer: no divergence, and the identity does not fire at $w\to z$. The collapse $f\,f\,U\,U\to N_{c}$ needs the two Wilson lines to sit at the same point and to be contracted through a common summed index, so that $\sum_{a}U^{ac'}U^{ac}=\delta^{c'c}$ fires first. In the cross terms $U(w)$ carries the observed gluon's colour $a$ and $U(z)$ carries the unobserved gluon's colour $b$: two different free indices, one on each $f$. Setting $w=z$ makes the matrices equal but leaves the index structure alone — no $\delta$, no $N_{c}$ (§3, numerically confirmed). The identity does fire in two other places (§2), and in both the transverse integrand is convergent anyway. And in any case (§4) the integrand at $w\to z$ is $\rho^{-1}$, not $\rho^{-2}$, with the full colour factor in place — and colour is a bounded contraction of orthogonal matrices, so it can only make a region more convergent, never less.

1. The identity, verified

Because $U(z)$ is an adjoint rotation and $f$ an invariant tensor, $f^{a'b'c'}U^{aa'}U^{bb'}U^{cc'}=f^{abc}$; contracting one leg with $U^{T}$ gives the form you want,

$$f^{abc}\,U^{cd}(z)\;=\;U^{aa'}(z)\,U^{bb'}(z)\,f^{a'b'd}\,,\qquad f^{acd}f^{bcd}=N_{c}\delta^{ab}.$$

Numerically: $|U^{T}U-\mathbb 1|_{\max}=4\cdot10^{-16}$, first identity $3\cdot10^{-16}$, second $1\cdot10^{-15}$. Both are needed: the second is what actually produces the $N_{c}$, and it needs a $\delta$ to have appeared on a pair of $f$ legs first.

2. Where the collapse really happens in this row

(a) The $(2\rho)\times(2\rho)$ colour factor — unconditionally

Bra and ket both carry $U(\cdot)$ at the same point $z$, sharing the summed index $b$:

$$\sum_{b}U^{bc'}(z)U^{bc}(z)=\delta^{c'c}\;\Longrightarrow\; \sum_{a,c'}f^{ac'd'}f^{ac'd}=N_{c}\delta^{d'd}\;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\;\mathcal C_{22}^{\,e'e}=N_{c}\,\big[U(x')U(x)\big]^{e'e}\;}$$

verified to $8\cdot10^{-15}$. This is your identity, and it is worth noticing: the $(2)\times(2)$ colour factor carries no $z$ and no $w$ dependence at all, so it pulls straight out of every transverse integral. (That is the piece still missing from your expression — see §1.3 of the previous note.)

(b) The $(1\rho)\times(1\rho)$ colour factor at $w'\to w$

Here the shared summed index is $a$:

$$\sum_{a}U^{ac'}(w')U^{ac}(w)\;\xrightarrow[\;w'\to w\;]{}\;\delta^{c'c} \;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal C_{11}^{\,d'd}\big|_{w'=w}=N_{c}\,\delta^{d'd}$$

verified to $2\cdot10^{-15}$, against $\|\mathcal C_{11}\|_{\max}=0.94$ at generic $w'$. So there is an $N_{c}$ enhancement — but at $w'=w$, not at $w=z$.

3. Why it does not fire at $w\to z$

The cross terms are

$$\Sigma^{d'd}=\sum_{a,b,c,c'}f^{ac'd'}\,U^{bc'}(z)\;\;f^{cbd}\,U^{ac}(w)\,.$$

The two Wilson lines sit on different free indices: $U^{ac}(w)$ on the observed gluon's colour $a$, $U^{bc'}(z)$ on the unobserved gluon's colour $b$; and $a$ and $b$ are summed independently, one against each $f$. Putting $w=z$ makes $U(w)=U(z)$ as matrices, but it does not bring $a$ and $b$ together, so $\sum_{a}U^{ac'}U^{ac}$ never appears and there is no $\delta$ to feed $f^{acd}f^{acd'}=N_{c}\delta^{dd'}$.

$\langle|{\rm full}|\rangle\,\rho$ is flat to ten digits: the integrand is $\rho^{-1}$, so against $d^{2}\rho\sim\rho\,d\rho$ it is $\int d\rho$ — linearly convergent, no log. A logarithm needs $\rho^{-2}$. Better still, the signed angular average tends to a constant, not to $\rho^{-1}$: every $\rho^{-1}$ piece of $\mathcal W^{ji}$ is odd in $(z-w)$ and averages away, so the true behaviour is $O(1)$ and the region is softer even than the modulus bound suggests.

The general point. A colour factor cannot manufacture a divergence. Every one of them here is a finite contraction of $f$'s with orthogonal matrices, so $|\mathcal C|$ is bounded uniformly in all transverse arguments. Colour can only cancel a would–be divergence (by vanishing in the limit); it can never create one. The power counting is therefore settled by the transverse structure alone — which is why the Wilson–line–free scan of the previous note was already conclusive.

6. Conclusion

  • No divergence at $w\to z$. Transverse behaviour $\rho^{-1}$ (signed: $O(1)$), measure $\rho\,d\rho$: convergent. This confirms the earlier conclusion with the colour structure fully included rather than bounded away.
  • The identity does not apply at $w\to z$, because $U(w)$ and $U(z)$ hang off different final–state colour indices. It applies at $w'\to w$ in $\mathcal C_{11}$, and unconditionally in $\mathcal C_{22}$.
  • The useful by–product: $\mathcal C_{22}=N_{c}[U(x')U(x)]^{e'e}$ exactly, with no $z$ or $w$ dependence — so when you add the missing $(2\rho)\times(2\rho)$ piece, its colour factor factorises out of the whole transverse integral. That should make it the easiest of the three pieces to do.
