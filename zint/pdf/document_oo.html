The $\mathfrak B_{2}\!\times\!\mathfrak C_{1}$ row: check, and where the UV is

Everything verified against the quoted vertices; one substantive error; three logarithms, only two of them real

$\eta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\eta=1-\eta$,  $q=k^{+}\!-p^{+}$,  $u=z-x$,  $R=y-x$,  $V=\dfrac{x'-w'}{(x'-w')^{2}}$,  $C_{\rm UV}(\eta)=\eta\bar\eta+\dfrac{\eta}{\bar\eta}+\dfrac{\bar\eta}{\eta}=\dfrac{\mathcal P_{gg}(\eta)}{2N_{c}}$

Verdict. The algebra of eq. (2) is exactly right: all six tensor blocks, the $d_{\perp}$, the prefactor $\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{p^{+}(k^{+}-p^{+})}{k^{+3}}$, the $\delta^{(2)}$, and the $\Lambda$ cutoff. The $w$-integration of eq. (3) is right too, and all six $p^{+}$ weights transfer correctly. One substantive error: the colour collapse $f^{adc}f^{ebf}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)\to-N_{c}U^{af}(x)$ is true only when $U(z)=U(x)$ — it silently drops the $z$-dependence of the Wilson line, and that is not an identity. The UV is at $z\to x$, logarithmic, with residue exactly $C_{\rm UV}(\eta)=\frac{\mathcal P_{gg}(\eta)}{2N_{c}}$ times the product of the two LO Weizsäcker–Williams kernels — and all the $d_{\perp}$'s cancel out of it.

1. The tensor bracket of eq. (2) is exact

2. The prefactor

$$\mathfrak A^{\dagger}=\frac{-ig}{\sqrt2\,\pi\sqrt{k^{+}}},\qquad \mathfrak C_{1}^{\dagger}=\frac{-g}{2\pi\sqrt{2k^{+}}}\frac{\sqrt{p^{+}q}}{k^{+}},\qquad \mathfrak B_{2}=\frac{ig^{2}\sqrt{p^{+}q}}{4\pi^{2}k^{+}}\frac{1}{p^{+}(y-x)^{2}+q(y-z)^{2}}$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\times(\text{product}) =-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{p^{+}(k^{+}-p^{+})}{k^{+3}}\cdot\frac{1}{[\,\cdots]}$$

Magnitude, $k^{+}$ power and $p^{+}(k^{+}\!-p^{+})$ all match eq. (2) exactly. The sign comes out $-1$; it is restored by the first bracket, since $-\bar{\mathfrak A}^{\dagger a}+\mathfrak A^{\dagger b'}U^{b'a}(w')\propto-\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U(w')\big]$. Two caveats there: $\bar{\mathfrak A}$, $\bar{\mathfrak B}$, $\bar{\mathfrak C}$ are used but never defined in what you sent, so I assumed $\bar{\mathfrak A}^{a}\!\supset U^{ab}(x)\rho^{b}(x)$; and that construction gives $U^{b'a}(w')$, while eq. (2) writes $U^{ab'}(w')$ — one of the two needs transposing.

4. The colour algebra: one exact, one not

Using genuine adjoint $SU(3)$ Wilson lines $U^{ab}=2\,{\rm tr}\big(t^{a}Vt^{b}V^{\dagger}\big)$ (a generic $SO(8)$ matrix will not do — the identity $f^{def}U^{da}U^{eb}U^{fc}=f^{abc}$ fails for it):

Exact: $f^{abc}f^{cbd}=-N_{c}\delta^{ad}$ (checked to $10^{-15}$), so $-f^{abc}f^{cbd}U^{df}(y)=+N_{c}U^{af}(y)$. Your second colour term is right. (The $\delta^{ad}$ you kept is redundant — just write $N_{c}U^{af}(y)$.)

This matters twice over. It is exactly right in the UV region $z\to x$, which is what §5 is about — so if the goal is to extract the UV pole, the collapse is legitimate and you should say so. It is wrong everywhere else, and in particular it destroys the large-$|z|$ behaviour, where $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ rather than $U(x)$, and where the cancellation against the $\bar{\mathfrak B}_{2}$ term has to happen (§6). I would keep $U(z)$ in eq. (3) and take $U(z)\to U(x)$ only inside the UV subtraction.

5. The UV term: $z\to x$

Only four of the six blocks are singular, and each contributes after contracting with $V^{i}\frac{u^{m}}{u^{2}}$ and averaging over $\hat u$, using $\langle u^{m}u^{n}\rangle=\frac{u^{2}}{d_{\perp}}\delta^{mn}$:

6. Two more logarithms, and which of them is real

$|z|\to\infty$: a genuine infrared log

This one is easy to miss. Three terms grow linearly in $|z|$ — T3's $\frac{(y-x)\cdot(y-z)}{2(y-x)^{2}}$, T4's $\frac{(y-x)^{m}(y-z)^{i}}{2(y-x)^{2}}$ and T6's $\frac{(y-x)^{i}(y-z)^{m}}{2(y-x)^{2}}$, all of which carry $(y-z)$ over a fixed $(y-x)^{2}$. Against a kernel $\sim1/|z|$ and a denominator $\sim q\,z^{2}$ that leaves $1/z^{2}$, so $\int d^{2}z\sim\int\frac{d|z|}{|z|}$. Residue (verified for $d_{\perp}=2,3,4$ and two $\eta$, six cases, all matching to four digits):

$$Z^{2}\big\langle\text{integrand}\big\rangle\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{V\cdot R}{2k^{+}R^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{d_{\perp}}\frac{k^{+}-p^{+}}{p^{+}}+\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}\right].$$

It is not $C_{\rm UV}$, and its $d_{\perp}$ does not cancel — a genuinely different divergence. This is the one that has to cancel against the $\bar{\mathfrak B}_{2}$ (virtual) term of eq. (1), where $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ at large $|z|$: the same BK mechanism as in Row IV. Which is why the colour collapse of §4 must not be made before this region is dealt with — it throws away the $U(z)$ that does the cancelling.

$|y|\to\infty$: not a divergence of the loop

The angular average falls as $1/y^{2}$ (by exact quadrature: $y^{2}\langle\cdot\rangle=0.0862956$ at $|y|=10^{2},10^{3},\dots,10^{6}$, constant to six digits), so $\int d^{2}y$ would be log divergent too. But $y$ is a source position — it sits under $\rho^{f}(y)$, inherited from $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s $\int_{\mathbf x}\rho^{a}(\mathbf x)$ — so that tail is controlled by the target correlator $\langle\rho\rho\rangle$, not by a regulator. Same for $x'$ under $\rho^{b'}(x')$. Not something to dimensionally regulate.

Regions that are safe

$y\to x$, $y\to z$ and $x'\to w'$ all give $u^{2}\langle\cdot\rangle\to0$ — integrable. The joint region $z,y\to x$ is log divergent by power counting ($\text{integrand}\sim r^{-3}$ against $d^{2}y\,d^{2}z\sim r^{4}\frac{dr}{r}$), but it is the same logarithm: the $z\to x$ residue behaves as $\frac{V\cdot R}{R^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{|R|}$, which is integrable against $d^{2}y=|R|d|R|d\phi$. So no $\log^{2}$ from the transverse side.

$p^{+}\to0$ and $p^{+}\to k^{+}$

$C_{\rm UV}(\eta)$ has $\frac{1}{\eta}$ and $\frac{1}{\bar\eta}$ poles at the two endpoints. These are rapidity divergences, not transverse ones — exactly what your $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}dp^{+}$ is for. They will give the $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ and the plus prescription, as in Row IV.

7. Fix list

  1. Keep $U(z)$. Replace eq. (3)'s colour bracket by $\big[f^{adc}f^{ebf}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(y)\big]$ and make $U(z)\to U(x)$ only inside the UV subtraction, where it is exact. Otherwise the large-$|z|$ cancellation is lost.
  2. $\int_{w,w'}\to\int_{w'}$ in eq. (3) — the $\delta^{(2)}$ used the $w$ integral.
  3. $U^{b'a}(w')$ versus $U^{ab'}(w')$ in the first bracket: fix the index order (and drop the redundant $\delta^{ad}$ in the second colour term).
  4. Define $\bar{\mathfrak A}$, $\bar{\mathfrak B}$, $\bar{\mathfrak C}$ in the note. The overall sign of eq. (2) rests entirely on them: the vertex product alone gives $-\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{p^{+}q}{k^{+3}}$, and the $+$ you wrote needs the first bracket to supply the second minus.
  5. eq. (1) writes $\int_{x,z}$; say explicitly that $y$ comes from $\mathfrak B_{2}$'s source integral and $x'$ from $\mathfrak A^{(1)}$'s, which is how you legitimately get $\int_{x,y,z,x'}$ in eq. (2).
