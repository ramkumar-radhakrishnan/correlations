$2b$ block by block: which parts are finite, and how to do them

Three of the six blocks need nothing at all; the other three carry one term of $C_{\rm UV}$ each

$\eta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\eta=1-\eta$,  $L=\log\dfrac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$,  $\mathcal W=\dfrac{(x'-w')\!\cdot\!(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}$,  $C_{\rm UV}=\eta\bar\eta+\dfrac{\eta}{\bar\eta}+\dfrac{\bar\eta}{\eta}$

Yes — and the split is cleaner than you might expect. The six blocks of $2b$ divide into two groups that need completely different treatment:
  • $T_{3}+T_{5}+T_{6}$: nothing at all. Their UV residues cancel within the group ($\frac{2}{d_{\perp}}(1-\bar\eta-\eta)=0$), and their weights $\bar\eta$, $\eta$, $-1$ are regular at both $p^{+}$ endpoints. No dimensional regularisation, no plus prescription — just integrate.
  • $T_{1}$, $T_{2}$, $T_{4}$: one term of $C_{\rm UV}$ each. Their UV residues are exactly $\eta\bar\eta$, $\frac{\eta}{\bar\eta}$, $\frac{\bar\eta}{\eta}$ — the three terms of $C_{\rm UV}$, one per block. These need the counterterm. And $T_{2}$, $T_{4}$ are also the only two with $p^{+}$ poles, which is why the transverse subtraction must come first.

2. Group (B): $T_{3}+T_{5}+T_{6}$ — nothing special

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{2b}^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\rm (B)}= \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\!\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi} \int_{x,y,z,x'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-\bar\eta z-\eta x)}\, \frac{(x'-w')^{i}(z-x)^{m}}{(x'-w')^{2}(z-x)^{2}}\; \Big[T_{3}+T_{5}+T_{6}\Big]_{im}\times(\text{colour})$$

with everything at $d_{\perp}=2$ and no prescription anywhere:

  • $z\to x$: finite, by the cancellation above.
  • Large $|z|$: cut by the phase $e^{i\bar\eta k\cdot z}$ at $|z|\sim\frac{1}{\bar\eta k_{\perp}}$.
  • $p^{+}\to0$ and $p^{+}\to k^{+}$: the weights $\bar\eta$, $\eta$, $-1$ are bounded and the companion $h(\eta)=\frac{e^{-i\eta\kappa}}{\eta A+\bar\eta B}$ is smooth, so the $dp^{+}$ integral simply converges. You may even extend it to $\int_{0}^{k^{+}}$ — the difference is $O(\Lambda)$.

3. Group (A): $T_{1}$, $T_{2}$, $T_{4}$ — subtract first, then $p^{+}$

Because $T_{2}$ and $T_{4}$ carry both a UV divergence and a $p^{+}$ pole, the order is forced. Subtract block by block, each with its own weight:

$$\mathcal S_{n}(\eta)=w_{n}(\eta)\;\mathcal W\;\frac{e^{i\bar\eta\,k\cdot(z-x)}}{k^{+}(z-x)^{2}}, \qquad w_{1}=\eta\bar\eta,\quad w_{2}=\frac{\eta}{\bar\eta},\quad w_{4}=\frac{\bar\eta}{\eta}.$$

(ii) The remainders — now the plus prescription is allowed

Check first that the subtraction really cured the endpoint residues. Each residue on its own is a constant in $|z-x|$ (i.e. log divergent, value $0.0322$ in the test configuration); the difference falls as $|z-x|^{2}$:

So the remainders can be plus-prescribed, one-sided each:

$$T_{4}\ \text{remainder}:\ \Big[\frac{1}{\eta}\Big]_{+}\ \text{at }p^{+}\to0, \qquad T_{2}\ \text{remainder}:\ \Big[\frac{1}{1-\eta}\Big]_{+}\ \text{at }p^{+}\to k^{+},$$

each producing one $\delta$ term with $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, while $T_{1}$'s remainder has no endpoint pole at all and is an ordinary convergent double integral.

Do not plus-prescribe before subtracting. Before the subtraction, $g(\text{endpoint})$ for $T_{2}$ and $T_{4}$ is a UV-divergent transverse integral, and $\int\frac{d\eta}{\eta}\big[g(\eta)-g(0)\big]$ requires $g(0)$ finite. You would be subtracting infinity from infinity and the finite part would be scheme-dependent garbage. This is the one place in $2b$ where the order of operations actually matters.

4. The recipe, in order

  1. Write $2b=\text{(A)}+\text{(B)}$ with (A) $=T_{1}\!+\!T_{2}\!+\!T_{4}$ and (B) $=T_{3}\!+\!T_{5}\!+\!T_{6}$.
  2. (B): set $d_{\perp}=2$, integrate $dp^{+}$ directly, done. No regulator, no prescription.
  3. (A): subtract $\mathcal S_{1}+\mathcal S_{2}+\mathcal S_{4}$ from the integrand.
  4. The subtracted counterterms: use the closed forms in the table of §3(i). This is where the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$, the $\log\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}}{\bar\mu^{2}}$, the $-\frac{11}{6}$ ($\beta_{0}$) and the $-L^{2}$ live.
  5. The remainders: $d_{\perp}=2$, then one-sided plus prescriptions on $T_{2}$ and $T_{4}$, nothing on $T_{1}$.

Net: one $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ in all of $2b$, four $\delta(1-\ldots)$-type $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ terms (two from the counterterms' $2L$, two from the remainders' plus prescriptions), and three finite multi-dimensional integrals that are done numerically.
