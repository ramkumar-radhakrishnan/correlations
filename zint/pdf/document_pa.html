Row V, part 2a: checking your plus-prescription rewrite

Is there a UV divergence? Is the rewrite correct term by term? Can the two log terms go into evolution? Every step is shown and checked numerically.

$r=z-x$,  $s=y-x$,  $P=x'-w'$,  $\xi=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\xi=1-\xi=\dfrac{q^{+}}{k^{+}}$ with $q^{+}=k^{+}-p^{+}$,  $L=\log\dfrac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}$,  $\mathcal W=\dfrac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}s^{2}}$

Verdict.
  1. No UV divergence: correct. The reason is the colour factor: it vanishes at $z=x$ (§2). Keep it as one piece; do not split $-f f\,U(x)U(z)$ from $N_{c}U(x)$.
  2. The rewrite has one error. The $T_{2}$ term $-\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}\big[\dots\big]$ is still in your first part, and it is moved to the $q^{+}$ part. So it is counted twice, and $\int_{0}^{k^{+}}$ of the leftover copy diverges at $p^{+}\to k^{+}$. Delete it from the first part. After that, everything else is right, including signs, prefactors, phases and $\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}$ (§3).
  3. The two log terms are not on the same footing (§4).
    • The $p^{+}\to0$ log has a finite $z$ integral. It is the evolution-type term, but its $x$ integral diverges logarithmically at large $|x|$, so it only makes sense after combining with the other rows.
    • The $p^{+}\to k^{+}$ log has a $z$ integral that diverges at large $|z|$. It is not "log $\times$ finite kernel". Done properly, it is a double log, $\frac{1}{2}\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}L^{2}$, and should not be put into the evolution kernel.

1. Notation

Put $k^{+}$ outside, so that $dp^{+}=k^{+}d\xi$. Your bracket has six blocks, which I call $T_{1}\dots T_{6}$ in the order written. Each block is contracted with $\frac{P^{i}r^{m}}{P^{2}r^{2}}$. The two blocks with poles are

$$T_{2}=-\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}\,B_{2},\qquad B_{2}\equiv\frac{P^{i}r^{m}}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[\frac{(y-x)^{i}(x-z)^{m}}{(x-z)^{2}}-\frac{(y-x)^{i}(y-z)^{m}}{2(y-z)^{2}}\Big],$$ $$T_{4}=-\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\,B_{4},\qquad B_{4}\equiv\frac{P^{i}r^{m}}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[\frac{(y-z)^{i}(x-z)^{m}}{(x-z)^{2}}+\frac{(y-x)^{m}(y-z)^{i}}{2(y-x)^{2}}\Big].$$

$B_{2}$ and $B_{4}$ do not depend on $p^{+}$. The rest of the integrand does:

$$\Phi(p^{+})=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}\,e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}},\qquad \frac{1}{\mathcal D(p^{+})}=\frac{1}{p^{+}(y-x)^{2}+(k^{+}-p^{+})(y-z)^{2}}.$$
A notation point. $\Phi$ depends on $p^{+}$, so it belongs inside $\int dp^{+}$ (and inside $\int_{x,z}$), not in front of the braces. It matters here: the plus prescription subtracts the integrand at the endpoint including $\Phi$. That is why your $\delta$ terms correctly carry $\Phi(0)=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}$ and $\Phi(k^{+})=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}$.

2. Is there a UV divergence? No — step by step

Step 2.1: where a UV divergence could come from. A 2D integral diverges only where the integrand grows like $\frac{1}{(\text{distance})^{2}}$ or faster. The possible points are $z\to x$, $z\to y$ and $y\to x$. At $z\to y$ and $y\to x$, every $\frac{1}{(y-z)^{2}}$ or $\frac{1}{(y-x)^{2}}$ comes with one power of the same vector upstairs, so it is only $\sim\frac{1}{\rm distance}$: finite. The denominator $\mathcal D=p^{+}(y-x)^{2}+(k^{+}-p^{+})(y-z)^{2}$ vanishes only if $y\to x$ and $y\to z$ together. There the measure is $\rho^{3}d\rho$ against at most $\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}$ (after the colour factor below), so that point is finite too. So only $z\to x$ ($r\to0$) needs checking.

Step 2.2: the blocks at $r\to0$. Several blocks go like $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ there. This is exactly the UV behaviour we found in 2b, $\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{r^{2}}$. So without the colour factor, 2a would be log divergent.

Step 2.3: the colour factor vanishes at $z=x$. The adjoint Wilson line leaves $f^{abc}$ invariant: $U^{aa'}U^{dd'}U^{cc'}f^{a'd'c'}=f^{adc}$. Multiplying by $U^{d b}U^{c e}$ and using $U^{T}U=1$ gives $f^{adc}U^{db}U^{ce}=U^{aa'}f^{a'be}$. Contract with $f^{fbe}$, using $f^{a'be}f^{fbe}=N_{c}\delta^{a'f}$:

$$f^{adc}f^{fbe}\,U^{db}(x)\,U^{ce}(x)=N_{c}\,U^{af}(x).$$

Hence your colour factor can be written as

$$-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)=-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big].$$

This was checked numerically with $SU(3)$ matrices, to $10^{-15}$.

Step 2.4: two powers are gained. $U(z)-U(x)=r\cdot\partial U(x)+O(r^{2})$ vanishes linearly. So the integrand becomes $\frac{1}{r^{2}}\times r\sim\frac{1}{r}$, which is integrable against $d^{2}r=\rho\,d\rho\,d\phi$. Moreover the linear term is odd in the direction $\hat r$, so its angular average vanishes, and what is left is $O(r^{0})$.

Check. Take $\rho^{2}\times$ the angular average of (integrand $\times$ colour) on a circle of radius $\rho$ around $x$. A constant would mean a log divergence. For the full 2a integrand it is $4.2\cdot10^{-5},\,10^{-7},\,10^{-9},\,10^{-11}$ at $\rho=10^{-2}\dots10^{-5}$.

For your two new $\delta$ terms, the same test gives:

term$\rho=10^{-2}$$10^{-3}$$10^{-4}$
$\delta(p^{+})$ term, $\frac{B_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}\times$ colour$-4.8\cdot10^{-4}$$-4.8\cdot10^{-6}$$-4.8\cdot10^{-8}$
$\delta(q^{+})$ term, $\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}\times$ colour$-4.7\cdot10^{-4}$$-4.7\cdot10^{-6}$$-4.7\cdot10^{-8}$

Both fall to zero, so there is no UV divergence anywhere. Your statement is correct.

Caution. This works only because the two colour terms are kept together. Taken separately, $-ff\,U(x)U(z)$ and $N_{c}U(x)$ are each UV divergent, with equal and opposite poles.

3. The plus-prescription rewrite, step by step

All constants $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi}$ and the colour factor are common to every term, so I suppress them until §3.4.

3.1 The $p^{+}\to0$ pole (block $T_{4}$)

(a) Split the coefficient: $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{k^{+}-p^{+}}{p^{+}}=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}-1$. So $T_{4}=-\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}B_{4}+B_{4}$. The $+B_{4}$ is regular, and you have it as $+\big[\dots\big]$. ✓

(b) Let $g(p^{+})\equiv\frac{\Phi(p^{+})}{\mathcal D(p^{+})}B_{4}$. Subtract and add $g(0)$:

$$-\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!dp^{+}\,\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\,g(p^{+}) =-\int_{0}^{k^{+}}\!dp^{+}\,\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\big[g(p^{+})-g(0)\big]\;-\;k^{+}g(0)\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}.$$
  • The first term is your $-\big[\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\big]_{+}$. Extending the limits to $0$ and $k^{+}$ costs only $O(\Lambda)$, because the bracket is finite at both ends.
  • In the second, $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}=\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}$ exactly.

(c) The endpoint value: $\Phi(0)=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}$ and $\mathcal D(0)=k^{+}(y-z)^{2}$, so $k^{+}g(0)=\frac{e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}}{(y-z)^{2}}B_{4}$.

(d) Restore the constants. The $\delta$ term is $-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}\;e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}\frac{B_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}$. Here $\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}=\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}$, which is exactly your third term. ✓

3.2 The $p^{+}\to k^{+}$ pole (block $T_{2}$)

(a) Split the coefficient: $\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}-1$. So $T_{2}=-\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}B_{2}+B_{2}$.

(b) Change variable, $q^{+}=k^{+}-p^{+}$, so $dp^{+}=-dq^{+}$ and $p^{+}:0\to k^{+}$ becomes $q^{+}:k^{+}\to0$. The phase and the denominator become:

  • $\Phi=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)}e^{+ik\cdot(z-x)\frac{q^{+}}{k^{+}}}=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\,e^{-ik\cdot(x-z)\frac{q^{+}}{k^{+}}}$;
  • $\mathcal D=(k^{+}-q^{+})(y-x)^{2}+q^{+}(y-z)^{2}$.

Both are as you wrote. ✓

(c) With $h(q^{+})\equiv\frac{\Phi}{\mathcal D}B_{2}$:

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!dp^{+}\,\Big(-\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}+1\Big)h =\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!dq^{+}\Big(-\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}+1\Big)h =-\int_{0}^{k^{+}}\!dq^{+}\Big\{\Big[\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}\Big]_{+}-1\Big\}h\;-\;k^{+}h(0)\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}.$$

Your $\int_{k^{+}}^{0}dq^{+}\big\{[\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}]_{+}B_{2}-B_{2}\big\}$ equals the first term, because reversing the limits gives the minus sign. ✓ (It is clearer to write $-\int_{0}^{k^{+}}$, since a plus distribution is defined with the limits in increasing order.)

(d) The endpoint value: $\Phi(q^{+}{=}0)=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}$ and $\mathcal D=k^{+}(y-x)^{2}$. This gives your fourth term, $-\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}\,e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}$. ✓

Steps 3.2(a)–(d) replace $T_{2}$. But your first part still contains $-\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}-p^{+})}\Big[\frac{(y-x)^{i}(x-z)^{m}}{(x-z)^{2}}-\frac{(y-x)^{i}(y-z)^{m}}{2(y-z)^{2}}\Big]$, now integrated over $\int_{0}^{k^{+}}$.

  • It is counted twice.
  • Its $\int_{0}^{k^{+}}dp^{+}\frac{1}{k^{+}-p^{+}}$ diverges at $p^{+}\to k^{+}$.

3.4 The corrected formula

In the first part, delete the term $-\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}-p^{+})}\Big[\frac{(y-x)^{i}(x-z)^{m}}{(x-z)^{2}}-\frac{(y-x)^{i}(y-z)^{m}}{2(y-z)^{2}}\Big]$. Everything else stays as you wrote it. Schematically, with $\mathcal C_{a}=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\big[-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{2a}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\int_{x,y,z,x',w'}\bigg\{ \int_{0}^{k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\Phi(p^{+})}{\mathcal D(p^{+})}\Big[T_{1}+T_{3}+T_{5}+T_{6}+B_{4}\Big] -\int_{0}^{k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\Big[\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\Big]_{+}\frac{\Phi(p^{+})}{\mathcal D(p^{+})}B_{4}$$ $$\qquad-\int_{0}^{k^{+}}\!\frac{dq^{+}}{2\pi}\Big\{\Big[\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}\Big]_{+}-1\Big\}\frac{\Phi}{\mathcal D}\bigg|_{p^{+}=k^{+}-q^{+}}\!B_{2} -\frac{\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}}{2\pi}\Big[e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}\frac{B_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}+e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}\Big]\bigg\}\,\mathcal C_{a}$$

Here $T_{1},T_{3},T_{5},T_{6}$ are your blocks with their $p^{+}$-dependent coefficients, and $\int_{0}^{k^{+}}dp^{+}\big[\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\big]_{+}f\equiv\int_{0}^{k^{+}}dp^{+}\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}\big[f(p^{+})-f(0)\big]$.

This identity holds at fixed $x,y,z,x',w'$, up to $O(\Lambda/k^{+})$.

4. Do the two log terms "enter the evolution"?

A term $L\times K(x,y,z)$ can be absorbed into an evolution equation only if $\int_{x,y,z}K$ is finite (possibly after combining rows). Check each $\delta$ term.

4.1 The $p^{+}\to0$ log: yes, with one caveat

$K_{0}=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}\frac{B_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}\,\mathcal C_{a}$.

  • $z$ integral: finite. The phase $e^{ik\cdot z}$ oscillates, and at large $|z|$ the term falls like $\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}$. At $z\to x$ it is finite (§2).
  • $x$ integral: log divergent at large $|x|$. The phase $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}$ contains no $x$. As $U(x)\to1$ the colour factor tends to $-f^{abc}f^{fbe}U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}\delta^{af}$, which is not zero ($-2.29$ in the test projection). And $|x|^{2}\langle\frac{B_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}\rangle_{\hat x}\to-\frac{P\cdot(y-z)}{2P^{2}(y-z)^{2}}$: numerically $0.396984$, $0.396348$, $0.396342$ at $|x|=10,10^{2},10^{3}$, against the prediction $0.396341$. So $\int d^{2}x\sim\int\frac{d^{2}x}{x^{2}}$ diverges.

This is the same large-$|x|$ tail we found for 2b ($\beta_{0}$). It is the usual situation for a soft-gluon log: the tail cancels against the virtual and other real diagrams, and only the sum gives the JIMWLK/BK kernel. So this log does go into evolution, but only after you add the other Group I rows, not from 2a alone.

4.2 The $p^{+}\to k^{+}$ log: no — it is really a double log

$K_{1}=e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}\,\mathcal C_{a}$.

  • $z$ integral: log divergent at large $|z|$. The phase $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}$ contains no $z$.
  • Outside the target $U(z)\to1$, and the colour factor tends to $\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}$ ($\propto-f^{adc}f^{fbc}U^{db}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)$), which is not zero ($-1.03$ in the test projection).
  • From the definition of $B_{2}$: $\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}=\frac{\mathcal W}{r^{2}}\Big[-1-\frac{r\cdot(s-r)}{2(s-r)^{2}}\Big]\xrightarrow{|r|\to\infty}-\frac{\mathcal W}{2r^{2}}$. Numerically, $|z-x|^{2}\langle\cdot\rangle=0.375000$ at $|z-x|=10,10^{2},10^{3}$, against $-\frac{\mathcal W}{2}=0.375000$.
  • So $\int d^{2}z\,K_{1}\sim-\pi\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\log R_{\max}$: infinite.

Why. At $q^{+}=0$ exactly, nothing cuts off large $z$: the phase $e^{-ik\cdot(x-z)q^{+}/k^{+}}\to1$ and the denominator $\to k^{+}(y-x)^{2}$. For $q^{+}>0$, large $z$ is cut off at $|z-x|\sim|y-x|\sqrt{k^{+}/q^{+}}$ by the denominator, and at $|z-x|\sim\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}|k|}$ by the phase. So the $z$ integral and the limit $q^{+}\to0$ do not commute.

Your $\big[\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}\big]_{+}$ term subtracts $h(0)$, and $\int_{z}h(0)$ is the same divergent integral. So after $\int_{z}$:

  • the $\big[\frac{k^{+}}{q^{+}}\big]_{+}$ term and the $\delta(q^{+})$ log term are separately infinite;
  • only their sum is finite.

Doing $\int_{z}$ first at fixed $q^{+}$ (rowV_full_derivation.pdf, §8), the sum behaves as $\frac{\pi\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\log(k^{+}/q^{+})}{q^{+}/k^{+}}$ near $q^{+}\to0$. Half of this comes from $T_{2}$ in $|s|<|r|<|s|\sqrt{k^{+}/q^{+}}$, and half from $T_{3},T_{4},T_{6}$ further out. After $\int dq^{+}$ this gives

$$\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\,L^{2}+(\text{single }L)+(\text{finite}),$$

not a single $L$ times a finite kernel.

Consequence. Do not put the $\delta(q^{+})$ term $-\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{k^{+}-\Lambda}{\Lambda}\,e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\frac{B_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}$ into the evolution kernel on its own. Its $z$ integral does not exist. If you want this endpoint in "log $\times$ kernel" form, do the $z$ integral first, keeping $q^{+}>0$. The result is the double log above; it is a Sudakov-type term, not evolution.
