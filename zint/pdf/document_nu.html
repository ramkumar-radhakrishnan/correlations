Where DGLAP comes from

It is not about how many gluons you measure — it is about which ones you don't

$\zeta=k^{+}/S$ the measured daughter's momentum fraction of the parent $S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$; $C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta=P_{gg}(\zeta)/2N_{c}$

No — and the logic runs the other way. Collinear (DGLAP) logarithms are produced by unresolved partons. Measuring a parton fixes its momentum and thereby removes it from the set that can generate a collinear log. So integrating one final–state gluon out is not what prevents fragmentation — it is the very thing that enables it. Your own Row 8 proves it: single–inclusive gluon production, one gluon measured, one integrated out, and it produces the complete $P_{gg}(\zeta)$ (§1). The reason $N_{2}$ does not is specific to $N_{2}$'s topology, not to single–inclusive kinematics (§2).

1. The counterexample you already computed: Row 8

Row 8 is the row with two $\mathfrak C$ vertices. Its kinematics are exactly the ones you are asking about: $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}$ creates one gluon of momentum $S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$ from $\rho$, and $\mathfrak C$ splits it into the measured gluon ($k^{+}$) and the unobserved one ($p^{+}$), which is integrated out. Doing the $z$ integral in $d=2-2\epsilon$ and assembling:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{\pi}{k^{+2}}\Big\{\,C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X\;+\;\zeta\bar\zeta\,\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\Big\}\;}$$

with $\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2})$ the $\overline{\rm MS}$ transverse log, and the symbolic difference against the direct expansion exactly $0$. The coefficient of the transverse log is the full splitting function — all three pieces $\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta$, including the regular $\zeta\bar\zeta$. The $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ term dies by $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$, which is the momentum sum rule. That is a DGLAP log, in single–gluon production, with one final–state gluon integrated out.

And its kinematics are the fragmentation ones: the $\mathfrak C$ delta ties the emission points as $w'-w=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y'-y)=\frac{1}{\zeta}(y'-y)$, so the phase is $e^{-i(k_{\perp}/\zeta)\cdot(y'-y)}$ — the hard factor sits at the parent's transverse momentum $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, with measure $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$.

That is the eikonal, one–on–each–side–of–the–shockwave configuration, and it produces a rapidity log — which is exactly what came out.

3. Why measuring both gluons would make it worse, not better

A collinear logarithm is $\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{q_{\perp}^{2}}$ over the relative transverse momentum of a splitting. That integral only exists if $q_{\perp}$ is integrated. If you measure both gluons, the pair's relative momentum is fixed by the measurement: there is no $q_{\perp}$ integral left, the splitting is fully resolved, and no collinear log arises from that pair at all. What you get instead is a finite two–particle correlation with a steep (but integrable in the measured variables) enhancement at small opening angle.

At NLO for two–gluon production the collinear logs come back — but from the third, unobserved parton, not from the measured pair. The rule is completely general:

Collinear logs are generated by unresolved partons only. Each measurement you impose removes one parton from that set. Single–inclusive production at NLO has exactly one unresolved parton, and it is precisely that parton which generates the three collinear/rapidity structures the calculation must contain.

5. The practical test

You do not have to guess which row is which. Two diagnostics settle it, and both are cheap:

  1. Power counting on the candidate collinear separation. A log needs the integrand $\sim\rho^{-2}$ there, i.e. both the amplitude and the conjugate kernel singular on the same variable. $\rho^{-1}$ means no log.
  2. The $\zeta$ weight. Strip the transverse factor and vary $\zeta$ at fixed $S$. If the coefficient tracks $C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta\bar\zeta}-2$ it is DGLAP; if it is $\frac{1}{\zeta\bar\zeta}$ alone (or $\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}$ alone) it is soft, hence a rapidity log. The missing $\zeta\bar\zeta-2$ is the tell — the regular part of $P_{gg}$ is what no soft approximation can produce.

Row 8 passes both. $N_{2}$ fails both, in exactly the way a soft region should.
