1. The leftover bracket

The second term carries $\big[\frac{\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}-\frac{\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}}{p^{+}} -\frac{\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}}{k^{+}}\big]$. It should not. Three reasons, any one sufficient:

  • the contraction has already been done — it is sitting in the numerator you wrote, $\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}$ on $(y'{-}z)^{m}(x'{-}y')^{k'}(y{-}w)^{i}(x{-}z)^{j}$ giving $m{=}j:(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!(x{-}z)$ and $i{=}k':(x'{-}y')\!\cdot\!(y{-}w)$;
  • the $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ has already been integrated — it is the $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$;
  • there is no $\int dp^{+}$ left, so a free $p^{+}$ in that term is undefined.

The second term should read

$$+\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{ig^{4}f^{c'd'a}}{8\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \log\!\left[\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}\right] \int_{w,y',z,y,x,x'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\, \frac{(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!(x{-}z)\;(x'{-}y')\!\cdot\!(y{-}w)} {(y'{-}z)^{2}(x'{-}y')^{2}(y{-}w)^{2}(x{-}z)^{2}}\;\times\;[\text{colour}].$$

2. The first term is transversely divergent

Write the three index structures by their $z$–dependence, with $a=x'-y'$ and $b=y-w$ the spectator vectors:

$$S_{1}=\frac{(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!a}{(y'{-}z)^{2}}\frac{(x{-}z)\!\cdot\!b}{(x{-}z)^{2}},\qquad S_{2}=\frac{(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!(x{-}z)}{(y'{-}z)^{2}(x{-}z)^{2}},\qquad S_{3}=\frac{(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!b}{(y'{-}z)^{2}}\frac{(x{-}z)\!\cdot\!a}{(x{-}z)^{2}}.$$

All three fall only as $1/z^{2}$, with nonvanishing angular average $\rho^{2}\langle S_{1}\rangle,\rho^{2}\langle S_{3}\rangle\to\frac{a\cdot b}{2}$ and $\rho^{2}\langle S_{2}\rangle\to1$:

       rho        S1 (d_k'm d_ij)        S2 (d_jm d_ik')        S3 (d_im d_jk')
     1e+02              -2.720213               1.000230              -2.720317
     1e+03              -2.719644               1.000002              -2.719645
     1e+04              -2.719638               1.000000              -2.719638
   predicted   (x'-y').(y-w)/2 = -2.719638             1     (y-w).(x'-y')/2 = -2.719638

so every structure is logarithmically divergent in $z$ unless a phase cuts it.

Which ones keep their phase

$S_{1}$ and $S_{3}$ sit on $\frac{1}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$ and $\frac{1}{k^{+}}$, which are untouched by the prescription, so they keep the full $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}$. The oscillation kills the angular average and their $z$ integrals converge for every $\xi>0$, leaving $\log\frac{1}{\xi}$; and neither coefficient has a $\frac{1}{\xi}$ pole, so $\int_{0}d\xi\,\log\frac1\xi$ converges. Those two are fine.

$S_{2}$ is not. The prescription replaces the phase by $\big(e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}-1\big)$, and the $-1$ has no phase at all. What is left under $\int d^{2}z$ is $S_{2}$ bare — and $S_{2}$ is exactly the BK real–emission kernel $\mathcal K(x,y';z)$, which falls as $1/z^{2}$. Measured $\rho^{2}\big|\langle(e^{-i\xi\kappa}-1)S_{2}\rangle\big|$ does not decay:

   xi = 0.3     rho=1e+02: 0.865102    rho=1e+03: 1.043748    rho=1e+04: 1.006886
   xi = 0.05    rho=1e+02: 1.038685    rho=1e+03: 1.107809    rho=1e+04: 0.967298

compared with $|\langle e^{-i\xi\kappa}S_{2}\rangle|\rho^{2}=1.4\times10^{-1},4.4\times10^{-2}, 6.9\times10^{-3}$ for the unsubtracted phase at $\xi=0.3$. So $\int\rho\,d\rho\,(-1/\rho^{2})=-\log$: the first term diverges logarithmically at large $|z|$, for every value of $p^{+}$.

And the same divergence sits in your second term — it is $g(0)$, whose $z$ integral is the same bare $\mathcal K$. The two divergences cancel only in the sum, i.e. only if you never split. That is the concrete form of the warning from last time: the $+$ prescription moves the transverse divergence, it does not remove it. Splitting a finite object into two infinite ones is not progress.

3. A second, independent problem: the upper limit

The unsplit $p^{+}$ integral has a finite $\vee\to\infty$ limit. The split does not: the subtracted piece is $\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\Xi)-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa\Xi)\to-\log\Xi$, and the log piece is $+\log\frac{\Xi}{\lambda}$. Each slides with $\vee$; only the sum is stable:

Fix: subtract only below $k^{+}$

$$\left[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\right]^{(k^{+})}_{+}g\;\equiv\;\frac{g(p^{+})-\theta(k^{+}-p^{+})\,g(0)}{p^{+}}, \qquad\text{isolating}\quad \ell_{k}=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}\ \text{ instead of }\ \log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}.$$

Now the $+$ piece is $\vee$–stable, and the isolated log is the one BK evolution wants:

   Xi=1e2:  + piece with theta cut = -1.105837 - 4.706860i

This also settles the scheme point from the previous note: the finite difference $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{k^{+}}$ is now explicitly where it belongs, in the impact factor.

4. What to do

  1. Delete the bracket from the second term.
  2. Collect the virtual diagrams first. They replace $-2\mathcal K$ by $\mathcal M(x,y';z)=\frac{(x-y')^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}} =\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(y'-z)^{2}}-2\mathcal K$, whose large–$\rho$ angular average vanishes at order $\rho^{-2}$ ($1+1-2=0$) so that it falls as $\rho^{-4}$. Only then is $g(0)$ transversely finite and the split legitimate.
  3. Use the $\theta(k^{+}-p^{+})$ subtraction so each piece has a $\vee\to\infty$ limit and the isolated log is $\ell_{k}$.
  4. Then the second term is $\ell_{k}\times\mathcal M\otimes(\text{LO})$, to be absorbed into the rapidity evolution of the Wilson–line correlators, and the first term is the NLO impact factor — finite in both $p^{+}$ and the transverse integrals.

A useful self–check at step 4: with the virtuals in, the answer must carry a single $\ell_{k}$. If you still find $\ell_{k}^{2}$, the subtraction term is still transversely divergent and something upstream is missing — the double log is the signature of exactly the entanglement diagnosed in §2.
