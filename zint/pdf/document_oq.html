The identity $f^{abc}f^{def}U^{be}U^{cf}=N_{c}S^{ad}$

Correct, with $S^{ad}=U^{ad}$; the two-line proof, and what it does and does not license

$U^{ab}(z)=2\,{\rm tr}\big(t^{a}V(z)t^{b}V^{\dagger}(z)\big)$ — the adjoint Wilson line;  $U^{T}U=\mathbb 1$;  $f^{abc}f^{gbc}=N_{c}\delta^{ag}$

The identity is correct, and $S^{ad}(z)$ is nothing new — it is $U^{ad}(z)$ itself, the adjoint Wilson line, in that index order (the transpose is wrong: $\|M-N_{c}U^{ad}\|=1.8\times10^{-15}$ versus $\|M-N_{c}U^{da}\|=5.39$). It requires both Wilson lines at the same point, so invoking it is taking the $x\to z$ limit — which is exactly why it isolates the UV, and exactly why the exact remainder must be kept separately. With your index names it reads $f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(z)U^{ce}(z)=N_{c}U^{af}(z)$.

1. The two-line proof

The only input is that the adjoint line preserves the structure constants,

$$f^{abc}=U^{aa'}(z)\,U^{bb'}(z)\,U^{cc'}(z)\,f^{a'b'c'}$$

(verified to $7.8\times10^{-16}$; note a generic $SO(8)$ rotation does not satisfy this — only a genuine adjoint $SU(3)$ line does). Multiply by $U^{ad}$ and use $U^{ad}U^{aa'}=\delta^{da'}$:

$$f^{def}\,U^{be}\,U^{cf}\;=\;U^{gd}\,f^{gbc}.$$

Contract with $f^{abc}$ and use $f^{abc}f^{gbc}=N_{c}\delta^{ag}$ (verified to $1.3\times10^{-15}$):

$$f^{abc}f^{def}U^{be}(z)U^{cf}(z)=U^{gd}\,f^{abc}f^{gbc}=N_{c}\,U^{ad}(z).\qquad\blacksquare$$

2. It holds only at coincident points — which is the whole point

With the two lines at different points there is no collapse at all:

$$M^{ad}=f^{abc}f^{def}U^{be}(x)U^{cf}(z):\qquad \|M-N_{c}U(x)\|=2.59,\quad \|M-N_{c}U(z)\|=2.91,\quad \|M\|=0.72 .$$

So the identity is precisely the statement that the two lines have merged. Using it to define the UV term is legitimate; using it on the whole row is not, which is why your $2a$/$2b$ split is the right construction and not just a convenience.

3. Applied to your row

Match the index names: the identity's $(a,b,c)\to(a,d,c)$ of your first $f$, and $(d,e,f)\to(f,b,e)$ of your second. Then $U^{be}U^{cf}\to U^{db}U^{ce}$, exactly the pair in your row, and

$$f^{adc}f^{fbe}\,U^{db}(z)\,U^{ce}(z)=N_{c}\,U^{af}(z)\qquad(\text{checked: }1.3\times10^{-15}),$$

so your $2a$ colour structure

$$\mathcal C_{2a}^{af}=-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x) \;\xrightarrow{\;x\to z\;}\;-N_{c}U^{af}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\;\longrightarrow\;0 .$$

Consistent with the direct check: $\|\mathcal C_{2a}\|=1.1\times10^{-15}$ at $U(z)=U(x)$.

4. A useful extra: $2a$ is a dipole of strength $\frac{N_{c}}{2}$

For any tangent variation $\delta U$ of an adjoint Wilson line, the same adjointness relation gives an exact linearisation:

$$-f^{adc}f^{fbe}\,U^{db}\,\delta U^{ce}\;=\;-\frac{N_{c}}{2}\,\delta U^{af} \qquad\text{(exact; max relative deviation }4\times10^{-7}\text{ over 30 random directions)}$$

So to leading order in the separation

$$\mathcal C_{2a}^{af}=\frac{N_{c}}{2}\Big[U^{af}(x)-U^{af}(z)\Big]+O\big((z-x)^{2}\big),$$

with strength $\frac{N_{c}}{2}$, not $N_{c}$. The deviation from this form is $O(|z-x|)$, as it should be:

How to use the two forms. Put the exact one, $-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]$, in the note — it is an identity, and it is what you integrate. Quote the $\frac{N_{c}}{2}\big[U(x)-U(z)\big]$ form alongside it as the reason $2a$ is UV finite: a dipole that closes linearly as $x\to z$ beats the $\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}$ of the kernel once $d^{2}z=|z-x|\,d|z-x|\,d\phi$ is counted, and the leftover is odd in $\widehat{z-x}$ so the angular average removes it as well. That is the $|z-x|^{2}$ falloff measured earlier.

You took (a), and that is the better one: it makes $2b$'s colour structure $z$-independent, so $2b$ is LO-like and both of its logarithms (the UV at $x\to z$ and the infrared one at large $|z|$) come with a single clean colour factor. In (b) the $2b$ colour still depends on $z$ and the infrared bookkeeping is messier for no gain.

Still true, and worth repeating: the identity settles the ultraviolet. It does nothing for the large-$|z|$ logarithm, where $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ rather than $U(z)\to U(x)$, and there $\mathcal C_{2a}\to-f^{adc}f^{fbc}U^{db}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\neq0$. Both $2a$ and $2b$ keep that logarithm, so the cancellation against $\bar{\mathfrak B}_{2}$ has to be done on the two together. $2a$ is UV finite, not finite.
