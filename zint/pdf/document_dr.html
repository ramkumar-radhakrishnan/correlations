Dimensional regularisation of the UV–projected row

$\overline{\rm MS}$ done where it is defined — in momentum space

the $1/r^{2}$ term Fourier–transformed to the one–loop transverse bubble, then subtracted

0. Conventions

$D=4-2\epsilon$, transverse plane $d\equiv d_\perp=2-2\epsilon$. $\overline{\rm MS}$ is a momentum–space prescription: the loop measure is $\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}$ and one subtracts

$$\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_E+\log 4\pi \qquad\Big(\text{equivalently, keep }\tfrac1\epsilon\text{ and write }\bar\mu^{2}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_E}\Big).$$

Kinematics: $r=z-x$, $s=y-x$, $P=x'-w'$, $q=\bar\xi k$, $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$, $M^{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$, $D_0=D|_{r=0}=s^{2}/\bar\xi$, $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}$, $\ell_k=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$.

Step 0. $C_{\rm UV}$ carries no $O(\epsilon)$. The instantaneous $\delta^{im}$ vertex supplies an explicit $d_\perp$ and the angular average supplies $1/d_\perp$; they cancel. Nothing multiplying the pole is itself $O(\epsilon)$, so no extra finite term is generated by $\epsilon\times\frac1\epsilon$.

1. What has to be regulated

$C_{\rm UV}(\xi)$ and $(s\cdot P)/P^{2}$ do not depend on $r$, so the whole transverse integration is one object:

$$\mathcal G=\int\! d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}\,D}.$$

2. Split the divergence off exactly

$$\frac{1}{r^{2}D}=\frac{1}{r^{2}D_0}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}\Big),\qquad \frac1D-\frac1{D_0}=\frac{2\,r\!\cdot\!s-r^{2}}{D\,D_0},$$

the second bracket vanishing linearly at $r=0$. So only the first term needs a regulator:

$$\mathcal G=\frac{1}{D_0}\;\mathcal T\;+\;\frac{1}{D_0}\big[2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi\big], \qquad \mathcal T\equiv\int\! d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}} .$$

3. $\mathcal T$ in momentum space — it is the one–loop transverse bubble

$1/r^{2}$ is the square of the Weizsäcker–Williams kernel, whose transform is

$$\int\! d^{2}r\;e^{i\ell\cdot r}\,\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}=\frac{2\pi i\,\ell^{i}}{\ell^{2}} .$$

By the convolution theorem, with $\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}$,

$$\mathcal T=\int\! d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{1}{r^{2}} =\int\!\frac{d^{2}\ell}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\frac{2\pi i\,\ell^{i}}{\ell^{2}}\;\frac{2\pi i\,(q-\ell)^{i}}{(q-\ell)^{2}} =(2\pi)^{2}\!\int\!\frac{d^{2}\ell}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\frac{\ell\cdot(\ell-q)}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} .$$

The divergence is now where $\overline{\rm MS}$ expects it: at large $\ell$ the integrand tends to $1/\ell^{2}$, logarithmically divergent. There is no infrared problem — at $\ell\to0$ the numerator $\ell\cdot(\ell-q)\to-\ell\cdot q$ is odd and averages to zero, and likewise at $\ell\to q$.

4. The loop in $d=2-2\epsilon$

Continue the loop measure in the standard way (the two $2\pi i\ell^{i}/\ell^{2}$ are vertex factors and stay in two dimensions):

$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=(2\pi)^{2}\,\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}\; \frac{\ell\cdot(\ell-q)}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} .$$

Reduce with $\ell\cdot(\ell-q)=\ell^{2}-\ell\cdot q$. The denominator is symmetric under $\ell\to q-\ell$, so $\int\frac{\ell\cdot q}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}}=\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int\frac{1}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}}$, while $\int\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(\ell-q)^{2}}$ is scaleless and vanishes:

$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=-\frac{q^{2}}{2}\,(2\pi)^{2}\,B,\qquad B\equiv\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} =\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{\Gamma(2-\frac d2)\Gamma(\frac d2-1)^{2}}{\Gamma(d-2)}(q^{2})^{\frac d2-2}.$$

At $d=2-2\epsilon$, using $\dfrac{\Gamma(-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(-2\epsilon)}=-\dfrac{2}{\epsilon}\dfrac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}$,

$$B=\frac{1}{4\pi q^{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\!\epsilon}\left(-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right)c_\Gamma, \qquad c_\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}=1-\gamma_E\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}),$$
$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{\epsilon}\,c_\Gamma\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\!\epsilon} =\pi\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_E+\log\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right]+O(\epsilon) =\pi\left[\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right].$$

5. $\overline{\rm MS}$

Subtract $\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}=\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\log4\pi$:

$$\mathcal T\Big|_{\overline{\rm MS}}=\pi\,\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \mathcal T_\epsilon=\pi\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\bar\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right],\quad\bar\mu^{2}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_E}.$$

Why the pole is $+\frac1\epsilon$ here and $-\frac1\epsilon$ if you continue the coordinate integral. Continuing $\int d^{2-2\epsilon}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}/r^{2}$ directly gives $\pi\Gamma(-\epsilon)\big(\tfrac{q^{2}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\big)^{\epsilon} =-\pi\big[\tfrac1\epsilon+\gamma_E+\log\tfrac{q^{2}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\big]$. The two differ by exactly $2\pi/\epsilon$ — a pure pole, no finite part (checked numerically, §10D). The reason is the scaleless integral dropped in §4: $\int\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(\ell-q)^{2}}=0=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}$, which trades the genuine large–$\ell$ ultraviolet pole for the bubble's small–$\ell$ infrared one. The $\overline{\rm MS}$ finite part — the only thing that enters the answer below — is identical either way: $\pi\log(\bar\mu^{2}/q^{2})$. The momentum–space form is the one that pairs with the standard $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterms, so it is used here.

6. The remainder $\mathcal R$, in full

The second piece of §2 needs no regulator: the bracket vanishes linearly at $r=0$. Using $\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}=\frac{2\,r\cdot s-r^{2}}{D\,D_0}$ and $\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}D}=\frac1D$,

$$\mathcal R\equiv\int\!d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}\right] =\frac{1}{D_0}\int\!d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{2\,r\!\cdot\!s-r^{2}}{r^{2}D} =\frac{2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi}{D_0},$$ $$\Psi^{i}\equiv\int\!d^{2}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}\,r^{i}}{r^{2}D},\qquad \Phi\equiv\int\!d^{2}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{D}.$$

$\Phi$ in closed form. Shift $r=s+u$: $\Phi=e^{iq\cdot s}\!\int\! d^{2}u\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot u}}{u^{2}+M^{2}}$, so

$$\Phi=2\pi\,e^{iq\cdot s}\,K_0\big(M|q|\big).$$

$\Psi^{i}$. Feynman–parametrise $\frac{1}{r^{2}D}$ with $A=r^{2}$, $B=D$: $\alpha A+\bar\alpha B=(r-b)^{2}+\Delta$, $b=\bar\alpha s$, $\Delta=\bar\alpha(\alpha s^{2}+M^{2})$ (no $m$ — this is finite). Shift $r=b+u$, so $e^{iq\cdot r}=e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s}e^{iq\cdot u}$ and $r^{i}=b^{i}+u^{i}$:

$$\Psi^{i}=\int_0^1\!d\alpha\;e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s}\Big[b^{i}\,U_2+V_2^{i}\Big],\qquad U_2=\frac{\pi|q|}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda),\quad V_2^{i}=i\pi q^{i}K_0(\lambda),\quad\lambda=|q|\sqrt\Delta,$$

the two transforms following from $\int\!\frac{d^{2}u\,e^{iq\cdot u}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}} =-\partial_\Delta\big[2\pi K_0(|q|\sqrt\Delta)\big]$ and $V_2^{i}=-i\partial U_2/\partial q_i$. Contracting with $s$:

$$s\!\cdot\!\Psi=\pi\!\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\;e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s} \left[\bar\alpha\,s^{2}\,\frac{|q|}{\sqrt\Delta}\,K_1(\lambda)\;+\;i\,(q\!\cdot\!s)\,K_0(\lambda)\right], \qquad \Delta=\bar\alpha\big(\alpha s^{2}+M^{2}\big).$$

Equivalently, as one convergent parameter integral (Feynman parameter $t=\bar\alpha$, the $t\to0$ subtraction removing what is already in $\mathcal T$),

$$\mathcal R=\frac{\pi}{D_0}\left[\hat I+\log\frac{e^{2\gamma_E}q^{2}D_0}{4}\right],\qquad \hat I=D_0\!\int_0^1\!\!dt\left[e^{itq\cdot s}\frac{|q|K_1(|q|\sqrt{\Delta_0})}{\sqrt{\Delta_0}}-\frac{1}{t\,D_0}\right],$$ $$\Delta_0=t\big[(1-t)s^{2}+M^{2}\big].$$

Checked against direct two–dimensional quadrature to $10^{-9}$, and the two forms against each other to $10^{-15}$ (§10E).

7. Adding the two pieces — the $q^{2}$ cancels

$$\mathcal G\Big|_{\overline{\rm MS}}=\frac{\mathcal T|_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{D_0}+\mathcal R =\frac{\pi}{D_0}\left[\underbrace{\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}}_{\text{from }\mathcal T} +\underbrace{\log\frac{e^{2\gamma_E}q^{2}D_0}{4}+\hat I}_{\text{from }\mathcal R}\right] =\frac{\pi}{D_0}\left[\log\frac{e^{2\gamma_E}\mu^{2}D_0}{4}+\hat I\right].$$

The $q^{2}$ drops out between the two. This matters: with $D_0=s^{2}/\bar\xi$ the logarithm is of the dipole size, not of the produced–gluon momentum,

$$\mathcal G\Big|_{\overline{\rm MS}} =\frac{\pi}{D_0}\left[\log\frac{\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi}+2\big(\gamma_E-\log2\big)+\hat I(\xi)\right],$$

and the $\overline{\rm MS}$ scheme constant appears as $2(\gamma_E-\log2)$. (Verified to $10^{-15}$, §10F. This supersedes the reading of the earlier draft, where only $\mathcal T$ was kept and the log appeared to carry $k^{2}$.)

8. Back into the row, and the $p^{+}$ integral

Since $\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\cdot\frac{1}{D_0}=\frac{1}{s^{2}}$, the $\bar\xi$ cancels:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}}C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\mathcal G =\pi\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}} \left[\log\frac{\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi}+2(\gamma_E-\log2)+\hat I(\xi)\right].$$

The three $\xi$ moments needed are

$$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}=2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6},\qquad \int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi} =\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36},$$ $$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\hat I(\xi) =e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+c_0\,\ell_k+c_1,$$

the last fixed by the endpoints: $\hat I\to e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\log\frac1\xi+c_0$ as $\xi\to0$, with

$$c_0=s^{2}\!\int_0^1\!\!dt\left[e^{itk\cdot s}\frac{k\,K_1\big(ks\sqrt{t(1-t)}\big)}{s\sqrt{t(1-t)}} -\frac{1}{t\,s^{2}}-\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}}{s^{2}(1-t)}\right],$$

while $\hat I\to0$ as $\xi\to1$, so that endpoint gives no rapidity logarithm from $\hat I$.

9. Final result

Prefactor $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}k^{+}}\times\frac{\pi}{2\pi} =\frac{\alpha_s^{2}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}}$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}_{2}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{\alpha_s^{2}(\mu)\,N_c}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\; \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\;\Big[\mathcal P+\mathcal F\Big]$$ $$\times\Big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\Big]\Big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\Big]\rho^{b'}\!(x')\rho^{f}\!(y),$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal P=\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\;\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\rm MS}\ }\;0\;}$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal F=\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big[\log\big(\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)+2(\gamma_E-\log2)\Big] +\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,\ell_k^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}+c_0\,\ell_k+c_1\;}$$

The scheme constant is the paper's. $\big(2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6}\big)\times2N_c =4N_c\ell_k-\tfrac{11N_c}{3}=4N_c\ell_k-b$ at $N_f=0$, so the constant riding on it enters as

$$2\,b\,\big(\gamma_E-\log 2\big),$$

which is exactly the term in $K_{JSJ}$, Eq. (2.64) of arXiv:1610.03453 — the one their footnote 2 records as having been missing from their earlier results. It comes out here only because $\mathcal R$ was kept: it is the $\log\frac{e^{2\gamma_E}}{4}$ left behind when the $q^{2}$ cancels in §7.

Two further readings. The $\ell_k^{2}$ coefficient is $\tfrac12\big(1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\big)$, one half from each endpoint of the $\xi$ integral ($\xi\to1$ through $\log\frac1{\bar\xi}$ against the $\tfrac{\xi}{1-\xi}$ pole, $\xi\to0$ through $\hat I$'s $z\to y$ logarithm against the $\tfrac{1-\xi}{\xi}$ pole); it is regulator–independent, and agrees with the mass–regulator computation. And $\mathcal P\times2N_c=\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}(4N_c\ell_k-b)$ is the coupling renormalisation, cancelling against the $-2/\epsilon$ of the self–energy counterterm.
