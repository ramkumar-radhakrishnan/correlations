Dimensional regularisation of the UV–projected row

$\overline{\rm MS}$ done where it is defined — in momentum space

the $1/r^{2}$ term Fourier–transformed to the one–loop transverse bubble, then subtracted

0. Conventions

$D=4-2\epsilon$, transverse plane $d\equiv d_\perp=2-2\epsilon$. $\overline{\rm MS}$ is a momentum–space prescription: the loop measure is $\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}$ and one subtracts

$$\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_E+\log 4\pi \qquad\Big(\text{equivalently, keep }\tfrac1\epsilon\text{ and write }\bar\mu^{2}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_E}\Big).$$

Kinematics: $r=z-x$, $s=y-x$, $P=x'-w'$, $q=\bar\xi k$, $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$, $M^{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$, $D_0=D|_{r=0}=s^{2}/\bar\xi$, $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}$, $\ell_k=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$.

Step 0. $C_{\rm UV}$ carries no $O(\epsilon)$. The instantaneous $\delta^{im}$ vertex supplies an explicit $d_\perp$ and the angular average supplies $1/d_\perp$; they cancel. Nothing multiplying the pole is itself $O(\epsilon)$, so no extra finite term is generated by $\epsilon\times\frac1\epsilon$.

1. What has to be regulated

$C_{\rm UV}(\xi)$ and $(s\cdot P)/P^{2}$ do not depend on $r$, so the whole transverse integration is one object:

$$\mathcal G=\int\! d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}\,D}.$$

2. Split the divergence off exactly

$$\frac{1}{r^{2}D}=\frac{1}{r^{2}D_0}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}\Big),\qquad \frac1D-\frac1{D_0}=\frac{2\,r\!\cdot\!s-r^{2}}{D\,D_0},$$

the second bracket vanishing linearly at $r=0$. So only the first term needs a regulator:

$$\mathcal G=\frac{1}{D_0}\;\mathcal T\;+\;\frac{1}{D_0}\big[2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi\big], \qquad \mathcal T\equiv\int\! d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}} .$$

3. $\mathcal T$ in momentum space — it is the one–loop transverse bubble

$1/r^{2}$ is the square of the Weizsäcker–Williams kernel, whose transform is

$$\int\! d^{2}r\;e^{i\ell\cdot r}\,\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}=\frac{2\pi i\,\ell^{i}}{\ell^{2}} .$$

By the convolution theorem, with $\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}\frac{r^{i}}{r^{2}}$,

$$\mathcal T=\int\! d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{1}{r^{2}} =\int\!\frac{d^{2}\ell}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\frac{2\pi i\,\ell^{i}}{\ell^{2}}\;\frac{2\pi i\,(q-\ell)^{i}}{(q-\ell)^{2}} =(2\pi)^{2}\!\int\!\frac{d^{2}\ell}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\frac{\ell\cdot(\ell-q)}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} .$$

The divergence is now where $\overline{\rm MS}$ expects it: at large $\ell$ the integrand tends to $1/\ell^{2}$, logarithmically divergent. There is no infrared problem — at $\ell\to0$ the numerator $\ell\cdot(\ell-q)\to-\ell\cdot q$ is odd and averages to zero, and likewise at $\ell\to q$.

4. The loop in $d=2-2\epsilon$

Continue the loop measure in the standard way (the two $2\pi i\ell^{i}/\ell^{2}$ are vertex factors and stay in two dimensions):

$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=(2\pi)^{2}\,\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}\; \frac{\ell\cdot(\ell-q)}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} .$$

Reduce with $\ell\cdot(\ell-q)=\ell^{2}-\ell\cdot q$. The denominator is symmetric under $\ell\to q-\ell$, so $\int\frac{\ell\cdot q}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}}=\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int\frac{1}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}}$, while $\int\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(\ell-q)^{2}}$ is scaleless and vanishes:

$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=-\frac{q^{2}}{2}\,(2\pi)^{2}\,B,\qquad B\equiv\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}(\ell-q)^{2}} =\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{\Gamma(2-\frac d2)\Gamma(\frac d2-1)^{2}}{\Gamma(d-2)}(q^{2})^{\frac d2-2}.$$

At $d=2-2\epsilon$, using $\dfrac{\Gamma(-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(-2\epsilon)}=-\dfrac{2}{\epsilon}\dfrac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}$,

$$B=\frac{1}{4\pi q^{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\!\epsilon}\left(-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right)c_\Gamma, \qquad c_\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}=1-\gamma_E\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}),$$
$$\mathcal T_\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{\epsilon}\,c_\Gamma\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{\!\epsilon} =\pi\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_E+\log\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right]+O(\epsilon) =\pi\left[\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right].$$

5. $\overline{\rm MS}$

Subtract $\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}=\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\log4\pi$:

$$\mathcal T\Big|_{\overline{\rm MS}}=\pi\,\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \mathcal T_\epsilon=\pi\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\bar\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}\right],\quad\bar\mu^{2}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_E}.$$

Why the pole is $+\frac1\epsilon$ here and $-\frac1\epsilon$ if you continue the coordinate integral. Continuing $\int d^{2-2\epsilon}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}/r^{2}$ directly gives $\pi\Gamma(-\epsilon)\big(\tfrac{q^{2}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\big)^{\epsilon} =-\pi\big[\tfrac1\epsilon+\gamma_E+\log\tfrac{q^{2}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\big]$. The two differ by exactly $2\pi/\epsilon$ — a pure pole, no finite part (checked numerically, §9D). The reason is the scaleless integral dropped in §4: $\int\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(\ell-q)^{2}}=0=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}$, which trades the genuine large–$\ell$ ultraviolet pole for the bubble's small–$\ell$ infrared one. The $\overline{\rm MS}$ finite part — the only thing that enters the answer below — is identical either way: $\pi\log(\bar\mu^{2}/q^{2})$. The momentum–space form is the one that pairs with the standard $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterms, so it is used here.

The finite remainder of §2 is untouched by the continuation:

$$\tilde I(\xi)\equiv\frac{2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi}{\pi},\qquad \Phi=2\pi e^{iq\cdot s}K_0(M|q|),$$ $$s\!\cdot\!\Psi=\pi\!\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s} \Big[\bar\alpha s^{2}\tfrac{|q|}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda)+i(q\!\cdot\!s)K_0(\lambda)\Big], \quad\Delta=\bar\alpha(\alpha s^{2}+M^{2}),\ \lambda=|q|\sqrt\Delta .$$

6. Back into the row — the $\bar\xi$ cancels

Since $\dfrac{1}{\bar\xi}\cdot\dfrac{1}{D_0}=\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}$,

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\,\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}}\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\mathcal G =\pi\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}} \left[\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}}+\tilde I(\xi)\right],$$

using $q=\bar\xi k$. The transverse structure is a product of two WW kernels.

7. The $p^{+}$ integral

$$\int_{\Lambda/k^{+}}^{1-\Lambda/k^{+}}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}=2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6},\qquad \int_{\Lambda/k^{+}}^{1-\Lambda/k^{+}}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi^{2}} =\ell_k^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{67}{18},$$

the second being exactly minus Eq. (C.10) of arXiv:1610.03453, since $\log\bar\xi^{-2}=-\log(\xi\bar\xi)+\log(\xi/\bar\xi)$ and the second piece is odd under $\xi\leftrightarrow\bar\xi$ while $C_{\rm UV}$ is even.

8. Final result

Prefactor $\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\dfrac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}k^{+}}\times\dfrac{\pi}{2\pi} =\dfrac{g^{4}}{128\pi^{7}k^{+}}=\dfrac{\alpha_s^{2}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}}$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}_{2}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{\alpha_s^{2}(\mu)\,N_c}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\; \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\;\Big[\mathcal P+\mathcal F\Big]$$ $$\times\Big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\Big]\Big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\Big]\rho^{b'}\!(x')\rho^{f}\!(y),$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal P=+\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big) =\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_E+\log4\pi\Big)\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big) \;\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\rm MS}\ }\;0\;}$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal F=\Big(2\ell_k-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{k^{2}} \;+\;\ell_k^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{67}{18} \;+\;\int_{\Lambda/k^{+}}^{1-\Lambda/k^{+}}\!\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\tilde I(\xi)\;}$$
  • The pole is the coupling renormalisation. $\mathcal P\times2N_c =\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\big(4N_c\ell_k-\tfrac{11N_c}{3}\big)=\frac{1}{\hat\epsilon}\big(4N_c\ell_k-b\big)$ at $N_f=0$: the $\tfrac{11}{6}$ is the $b$–function coefficient. In $\overline{\rm MS}$ it is removed together with $-\gamma_E+\log4\pi$.
  • The scale in the logarithm is $k^{2}$, not the dipole size. After the $\bar\xi$ cancellation the only scale the projected integrand retains is $q=\bar\xi k$, so $\overline{\rm MS}$ delivers $\log(\mu^{2}/k^{2})$ and the $\bar\xi$–dependence becomes $\ell_k^{2}+\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\tfrac{67}{18}$. Trading it for $\log\big(\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)$ moves $\log\big(k^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)$ between the two pieces — a rescheming, not done here.
  • $\tilde I$ is regulator–independent. It is finite in two dimensions, so the continuation does not touch it. Its $\xi$–integral is not elementary; the endpoints give $e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\tfrac12\ell_k^{2}+c_0\ell_k+c_1$.
