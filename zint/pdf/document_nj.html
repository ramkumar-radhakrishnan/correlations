The DGLAP content of the two–$\mathfrak C$ row

$z$ integrated first — and the splitting function that comes out

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $\zeta\equiv\dfrac{k^{+}}{S}$,  $\bar\zeta=1-\zeta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{S}$,  $r\equiv y'-y$,  $d=2-2\epsilon$

Yes — and it comes out exactly. Doing the $z$ integral first and then writing the bracket in the splitting variable $\zeta=k^{+}/(p^{+}+k^{+})$, the entire $z$–integrated structure collapses to

$$\frac{\pi}{k^{+2}}\Big\{\,\underbrace{C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)}_{P_{gg}/2N_{c}}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X \;+\;\zeta\bar\zeta\,\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\Big\},\qquad C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta .$$

The coefficient of the collinear logarithm is precisely the $g\to gg$ DGLAP splitting function, with no leftover factors: symbolic difference $0$, and the whole expression agrees exactly with the $p^{+}$–variable form established earlier. The non–logarithmic remainder is governed by $\zeta\bar\zeta$ — the regular piece of $P_{gg}$ alone. This also settles what to do with the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$: it is the collinear factorisation pole, absorbed into the gluon distribution with $\mu\to\mu_{F}$ (§6).

1. Which variable is the splitting variable

Read the row as a process. $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}(p^{+}+k^{+},-y')$ and $\mathfrak A(p^{+}+k^{+},-y)$ create a single gluon of light–cone momentum $S=p^{+}+k^{+}$ off the source $\rho$. The two $\mathfrak C$ vertices then split it into the measured gluon ($k^{+}$) and the unobserved one ($p^{+}$). Two gluons in the final state, one parent. So the DGLAP variable is the measured daughter's fraction of the parent,

$$\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}},\qquad\bar\zeta=\frac{p^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}},\qquad\zeta+\bar\zeta=1,$$

not $\xi=p^{+}/k^{+}$. The two combinations that appear in $\mathfrak C_{1jik}$ become

$$a\equiv\frac{S}{p^{+}}=\frac{1}{\bar\zeta},\qquad b\equiv\frac{S}{k^{+}}=\frac{1}{\zeta},$$

and the net longitudinal factor left by the two $\delta^{(2)}$'s is $\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}=\dfrac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta\,S^{2}}$.

2. The bracket in $\zeta$

From the index–engine contraction (verified in general $d$), the three structures carry

$$c_{1}=d_{\perp}-2a-2b=d_{\perp}-\frac{2}{\bar\zeta}-\frac{2}{\zeta},\qquad c_{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}=\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar\zeta^{2}},\qquad c_{3}=2ab=\frac{2}{\zeta\bar\zeta},$$

multiplying $\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}$, $\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}$, $\delta_{km'}\delta_{k'm}$ respectively.

3. The $z$ integration, done first

All the $z$ dependence is the rank–2 tensor, which in $d=2-2\epsilon$ is

$$T^{mm'}=\int d^{d}z\,\frac{(y-z)^{m}(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}} =C(\epsilon)\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon}\Big[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\Big], \qquad r=y'-y,$$

with $C(\epsilon)=\pi^{1-\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)}$. Writing $X^{k}=\frac{(x-y)^{k}}{(x-y)^{2}}$, $X'^{k'}=\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$ and

$$\mathcal X=X\!\cdot\!X',\qquad\mathcal R=(\hat r\!\cdot\!X)(\hat r\!\cdot\!X'),$$

the three structures contract as

$$\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}\;\to\;T^{kk'}X^{k}X'^{k'},\qquad \delta_{km'}\delta_{k'm}\;\to\;T^{k'k}X^{k}X'^{k'},\qquad \delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}\;\to\;(\mathrm{tr}\,T)\,\mathcal X .$$
The momentum sum rule kills the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ term. $T^{mm'}$ is symmetric, so the first two contractions are identical and only $c_{1}+c_{3}$ matters:

$$c_{1}+c_{3}=d_{\perp}-\frac{2}{\bar\zeta}-\frac{2}{\zeta}+\frac{2}{\zeta\bar\zeta} =d_{\perp}+\frac{2}{\zeta\bar\zeta}\Big[1-\zeta-\bar\zeta\Big]=d_{\perp}.$$

In the $p^{+}$ variables this cancellation looked like an accident. Here it is manifestly the statement $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$ — the two daughters share the parent's momentum.

4. Expanding in $\epsilon$

With $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ kept consistently (CDR $=\overline{\rm MS}$),

$$\frac{d_{\perp}}{2\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-1,\qquad \frac{\mathrm{tr}\,T}{C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}}=\frac{d}{2\epsilon}-1=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-2,$$

and $\dfrac{C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}}{\epsilon}=\pi\big[\mathcal L+2\big]$ in $\overline{\rm MS}$, with $\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2})$. So the brace is

$$\underbrace{d_{\perp}\Big[\frac{\mathcal X}{2\epsilon}-\mathcal R\Big]}_{c_{1}+c_{3}} +\underbrace{c_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-2\Big)\mathcal X}_{c_{2}} \;\longrightarrow\;\Big[(\mathcal L+1)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\Big]+\Big(\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar\zeta^{2}}\Big)\mathcal L\,\mathcal X .$$

Multiplying by the net longitudinal factor $\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta S^{2}}$ and using $S^{2}=k^{+2}/\zeta^{2}$, i.e. $\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta S^{2}}=\frac{\zeta\bar\zeta}{k^{+2}}$:

$$\frac{\pi}{k^{+2}}\left\{\ \underbrace{\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}\Big[1+\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar\zeta^{2}}\Big]\zeta^{2}}_{\textstyle =\;C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)}\;\mathcal L\,\mathcal X \;+\;\zeta\bar\zeta\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\right\}$$

Two checks, both exact: the difference from the direct $\epsilon$–expansion is $0$, and substituting $\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+p^{+}}$ reproduces the $p^{+}$–variable result $\frac{1}{k^{+}}\big[\frac{\mathcal L\mathcal X}{p^{+}}+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}\mathcal L\mathcal X +\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}+p^{+})^{2}}((\mathcal L+1)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R)\big]$ with difference $0$.

5. That coefficient is $P_{gg}$

$$C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta =\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta\bar\zeta}-2 =\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}},\qquad P_{gg}(\zeta)=2N_{c}\Big[\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta\Big].$$

The colour reduction of this row supplies exactly one factor $N_{c}$ (from $U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$ then $f^{c'da}f^{cda}=N_{c}\delta^{c'c}$), so the two combine into

$$N_{c}\,C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}$$

with no spare factors. And the non–logarithmic remainder carries $\zeta\bar\zeta$ — the regular, non–singular piece of the splitting function, the one term of $P_{gg}$ that has no pole at either endpoint.

6. What the logarithm and the pole are

$r=y'-y$ is conjugate to the measured transverse momentum: the surviving phase is $e^{-ik\cdot r}$. So $\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2})$ is the logarithm between the scale $\mu$ and the hard transverse scale $r^{-1}$, which the remaining $y,y'$ Fourier transform ties to $k_{\perp}$. $P_{gg}(\zeta)$ times a logarithm of that ratio, plus a $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ pole — that is the DGLAP structure.

This sharpens what I said before. I established that the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is infrared (the $|z|\to\infty$ end), not a UV renormalisation pole, and said it must cancel against the companion rows. The $P_{gg}$ identification says something better: a collinear (mass) singularity is infrared in origin, and it does not have to cancel — it is absorbed into the gluon distribution by DGLAP, with $\mu$ becoming the factorisation scale $\mu_{F}$. Both statements hold together:
  • Do not run $\alpha_{s}$ with this $\mu$, and do not treat the pole as UV. (unchanged)
  • Do remove it with the collinear counterterm $-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{P_{gg}}{2}\otimes(\text{LO})$; the coefficient is exactly right for that, which is the evidence that this is a factorisation pole and not an uncancelled divergence.

Worth confirming against the companion rows that nothing is double counted, but the coefficient coming out as $P_{gg}$ with no leftovers is a strong check.

7. The $\zeta$ integral

At fixed $k^{+}$, $S=k^{+}/\zeta$, so

$$dp^{+}=\frac{k^{+}}{\zeta^{2}}\,d\zeta,\qquad \zeta\in\Big[\ \zeta_{-}=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\ ,\ \ \zeta_{+}=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+\Lambda}\ \Big],$$

and $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}\frac{dS}{S}$, which is the usual DGLAP convolution measure. The primitive is elementary:

$$\int\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\,C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=F(\zeta),\qquad F(\zeta)=2\log\zeta-\log(1-\zeta)-\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta}-\frac{1}{2\zeta^{2}} \quad\big(F'=C_{\rm UV}/\zeta^{2}\ \checkmark\big),$$ $$\int_{\zeta_{-}}^{\zeta_{+}}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}C_{\rm UV}(\zeta) =\underbrace{\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}}_{\text{rapidity}}-\frac{1}{2} +\underbrace{2\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}+\frac{\vee^{2}}{2k^{+2}}}_{\text{kinematic end}} .$$

If instead you impose $p^{+}\le k^{+}$ (the measured gluon the harder one, $\zeta\ge\frac12$) the $\vee$ problem vanishes and the integral is clean: $$\int_{1/2}^{\zeta_{+}}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}+\log 2 =\log\frac{2k^{+}}{\Lambda}\qquad(\text{checked to }3\times10^{-10}).$$

8. The result in DGLAP form

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\mathfrak C\mathfrak C} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\,\frac{1}{2k^{+}} \int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{k^{+}/(k^{+}+\Lambda)}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}} \left\{\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X +N_{c}\,\zeta\bar\zeta\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\right\}$$ $$\times\int_{x,x',y,y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}\; \big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$$

$\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$,  $\bar\zeta=1-\zeta$,  $r=y'-y$,   $\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2})$,   $\mathcal X=\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}$,   $\mathcal R=\frac{[r\cdot(x-y)][r\cdot(x'-y')]}{r^{2}(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}$,   $P_{gg}(\zeta)=2N_{c}\big[\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta\big]$.

Equivalently, with $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}\frac{dS}{S}$, the first term is the standard convolution $\int\frac{dS}{S}\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2\zeta}\otimes(\text{LO transverse structure})$, and the collinear counterterm that removes the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is the matching $-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{P_{gg}}{2}\otimes(\text{LO})$.
