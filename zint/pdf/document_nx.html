The six parts are unchanged and still exact, and both fixes landed: $2f$ now carries $\int_{z,w,w',x,x'}$, and the $N_{c}$–collapsed colour bracket is used throughout (§1). On divergences: there is exactly one, and it is soft. No UV and no collinear log anywhere — all 26 short–distance regions are convergent after the $p^{+}$ integration as well as before (§2). The single surviving transverse log is $|z|\to\infty$, and its coefficient is $$\frac{\mathcal K_{ww'}}{4k^{+}}\,\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}\,,$$ matched to 7–8 digits at four choices of $(\Lambda,\vee)$ (§3). Two colour index–ordering issues remain, and one of them now decides whether your $N_{c}$ exists at all (§5).

2. Short distance: no UV, no collinear

A log needs the integrand $\sim\rho^{-2(n-1)}$ when $n$ points collapse, since the measure is $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$. Scanning all 26 subsets of $\{x,x',z,w,w'\}$ on the $p^{+}$–integrated bracket:

no short–distance divergence in any of the 26 regions.

Why there is no collinear log, restated for the integrated expression. At $z\to w$ only one of the two vertices is singular: the conjugate sits at $w'\neq w$ and is regular there, and $e^{-ik_{\perp}(w'-w)}$ is what keeps $w'$ from following $w$. One singular kernel gives $\rho^{-1}$; a log needs $\rho^{-2}$, i.e. both. Forcing both by sending $w'\to w$ too does give $\rho^{-2}$, but then the measure is $\rho^{3}d\rho$ and you are further from divergence, not closer. The $p^{+}$ integration does not change this: it multiplies each region by a bounded factor.

The two soft regions are one region. $|z|\to\infty$ is the unobserved gluon's transverse momentum going to zero; $p^{+}\to\Lambda$ is its longitudinal momentum going to zero. They are not independent divergences — the transverse log's coefficient is the rapidity span $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, and the transverse structure left over, $\mathcal K_{ww'}$, is exactly the LO single–gluon–production structure. The $z$–gluon has decoupled completely. That is the eikonal region, and it is what the JIMWLK/BK subtraction removes. In your decomposition it is $2b$ and $2c$ that produce it: their $\log|z|^{2}$ pieces cancel against each other and the $\frac{B+B'}{B'-B}\log\frac{B'}{B}$ leftovers cancel against $2a$.

3.2 The $|x|,|x'|\to\infty$ tails are not loop divergences

$x$ and $x'$ are the arguments of $\rho$. The colour–charge correlator has the transverse support of the target, so these ends are cut at the target radius in the standard way. Nothing to regulate.

5. Colour: two different index–ordering problems

5.1 The $z$–side decides whether you get $N_{c}$ at all

The collapse runs through $\sum_{b}[\text{bra }U(z)]^{bc'}[\text{ket }U(z)]^{bc}=\delta^{c'c}$. That needs the same index order on both sides:

bra / ket at $z$$\|\mathcal C_{22}-N_{c}[\,\cdot\,]\|$result
both $U(z)$  (your six parts)$9\cdot10^{-15}$collapses
both $U^{\dagger}(z)$$5\cdot10^{-15}$collapses

Because $\sum_{b}U^{bc'}U^{bc}=\delta^{c'c}$ and $\sum_{b}U^{c'b}U^{cb}=\delta^{c'c}$, but $\sum_{b}U^{bc'}U^{cb}=[UU]^{cc'}$ is not a delta. Your top–line expression writes $U^{\dagger\,bc}(z)$ in the ket and $U^{bc'}(z)$ in the bra — that combination gives no $N_{c}$. Your expanded parts use $U^{bc}(z)$ on both sides, which does. Fix the top line to match; either convention works, as long as it is the same one twice.

Numerical warning, restated because it bit the scan. The $\{z,w,w'\}$ collapse drives $AB'-A'B\to0$, and float64 loses the cancellation below $\rho\sim10^{-3}$: it reported $1.2\cdot10^{21}$ where the true value is $3.46\cdot10^{12}$. The $|x|\to10^{5}$ entry above is degraded for the same reason. Any numerical integration of these six parts must expand analytically near $A=B$, $A'=B'$ and $AB'=A'B$, or work in extended precision there.
