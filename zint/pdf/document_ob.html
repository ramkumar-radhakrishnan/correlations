Contracting $T^{mm'}$ with the bracket

Every index step, and the final cross section

$S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$,  $\zeta=\dfrac{k^{+}}{S}$,  $\bar\zeta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{S}=1-\zeta$,  $r=y'-y$,  $\hat r=r/|r|$,  $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$

0. The three objects being contracted

After the $w,w'$ integration and the Wilson–line reduction, the transverse integrand is

$$\underbrace{\frac{(y-z)^{m}(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}}_{\textstyle\to\;T^{mm'}\ \text{after}\int\! d^{d}z} \;\times\;\underbrace{\frac{(x-y)^{k}}{(x-y)^{2}}}_{\textstyle X^{k}} \;\underbrace{\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}}_{\textstyle X'^{k'}} \;\times\;\mathcal B_{mkm'k'}$$ $$\mathcal B_{mkm'k'}=b_{1}\,\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}+b_{2}\,\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}+b_{3}\,\delta_{mk'}\delta_{km'}$$ $$b_{1}=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}-\frac{2}{k^{+}},\qquad b_{2}=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}},\qquad b_{3}=\frac{2}{k^{+}}$$ $$T^{mm'}=C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\right],\qquad \frac{C_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\to\pi(\mathcal L+2),\qquad C_{\epsilon}\to\pi$$

Call the whole contraction $\;\mathcal Q\equiv T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}\mathcal B_{mkm'k'}$. Four indices, three terms — do them one at a time.

1. The three index contractions, one at a time

1.1 The $b_{1}$ term: $\;\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}$

$\delta_{km}$ is summed over $k$ and replaces $k$ by $m$ wherever $k$ appears, i.e. in $X^{k}$. $\delta_{k'm'}$ does the same for $k'$ in $X'^{k'}$. Nothing else carries $k$ or $k'$. So

$$\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}=T^{mm'}\,X^{m}X'^{m'}\;\equiv\;T(X,X')$$

1.2 The $b_{2}$ term: $\;\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}$

Here the two deltas act on separate pairs: $\delta_{mm'}$ ties $T$'s own two indices together, $\delta_{kk'}$ ties $X$ to $X'$. The two factors therefore decouple:

$$\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}=\big(\delta_{mm'}T^{mm'}\big)\big(X^{k}X'^{k}\big) =\big(\operatorname{tr}T\big)\,\mathcal X,\qquad \mathcal X\equiv X\!\cdot\!X'$$

1.3 The $b_{3}$ term: $\;\delta_{mk'}\delta_{km'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}$

This one crosses the indices. $\delta_{mk'}$ replaces $k'$ by $m$ in $X'^{k'}$; $\delta_{km'}$ replaces $k$ by $m'$ in $X^{k}$:

$$\delta_{mk'}\delta_{km'}\,T^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'}=T^{mm'}\,X'^{m}X^{m'}\;\equiv\;T(X',X)$$

So $X$ and $X'$ have swapped which slot of $T$ they sit in.

1.4 But $T$ is symmetric, so 1.3 equals 1.1

Recall the symmetry proof: in $T^{mm'}=\int d^{d}u\,\frac{u^{m}(u+r)^{m'}}{u^{2}(u+r)^{2}}$ send $u\to-u-r$. The measure is invariant, both denominators are invariant, and the numerator becomes $(u+r)^{m}u^{m'}$ — which is the original with $m\leftrightarrow m'$. Hence $T^{mm'}=T^{m'm}$, and

$$T(X',X)=T^{mm'}X'^{m}X^{m'}=T^{m'm}X'^{m}X^{m'}=T(X,X').$$

2. $b_{1}+b_{3}$: the $\tfrac{2}{k^{+}}$ cancels, and that is the momentum sum rule

Trace the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ back to where it came from. Before the $w$ integration the three coefficients were

$$c_{1}=d_{\perp}-\frac{2S}{p^{+}}-\frac{2S}{k^{+}},\qquad c_{2}=S^{2}\Big[\frac{1}{p^{+2}}+\frac{1}{k^{+2}}\Big],\qquad c_{3}=\frac{2S^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+}}$$

and $b_{i}=\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}c_{i}$. So

$$b_{1}=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}-\underbrace{\frac{2}{S}}_{\textstyle \frac{2\zeta}{k^{+}}} -\underbrace{\frac{2p^{+}}{Sk^{+}}}_{\textstyle \frac{2\bar\zeta}{k^{+}}} =\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}-\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\zeta+\bar\zeta\big)=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}-\frac{2}{k^{+}}$$

because $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$. And $b_{3}=\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}\frac{2S^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+}}=\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ exactly. Therefore

$$\boxed{\;b_{1}+b_{3}=\frac{d_{\perp}\,p^{+}}{S^{2}}\;}\qquad\text{(symbolic check: difference }0)$$
The cancellation is not an accident of algebra. $\frac{2}{S}$ came from the $\frac{S}{p^{+}}$ structure of the $\mathfrak C$ vertex and $\frac{2p^{+}}{Sk^{+}}$ from the $\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ one; they add to $\frac{2}{k^{+}}(\zeta+\bar\zeta)$, and $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$ is momentum conservation at the splitting vertex. That is what lets the $b_{3}$ term cancel it cleanly. If you ever find this not cancelling, you have made a kinematic error upstream.

3. The two $T$–contractions in $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$

3.1 $T(X,X')$ — nothing is eaten

$$T(X,X')=X_{m}X'_{m'}\,C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\right] =C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{X\!\cdot\!X'}{2\epsilon}-(X\!\cdot\!\hat r)(X'\!\cdot\!\hat r)\right] =C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\mathcal X}{2\epsilon}-\mathcal R\right]$$

$X$ and $X'$ are ordinary transverse vectors built from $x,y,x',y'$ — they carry no $\epsilon$. So the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ pole meets nothing that could cancel it, and $C_{\epsilon}\big[\tfrac{1}{2\epsilon}\big]\to\frac{\pi}{2}(\mathcal L+2)$ intact:

$$T(X,X')\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{\pi}{2}\,(\mathcal L+2)\,\mathcal X-\pi\,\mathcal R$$

3.2 $\operatorname{tr}T$ — here the $+2$ is eaten

$$\operatorname{tr}T=\delta_{mm'}\,C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\right] =C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\delta_{mm}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r\!\cdot\!\hat r\right] =C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{d_{\perp}}{2\epsilon}-1\right]$$

Two things to be careful about: $\delta_{mm}=d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ (this is the whole point of dimensional regularisation), while $\hat r\cdot\hat r=1$ exactly, because $\hat r$ is a unit vector by construction and carries no $\epsilon$. Then

$$\frac{d_{\perp}}{2\epsilon}=\frac{2-2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \operatorname{tr}T=C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-2\right] =\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}-2C_{\epsilon}$$ $$\longrightarrow\;\pi(\mathcal L+2)-2\pi=\boxed{\,\pi\,\mathcal L\,}$$

The $-2\epsilon$ in $d_{\perp}$ struck the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ and left a finite $-\pi$; together with the $-C_{\epsilon}$ from the $\hat r\hat r$ term it removes the $+2$ entirely. Setting $d_{\perp}=2$ before this step would have given $\pi(\mathcal L+2)$ — wrong by $2\pi$.

4. Assemble

$$\mathcal Q=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}\;C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\mathcal X}{2\epsilon}-\mathcal R\right] \;+\;\left(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\right)\pi\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X$$

The first term also carries an explicit $d_{\perp}$, so it gets its own partial eating:

$$\frac{(2-2\epsilon)p^{+}}{S^{2}}C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\mathcal X}{2\epsilon}-\mathcal R\right] =\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}\left[\underbrace{(1-\epsilon)\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}}_{\textstyle \frac{C_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}-C_{\epsilon}\to\pi(\mathcal L+1)}\mathcal X -\underbrace{(2-2\epsilon)C_{\epsilon}}_{\textstyle \to 2\pi}\mathcal R\right] =\frac{\pi p^{+}}{S^{2}}\Big[(\mathcal L+1)\,\mathcal X-2\,\mathcal R\Big]$$

One unit of the $+2$ is eaten here, leaving $\mathcal L+1$. So

$$\mathcal Q=\frac{\pi p^{+}}{S^{2}}\Big[(\mathcal L+1)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\Big] +\pi\left(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\right)\mathcal L\,\mathcal X$$

5. To $\zeta$ variables — and $P_{gg}$ appears

With $S=\dfrac{k^{+}}{\zeta}$ and $p^{+}=\bar\zeta S=\dfrac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}k^{+}$:

$$\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}=\frac{\bar\zeta}{S}=\frac{\zeta\bar\zeta}{k^{+}},\qquad \frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}=\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta\,k^{+}},\qquad \frac{1}{p^{+}}=\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta\,k^{+}}$$ $$\mathcal Q=\frac{\pi}{k^{+}}\left\{\zeta\bar\zeta\big[(\mathcal L+1)\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big] +\left(\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\right)\mathcal L\,\mathcal X\right\}$$

Now split $\zeta\bar\zeta(\mathcal L+1)\mathcal X=\zeta\bar\zeta\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X+\zeta\bar\zeta\,\mathcal X$ and collect the three $\mathcal L\mathcal X$ pieces:

$$\mathcal Q=\frac{\pi}{k^{+}}\left\{\underbrace{\left[\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta\right]}_{\textstyle C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\;=\;P_{gg}(\zeta)/2N_{c}}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X \;+\;\zeta\bar\zeta\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\right\}$$

Symbolic difference against the direct expansion: exactly $0$.

Notice what supplied what:

  • $\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}$ came from $b_{2}$'s $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ — the soft end;
  • $\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}$ came from $b_{2}$'s $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}$;
  • $\zeta\bar\zeta$ — the regular part of $P_{gg}$, the piece no soft approximation can produce — came from $b_{1}+b_{3}$, i.e. from the $d_{\perp}$ term after the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ cancellation;
  • and the leftover "$+1$" from §4 is exactly what supplies the finite $\zeta\bar\zeta\,\mathcal X$.

6. The final cross section

Restoring the prefactor $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}}$, the $\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}$, and $\mathcal Q$'s own $\frac{\pi}{k^{+}}$:

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\cdot\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{k^{+}} =\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{64\pi^{7}k^{+2}}$$
$$\frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{64\pi^{7}\,k^{+2}} \int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\!dp^{+}\int_{x,x',y,y'}e^{-i\frac{k}{\zeta}\cdot(y'-y)}\; \Big\{C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X+\zeta\bar\zeta\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\Big\}$$ $$\times\;\Big\langle\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]^{e'c}\,\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]^{ec}\;\rho^{e'}(x')\,\rho^{e}(x)\Big\rangle$$

with everything written out:

$$\mathcal X=\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}\,(x'-y')^{2}},\qquad \mathcal R=\frac{\big[(x-y)\cdot(y'-y)\big]\big[(x'-y')\cdot(y'-y)\big]}{(x-y)^{2}\,(x'-y')^{2}\,(y'-y)^{2}}$$ $$C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}+\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta(1-\zeta)=\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}}, \qquad \zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$$ $$\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\overline{\rm MS}}}-\log\!\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}(y'-y)^{2}\big), \qquad \frac{1}{\epsilon_{\overline{\rm MS}}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_{E}+\log4\pi$$

6.1 The same thing in $\zeta$ — the fragmentation form

$$p^{+}=k^{+}\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta}\ \Longrightarrow\ \big|dp^{+}\big|=\frac{k^{+}}{\zeta^{2}}\,d\zeta, \qquad p^{+}=\Lambda\ \to\ \zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+\Lambda},\qquad p^{+}=\vee-k^{+}\ \to\ \zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}$$
$$\frac{d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{64\pi^{7}\,k^{+}} \int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{\,k^{+}/(k^{+}+\Lambda)}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}} \int_{x,x',y,y'}e^{-i\frac{k}{\zeta}\cdot(y'-y)} \Big\{C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X+\zeta(1-\zeta)\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\Big\}\;\big\langle\cdots\big\rangle$$

All three fragmentation hallmarks are now manifest: the measure $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$, the hard factor at the parent momentum $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, and $P_{gg}(\zeta)$ multiplying $\log\mu^{2}$.

6.2 After $\overline{\rm MS}$ and the $+$ prescription

Absorb $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\overline{\rm MS}}}$ into the gluon fragmentation function $D_{g/g}(\zeta,\mu)$, so $\mathcal L\to-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}(y'-y)^{2}\big)$. Then isolate the $\zeta\to1$ endpoint, the only singular piece of $C_{\rm UV}$:

$$C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\;\longrightarrow\;\zeta\left[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\right]_{+}+\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta(1-\zeta) \;\;+\;\;\delta\text{-term}$$

where, with $a=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}$ and $\delta=\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$, the $\delta$–term is the explicit

$$F(1)\,\log\frac{k^{+}(\vee-k^{+})}{\vee\,\Lambda},\qquad F(1)=e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X\ \ \text{(the LO structure)},$$

which is the piece that goes into the small–$x$ evolution. Everything else is finite and $\Lambda$–independent.

7. Summary of the logic

  1. $T$ is symmetric $\Rightarrow$ the $\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}$ and $\delta_{mk'}\delta_{km'}$ structures collapse onto one, so $b_{1}$ and $b_{3}$ add.
  2. $b_{1}+b_{3}=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}$: the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ cancels by $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$.
  3. $T(X,X')$ keeps its $\mathcal L+2$; $\operatorname{tr}T$ loses it entirely and becomes $\pi\mathcal L$; the $d_{\perp}$ in $b_{1}+b_{3}$ eats one unit, giving $\mathcal L+1$.
  4. In $\zeta$ variables the three surviving $\mathcal L\mathcal X$ coefficients assemble into exactly $C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=P_{gg}/2N_{c}$, with $\zeta\bar\zeta[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R]$ as the finite remainder.
