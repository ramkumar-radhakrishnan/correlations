The $\mathfrak B_{3}$ row

$\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)}\times\bar{\mathfrak B}_{3}^{\dagger}\mathfrak A^{(1)}$ — checking the five terms, the divergences, and the $p^{+}$ integral

$A\equiv(x-z)^{2}$,  $B\equiv(x-w)^{2}$,  $\beta\equiv\dfrac{k^{+}B}{A}$,  $V^{m}\equiv k^{+}(x-w)^{m}-p^{+}(x-z)^{m}$,  $W\equiv k^{+}B-p^{+}A$,  $\mathcal K\equiv\dfrac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-z)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-z)^{2}}$

Verdict. All five terms are right — every prefactor reproduces with ratio exactly $1$, the argument map $\mathfrak B_{3}(k^{+},z;p^{+},-u)\to \mathfrak B_{3}(k^{+},w;p^{+},-z)$ is applied correctly in all five, and the colour structure is correct with $f^{abd}$ legitimately an overall factor this time (§1–2). Four things need fixing (§3), one of which is substantive: the $\Theta$'s on terms 4 and 5 are effectively swapped relative to the definition, and that decides whether a double pole cancels. On divergences: no UV anywhere in the transverse integrand; one unprotected infrared log, at $|z|\to\infty$ in term 3 (§4). The $p^{+}$ integral has three distinct singularities, and the important one is not a logarithm — it is a $\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ power divergence from the double pole at $p^{+}=k^{+}$, plus an on–contour pole at $p^{+}=\beta$ that needs a prescription (§5). Closed forms for terms I and III in §6.

2. Colour — and this time $f$ can come out

$\mathfrak B^{ba}_{3}$ carries $f^{abc}\rho^{c}(x)$; relabel $c\to d$. Barring rotates the source, and the $\mathfrak A^{(1)}$ half gives $[U^{bc}(z)-U^{bc}(y)]\rho^{c}(y)$, so

$$f^{abd}\Big[U^{de}(x)U^{bc}(z)-U^{de}(x)U^{bc}(y)\Big]\rho^{e}(x)\rho^{c}(y)\quad\checkmark$$

Here $a$ is free and both $b$ and $d$ are contracted with Wilson lines, so $f^{abd}$ is a genuine overall factor — unlike the $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$ row, where it was not. Your colour structure is correct as written.

3. Four fixes

  1. Term 3 has no measure. Terms 1, 2, 4, 5 all carry $\int_{x,y,z,x'}$; term 3 does not, although $x,y,z,x'$ all appear in it.
  2. Bra transpose. The row gives $[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{b'a}(w')]$; you wrote $[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')]$. Same issue as in the previous two rows — pick one ordering.
  3. The $\mathfrak B_{3}$ definition has an unbalanced bracket. The \left[ opening $\big[\frac{1}{((x-z)-(x-u))^{2}}\Theta(\cdots)-\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{(\cdots)^{2}}$ never closes, so piece III is typeset inside piece II's bracket. The five terms you extracted show you meant them separate, but the source needs fixing.
  4. The $\Theta$'s on terms 4 and 5 are swapped relative to the definition. The definition puts $\Theta(p^{+}-k^{+}-\Lambda)$ on the $\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}$ half (so $p^{+}>k^{+}+\Lambda$) and none on the $-\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{V^{2}}$ half (full range). You have term 4 on $[\Lambda,k^{+}-\Lambda]$ and term 5 on $[k^{+}+\Lambda,\vee]$. This is not cosmetic — §5 shows it decides whether a double pole cancels.

4. Transverse divergences

4.1 No ultraviolet divergence

Every subset of $\{x,y,z,x',w,w'\}$ of size $\ge2$ was collapsed at scale $\rho$ against the measure $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$, for all four structures. Not one region diverges. The two that look dangerous both turn out safe:

  • $x,z,w\to$ a point, where $p^{+}A-k^{+}B\to0$ like $\rho^{2}$: integrand $\rho^{-2}$ against a three–point measure $\rho^{3}d\rho$ — convergent.
  • $V=0$, i.e. $k^{+}(x-w)=p^{+}(x-z)$ as vectors, at $x_{*}=\frac{k^{+}w-p^{+}z}{k^{+}-p^{+}}$. Term 5 has $\frac{1}{V^{2}}$ there, but its numerator $V^{m}$ vanishes linearly, so the integrand goes as $\frac{1}{\rho}$ against $\rho\,d\rho$ — integrable.

5. The $p^{+}$ integral has three singularities

5.1 A double pole at $p^{+}=k^{+}$ — and it does not fully cancel

Residues $\lim_{p^{+}\to k^{+}}(k^{+}-p^{+})^{2}\times(\text{term})$, computed with the quoted prefactors, converge cleanly (8 digits, from both sides):

$$\text{residue}(\mathrm{I})=\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}\,\frac{\mathcal K}{A-B}\quad\text{(predicted, matches to 8 digits)} .$$
IIa and IIb cancel each other exactly. That is clearly why piece II is written as a difference: at $p^{+}=k^{+}$ one has $V\to k^{+}(z-w)$, so $\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{V^{2}}\to\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}$ and the two halves coincide. But term I's double pole has nothing to cancel against.

Consequently the $\Theta$ strip $|p^{+}-k^{+}|>\Lambda$ leaves a power divergence:

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!\!+\int_{k^{+}+\Lambda}^{\vee}\frac{dp^{+}}{(k^{+}-p^{+})^{2}} =\frac{2}{\Lambda}-\frac{1}{k^{+}}-\frac{1}{\vee-k^{+}} \ \Longrightarrow\ \boxed{\ \frac{4}{\Lambda\,(A-B)}\ }$$
This is $\frac{1}{\Lambda}$, not $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$. It cannot be absorbed by rapidity evolution and it will not cancel inside this row. Two things follow. First, with your ranges the IIa/IIb cancellation happens above $k^{+}$ (terms 1 and 5) but below $k^{+}$ terms 2 and 4 add, doubling the residue — so the range assignment is not self–consistent. With the definition's $\Theta$'s it cancels below and survives above. Neither choice makes it cancel on both sides. Second, either way term I's own double pole survives. Resolve fix 4 of §3 first; if the $\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ still stands, it has to cancel against a companion row.

5.2 An on–contour pole at $p^{+}=\beta$

Every term contains $p^{+}A-k^{+}B$, which vanishes at

$$p^{+}_{*}=\beta=k^{+}\frac{(x-w)^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}},$$

a transverse–dependent point that lies inside $[\Lambda,\vee]$ for generic configurations. This is a simple pole on the integration contour, so the $p^{+}$ integral is not defined until you supply the prescription. It is an energy denominator, so the $+i\epsilon$ of the propagator gives

$$\frac{1}{p^{+}A-k^{+}B\pm i\epsilon}=\mathrm{PV}\frac{1}{p^{+}A-k^{+}B}\mp i\pi\,\delta(p^{+}A-k^{+}B).$$
The $\delta$ piece is not optional: every prefactor here carries an explicit $i$, so $i\times(\mp i\pi)$ is real and contributes to the cross section. Physically it is the on–shell intermediate state — a genuine real–emission contribution. Dropping it, or taking a bare principal value, loses a real part of the answer.

5.3 Soft poles at $p^{+}\to0$

Term 3 carries $\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}$ and term 4's bracket carries $-\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}$. These are the usual rapidity poles and are handled by the $[\frac{1}{p^{+}}]_{+}$ prescription as before.

6. The $p^{+}$ integrals in closed form

Both primitives verified symbolically ($\frac{dF}{dp^{+}}-\text{integrand}=0$ exactly) and against principal–value quadrature to six digits.

6.1 Term I

$$F_{\rm I}(p^{+})=-\frac{2}{(A-B)(p^{+}-k^{+})} +\frac{A+B}{k^{+}(A-B)^{2}}\log\left|\frac{p^{+}-\beta}{p^{+}-k^{+}}\right|$$
$$\mathcal T_{\rm I}=\frac{4}{\Lambda(A-B)}-\frac{2}{A-B}\left[\frac{1}{k^{+}}+\frac{1}{\vee-k^{+}}\right] +\frac{A+B}{k^{+}(A-B)^{2}}\left[\log\frac{A}{B}+\log\frac{\vee-\beta}{\vee-k^{+}}\right] \;\mp\;i\pi\,\frac{A+B}{k^{+}(A-B)^{2}}$$

There is no $\log\Lambda$ at all — the logarithms from the two strip edges cancel against each other, leaving only the $\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ power term. As $\vee\to\infty$ the second logarithm vanishes and

$$\mathcal T_{\rm I}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{4}{\Lambda(A-B)}-\frac{2}{k^{+}(A-B)} +\frac{A+B}{k^{+}(A-B)^{2}}\log\frac{A}{B}\;\mp\;i\pi\frac{A+B}{k^{+}(A-B)^{2}} .$$

6.2 Term III

Partial fractions give $\frac{k^{+}B+p^{+}A}{p^{+}(p^{+}-\beta)}=-\frac{A}{p^{+}}+\frac{2A}{p^{+}-\beta}$, so

$$\mathcal T_{\rm III}=\frac{1}{k^{+}}\left[-\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda} +2\log\left|\frac{\vee-\beta}{\Lambda-\beta}\right|\right] \ \xrightarrow[\ \Lambda\to0,\ \vee\to\infty\ ]{}\ \frac{1}{k^{+}}\log\frac{\vee\Lambda\,A^{2}}{k^{+2}B^{2}}\;\mp\;\frac{2i\pi}{k^{+}}$$

Note the unusual shape: the rapidity logarithm and the $\vee$ logarithm appear as a product $\log(\vee\Lambda)$, not a ratio. The residue $2$ at $p^{+}=\beta$ (against $-1$ at $p^{+}=0$) is what produces it.

6.3 Terms IIa and IIb

Here $p^{+}$ sits inside the tensor contraction — both $V^{m}$ and $W$ depend on it — so there is no single scalar master. What is fixed is the pole content: the $\frac{1}{(k^{+}-p^{+})^{2}}$ pieces cancel between the two (§5.1), the remaining poles are the $p^{+}=\beta$ pole of §5.2 (from $W$), the $p^{+}\to0$ pole of §5.3 (from the $-\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}$ in the bracket), and the $V^{2}$ zero in IIb, which §4.1 shows is integrable. Expand the $\delta_{ik}\delta_{jm}$, $\delta_{jk}\delta_{im}$, $\delta_{ij}\delta_{km}$ structures separately and each piece reduces to the masters above with $A,B$ replaced by the appropriate contractions.
