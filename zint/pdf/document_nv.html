2. The colour bracket — and your $N_{c}$ term

All four combinations are present and correctly assembled. The fourth is the one you collapsed:

$$\sum_{b}U^{bc'}(z)U^{bc}(z)=\delta^{c'c}\;\Longrightarrow\;\sum_{a,c'}f^{ac'd'}f^{ac'd}=N_{c}\delta^{d'd} \;\Longrightarrow\;N_{c}\,U^{e'd}(x')\,U^{de}(x)$$

Verified to $4\cdot10^{-15}$: given the bra ordering you wrote, your term 4 is exactly right. It works because both Wilson lines already sit at the same point $z$ and share the summed index $b$ — which is why it needed no limit; it holds for all $w,w'$.

But it inherits the transpose. Your bra carries $U^{e'd'}(x')$ ($\rho$ index first) while your ket carries $U^{de}(x)$ ($f$ index first). Conjugating the ket does not transpose a real orthogonal matrix, so the bra should read $U^{d'e'}(x')$. With that ordering the same collapse gives $$\mathcal C_{22}^{\,e'e}=N_{c}\big[U^{\dagger}(x')U(x)\big]^{e'e}$$ (verified to $1\cdot10^{-14}$) — the adjoint dipole, which is what you want physically. The two differ by $\|N_{c}U(x')U(x)-N_{c}U^{\dagger}(x')U(x)\|=3.72$, so it is not cosmetic. The same transpose sits in your term 3.
