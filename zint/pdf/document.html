The transverse $z$–integration of $d^3N_2^{\rm NLO}/d^3k$

UV logarithms, their finite companions, and all the remaining terms

Group I, Row V (the $C_1\otimes B_2$ two–$\rho$ row) — complete separation at $z\to x$

0. What this note delivers

The transverse integrand of Eq. (1.1) is singular when $z\to x$. The object of the exercise is to isolate that singularity. The previous note stopped after extracting the divergent logarithm; everything else was dropped. Here nothing is dropped. The $z$–integral is performed in full and split into three groups:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal W \;\equiv\;\int d^2r\; e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T(r)}{D} \;=\; \underbrace{\mathcal W_{\rm UV}}_{\text{the }\log(1/m^2)} \;+\; \underbrace{\mathcal W_{\rm fin}}_{\substack{\text{finite companion}\\ \text{of that log}}} \;+\; \underbrace{\mathcal W_{\rm reg}}_{\substack{\text{the eight structures that}\\ \text{were never divergent}}}\;}$$

The three groups are given in closed form in §8. The logic is:

  1. An exact algebraic identity (§4) $\;T(r)=C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\dfrac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}}+T_{\rm fin}(r)$, valid at every $r$, not just asymptotically. The first piece carries the whole $z\to x$ divergence; $T_{\rm fin}$ is integrable at $r=0$. $C_{\rm UV}=P_{gg}(\xi)/2N_c$.
  2. One divergent master integral (§6), evaluated exactly — log plus its finite companion, with no expansion.
  3. Eight regular structures (§7), each reduced to Bessel–function master integrals and written out.

Every step below is checked numerically (§12): the algebra symbolically against Eq. (1.1) itself, every master integral against direct two–dimensional quadrature, the assembled answer against a direct quadrature of the whole integrand, and the UV coefficient against the measured slope $d\mathcal W/d\log(1/m^2)$.

1. Variables, and the two typos in the source

With $r=z-x$, $s=y-x$, $P=x'-w'$, $\xi=p^+/k^+$, $\bar\xi=1-\xi$:

$$y-z=s-r,\qquad x-z=-r,\qquad (y-z)^2=(r-s)^2,\qquad (y-x)^2=s^2 .$$

The longitudinal denominator and the phase are

$$p^+(y-x)^2+(k^+-p^+)(y-z)^2 = k^+\bar\xi D,\qquad D=(r-s)^2+M^2,\qquad M^2=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}\,s^2 ,$$ $$e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}e^{-i\xi k\cdot(z-x)}=e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\,e^{iq\cdot r}, \qquad q\equiv\bar\xi\,k .$$

Two abbreviations are used throughout:

$$D_0\;\equiv\;D\big|_{r=0}\;=\;s^2+M^2\;=\;\frac{s^2}{\bar\xi}, \qquad\qquad q\equiv|q| .$$

The singularity of the problem sits at $r\to0$, i.e. $z\to x$: that is where $1/r^2$ and $1/r^4$ blow up. There is a second place where denominators vanish, $r\to s$ i.e. $z\to y$; §3 shows it is harmless, and §10 shows what it does at $\xi\to0$.

Two things in the source that had to be fixed before anything else.

(i) Eq. (1.8) is off by a factor 2 in one denominator. Contracting $-\frac{k^+-p^+}{p^+}\big[\frac{(y-z)^i(x-z)^m}{(x-z)^2}+\frac{(y-x)^m(y-z)^i}{2(y-x)^2}\big]$ with $\frac{P^i r^m}{P^2r^2}$ gives $\frac{(P\cdot(s-r))}{P^2r^2}\big[-1+\frac{r\cdot s}{2s^2}\big]\cdot(-\bar\xi/\xi)$, i.e.

$$\Theta_4=\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\,\frac{(s\cdot P)-(P\cdot r)}{P^2r^2} \left[1-\frac{r\cdot s}{\,\mathbf{2}\,s^2}\right],$$

not $\big[1-\frac{r\cdot s}{s^2}\big]$ as printed. This has been verified symbolically (§12A); Eqs. (1.4)–(1.7), (1.9), (1.10) are all correct as printed. The corrected $\Theta_4$ is used below — it changes $c_3$, $c_4$, $c_5$ but not $C_{\rm UV}$.

(ii) Two conventions in (1.1) vs. (1.11). (1.1) carries a phase $e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)}$ and a prefactor $p^+/k^+$; (1.3) and (1.11) require instead $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(z-x)}$ (otherwise no $e^{iq\cdot r}$ survives and the $z$–integral has nothing to converge on at large $r$) and no explicit $p^+/k^+$. This note follows (1.3)/(1.11). The factor is overall and does not touch the $z$–integral; it only reweights the $p^+$ integral of §10, where the alternative is quoted.

First, $c_1$ is the only place the transverse dimension enters: $c_1=d_\perp\xi\bar\xi$, from the instantaneous $\delta^{im}d_\perp$ term of $\Theta_1$, and $d_\perp=2$ has been used elsewhere. (In $d_\perp=2-2\epsilon$ the $d_\perp$ of $c_1$ is cancelled by the $1/d$ of the angular average, $\langle\hat r^i\hat r^j\rangle=\delta^{ij}/d$, so the $\xi(1-\xi)$ of $P_{gg}$ is exactly $\xi\bar\xi$ with no $O(\epsilon)$ correction; see the UV note.) Second, the coefficient of $S_1$ is not $\Theta_1$ alone: $\Theta_1$ gives $d_\perp\xi\bar\xi$, and $\Theta_3,\Theta_5,\Theta_6$ give $-2\bar\xi-2\xi+2=0$. Their cancellation is what the previous note meant by "$T_3,T_5,T_6$ drop out"; it is a statement about $S_1$ only, and those three structures certainly do not drop out of the rest of the answer.

3. Where the singularities are — power counting

3.1 $r\to0$  ($z\to x$) — the UV

Near $r=0$ one has $D\to D_0$ and $(r-s)^2\to s^2$, both finite and nonzero, and $e^{iq\cdot r}\to1$. In two dimensions $d^2r=r\,dr\,d\theta$, so a term behaving like $r^{-n}$ is log–divergent for $n=2$, convergent for $n\le1$. Using $\langle \hat r^i\rangle=0$ and $\langle\hat r^i\hat r^j\rangle=\delta^{ij}/2$:

$$\Big\langle\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^4}\Big\rangle=\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^2},\qquad \Big\langle\frac{s\cdot P}{r^2}\Big\rangle=\frac{s\cdot P}{r^2},\qquad \Big\langle\frac{P\cdot r}{r^2}\Big\rangle=0 .$$

Only $S_1$ and $S_2$ survive. $S_3,S_7,S_8,S_9$ go like $1/r$ — odd, hence angular–averaging to zero at leading order, and in any case integrable; $S_4,S_5,S_6$ are bounded. So the entire $z\to x$ divergence lives in $S_1$ and $S_2$, with

$$\frac{c_1}{2}+c_2\;=\;\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\;\equiv\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi) \;=\;\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c},\qquad P_{gg}=2N_c\Big[\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\xi(1-\xi)\Big].$$

The one–to–one correspondence is: $\xi(1-\xi)$ from the instantaneous $\Theta_1$, $\xi/(1-\xi)$ from $\Theta_2$, $(1-\xi)/\xi$ from $\Theta_4$.

3.2 $r\to s$  ($z\to y$) — not a divergence

$S_7,S_8,S_9$ have $(r-s)^2$ in the denominator, but each numerator vanishes linearly there,

$$r\cdot(r-s)\Big|_{r\to s}\!\!=s\cdot(r-s)+O\big((r-s)^2\big),\qquad s\cdot(r-s),\qquad P\cdot(r-s),$$

so each behaves as $1/|r-s|$, which is integrable in two dimensions. $D=(r-s)^2+M^2$ is regular there for $M^2>0$. Hence there is no divergence at $z\to y$ and no second regulator is needed — unlike in the previous note, where an auxiliary $\mu^2$ was introduced and its cancellation checked. Once $M^2\to0$, however ($\xi\to0$), the $z\to y$ region does generate a $\log M^2$; this is worked out in §10.3 and, importantly, it cancels between the groups.

3.3 $r\to\infty$

$S_6$ carries no $1/r^2$ at all, so $S_6/D\sim r^{-2}$: the $z$–integral converges at large $z$ only through the oscillation $e^{iq\cdot r}$. Several other terms are likewise only conditionally convergent. This is why the phase must be $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(z-x)}$ (see §1) — without $q=\bar\xi k$ the large–$z$ end does not converge.

4. The exact UV/finite split of the integrand

The angular argument of §3.1 identifies the divergence but is asymptotic. It can be turned into an exact identity. Split $S_1$ into its trace and its traceless part,

$$\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^4} =\underbrace{\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^2}}_{\text{trace}} +\underbrace{P_is_j\Big[\frac{r^ir^j}{r^4}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{2r^2}\Big]}_{\text{traceless}} .$$

The traceless bracket is orthogonal to every function of $r^2$ alone, so it contributes no logarithm: its $z$–integral converges at $r=0$. Since $\tfrac{c_1}{2}+c_2=C_{\rm UV}$, the trace piece of $S_1$ and all of $S_2$ combine into a single term and

$$T(r)\;=\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}}\;+\;T_{\rm fin}(r), \qquad C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c},$$ $$\begin{aligned} T_{\rm fin}(r)=\;&2\xi\bar\xi\left[\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^{4}}-\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^{2}}\right] &&\text{(traceless, UV finite)}\\[2pt] &+\Big(\xi^{2}-\tfrac52\xi+2-\tfrac1\xi\Big)\frac{P\cdot r}{r^{2}} \;+\;\frac{\bar\xi^{\,2}}{2\xi}\,\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{s^{2}r^{2}} \;-\;\frac{1}{2\xi}\,\frac{(s\cdot P)(s\cdot r)}{s^{2}r^{2}} \;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{s\cdot P}{s^{2}}\\[2pt] &-\frac{\xi}{2\bar\xi}\,\frac{(s\cdot P)\,r\cdot(r-s)}{r^{2}(r-s)^{2}} \;+\;\frac{\xi}{2}\,\frac{(P\cdot r)\,s\cdot(r-s)}{r^{2}(r-s)^{2}} \;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(s\cdot r)\,P\cdot(r-s)}{r^{2}(r-s)^{2}} . \end{aligned}$$

This is the central algebraic statement of the note, and it is an identity in $r$ — verified symbolically against Eq. (1.1) itself (§12A). Everything after this point is just the evaluation of two–dimensional Fourier integrals. Note that $T_{\rm fin}$ is not small: it contains $1/\xi$ and $1/\bar\xi$ poles of its own, and at $\xi\to1$ it does not vanish (§10.4).

5. Master integrals

Every term of $T/D$ is of the form $N(r)\,\big/\big[(r^2)^a\,(r-s)^{2b}\,D\big]$ with $a\in\{0,1,2\}$, $b\in\{0,1\}$ and $N\in\{1,r^i,r^ir^j\}$. Since $(r-s)^2$ and $D=(r-s)^2+M^2$ differ only by a constant, partial fractions

$$\frac{1}{(r-s)^2\,D}=\frac{1}{M^2}\left[\frac{1}{(r-s)^2}-\frac{1}{D}\right]$$

reduce everything to two–denominator integrals: the pair $\{r^2,D\}$ and the massless pair $\{r^2,(r-s)^2\}$.

5.1 Feynman parametrisation

With $A=r^2+m^2$ ($m$ the UV regulator, needed only for the divergent master) and $B=(r-s)^2+\Delta^2$,

$$\alpha A+(1-\alpha)B=(r-b)^2+\Delta,\qquad b=\bar\alpha\,s,\qquad \Delta(\alpha)=\bar\alpha\big(\alpha s^2+\Delta^2\big)+\alpha m^2, \qquad\bar\alpha\equiv1-\alpha ,$$ $$\frac1{AB}=\int_0^1\frac{d\alpha}{[\alpha A+\bar\alpha B]^2},\qquad \frac1{A^2B}=\int_0^1\frac{2\alpha\,d\alpha}{[\alpha A+\bar\alpha B]^3}.$$

For the pair $\{r^2,D\}$ take $\Delta^2=M^2$; for the massless pair $\{r^2,(r-s)^2\}$ take $\Delta^2=m^2=0$, i.e. $\Delta_0(\alpha)=\alpha\bar\alpha s^2$.

5.2 The two–dimensional Bessel transforms

Shift $r=b+u$; the phase factorises, $e^{iq\cdot r}=E\,e^{iq\cdot u}$ with $E\equiv e^{i\bar\alpha\,q\cdot s}$. Everything then follows from the 2D Yukawa transform and two derivative rules, with $\lambda\equiv q\sqrt{\Delta}$:

$$U_1=\int\!\frac{d^2u\,e^{iq\cdot u}}{u^2+\Delta}=2\pi K_0(\lambda),\qquad U_{n+1}=-\frac1n\frac{\partial U_n}{\partial\Delta},\qquad V^i_n=-i\frac{\partial U_n}{\partial q_i},\qquad T^{ij}_n=-\frac{\partial^2U_n}{\partial q_i\partial q_j}.$$

Using $K_0'=-K_1$ and $K_1'=-K_0-K_1/x$, and $\tfrac{d}{d\Delta}\big[\sqrt\Delta K_1(q\sqrt\Delta)\big]=-\tfrac{q}{2}K_0$:

$$U_2=\frac{\pi q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda),\qquad U_3=\frac{\pi q}{2}\left[\frac{K_1(\lambda)}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{q\,K_0(\lambda)}{2\Delta}\right],$$ $$V_2^i=i\pi q^iK_0(\lambda),\qquad V_3^i=\frac{i\pi q^i q}{4\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda),$$ $$T_2^{ij}=\pi\left[\delta^{ij}K_0(\lambda)-\frac{q^iq^j\sqrt\Delta}{q}K_1(\lambda)\right],\qquad T_3^{ij}=\frac{\pi}{4}\left[\frac{\delta^{ij}q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda)-q^iq^jK_0(\lambda)\right].$$

5.3 The masters

Collecting, with $b=\bar\alpha s$ and $E=e^{i\bar\alpha q\cdot s}$, the six masters that occur are (all UV finite except $\mathcal G$):

$$\Phi\;=\;\int d^2r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{D}\;=\;2\pi\,e^{iq\cdot s}K_0(Mq) \qquad\text{(closed form, no parameter integral)},$$ $$\Psi^i=\int d^2r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}\,r^i}{r^2D} =\pi\!\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\left[\bar\alpha\,s^i\frac{q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda)+i\,q^iK_0(\lambda)\right],$$ $$\mathcal N^{ij}=\int d^2r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}\,r^ir^j}{r^2D} =\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\Big[b^ib^jU_2+b^iV_2^j+b^jV_2^i+T_2^{ij}\Big],$$ $$\mathcal T^{ij}=\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r^ir^j-\tfrac12\delta^{ij}r^2}{r^4\,D} =\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\;\Big\{2\alpha\big[b^ib^jU_3+b^iV_3^j+b^jV_3^i+T_3^{ij}\big]\Big\}_{\rm traceless},$$ $$\mathcal G=\int d^2r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{(r^2+m^2)\,D} =\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\;U_2\big(\Delta\big),\qquad \Delta=\bar\alpha(\alpha s^2+M^2)+\alpha m^2 .$$

In $\Phi,\Psi,\mathcal N,\mathcal T$ one may set $m=0$, so $\Delta=\bar\alpha(\alpha s^2+M^2)$. Three checks that the reader can make immediately: $\mathcal N^{ii}=\Phi$ (because $r^ir^i/r^2=1$); $\mathcal T^{ii}=0$ by construction; and $\mathcal T$ is finite at $\alpha\to1$ only after the traceless projection — the divergent piece of $2\alpha T_3^{ij}$ is pure $\delta^{ij}$. Taking the trace before projecting is the one trap here: $\int\!\frac{r^ir^j}{(r^2+m^2)^2D}$ has trace $\mathcal G-m^2\!\int\!\frac{1}{(r^2+m^2)^2D}$, and the second term does not vanish as $m\to0$ — it tends to $\pi/D_0$.

5.4 The massless masters

For the pair $\{r^2,(r-s)^2\}$, with $\Delta_0=\alpha\bar\alpha s^2$, $\lambda_0=qs\sqrt{\alpha\bar\alpha}$, $r=u+\bar\alpha s$ and $r-s=u-\alpha s$:

$$A_7=\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2} =\pi\!\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\Big[2K_0(\lambda_0)-2\lambda_0K_1(\lambda_0) +i(1-2\alpha)(q\cdot s)K_0(\lambda_0)\Big],$$ $$A_8=\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{(P\cdot r)\,s\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2} =\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\Big[P_is_jT_2^{ij}-\alpha s^2(P\!\cdot\!V_2) +\bar\alpha(P\!\cdot\!s)(s\!\cdot\!V_2)-\alpha\bar\alpha s^2(P\!\cdot\!s)U_2\Big],$$ $$A_9=\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{(s\cdot r)\,P\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2} =\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\,E\Big[s_iP_jT_2^{ij}-\alpha(P\!\cdot\!s)(s\!\cdot\!V_2) +\bar\alpha s^2(P\!\cdot\!V_2)-\alpha\bar\alpha s^2(P\!\cdot\!s)U_2\Big].$$

($A_7$ used $u^2=(u^2+\Delta_0)-\Delta_0$, so that $\int u^2e^{iqu}/(u^2+\Delta_0)^2 =U_1-\Delta_0U_2$, and $\Delta_0=\alpha\bar\alpha s^2$ collapses the two $U_2$ terms into $-2\lambda_0K_1$.) These are $\xi$–independent: they depend on $q$ and $s$ only. A useful identity: $A_8=A_9$ when $P=s$, since then the two numerators coincide.

6. The divergent master, exactly — log and companion

There is no need to expand anything. Use the identity $\frac1{r^2D}=\frac1{r^2D_0}+\frac1{r^2}\big(\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}\big)$ together with

$$\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}=\frac{D_0-D}{D\,D_0}=\frac{2r\cdot s-r^2}{D\,D_0} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{1}{r^2}\Big(\frac1D-\frac1{D_0}\Big)=\frac{1}{D_0}\left[\frac{2\,r\cdot s}{r^2D}-\frac{1}{D}\right],$$

which is regular at $r=0$ (the bracket vanishes linearly there), so $m$ may be set to zero in it. Hence, exactly,

$$\mathcal G=\int\!\frac{d^2r\;e^{iq\cdot r}}{(r^2+m^2)\,D} =\frac{2\pi}{D_0}K_0(mq)\;+\;\frac{1}{D_0}\Big[\,2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi\,\Big]\;+\;O(m^2),$$ $$2\pi K_0(mq)\;=\;\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{m^2q^2} \;=\;\underbrace{\pi\log\frac{D_0}{m^2}}_{\text{the UV log}} \;+\;\underbrace{\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{q^2D_0}}_{\text{finite}} .$$

The second line is a choice of where to cut the logarithm; the argument $D_0=s^2/\bar\xi$ is picked so that the UV log reads $\log\!\big[(y-x)^2/\bar\xi m^2\big]$, which is the combination that pairs with the running coupling. Any other choice moves a constant between $\mathcal W_{\rm UV}$ and $\mathcal W_{\rm fin}$ and changes neither separately–meaningful physics nor the sum (see §11).

Dimensional regularisation. The whole $m$–dependence is the single factor $\pi\log(1/m^2)$. In $d_\perp=2+2\epsilon_{\rm UV}$, $\int d^{d_\perp}\!r\,e^{iq\cdot r}/r^2=\pi^{1+\epsilon}2^{2\epsilon}\Gamma(\epsilon)(q^2)^{-\epsilon}$, and matching the two regulators gives the dictionary

$$\log\frac{1}{m^{2}}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\!\big(\pi e^{\gamma_E}\mu^{2}\big),$$

i.e. $\pi\log(1/m^2)\to\pi/\epsilon_{\rm UV}+\ldots$, the pole that cancels against the $-2/\epsilon$ of the gluon self–energy counterterm. (With the opposite convention $d_\perp=2-2\epsilon$ the pole is $-1/\epsilon$.)

7. The eight regular structures, reduced

Each surviving structure maps onto the masters of §5. The three $(r-s)^2$ structures are handled by the partial fraction of §5, which turns $\int N/[r^2(r-s)^2D]$ into $\frac{1}{M^2}\big[\int\frac{N}{r^2(r-s)^2}-\int\frac{N}{r^2D}\big]$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal S_7&\equiv\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{(s\cdot P)\,r\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2 D} =\frac{s\cdot P}{M^2}\Big[A_7-\Phi+s\!\cdot\!\Psi\Big],\\ \mathcal S_8&\equiv\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{(P\cdot r)\,s\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2 D} =\frac{1}{M^2}\Big[A_8-P_is_j\mathcal N^{ij}+s^2\,(P\!\cdot\!\Psi)\Big],\\ \mathcal S_9&\equiv\int d^2r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{(s\cdot r)\,P\cdot(r-s)}{r^2(r-s)^2 D} =\frac{1}{M^2}\Big[A_9-s_iP_j\mathcal N^{ij}+(P\!\cdot\!s)\,(s\!\cdot\!\Psi)\Big], \end{aligned}$$

using $r\cdot(r-s)=r^2-r\cdot s$, $s\cdot(r-s)=s\cdot r-s^2$, $P\cdot(r-s)=P\cdot r-P\cdot s$ and $\int e^{iq\cdot r}r^2/(r^2D)=\Phi$. The apparent $1/M^2$ poles are spurious: at $M^2=0$ one has $D=(r-s)^2$ and the bracket vanishes identically, so each $\mathcal S_k$ is finite as $\xi\to0$.

8. The result

With $q=\bar\xi k$, $D_0=s^2/\bar\xi$, $M^2=(\xi/\bar\xi)s^2$, $C_{\rm UV}=P_{gg}/2N_c$:

$$\mathcal W\;=\;\int d^2r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T(r)}{D}\;=\; \mathcal W_{\rm UV}+\mathcal W_{\rm fin}+\mathcal W_{\rm reg},$$ $$\textbf{(I) UV log:}\qquad \mathcal W_{\rm UV}=C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,(s\cdot P)\,\frac{\pi\bar\xi}{s^{2}}\; \log\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi\,m^{2}} ,$$ $$\textbf{(II) its finite companion:}\qquad \mathcal W_{\rm fin}=C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,(s\cdot P)\,\frac{\bar\xi}{s^{2}} \left[\;\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}\,\bar\xi}{q^{2}s^{2}}\;+\;2\,s\!\cdot\!\Psi\;-\;\Phi\;\right],$$ $$\textbf{(III) the other terms:}\qquad \begin{aligned} \mathcal W_{\rm reg}=\;&2\xi\bar\xi\;P_is_j\,\mathcal T^{ij} \;+\;\Big(\xi^{2}-\tfrac52\xi+2-\tfrac1\xi\Big)\,P\!\cdot\!\Psi \;+\;\frac{\bar\xi^{\,2}}{2\xi\,s^{2}}\,P_is_j\,\mathcal N^{ij}\\ &-\;\frac{(s\cdot P)\,(s\!\cdot\!\Psi)}{2\xi\,s^{2}} \;+\;\frac{(s\cdot P)\,\Phi}{2s^{2}} \;-\;\frac{\xi}{2\bar\xi}\,\mathcal S_7 \;+\;\frac{\xi}{2}\,\mathcal S_8 \;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal S_9 . \end{aligned}$$

Group (I) is the only place $m$ (or $1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}$) appears. Groups (II) and (III) are finite, and are given entirely by the six masters of §5 — one closed form ($\Phi$) and five single $\alpha$–integrals of Bessel functions. Nothing has been expanded, approximated or discarded anywhere between §2 and here.

9. Back to the cross section

Inserting into (1.11) — the bracket is $T/P^2$, so the $z$–integral of the bracket is $\mathcal W/P^2$ —

$$\frac{d^3N_2^{\rm NLO}}{d^3k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{g^4}{8\pi^4k^+} \int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x} \int_{\Lambda/k^+}^{1-\Lambda/k^+}\!\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}\;\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\; \frac{\mathcal W_{\rm UV}+\mathcal W_{\rm fin}+\mathcal W_{\rm reg}}{P^{2}} \;\Big[\cdots\Big]\rho^{b'}\!(x')\rho^{f}\!(y),$$

where $[\cdots]$ is the colour structure of (1.1). For group (I) the explicit $\bar\xi$ of $\mathcal W_{\rm UV}$ cancels the $1/\bar\xi$ exactly and one is left with a product of two Weizsäcker–Williams kernels times a logarithm of the dipole size:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\frac{\mathcal W_{\rm UV}}{P^2} =\pi\,\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\; \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}\,(y-x)^{2}}\; \log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi\,m^{2}} ,$$

which is the object that pairs with the $-2/\epsilon$ of $A^{(3)}_{N_c}$ and becomes $\alpha_s\big((y-x)^{-2}\big)$. Groups (II) and (III) carry a different transverse structure — they are not proportional to the WW$\times$WW kernel — and this is precisely the content that was missing before.

10. The $p^+$ integration

10.1 Group (I): $P_{gg}$, and $b_0$

Write $dp^+=k^+d\xi$, $\lambda=\Lambda/k^+$, $\ell_k=\log(k^+/\Lambda)$, $L=\log\!\big[(y-x)^2/m^2\big]$; the logarithm of §9 is $L+\log\frac{1}{1-\xi}$. All six integrals are elementary:

Three readings. The double logarithm comes only from $\Theta_2$ and the $\pi^2/6$ only from $\Theta_4$; it is the instantaneous $\Theta_1$ that turns $-2$ into $-\tfrac{11}{6}$ and $-2$ into $-\tfrac{67}{36}$. And $-\tfrac{11}{6}\times 2N_c=-\tfrac{11N_c}{3}=-b_0$ at $N_f=0$: this row supplies, on its own, the $\tfrac{11N_c}{6}\delta(1-\xi)$ of the regularised splitting function, hence part of the running coupling.

If the $p^+/k^+$ of (1.1) is kept (see §1(ii)), every weight acquires an extra $\xi$ and the plain integrals become $\int\xi\cdot\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}=\tfrac12$, $\int\xi\cdot\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}=\ell_k-\tfrac32$, $\int\xi\cdot\xi(1-\xi)=\tfrac1{12}$, total $\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{12}$ — one rapidity logarithm instead of two, and no $b_0$. Which convention is intended should be settled before this row is used numerically.

10.2 Groups (II) and (III): why their $\xi$–integral is not elementary

$q=\bar\xi k$ depends on $\xi$, and so do $M^2=(\xi/\bar\xi)s^2$ and $D_0=s^2/\bar\xi$. The masters therefore depend on $\xi$ both through their coefficients and through their arguments, and $\int d\xi$ cannot be done in closed form. What can be established is the endpoint behaviour, which is what decides the rapidity logarithms.

10.3 $\xi\to0$: a $z\to y$ logarithm that cancels between (II) and (III)

As $\xi\to0$, $M^2\to0$ and $D$ develops a genuine singularity at $r=s$. For any smooth $f$, $\int d^2r\,f(r)/[(r-s)^2+M^2]=\pi f(s)\log(1/M^2)+\ldots$, so

$$\Psi^i\Big|_{\log}=\pi e^{iq\cdot s}\frac{s^i}{s^2}\Lambda_M,\quad \mathcal N^{ij}\Big|_{\log}=\pi e^{iq\cdot s}\frac{s^is^j}{s^2}\Lambda_M,\quad \Phi\Big|_{\log}=\pi e^{iq\cdot s}\Lambda_M,\quad \mathcal T^{ij}\Big|_{\log}=\pi e^{iq\cdot s}\Big[\tfrac{s^is^j}{s^4}-\tfrac{\delta^{ij}}{2s^2}\Big]\Lambda_M,$$

with $\Lambda_M\equiv\log(1/M^2)=\log(\bar\xi/\xi s^2)$. For $\mathcal S_{7,8,9}$ the numerators vanish at $r=s$, and the logarithm comes only from the $e^{iq\cdot\rho}$ expansion, $\mathcal S_k|_{\log}=i\pi e^{iq\cdot s}(q\cdot c_k)\Lambda_M/2s^2$ with $c_7=(s\cdot P)s$, $c_8=(P\cdot s)s$, $c_9=s^2P$ — all $O(1)$, not $O(1/\xi)$. Keeping only the $1/\xi$ poles ($c_3\to-\tfrac1\xi$, $c_4\to\tfrac1{2\xi}$, $c_5\to-\tfrac1{2\xi}$; sum $-\tfrac1\xi$):

$$\mathcal W_{\rm reg}\;\xrightarrow[\xi\to0]{}\;-\frac{\pi}{\xi}\,e^{iq\cdot s}\, \frac{s\cdot P}{s^{2}}\,\Lambda_M+O\!\left(\frac1\xi\right),\qquad \mathcal W_{\rm fin}\;\xrightarrow[\xi\to0]{}\;+\frac{\pi}{\xi}\,e^{iq\cdot s}\, \frac{s\cdot P}{s^{2}}\,\Lambda_M+O\!\left(\frac1\xi\right),$$

because $C_{\rm UV}\to1/\xi$, $D_0\to s^2$ and $(2s\!\cdot\!\Psi-\Phi)|_{\log} =(2-1)\pi e^{iq\cdot s}\Lambda_M$. The two cancel exactly. Both statements are confirmed numerically to five digits (§12E), where $\xi(\mathcal W_{\rm fin}+\mathcal W_{\rm reg})$ is seen to approach a constant while each piece separately grows like $\log(1/\xi)$.

Consequence. The $z\to y$ (collinear) logarithm is an artefact of where the UV cut is placed; it is absent from the sum. What survives at $\xi\to0$ is a simple pole $\propto1/\xi$, i.e. one rapidity logarithm $\ell_k$ from the finite and regular terms together — in addition to the ones carried by $P_{gg}$ in group (I).

10.4 $\xi\to1$: the regular block does not vanish

At fixed $q$, as $\bar\xi\to0$ one has $M^2\to\infty$: every master falls off like $1/M^2$ and the only survivor is $-\tfrac{\xi}{2\bar\xi}\mathcal S_7\to-\tfrac{\xi}{2\bar\xi}\cdot \tfrac{(s\cdot P)A_7}{M^2}$, so

$$\lim_{\xi\to1}\;\mathcal W_{\rm reg}\Big|_{q\ \rm fixed}\;=\;-\,\frac{(s\cdot P)}{2s^{2}}\,A_7(q,s) \qquad\text{(verified to 6 digits, §12F)} .$$

With the physical $q=\bar\xi k$ the two limits do not commute — $A_7\sim2\pi\log(1/qs)$ as $q\to0$ and the other masters grow too — but the conclusion is the same and is what matters: $\mathcal W_{\rm reg}$ tends to a finite nonzero constant at the endpoint (numerically $+0.868\ldots$ for the test kinematics), so the explicit $1/\bar\xi$ of the cross section turns it into a rapidity logarithm $\ell_k$ with a coefficient that is not contained in $P_{gg}$. Any rapidity subtraction applied to this row must therefore act on the regular terms as well, not only on group (I).

11. What the split does and does not mean

  • Unambiguous. The coefficient of $\log(1/m^2)$ — equivalently of $1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}$ — is $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)(s\cdot P)\pi\bar\xi/s^2$, i.e. $P_{gg}/2N_c$ times two WW kernels. So is the algebraic identity of §4, and so is the sum $\mathcal W_{\rm UV}+\mathcal W_{\rm fin}+\mathcal W_{\rm reg}$.
  • Scheme–dependent. The boundary between (I) and (II) is the argument chosen for the logarithm, here $D_0=s^2/\bar\xi$. Choosing $q^{-2}$ or $s^2$ instead moves constants (and, at $\xi\to1$, a $\log\bar\xi$) between the two. The boundary between (II) and (III) is fixed by the traceless projection of §4, which is natural but not forced.
  • The $U(z)$ caveat. In (1.1) the colour structure contains $U^{ce}(z)$, so the $z$–integral is not literally separable from it; (1.11) writes it as if it were. For group (I) this is harmless and is in fact the point: the divergence is local, $z\to x$, so $U^{ce}(z)\to U^{ce}(x)$ there and the coefficient multiplies a genuinely local colour structure — which is why it can be absorbed into the running coupling. For groups (II) and (III) the results above are the $z$–integral at fixed colour structure, i.e. they are exact if $U^{ce}(z)$ is taken outside, and otherwise they are the decomposition of the $z$–integrand, to be convolved with $U^{ce}(z)$. The algebraic split of §4 is unaffected either way, since it is pointwise in $r$.
  • $d_\perp$. Only $c_1$ (hence the $\xi(1-\xi)$ of $P_{gg}$) knows about the transverse dimension — but only apparently. Continuing the transverse plane to $d_\perp$ dimensions changes the angular average as well, $\langle\hat r^i\hat r^j\rangle=\delta^{ij}/d_\perp$, and the two factors cancel: $c_1\langle S_1\rangle=\tfrac{d_\perp}{d_\perp}\xi\bar\xi\tfrac{s\cdot P}{r^2}$. The $\xi(1-\xi)$ piece of $P_{gg}$ therefore receives no $O(\epsilon)$ correction from this term.

13. Summary card

Integrand   $T(r)=C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\dfrac{s\cdot P}{r^2}+T_{\rm fin}(r)$,   $C_{\rm UV}=\xi\bar\xi+\dfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\dfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\dfrac{P_{gg}}{2N_c}$   (exact, pointwise).

$z$–integral   $\mathcal W=\mathcal W_{\rm UV}+\mathcal W_{\rm fin}+\mathcal W_{\rm reg}$  of §8, built from $\Phi$, $\Psi^i$, $\mathcal N^{ij}$, $\mathcal T^{ij}$, $A_{7,8,9}$ and $\mathcal S_{7,8,9}$.

UV   $\dfrac{1}{\bar\xi}\dfrac{\mathcal W_{\rm UV}}{P^2} =\pi\dfrac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\dfrac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^2(y-x)^2} \log\dfrac{(y-x)^2}{\bar\xi m^2}$,   $\displaystyle\int\!\frac{dp^+}{2\pi}\to\frac{k^+}{2\pi} \Big[(2\ell_k-\tfrac{11}{6})L+\tfrac12\ell_k^2+\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}-\tfrac{67}{36}\Big]$.

Everything else   finite; carries a different transverse structure; has its own $1/\xi$ and $1/\bar\xi$ endpoint behaviour, hence its own rapidity logarithms (§10.3–10.4); and its $\log M^2$ cancels between groups (II) and (III).
