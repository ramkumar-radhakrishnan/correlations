The $z$ integration, done first

Every step, and the final result for the $\mathfrak A^{(1)\dagger}\bar{\mathfrak C}^{\dagger}\times\mathfrak B_{2}$ row

$S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$,  $\beta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $B=(x-w)^{2}$,  $X^{k'}=\dfrac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$,  $u=z-y'$,  $\hat k=k_{\perp}/|k_{\perp}|$

First, the honest constraint: this $z$ integral cannot be done in closed form, because the colour factor contains $U(z)$ — a Wilson line at the integration point. No amount of algebra removes it; it depends on the target field.

What can be done, and is the standard and correct thing: split the integral into an asymptotic piece that carries all the logarithms and is exactly integrable, plus an absolutely convergent remainder. That is what §3–§6 do. The result:

$$\int d^{2}z\;\mathcal I(z)\;=\;\underbrace{\int d^{2}u\Big[\mathcal I-\theta(|u|-\rho_{0})\,\mathcal I_{\rm asy}\Big]}_{\text{absolutely convergent, }|u|^{-3}\text{ tail}} \;+\;\underbrace{e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\,\mathcal C_{\infty}\,\frac{\pi}{p^{+}}\Big\{\tfrac{2\mathcal K}{k^{+}}L+\tfrac{p^{+2}}{k^{+2}S}\big[(L+\tfrac12)\mathcal K-\mathcal K_{k}\big]\Big\}}_{\text{exact}}$$

with $L=\log\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}k^{+}}{p^{+}|k_{\perp}|\rho_{0}}$, and the $p^{+}$ integral of the second piece is elementary (§8), giving the $\log^{2}$ in closed form. Both halves verified numerically (§7).

A correction to what I told you last time: the coefficient of the double log is $\frac{2\pi\mathcal K}{k^{+}}$, not the $-0.0490$ I quoted. That number came from a least–squares fit over too wide a $p^{+}$ range; the correct value is derived analytically here and confirmed by the converging local slope (§7.3). The existence of the $\log^{2}$ stands.

0. Why there is no closed form, and what to do instead

After the $w'$ integration the integrand is

$$\mathcal I(z)=e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\,e^{-i\beta\,k\cdot(y'-z)}\;\frac{N^{m}\,X^{k'}}{D}\;\mathcal R^{mk'}(z)\;\mathcal C(z), \qquad N^{m}=\frac{(y'-z)^{m}}{(y'-z)^{2}},\quad D=p^{+}(x-z)^{2}+k^{+}(x-w)^{2}$$

and $z$ appears in five places: $N^{m}$, the phase, $D$, the bracket $\mathcal R^{mk'}$, and the colour factor $\mathcal C(z)$ through $U(z)$. The first four are ordinary functions and could in principle be integrated. $U(z)$ cannot: it is a path–ordered exponential of the target field at the integration point.

So the goal is not "do the integral" — it is "extract everything that is divergent or logarithmically enhanced in closed form, and leave behind something that converges absolutely and can be evaluated once you specify the target." That is a subtraction, and it is how every calculation of this type is organised. The piece you extract must (i) reproduce the exact large–$|z|$ behaviour, and (ii) be integrable in closed form.

1. Change of variable: use $u=z-y'$, not $z$

This looks cosmetic and is not. Two reasons:

  • Exactness. $N^{m}=\frac{(y'-z)^{m}}{(y'-z)^{2}}=-\frac{u^{m}}{u^{2}}$ — exactly, not just asymptotically.
  • The phase. $e^{-i\beta k\cdot(y'-z)}=e^{+i\beta k\cdot u}$. If instead you centre on $z$, you must write $e^{-i\beta k\cdot y'}e^{+i\beta k\cdot z}$, and the stray factor $e^{-i\beta k\cdot y'}$ sits outside the $z$ integral while still depending on $\beta=p^{+}/k^{+}$ — it would then contaminate the $p^{+}$ integral and destroy the clean $\log^{2}$. Centring on $y'$ puts all the $p^{+}$–dependent phase inside the transverse integral, where the Bessel masters absorb it.

The measure is unchanged: $d^{2}z=d^{2}u$ (a shift, Jacobian $1$).

2. Power counting: what survives at large $|u|$

We need the integrand to order $|u|^{-2}$, since $\int d^{2}u\,|u|^{-2}$ is the log. Collect the scalings ($A=(x-z)^{2}\to|u|^{2}$, $C=(z-w)^{2}\to|u|^{2}$, $B$ fixed):

$$N^{m}=-\frac{u^{m}}{u^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{|u|},\qquad \frac{1}{D}\to\frac{1}{p^{+}|u|^{2}},\qquad X^{k'}\ \text{fixed}$$

so we need the $O(|u|)$ part of $\mathcal R^{mk'}$. Going through the six groups with $\mathcal V_{w}\to-\frac{x-w}{2B}$ (order $1$) and $\mathcal V_{z}\to\frac{u}{2|u|^{2}}$ (order $|u|^{-1}$):

  • group $-1/k^{+}$: $\;+\frac{1}{k^{+}}\frac{(x-w)\cdot X}{2B}$  (using $u\cdot N=-1$)

The first two combine, $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}-\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S}=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\frac{S-k^{+}}{k^{+}S}=\frac{p^{+2}}{k^{+2}S}$:

$$\mathcal I_{\rm asy}(u)=e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\;\mathcal C_{\infty}\;\frac{e^{i\beta k\cdot u}}{|u|^{2}}\;\frac{1}{p^{+}} \left[\frac{\mathcal K}{k^{+}}+\frac{p^{+2}}{k^{+2}S}\,\frac{(x-w)^{m}X^{k'}}{2B}\,\hat u^{m}\hat u^{k'}\right]$$ $$\mathcal K\equiv\frac{(x-w)\cdot X}{2B}=\frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-y')}{2\,(x-w)^{2}\,(x'-y')^{2}}$$

Checked: the angular average of $\mathcal I$ times $|u|^{2}$ against $\frac{\mathcal K}{p^{+}}\big[\frac{1}{k^{+}}+\frac{p^{+2}}{2k^{+2}S}\big]$ at $p^{+}=0.05,0.15,0.37,1.0,3.0$: ratio $=1.00000$ to five decimals in every case.

3. The colour at large $|u|$

Far from the target $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$. Setting $U(z)=\mathbb 1$ in the (uniformly ordered) colour factor:

$$\text{bra}\to f^{c'ba}\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]^{c'e'}\rho^{e'}(x'),\qquad \text{ket}\to f^{cbd}U^{ac}(w)\rho^{d}(x)-f^{abd}U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)$$

The second product collapses: $-\sum_{a,b}f^{c'ba}f^{abd}=+\sum_{a,b}f^{c'ab}f^{abd}=+N_{c}\delta^{c'd}$ (verified to $2\cdot10^{-15}$). The first does not — $\sum_{b}f^{c'ba}f^{cbd}$ is an irreducible four–index object. So

$$\mathcal C_{\infty}=\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]^{c'e'}\rho^{e'}(x') \Big\{f^{c'ba}f^{cbd}\,U^{ac}(w)\,\rho^{d}(x)\;+\;N_{c}\,U^{c'e}(x)\,\rho^{e}(x)\Big\}$$

a constant (in $u$), which therefore comes outside the transverse master integrals.

4. The two Bessel masters

4.1 The scalar one

Average over the direction of $u$ first, using $\langle e^{iq\cdot u}\rangle_{\hat u}=J_{0}(q|u|)$:

$$\mathcal M_{0}(q,\rho_{0})\equiv\int_{|u|>\rho_{0}}\!d^{2}u\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot u}}{|u|^{2}} =2\pi\!\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\infty}\!\frac{dr}{r}J_{0}(qr)=2\pi\!\int_{q\rho_{0}}^{\infty}\!\frac{J_{0}(t)}{t}dt$$

and for $q\rho_{0}\ll1$ the standard expansion (from $\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{0}(t)t^{s-1}dt=2^{s-1}\Gamma(\frac s2)/\Gamma(1-\frac s2)$ near $s=0$) gives

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal M_{0}=2\pi\Big[\log\frac{2}{q\rho_{0}}-\gamma_{E}\Big]+O\big((q\rho_{0})^{2}\big)\;}$$

Verified with oscillatory quadrature: at $q\rho_{0}=10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6}$ the numerical values match to $1.2\cdot10^{-5},\,1.2\cdot10^{-9},\,1.2\cdot10^{-13}$ — i.e. the error really is $O((q\rho_{0})^{2})$. (Plain quadrature gets this wrong by $5\cdot10^{-3}$; the tail must be done with an oscillatory method.)

4.2 The tensor one

Using the exact two–dimensional angular identity

$$\big\langle\hat u^{m}\hat u^{k'}e^{it\,\hat u\cdot\hat q}\big\rangle_{\hat u} =\frac{J_{1}(t)}{t}\delta^{mk'}+\Big(J_{0}(t)-\frac{2J_{1}(t)}{t}\Big)\hat q^{m}\hat q^{k'}$$

(check by taking the trace: $\frac{2J_{1}}{t}+J_{0}-\frac{2J_{1}}{t}=J_{0}$ ✓), one gets

$$\mathcal M_{2}^{mk'}\equiv\int_{|u|>\rho_{0}}\!d^{2}u\,\frac{\hat u^{m}\hat u^{k'}}{|u|^{2}}e^{iq\cdot u} =2\pi\Big[a_{1}\,\delta^{mk'}+a_{2}\,\hat q^{m}\hat q^{k'}\Big]$$ $$a_{1}=\int_{q\rho_{0}}^{\infty}\!\frac{J_{1}(t)}{t^{2}}dt,\qquad a_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\Big(J_{0}(t)-\frac{2J_{1}(t)}{t}\Big)\frac{dt}{t}$$

$a_{2}$ is finite at small $t$ (the integrand is $\simeq-t/8$), and numerically it is exactly

$$\boxed{\;a_{2}=-\tfrac12\;}\qquad\text{(to all 20 digits computed)}$$

while $a_{1}$ follows from the trace constraint $2a_{1}+a_{2}=\mathcal M_{0}/2\pi$, i.e. $a_{1}=\frac12\big[\log\frac{2}{q\rho_{0}}-\gamma_{E}+\frac12\big]$ (verified to $3\cdot10^{-14}$). Hence

$$\mathcal M_{2}^{mk'}=\pi\Big[\big(L+\tfrac12\big)\,\delta^{mk'}-\hat q^{m}\hat q^{k'}\Big],\qquad \mathcal M_{0}=2\pi L,\qquad L\equiv\log\frac{2}{q\rho_{0}}-\gamma_{E}$$

Note the shape: the same $\big[(\text{log})\,\delta-\hat q\hat q\big]$ structure as the $T^{mm'}$ master of the two–$\mathfrak C$ row, and the log sits in the $\delta$ part with half weight — which is just the angular average $\langle\hat u^{m}\hat u^{k'}\rangle=\frac12\delta^{mk'}$.

5. Assembling the asymptotic piece

Here $q=\beta k_{\perp}$, so $|q|=\frac{p^{+}|k_{\perp}|}{k^{+}}$ and $\hat q=\hat k$, giving

$$L=\log\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}k^{+}}{p^{+}\,|k_{\perp}|\,\rho_{0}}\;\equiv\;\log\frac{\Xi}{p^{+}}, \qquad \Xi\equiv\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}k^{+}}{|k_{\perp}|\rho_{0}}$$

Contracting $\frac{(x-w)^{m}X^{k'}}{2B}$ with $\mathcal M_{2}^{mk'}$ gives $\pi\big[(L+\frac12)\mathcal K-\mathcal K_{k}\big]$ with $\mathcal K_{k}\equiv\frac{[(x-w)\cdot\hat k][X\cdot\hat k]}{2B}$, and $\frac{\mathcal K}{k^{+}}\mathcal M_{0}=\frac{2\pi\mathcal K L}{k^{+}}$. Therefore

$$\mathcal G_{\rm log}(p^{+})\equiv\int_{|u|>\rho_{0}}\!\!d^{2}u\;\mathcal I_{\rm asy} =e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\,\mathcal C_{\infty}\;\frac{\pi}{p^{+}} \left\{\frac{2\mathcal K}{k^{+}}\,L+\frac{p^{+2}}{k^{+2}S}\Big[\big(L+\tfrac12\big)\mathcal K-\mathcal K_{k}\Big]\right\}$$

6. The subtraction

$$\int d^{2}z\,\mathcal I\;=\;\underbrace{\int d^{2}u\Big[\mathcal I(u)-\theta(|u|-\rho_{0})\mathcal I_{\rm asy}(u)\Big]}_{\mathcal G_{\rm fin}(p^{+})}\;+\;\mathcal G_{\rm log}(p^{+})$$

$\rho_{0}$ is arbitrary: any change in it moves the same amount between the two pieces. Choose it of order the typical transverse separation so that $\mathcal G_{\rm fin}$ is small.

8. The $p^{+}$ integral, in closed form

8.1 The leading (double–log) term

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\!\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}\,\log\frac{\Xi}{p^{+}} =\Big[-\tfrac12\log^{2}\tfrac{\Xi}{p^{+}}\Big]_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}} =\tfrac12\Big[\log^{2}\tfrac{\Xi}{\Lambda}-\log^{2}\tfrac{\Xi}{\vee-k^{+}}\Big]$$

(primitive verified symbolically: $\frac{d}{dp}\big[-\frac12\log^{2}\frac\Xi p\big]-\frac1p\log\frac\Xi p=0$.)

8.2 The subleading term — finite, and a dilogarithm

$$\int\!dp\;\frac{p}{p+k^{+}}\Big(\log\frac{\Xi}{p}+\tfrac12\Big) =p\Big(\log\frac{\Xi}{p}+\tfrac32\Big) -k^{+}\Big[\big(\log\Xi+\tfrac12\big)\log(p+k^{+})-\log p\,\log\!\big(1+\tfrac{p}{k^{+}}\big)-{\rm Li}_{2}\!\big(-\tfrac{p}{k^{+}}\big)\Big]$$ $$\int\!dp\;\frac{p}{p+k^{+}}=p-k^{+}\log(p+k^{+})$$

(first primitive checked against quadrature to $3\cdot10^{-21}$). Both are finite at $p^{+}\to0$, so they contribute no divergence — only the term in §8.1 does.

9. The final result

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{2\,\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}} \int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\int_{w,x,x',y'} \Big\{\mathcal G_{\rm fin}(p^{+})+\mathcal G_{\rm log}(p^{+})\Big\}$$

and the $p^{+}$ integral of the closed piece is

$$-\frac{g^{4}}{64\pi^{7}k^{+}}\int_{w,x,x',y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}\;\mathcal C_{\infty}\;\mathcal K\; \left[\log^{2}\frac{\Xi}{\Lambda}-\log^{2}\frac{\Xi}{\vee-k^{+}}\right]\;+\;(\text{finite, §8.2})$$

with

$$\mathcal K=\frac{(x-w)\cdot(x'-y')}{2\,(x-w)^{2}\,(x'-y')^{2}},\qquad \mathcal K_{k}=\frac{\big[(x-w)\cdot\hat k\big]\big[(x'-y')\cdot\hat k\big]}{2\,(x-w)^{2}\,(x'-y')^{2}},$$ $$\Xi=\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}k^{+}}{|k_{\perp}|\,\rho_{0}},\qquad \mathcal C_{\infty}=\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]^{c'e'}\rho^{e'}(x')\Big\{f^{c'ba}f^{cbd}U^{ac}(w)\rho^{d}(x)+N_{c}U^{c'e}(x)\rho^{e}(x)\Big\}$$ $$\mathcal G_{\rm fin}(p^{+})=\int d^{2}u\Big[\mathcal I(u)-\theta(|u|-\rho_{0})\,\mathcal I_{\rm asy}(u)\Big] \quad\text{— absolutely convergent, needs the target.}$$

10. Reading the answer

  • The whole $\Lambda$–dependence is now explicit, and it is a double logarithm. That is the characteristic signature of a soft–collinear region, and it is why the $\big[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\big]_{+}$ prescription could not be applied.
  • $\rho_{0}$ cancels. It appears in $\Xi$ inside the $\log^{2}$ and, with the opposite sign, in $\mathcal G_{\rm fin}$. Any convenient value works; the physics does not depend on it.
  • Nothing needed dimensional regularisation. There was no UV and no collinear pole — the phase supplied the upper cutoff and $\rho_{0}$ is an artificial splitting point, not a regulator.
  • What remains is $\mathcal G_{\rm fin}$, which is a genuinely convergent five–fold transverse integral containing $U(z)$, to be done once the target is specified (MV model, or numerically on JIMWLK configurations).
  • Before you use any of this, apply the fixes from the previous note: the $(z-w)^{k'}\to(x-w)^{k'}$ typo in the $-\frac{S^{2}}{p^{+2}}$ group, the uniform Wilson–line index ordering, and the measure ($\int_{y}$ out, $\int_{x'}$ in).
