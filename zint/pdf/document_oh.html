Where DGLAP comes from in Row IV

The $p^{+}$ bracket is $P_{gg}$: the plus prescription, the fragmentation measure, and the collinear pole

$\zeta=\dfrac{k^{+}}{k^{+}\!+p^{+}}$,  $\bar\zeta=1-\zeta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}\!+p^{+}}$,  $r=y'-y$,  $\kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r$,  $X=\dfrac{x-y}{(x-y)^{2}}$,  $X'=\dfrac{x'-y'}{(x'-y')^{2}}$,  $\mathcal M=\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}+1-\log\!\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big)$

The one line that does it. The $\mathfrak C_{1}$ vertex splits a parent of $S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$ into the measured gluon ($k^{+}$) and an unmeasured one ($p^{+}$). Call the measured fraction $\zeta=k^{+}/S$. Then $$\frac{p^{+}}{(k^{+}\!+p^{+})^{2}}+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}} =\frac{1}{k^{+}}\bigg[\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\bigg] =\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}\,k^{+}}$$ exactly — your three $p^{+}$ structures are the three terms of the gluon splitting function, term by term. Everything below follows from that.

1. The three signatures of fragmentation, all present

(i) The measure. $p^{+}=k^{+}\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta}\Rightarrow dp^{+}=-\frac{k^{+}}{\zeta^{2}}d\zeta$, so

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!dp^{+}\;=\;k^{+}\!\!\int_{\zeta_{0}}^{\zeta_{1}}\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}, \qquad \zeta_{0}=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee},\quad \zeta_{1}=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+\Lambda}\simeq1-\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$$

$\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$ is the fragmentation measure, not $d\zeta$.

(ii) The hard scale. Your phase is $e^{-ik\cdot r}e^{-ik\cdot r\,p^{+}/k^{+}}=e^{-ik\cdot r\,\frac{S}{k^{+}}}$ and $\frac{S}{k^{+}}=\frac1\zeta$, so

$$e^{-i\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\cdot(y'-y)}$$

— the hard factor is evaluated at $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, not at $k_{\perp}$. This is the single most characteristic fragmentation signature, and it is exactly the $\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ that the $w,w'$ delta functions put into the exponent.

(iii) Where the Wilson lines sit. At $x,x',y,y'$ — the parent positions, with no $\zeta$ dependence at all. The splitting is a final-state one, after the target has been traversed. If the Wilson lines depended on $\zeta$ there would be no factorisation.

2. The row in $\zeta$ variables

Starting from the (corrected) three-block form and substituting $\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}=\frac{\zeta\bar\zeta}{k^{+}}$, $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}=\frac{1}{k^{+}}\big[\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\big]$, with $\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\cdot\pi\cdot\frac{1}{k^{+}}=\frac12$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{\zeta_{0}}^{\zeta_{1}}\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\int_{x,x',y,y'}\!\!e^{-i\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\cdot(y'-y)}\; \Bigg\{\;\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\;\mathcal M\;(X\!\cdot\!X')$$ $$-\;N_{c}\bigg[d_{\perp}\,\zeta\bar\zeta\,(X\!\cdot\!\hat r)(X'\!\cdot\!\hat r) +\Big(\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\Big)(X\!\cdot\!X')\bigg]\Bigg\}\;\times\;\mathcal U$$ with $\mathcal U=\big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$ and $P_{gg}(\zeta)=2N_{c}\big[\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\big]$.

3. The plus prescription is the DGLAP one

$p^{+}\to0$ is $\zeta\to1$, and the pole there is the $\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}$ of $P_{gg}$ — the standard DGLAP endpoint. The test is whether the companion is regular there. Collecting everything that multiplies $\frac{1}{1-\zeta}$ in the integrand,

$$\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}\,e^{-i\frac{\kappa}{\zeta}}\,\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\;\Big[\mathcal M-1\Big](X\!\cdot\!X') \;=\;\frac{h(\zeta)}{1-\zeta}\,\Big[\mathcal M-1\Big](X\!\cdot\!X'),\qquad h(\zeta)=\frac{e^{-i\kappa/\zeta}}{\zeta}$$

and $h(1)=e^{-i\kappa}$ is finite. $\mathcal M$, $X$, $X'$, $\hat r$ and the Wilson lines carry no $\zeta$. So the prescription applies with nothing extra:

$$\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\;\longrightarrow\;\bigg[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\bigg]_{+} \;+\;\delta(1-\zeta)\,\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda},\qquad \int_{0}^{1}\!d\zeta\bigg[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\bigg]_{+}f(\zeta)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\!d\zeta\,\frac{f(\zeta)-f(1)}{1-\zeta}$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}}\;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{[1-\zeta]_{+}}}_{\text{regularised splitting function}} \;+\;\delta(1-\zeta)\,\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$$

Reading the two pieces. The $\delta(1-\zeta)\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ is the rapidity divergence — a virtual-like, $\zeta$-integrated term that goes with the JIMWLK evolution of $\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]$. The $\big[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\big]_{+}$ part is genuine $\zeta<1$ splitting and is what DGLAP acts on. In the complete $P_{gg}$ the $\delta(1-\zeta)$ coefficient is $\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}=\frac{11N_{c}}{6}$ (pure glue), built from the virtual rows; here you get $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ instead, because this single row has no virtual partner. That coefficient is only assembled once all of Group II is summed.

4. The collinear pole, in collinear-factorisation form

Define the LO structure (the only $\zeta$-dependence is through the hard scale)

$$\mathcal N_{\rm LO}(q_{\perp})\equiv\int_{x,x',y,y'}\!\!e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot(y'-y)}\; \frac{(x-y)\!\cdot\!(x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}\;\mathcal U$$

Then the $\frac1\epsilon$ part of the row is, with no approximation,

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\rm pole} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\,\frac{1}{k^{+}}\;\frac{1}{\epsilon} \int_{\zeta_{0}}^{\zeta_{1}}\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\;\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\; \mathcal N_{\rm LO}\!\left(\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\right)$$

which is exactly the shape of collinear factorisation for fragmentation,

$$\frac{d\sigma^{h}}{d^{3}k}=\int\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\;D_{h/g}(\zeta,\mu_{F})\; \frac{d\hat\sigma}{d^{3}k}\!\left(\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\right)$$

with the $\frac1\epsilon$ removed by the bare-to-renormalised relation

$$D_{g/g}(\zeta,\mu_{F})=\delta(1-\zeta)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\bigg[-\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu_{F}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\bigg]P_{gg}(\zeta)+O(\alpha_{s}^{2})$$

The normalisation check

With the LO written in the same convention, $\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\big|\mathfrak A^{(1)}\big|^{2} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{2}}{\pi^{2}k^{+}}\mathcal N_{\rm LO}$, the ratio of coefficients is

$$\frac{g^{4}/(8\pi^{4})}{g^{2}/\pi^{2}}=\frac{g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}, \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\rm pole}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{\epsilon} \int\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\;\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\;\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm LO}}{d^{3}k}\!\left(\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\right)$$

i.e. the textbook $\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi\epsilon}P_{gg}$, up to the leftover $\frac12$.

That residual $\frac12$ is not something I can settle from this row alone: it depends on how you normalise the LO ($\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}$ vs $\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}$, the same ambiguity as the $\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}$ in your (0.1)) and on whether the hermitian-conjugate row $\bar{\mathfrak C}\mathfrak A^{(1)}\times\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)}\bar{\mathfrak C}^{\dagger}$ is counted separately or already included. Fix it against your own LO expression — the $\zeta$ structure, which is what matters for DGLAP, is exact either way.

5. What the $\frac1\epsilon$ actually is — and a correction to what I said before

Earlier I read the pole of $\mathcal G^{mm'}$ as an infrared one to be cancelled against the Group II rows in which $U(z)$ survives. The $\zeta$ rewriting shows the correct home. The pole comes from $|z|\to\infty$ in $\int d^{2}z\,\frac{(y-z)^{m}(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}$, i.e. from small transverse momentum of the emitted gluon relative to its parent — that is the final-state collinear region of the $g\to gg$ splitting. Its residue is $P_{gg}(\zeta)$ with the $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$ measure and the hard factor at $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, which is the definition of a fragmentation contribution. So it is absorbed into the gluon fragmentation function at the scale $\mu_{F}$, not cancelled. The statement that survives from before is only the narrower one: the $\delta(1-\zeta)$ piece, carrying $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, still has to be combined with the other rows before it can be handed to the evolution.

6. What is left after the subtraction

Once $\frac{1}{\epsilon}P_{gg}$ is absorbed into $D_{g/g}(\zeta,\mu_{F})$, the finite remainder of this row is the NLO coefficient function:

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\int\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}\!\int_{x,x',y,y'}\!\! e^{-i\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\cdot r}\Bigg\{\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\Big[1-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu_{F}^{2}r^{2}\big)\Big](X\!\cdot\!X')$$ $$-\;N_{c}\bigg[d_{\perp}\zeta\bar\zeta\,(X\!\cdot\!\hat r)(X'\!\cdot\!\hat r)+\Big(\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\Big)(X\!\cdot\!X')\bigg]\Bigg\}\,\mathcal U$$

Three remarks on it:

  • The $\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu_{F}^{2}r^{2}\big)$ sits under the $x,x',y,y'$ integrals, so it does not factor out of $\mathcal N_{\rm LO}$. That is the usual statement that the coefficient function is not a pure number: the position-space log is the NLO hard factor.
  • The $d_{\perp}\zeta\bar\zeta(X\!\cdot\!\hat r)(X'\!\cdot\!\hat r)$ term is the azimuthal partner of the $\zeta\bar\zeta$ piece of $P_{gg}$. It carries no pole and no $\frac{1}{1-\zeta}$, so it neither fragments nor needs the plus prescription.
  • The $-\big(\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}\big)(X\!\cdot\!X')$ term does have the $\zeta\to1$ pole, and it is what turns $\mathcal M$ into $\mathcal M-1$ there — so the $\big[\ \big]_{+}$ acts on the sum of it and the $P_{gg}\mathcal M$ term, with the finite residue $(\mathcal M-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')$ quoted in §3.

7. Two loose ends that affect the DGLAP reading

  • The small-$\zeta$ end. $\zeta\to\zeta_{0}=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}$ is the measured gluon carrying a vanishing fraction. There $P_{gg}\to\frac{2N_{c}}{\zeta}$ and the measure is $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$, so the integrand grows as $\zeta^{-3}$. What controls it here is the phase $e^{-ik_{\perp}\cdot r/\zeta}$, which oscillates ever faster as $\zeta\to0$; in a genuine FF treatment it is the small-$z$ behaviour of $D_{g/g}$. Either way, do not send $\vee\to\infty$ before the $y,y'$ integrals.
  • Where $\Lambda$ comes from. The $\Theta\big(k^{+}_{\rm arg}-p^{+}_{\rm arg}-\Lambda\big)$ in your $\mathfrak C_{1jki}$ reduces to $\Theta(k^{+}-\Lambda)$ in this row and therefore does not enforce $\zeta<1-\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$. The endpoint that the plus prescription regulates needs its own $\Theta(p^{+}-\Lambda)$. Worth settling before quoting the $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ coefficient.

8. Summary

questionanswer
Can you use the plus prescription for $p^{+}$? Yes. In $\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+p^{+}}$ it is the standard DGLAP $\big[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\big]_{+}$; the companion $h(\zeta)=\frac{e^{-i\kappa/\zeta}}{\zeta}$ is regular at $\zeta=1$.
Does DGLAP appear? Yes. The $p^{+}$ bracket is exactly $\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}k^{+}}$, the measure is $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$, the hard factor sits at $\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}$ and the Wilson lines are $\zeta$-independent.
What absorbs the $\frac1\epsilon$? The gluon fragmentation function $D_{g/g}(\zeta,\mu_{F})$ — this is a final-state collinear pole, not a charge renormalisation and not a cancellation.
What carries $\Lambda$? The $\delta(1-\zeta)$ term, $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ — the rapidity log, for JIMWLK.
