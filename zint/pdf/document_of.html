$S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$,  $\sigma=\dfrac{S}{k^{+}}$,  $r=y'-y$,  $\kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\! r$,  $X^{k}=\dfrac{(x-y)^{k}}{(x-y)^{2}}$,  $X'^{k'}=\dfrac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$,  $\mathcal L=\dfrac1\epsilon+2-\log\!\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big)$

Short answer on $d_{\perp}$. It cancels exactly inside $\mathcal G^{mm'}$ — the $\frac{1}{d_{\perp}}$ of your (0.12) is eaten by the $\frac{d}{2}$ of master (0.13), so $\frac{1}{d_{\perp}}\big(\frac1\epsilon-1\big)=\frac{1}{2\epsilon}$ with no remainder. It then comes back twice at the contraction in (0.21), and there it does not cancel: the explicit $d_{\perp}$ in the first bracket term turns $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L-1$, and the trace $\delta^{mm'}\delta^{mm'}=d_{\perp}$ in the third term turns $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L-2$. Setting $d_{\perp}=2$ too early loses both.

2. The $w,w'$ integration — where the stray factor comes from

With $\beta=\frac{p^{+}}{S}$, the delta is $\delta^{(2)}\big[y-(1-\beta)w-\beta z\big]$, so

$$w=\frac{y-\beta z}{1-\beta},\qquad \text{Jacobian}=\frac{1}{(1-\beta)^{2}}=\frac{S^{2}}{k^{+2}}\ \text{ per delta},$$ $$w-z=\frac{y-z}{1-\beta}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{(w-z)^{m}}{(w-z)^{2}}=(1-\beta)\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{k^{+}}{S}\ \text{ per kernel},$$ $$w'-w=\frac{y'-y}{1-\beta}=\frac{S}{k^{+}}\,(y'-y)\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}=e^{-i\frac{S}{k^{+}}k\cdot(y'-y)}.$$

Net from two deltas and two kernels: $\frac{S^{4}}{k^{+4}}\cdot\frac{k^{+2}}{S^{2}}=\frac{S^{2}}{k^{+2}}$. Together with the $\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{4}}$ already in (0.1) this gives $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}$ in front of the bare three–term tensor. Comparing that with your $\frac{1}{k^{+}}\cdot\frac{S}{k^{+}}\times[\text{bracket}]$, every one of the three structures comes out with the same ratio $\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ — so the bracket is right and the extra $\frac{(k^{+}+p^{+})}{k^{+}}$ must go.

Corrected (0.2)/(0.3): $$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}} \int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\int_{z}\int_{x,x',y,y'} e^{-i\frac{S}{k^{+}}k\cdot(y'-y)}\; \frac{(y-z)^{m}(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}\,X^{k}X'^{k'}\;\mathcal B^{mm'kk'}\times\mathcal U$$ $$\mathcal B^{mm'kk'}=\frac{d_{\perp}p^{+}}{S^{2}}\delta^{km}\delta^{k'm'} +\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\delta^{mk'}\delta^{m'k}-\delta^{m'k'}\delta^{km}\big) +\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big)\delta^{mm'}\delta^{kk'}$$ with $\mathcal U=\big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$.

3. Where $d_{\perp}$ goes — in detail

(a) Inside $\mathcal G^{mm'}$ it cancels exactly

Your (0.12) carries $\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{d_{\perp}}$; master (0.13) carries $\frac{d}{2}$:

$$\frac{1}{d_{\perp}}\cdot\frac{d}{2}\,\Gamma\Big[1-\frac d2\Big]=\frac12\,\Gamma[\epsilon]=\frac{\Gamma[1+\epsilon]}{2\epsilon}$$

which is the $(1-\epsilon)$ sitting next to $\Gamma[\epsilon]$ in your (0.16). Equivalently, in (0.18),

$$\frac{1}{d_{\perp}}\Big(\frac1\epsilon-1\Big)=\frac{1}{2(1-\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\qquad\text{exactly.}$$

No $O(\epsilon)$ remainder, so $d_{\perp}$ leaves no trace in $\mathcal G$ itself. That is why (0.20) has a clean $\frac{\pi\delta^{mm'}}{2}$.

(b) At the contraction it returns, twice

Writing $\mathcal G^{mm'}=\frac{\pi\delta^{mm'}}{2}\mathcal L-\pi\frac{r^{m}r^{m'}}{r^{2}}$ and using $\epsilon\,\mathcal L\to1$, $\epsilon\times(\text{finite})\to0$:

$$\delta^{mm'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=\frac{\pi d_{\perp}}{2}\mathcal L-\pi=\pi(1-\epsilon)\mathcal L-\pi=\pi\big(\mathcal L-2\big),$$ $$d_{\perp}\,X_{m}X'_{m'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=2(1-\epsilon)\Big[\frac{\pi}{2}\mathcal L(X\!\cdot\!X')-\pi\frac{(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}}\Big] =\pi\big(\mathcal L-1\big)(X\!\cdot\!X')-2\pi\frac{(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}}.$$

So $d_{\perp}$ is responsible for $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L-1$ in the first bracket term and for one of the two units in $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L-2$ in the third (the other unit is the trace of $-\pi\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}$). Setting $d_{\perp}=2$ before contracting would give $\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal L-1$ instead — wrong by $\pi(X\!\cdot\!X')$ in each.

4. The $\mu$ power and $\overline{\rm MS}$: (0.19) and (0.20) disagree

Expanding the prefactor of (0.19) as written, with $\mu^{+2\epsilon}$,

$$\pi^{1-\epsilon}(r^{2})^{-\epsilon}\mu^{2\epsilon}\,\mathcal N(\epsilon)=\pi\Big[1+\epsilon\Big(2-\gamma_{E}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi r^{2}}\Big)\Big],\qquad \mathcal N(\epsilon)=\frac{\Gamma[1+\epsilon]\Gamma[1-\epsilon]^{2}}{\Gamma[2-2\epsilon]},$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal G^{mm'}=\frac{\pi\delta^{mm'}}{2}\Big[\frac1\epsilon+2-\gamma_{E}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi r^{2}}\Big]-\frac{\pi r^{m}r^{m'}}{r^{2}}.$$

Now $\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}=4\pi e^{-\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}$, i.e. $\mu^{2}=\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}$, gives

$$\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi r^{2}}=\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{4\pi^{2}r^{2}}+\gamma_{E} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathcal G^{mm'}=\frac{\pi\delta^{mm'}}{2}\Big[\frac1\epsilon+2+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{4\pi^{2}r^{2}}\Big]-\frac{\pi r^{m}r^{m'}}{r^{2}},$$

with the log the other way up from your (0.20). Which one you want is fixed by the power of $\mu$:

5. The contraction — finishing (0.21)

$\mathcal G^{mm'}$ is symmetric, so the two index pairings in the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ term give the same thing:

$$\delta^{mk'}\delta^{m'k}X^{k}X'^{k'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=X'^{m}X^{m'}\mathcal G^{mm'} =X^{m}X'^{m'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=\delta^{m'k'}\delta^{km}X^{k}X'^{k'}\mathcal G^{mm'}$$
So the entire $\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\delta^{mk'}\delta^{m'k}-\delta^{m'k'}\delta^{km}\big)$ term drops out identically. It never contributes to this row.

What is left is two structures. Writing $\mathcal G_{XX'}\equiv X^{m}X'^{m'}\mathcal G^{mm'}$:

$$\frac{1}{k^{+}}\mathcal B^{mm'kk'}\mathcal G^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'} =\underbrace{\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}\,d_{\perp}\,\mathcal G_{XX'}}_{\text{first term}} +\underbrace{\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}\Big)\delta^{mm'}\mathcal G^{mm'}(X\!\cdot\!X')}_{\text{third term}}$$
$$\mathcal C\;=\;\frac{\pi p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}\Big[(\mathcal L-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}}\Big] \;+\;\pi\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}\Big)(\mathcal L-2)\,(X\!\cdot\!X')$$

Note $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}=\frac{p^{+2}+k^{+2}}{p^{+}k^{+3}}$, which is exactly $\frac{1}{k^{+}}\big[\frac{S^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+2}}-\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big]$ — i.e. the $-\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ of the vanishing middle term has already been absorbed, as it must be.

6. The $p^{+}$ integration

Change variable to $\sigma=\frac{S}{k^{+}}=1+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$, so $dp^{+}=k^{+}d\sigma$ and $\sigma\in\big[1+\lambda,\ \sigma_{\vee}\big]$ with $\lambda=\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$, $\sigma_{\vee}=\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}$:

$$\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}S^{2}}=\frac{1}{k^{+2}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma^{2}},\qquad \frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}k^{+}}=\frac{1}{k^{+2}}\Big[(\sigma-1)+\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\Big].$$

The phase is $e^{-i\sigma\kappa}$ with $\kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r$ and $\mathcal L$ does not depend on $\sigma$, so with $\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\cdot\pi\cdot\frac{1}{k^{+2}}=\frac{1}{2k^{+}}$ the whole $p^{+}$ integral reduces to two masters:

$$\int\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\,e^{-i\sigma\kappa}\,\mathcal C=\frac{1}{2k^{+}}\Big[\,A\,J_{1}(\kappa)+B\,J_{2}(\kappa)\,\Big],$$ $$A=(\mathcal L-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}},\qquad B=(\mathcal L-2)(X\!\cdot\!X').$$

$J_{1}$ — convergent, closed form

$$J_{1}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\!d\sigma\,\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma^{2}}e^{-i\sigma\kappa} =\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\Big(\frac1\sigma-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)e^{-i\sigma\kappa}d\sigma =E_{1}(i\kappa)-E_{2}(i\kappa)$$

and with $E_{2}(z)=e^{-z}-zE_{1}(z)$,

$$\boxed{\,J_{1}(\kappa)=(1+i\kappa)\,E_{1}(i\kappa)-e^{-i\kappa}\,}$$

(verified against oscillatory quadrature to 8 digits at $\kappa=0.3,1.0,2.5$). Small $\kappa$: $J_{1}\to-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-1$, so this term carries a $\log\kappa$ but no $\Lambda$.

$J_{2}$ — this is where the rapidity log lives

Substitute $t=\sigma-1$ and use $(\sigma-1)+\frac{1}{\sigma-1}=t+\frac1t$:

$$J_{2}=e^{-i\kappa}\Bigg[\underbrace{\int_{\lambda}^{T}\!t\,e^{-i\kappa t}dt}_{\text{large-}p^{+}} +\underbrace{\int_{\lambda}^{T}\!\frac{e^{-i\kappa t}}{t}dt}_{\text{small-}p^{+}}\Bigg],\qquad T=\sigma_{\vee}-1,$$ $$=e^{-i\kappa}\Bigg[\frac{e^{-i\kappa T}(1+i\kappa T)-e^{-i\kappa\lambda}(1+i\kappa\lambda)}{\kappa^{2}} +E_{1}(i\kappa\lambda)-E_{1}(i\kappa T)\Bigg]$$

(this closed form reproduces direct quadrature to 8 digits). Now take $\lambda\to0$ with $E_{1}(x)\to-\gamma_{E}-\log x$:

$$E_{1}(i\kappa\lambda)\ \longrightarrow\ \log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa) =\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log\kappa-i\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

The $\vee$–dependent pieces ($e^{-i\kappa T}$, $E_{1}(i\kappa T)$) oscillate in $\kappa$ with $T\to\infty$ and are killed by Riemann–Lebesgue once the remaining $\int_{x,x',y,y'}$ are done. What survives is

$$\boxed{\,J_{2}(\kappa)=e^{-i\kappa}\Big[\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\Big]+(\text{oscillating, drops})\,}$$
Why the phase’s $p^{+}$ dependence is not optional. If you keep $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}$ as in (0.2)–(0.3), the $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}$ half of the third term integrates to $\int^{\vee}dp^{+}\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+3}}=\frac{\vee^{2}}{2k^{+3}}$ — a quadratic divergence in the longitudinal cutoff, which cannot be right. Restoring $\sigma$ in the exponent turns it into the bounded oscillating term above. That is the cross–check that the $\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ belongs in the phase and not as a prefactor.

7. The finished result

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{N_{c}}{2k^{+}} \int_{x,x',y,y'}\Big\{J_{1}(\kappa)\,A(r)+J_{2}(\kappa)\,B(r)\Big\}\; \big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$$ $$A=(\mathcal L-1)\,(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}},\qquad B=(\mathcal L-2)\,(X\!\cdot\!X'),\qquad \mathcal L=\frac1\epsilon+2-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big)$$ with $r=y'-y$, $\kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r$, $X=\frac{x-y}{(x-y)^{2}}$, $X'=\frac{x'-y'}{(x'-y')^{2}}$.

Reading off the divergences

  • $\frac1\epsilon$ with coefficient $(X\!\cdot\!X')\big[J_{1}(\kappa)+J_{2}(\kappa)\big]$ — the transverse UV of the $z$ integral, to be removed by the $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterm / charge renormalisation.
  • $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ from $J_{2}$, with coefficient $e^{-i\kappa}(\mathcal L-2)(X\!\cdot\!X')$ — the rapidity divergence, absorbed into the JIMWLK evolution of $\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]$.
  • $\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ — the same mixed UV$\times$rapidity term this row produces as the running–coupling row did. It cannot stand alone; check it against the companion rows of Group II in the same scheme.
  • No collinear pole. The would–be $r\to0$ singularity is only $\log r^{2}$, which is what $\mathcal L$ carries; $J_{1},J_{2}$ contribute $\log\kappa=\log(k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r)$, so the $\mu$ dependence combines into $\log\big(\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}/k_{\perp}^{2}\big)$ once the $r$ integral is done — no extra $\frac1\epsilon$.
