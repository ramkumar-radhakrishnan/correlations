Row 8, rebuilt from scratch

Is there any other UV divergence? — and the result in $\overline{\rm MS}$

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $r\equiv y'-y$,  $\kappa\equiv\dfrac{k\cdot r}{k^{+}}$,  $P_{\vee}\equiv\vee-k^{+}$,  $d=2-2\epsilon$

Answer. No — there is no other UV divergence. Redone from scratch: the tensor contraction by explicit index sums in general $d$ (§1), all $26$ collapsing regions with the Wilson lines actually present (§2), and the large–distance end of every variable done analytically with an exact Bessel identity (§3). The only divergence in this row is the one already found: the logarithmic infrared end of the $z$ integral. But there is a trap, and it is exactly the one your question walks into: the standard way of doing that $z$ integral does generate $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$ poles in intermediate steps, which cancel (§4). If you $\overline{\rm MS}$–subtract the surviving $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ you are subtracting an infrared pole. §5 gives the honest $\overline{\rm MS}$ form, and it corrects the scheme constant I quoted last time.

3. The large–distance end, one variable at a time

This is where the previous write–up was thin, so here it is analytically. For a variable swept to $|p|=R$ the radial integral converges iff the angular average $A(R)$ times $R^{2}$ falls with $R$. The exact 2D identity (verified numerically to 7 digits, $x=10\ldots10^{5}$):

$$\Big\langle\,\hat n^{m}\hat n^{k}\,e^{i x\,\hat n\cdot\hat k}\,\Big\rangle_{\theta} =\frac{J_{1}(x)}{x}\,\delta^{mk}+\Big(J_{0}(x)-\frac{2J_{1}(x)}{x}\Big)\hat k^{m}\hat k^{k}.$$

For $x$ the cancellation is worth writing out, because it is not obvious:

$$\frac{(x-y)^{k}}{(x-y)^{2}}=\frac{\hat n^{k}}{R}+\frac{2\hat n^{k}(\hat n\cdot y)-y^{k}}{R^{2}}+O(R^{-3}), \qquad\langle\hat n^{k}\rangle=0,\quad\Big\langle 2\hat n^{k}(\hat n\cdot y)-y^{k}\Big\rangle=2\cdot\frac{y^{k}}{2}-y^{k}=0 .$$

Numerically, $z$ flatlines at $|A(R)|R^{2}=92.9151$ from $R=10^{2}$ to $10^{5}$ — six digits, no drift. Everything else falls.

So the mechanism is sharp: $y$ and $y'$ are saved by the phase, $x$ and $x'$ by the vanishing of the first two angular moments of a single WW kernel. $z$ has neither — it is the one variable the phase never touches, and the colour reduction $U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$ removed the last $U(z)$ that could have cut it off. The entire divergence of this row is that one factor of $\tfrac12$ in $\langle\hat n^{m}\hat n^{m'}\rangle$.

4. The trap: where $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$ does appear

First, the direct evidence that the physical integrand has no UV. Excise discs of radius $a$ around $y$ and $y'$ from the trace integral (outer radius fixed at $R=400$):

$a$$10^{-1}$$10^{-2}$ $10^{-3}$$10^{-4}$$10^{-5}$
trace integral36.513236.512536.5160 36.465436.4654

Total drift over four decades: $0.05$, which is grid noise. A logarithmic UV divergence would have moved it by $\pi\log(10^{4})^{2}\approx58$. Growing the outer radius instead ($a=10^{-6}$) tracks $\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}$ to $2\times10^{-2}$ at $R=50\ldots3200$. The divergence is infrared, full stop.

Now the trap. The trace is almost always evaluated by writing $u\cdot v=\frac12(u^{2}+v^{2}-r^{2})$:

$$\int d^{d}z\,\frac{u\cdot v}{u^{2}v^{2}} =\frac12\int\frac{d^{d}z}{v^{2}}+\frac12\int\frac{d^{d}z}{u^{2}}-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\,G(1,1), \qquad G(1,1)=\int\frac{d^{d}z}{u^{2}v^{2}} .$$
Both pieces on the right are UV divergent.
  • The tadpole $\int\frac{d^{d}z}{u^{2}}$ is scaleless. It is set to zero, but only because $$\int\frac{d^{d}z}{(z-y)^{2}}=\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}\Big],$$ i.e. a genuine short–distance divergence at $z\to y$ cancelled against a genuine large–distance one. It is not finite; it is two divergences set equal.
  • The bubble $G(1,1)$ goes as $\rho^{d-3}d\rho$ at $z\to y$, so it is logarithmically UV divergent at $d=2$. Its $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$.

The spurious $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$'s cancel between the tadpoles and the bubble, and the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ that survives is $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}$ — which is what the numbers above measure directly. If you hand that $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ to an $\overline{\rm MS}$ subtraction as though it were ultraviolet, you have absorbed an infrared divergence into your coupling. It must cancel against the companion rows instead.

5. $\overline{\rm MS}$

With the standard $\overline{\rm MS}$ factor riding on $g^{2}\mu^{2\epsilon}$, and $C(\epsilon)=\pi^{1-\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)} =\pi\,e^{-\epsilon(\log\pi+\gamma_{E}-2)}$:

$$\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\Big)^{\epsilon}\,C(\epsilon)\,\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon}\,\frac{1}{\epsilon} \;=\;\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+2-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2}\big)\Big] \;\equiv\;\pi\big[\mathcal L+2\big],\qquad \boxed{\;\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2}\big).\;}$$
This corrects the constant I gave you last time. I wrote $\bar\mu^{2}=\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}$, which is a position–space convention, not $\overline{\rm MS}$. Proper $\overline{\rm MS}$ gives $4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}$; the two differ by $\log\frac{4\pi}{e^{\gamma_{E}}}$, precisely the $\overline{\rm MS}$ factor. The $4\pi^{2}$ rather than a bare $\mu^{2}$ is there because $\int d^{d}z$ carries no $(2\pi)^{-d}$ — if your convention puts one there, drop it accordingly. Everything structural below is unchanged.

6.1 What $\mu$ means here

Matching to the cutoff form $\frac{\pi}{2}\big(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\big)$ gives

$$R=\frac{e^{1/2}}{2\pi\mu}.$$
$R$ is the outer radius of the $z$ integration. So $\mu$ here is an infrared scale, not a renormalisation scale — large $\mu$ means a small $z$–plane. Running the coupling with it, or absorbing $\mathcal L$ into $\alpha_{s}(\mu)$, would be wrong. The $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ has to cancel against the rows where a $U(z)$ survives to cut the same integral off at the target size.

6.2 Two things unchanged, one changed

  • Unchanged: no UV anywhere; the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ term cancels identically; the pole is IR and rides on the LO structure; the $+$ prescription on $\mathcal I_{1}$ is legitimate because the $z$ integral was made finite first and the $p^{+}\to0$ subtraction keeps the LO phase $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}$.
  • Changed: the scheme constant inside the log, $\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}\to4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}$ (§5), and the $d_{\perp}$ scheme is now pinned to CDR, giving $(\mathcal L+1)$ rather than $(\mathcal L+2)$ (§5.1).
  • Caveat worth checking separately: if you then take an MV/Gaussian average $\langle\rho^{a}(x)\rho^{b}(x')\rangle\propto\delta^{ab}\delta^{(2)}(x-x')$, the $x=x'$ identification turns $\mathcal X$ into the dipole kernel $\mathcal K(y,y';x)$, and the $x$ integral then has the same unprotected structure the $z$ integral had — a second infrared log. That is a statement about the averaging, not about this row, but it will double the pole.
