Assembling the row from $\mathfrak A$ and $\mathfrak C$

Does the quoted result follow from the definitions?

$K^{+}\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$ — the first momentum argument of $\mathfrak C$

0. Verdict

Yes. All three steps are right — the assembly, the $w'$ integration, and the $+$ prescription. Every factor was checked symbolically:

  • numeric prefactor $\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}$ — exact;
  • overall $-i$ — exact;
  • $\frac{1}{k^{+2}}$ — exact, and it arises in a way worth noticing (§2);
  • the index bracket $\big[\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}-\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{p^{+}}\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'} -\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{k^{+}}\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}\big]$ — exact, all 16 index assignments;
  • all four Weizsäcker–Williams kernels and both colour brackets — exact.

1. The coefficient functions

From $-\mathfrak A^{a}_{i}(k^{+},\mathbf z)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2\pi}\frac{ig}{\sqrt{k^{+}}} \int_{x}\frac{(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)^{i}}{(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)^{2}}\rho^{a}(\mathbf x)$,

$$\mathfrak A\big(K^{+}\big)=\frac{ig}{\sqrt2\,\pi\sqrt{K^{+}}},\qquad \mathfrak A^{\dagger}\big(K^{+}\big)=\frac{-ig}{\sqrt2\,\pi\sqrt{K^{+}}},$$

and from the definition of $\mathfrak C$, evaluated at $\bar{\mathfrak C}^{\dagger d'ac'}_{1jik'}\big(p^{+}{+}k^{+},\,y'{-}w';\,p^{+},\,w'{-}z\big)$,

$$\mathfrak C\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{g\,f^{c'd'a}}{2\pi\sqrt{2K^{+}}}\; \frac{\sqrt{p^{+}(K^{+}-p^{+})}}{K^{+}} =-\frac{g\,f^{c'd'a}}{2\pi\sqrt{2K^{+}}}\;\frac{\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}.$$

There are three $\mathfrak A$'s and one $\mathfrak C$, so $g^{4}$ and, for the phases, $(-i)\times(-1)\times(i)\times(i)=-i$. Multiplying the four numeric factors and the $\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}$ out front:

$$\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\sqrt2\,\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt2\,\pi} =\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{8\pi^{4}} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}\quad\checkmark$$

The $k^{+}$ powers

The $\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}$ carried by $\mathfrak C$ cancels the $\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{+}}}$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{+}}}$ of the two $\mathfrak A$'s exactly, leaving

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{+}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{+}}}\cdot\frac{1}{K^{+}}\cdot\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}} \cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{+}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{+}}}=\frac{1}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}.$$

2. Where the $\frac{1}{k^{+2}}$ comes from — the delta Jacobian

This is the one step that is easy to lose. $\mathfrak C$'s delta is $\delta^{(2)}\!\big(z_{\rm arg}+\frac{p^{+}}{K^{+}}x_{\rm arg}\big)$ with $z_{\rm arg}=y'-w'$ and $x_{\rm arg}=w'-z$, i.e.

$$\delta^{(2)}\!\Big(y'-w'+\tfrac{p^{+}}{K^{+}}(w'-z)\Big) =\delta^{(2)}\!\Big(-\tfrac{k^{+}}{K^{+}}\Big[w'-\tfrac{(p^{+}+k^{+})y'-p^{+}z}{k^{+}}\Big]\Big) =\Big(\tfrac{K^{+}}{k^{+}}\Big)^{2}\, \delta^{(2)}\!\Big[w'-\tfrac{(p^{+}+k^{+})y'-p^{+}z}{k^{+}}\Big].$$

The root is the quoted one, but rewriting it in that form costs $\big(\frac{K^{+}}{k^{+}}\big)^{2}$ in two dimensions — and that is exactly what turns $\frac{1}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}$ into $\frac{1}{k^{+2}}$:

$$\frac{1}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}\times\frac{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}{k^{+2}}=\frac{1}{k^{+2}}\quad\checkmark$$

Had the Jacobian been dropped, the quoted prefactor would have read $\frac{1}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}$ instead. It reads $\frac{1}{k^{+2}}$, so it was included correctly.

3. The index bracket

The definition is $\mathfrak C_{1jki}$ with $\big[\delta_{im}\delta_{jk}-\frac{K^{+}}{p^{+}}\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik} -\frac{K^{+}}{K^{+}-p^{+}}\delta_{km}\delta_{ij}\big]$, and the object needed is $\mathfrak C_{1jik'}$ — i.e. the relabelling $j\to j$, $k\to i$, $i\to k'$. Under it

$$\delta_{im}\delta_{jk}\to\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij},\qquad \delta_{jm}\delta_{ik}\to\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'},\qquad \delta_{km}\delta_{ij}\to\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'},$$

and with $K^{+}=p^{+}+k^{+}$ so that $K^{+}-p^{+}=k^{+}$,

$$\Big[\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}-\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{p^{+}}\,\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'} -\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{k^{+}}\,\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}\Big]\quad\checkmark$$

Checked over all 16 index assignments: zero mismatches.

5. The two later steps

The $w'$ integration. Verified previously: the delta sets $w'=y'+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}(y'-z)$, so $w'-z=\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{k^{+}}(y'-z)$, the first kernel supplies $\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$, the phase splits as $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}e^{-i\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}k\cdot(y'-z)}$, and pulling $(p^{+}+k^{+})$ out of the bracket leaves $\frac{1}{k^{+}}\big[\frac{\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}}{p^{+}+k^{+}} -\frac{\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}}{p^{+}}-\frac{\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}}{k^{+}}\big]$. Correct.

The $+$ prescription. Verified previously: sign, prefactor $\frac{1}{8\pi^{5}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}$, the $\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}$ contraction $(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!(x{-}z)\,(x'{-}y')\!\cdot\!(y{-}w)$, the dropped phase, and keeping the lower limit at $\Lambda$, which makes the rearrangement exact. Correct.

Two things not derived from the definitions, because they cannot be — they are inputs: the limits $\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}dp^{+}$ (the rapidity cutoffs you impose), and the convention that $\mathfrak A(K^{+},-\mathbf z)$ carries the kernel $\frac{(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)^{i}}{(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)^{2}}$ rather than $\frac{(\mathbf x+\mathbf z)^{i}}{(\mathbf x+\mathbf z)^{2}}$. Both are consistent throughout.

And the standing caveat: the first term of the last equation is the soft–subtracted remainder, not a finite contribution — it still diverges at large $|y'-z|$.
