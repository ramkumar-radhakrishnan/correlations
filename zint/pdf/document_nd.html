The second row

Same transverse structure, new colour — does that change the answer?

$K^{+}\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $\xi\equiv p^{+}/k^{+}$

0. Verdict

Both quoted equations are correct, by exactly the argument verified for the previous row — nothing in the derivation changes, because the ket bracket differs only in its colour structure.

The transverse situation is also unchanged: no ultraviolet divergence, but the $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ term's $z$–integral is again the BK real–emission kernel, falling as $1/z^{2}$. So the $+$ prescription is again an exact identity you may write, and again it does not produce two separately finite pieces.

The new colour structure does not rescue it (§3) — though it does buy you something else, at $w\to y$ (§4).

1. Why the derivation is unchanged

Compare the two ket brackets:

$$\text{previous:}\quad U^{bc}(z)\,\bar{\mathfrak A}^{a}_{i}(k^{+},-w)\,\bar{\mathfrak A}^{c}_{j}(p^{+},-z) \;-\;\bar{\mathfrak A}^{a}_{i}(k^{+},-w)\,\mathfrak A^{b}_{j}(p^{+},-z)$$ $$\text{this row:}\quad U^{bc}(z)\,\bar{\mathfrak A}^{c}_{j}(p^{+},-z)\,\bar{\mathfrak A}^{a}_{i}(k^{+},-w) \;-\;U^{ac}(w)\,\bar{\mathfrak A}^{d}_{j}(p^{+},-z)\,U^{db}(z)\,\mathfrak A^{c}_{i}(k^{+},-w)$$

Each term still contains exactly one $\mathfrak A(p^{+},-z)$ and one $\mathfrak A(k^{+},-w)$, at the same momenta and the same positions. The bra bracket and $\bar{\mathfrak C}^{\dagger}$ are untouched. So the entire assembly — three $\mathfrak A$'s and one $\mathfrak C$, giving $g^{4}$ and $-i$; the numeric factor $\frac{1}{4\pi^{4}}$; the cancellation of $\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}$ leaving $\frac{1}{(p^{+}+k^{+})^{2}}$; the delta Jacobian $\big(\frac{K^{+}}{k^{+}}\big)^{2}$ turning that into $\frac{1}{k^{+2}}$; and the index bracket $\big[\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}-\frac{K^{+}}{p^{+}}\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'} -\frac{K^{+}}{k^{+}}\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}\big]$ — carries over verbatim. Likewise the $w'$ integration.

Two typos in the measures. Both displayed equations integrate over $\int_{w,w',y',z,y,x}$ and $\int_{w,y',z,y,x}$, but $x'$ appears in the integrand, in $\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$ and $\rho^{e'}(x')$. It should be $\int_{w,w',y',z,y,x,x'}$ and $\int_{w,y',z,y,x,x'}$. (The same slip is in the previous row's first equation.)

2. Transverse divergences: identical to before

The four kernels are the same four kernels,

$$\frac{(y'-z)^{m}}{(y'-z)^{2}}\;\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}\; \frac{(y-w)^{i}}{(y-w)^{2}}\;\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{(x-z)^{2}},$$

so every statement about the transverse integrations carries over:

  • No ultraviolet divergence. Each point is a coincidence of exactly one kernel, so the integrand is $O(1/\rho)$ there, and the leading piece integrates to zero by parity. The collapses of three and four points converge too, by measure counting.
  • The $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ structure is the problem. $\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}$ contracts the kernels into $\frac{(y'-z)\cdot(x-z)}{(y'-z)^{2}(x-z)^{2}}$ — the BK real–emission kernel, falling only as $1/z^{2}$. For $p^{+}\neq0$ the phase $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}$ cuts it at $|z|\sim k^{+}/(p^{+}|k|)$; at $p^{+}=0$, where the subtraction sits, nothing does.

3. Does the new colour structure save the large–$|z|$ region?

This is the one thing genuinely worth re–checking, since a colour factor that vanished as $|z|\to\infty$ would kill the logarithm. It does not. Taking $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ (adjoint identity) and evaluating with random adjoint Wilson lines:

The divergence is exactly as before, and so is the remedy: the virtual diagrams, which replace $-2\mathcal K$ by $\mathcal M=\frac{(x-y')^{2}}{(x-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}$ and $\rho^{-2}$ by $\rho^{-4}$.

4. What the new colour structure does buy

Notice what the limit is: a difference of Wilson lines at $y$ and $w$. The previous row's ket bracket gave a difference in $x$ instead. This one therefore vanishes as $U(w)\to U(y)$ — linearly, as checked with $U(w)=U(y)e^{\epsilon X}$, $X$ antisymmetric:

   |U(w)-U(y)|_max = 3.375e-01   max|bracket| = 5.5751e-01
   |U(w)-U(y)|_max = 2.870e-02   max|bracket| = 5.5111e-02
   |U(w)-U(y)|_max = 2.803e-03   max|bracket| = 5.5770e-03
   |U(w)-U(y)|_max = 2.797e-04   max|bracket| = 5.5835e-04

That is a real–virtual structure in the $(y,w)$ pair, and it softens precisely the coincidence where the kernel $\frac{(y-w)^{i}}{(y-w)^{2}}$ is singular. It is the analogue, in the $w$ integration, of what the virtual diagrams must still do for the $z$ integration. Useful — but it says nothing about $z$.

5. So, on the $+$ prescription

Yes, you can write the same $+$ prescription, in exactly the same form:

$$\frac{1}{p^{+}}\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\right]_{+} \;+\;\delta\text{-part}\times\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda},$$

with the second term carrying the $\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}$ contraction $(y'{-}z)\!\cdot\!(x{-}z)\,(x'{-}y')\!\cdot\!(y{-}w)$, no $\xi$–phase, prefactor $\frac{1}{8\pi^{5}}$ and a $+$ sign, and this row's colour brackets. Keeping the lower limit at $\Lambda$ makes it exact.

And the same caveat, unchanged. Neither resulting term is finite on its own: both diverge logarithmically at large $|y'-z|$, with equal and opposite coefficients. So write it if you want the rapidity logarithm displayed, but call the first term the soft–subtracted remainder, not a finite contribution — and do not absorb the second into the evolution until the virtual diagrams are in.
