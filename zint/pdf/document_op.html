The $2a\,/\,2b$ split: yes, and it does exactly what you want

The identity checks out, the UV lives entirely in $2b$ — but the infrared log is shared

$\eta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $q=k^{+}\!-p^{+}$,  $\mathcal C_{2a}^{af}=-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)$,  $\mathcal C_{2b}^{af}=N_{c}\big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\big]$,  $C_{\rm UV}(\eta)=\dfrac{\mathcal P_{gg}(\eta)}{2N_{c}}$

Yes, this is correct, and it is the right split — it repairs exactly the error I flagged last time. $\mathcal C_{2a}+\mathcal C_{2b}$ reproduces the original colour structure identically (max deviation $2.2\times10^{-15}$), the index reshuffle $f^{ebf}=-f^{fbe}$ that makes it work is exact, and $\mathcal C_{2a}$ vanishes when $U(z)=U(x)$ — so $2b$ is the collapsed piece and $2a$ is the genuine three-Wilson-line remainder. The UV at $z\to x$ sits entirely in $2b$; $2a$ is UV finite. One thing to watch: the large-$|z|$ infrared logarithm is shared between $2a$ and $2b$, because $\mathcal C_{2a}\not\to0$ when $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$. Plus three placement slips in the LaTeX (§5).

1. The split is an exact identity

The original bracket was $f^{adc}f^{ebf}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)-f^{abc}f^{cbd}U^{df}(y)$. Your two pieces sum to it:

$$\underbrace{-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)}_{2a} \;+\;\underbrace{N_{c}U^{af}(y)-N_{c}U^{af}(x)}_{2b} \;=\;f^{adc}f^{ebf}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(y).$$

Two identities do the work, both verified with genuine adjoint $SU(3)$ lines $U^{ab}=2\,{\rm tr}(t^{a}Vt^{b}V^{\dagger})$:

  • $f^{ebf}=-f^{fbe}$ (swap of the outer indices) — deviation exactly $0$. This is what turns your $-f^{adc}f^{fbe}$ into the original's $+f^{adc}f^{ebf}$.
  • $-f^{abc}f^{cbd}U^{df}(y)=+N_{c}U^{af}(y)$ — already checked, $10^{-15}$.

Over 40 random configurations: $\max|\mathcal C_{2a}+\mathcal C_{2b}-\mathcal C_{\rm orig}| =2.2\times10^{-15}$ against $\max|\mathcal C_{\rm orig}|=3.69$. Nothing is lost and nothing is added.

2. $2a$ vanishes at $U(z)=U(x)$ — and there is a cleaner way to write it

$\|\mathcal C_{2a}\|$ with $U(z)=U(x)$ is $1.1\times10^{-15}$; with $U(z)$ free it is $0.73$. So $2b$ is precisely the piece that the (illegitimate) collapse would have left, and $2a$ is precisely what the collapse threw away. That is the right way to organise this.

$$-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x) \;=\;-f^{adc}f^{fbe}\,U^{db}(x)\,\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]$$

(max deviation $2.2\times10^{-15}$ over 40 configurations.) I would write $2a$ this way. The $\big[U(z)-U(x)\big]$ makes the UV finiteness of §3 visible without any computation, and it makes the physics obvious: $2a$ only knows about the difference between the gluon's Wilson line and the source's.

3. The UV is entirely in $2b$

$u^{2}\times\big\langle\,\text{transverse}\times\text{colour}\,\big\rangle_{\hat u}$ as $u=z-x\to0$; a constant means log divergent, a vanishing entry means finite. Angular average by exact quadrature, with $U(z)$ a smooth adjoint field so that $U(z)\to U(x)$ properly:

$2a$'s residue falls as $|z-x|^{2}$, two powers better than it had to. Two reasons stack:

  1. $\mathcal C_{2a}=O(|z-x|)$, since $U(z)-U(x)=(z-x)\!\cdot\!\partial U+\dots$ (checked: $\|\mathcal C_{2a}\|/|z-x|$ is flat at $\approx1.7$ from $|z-x|=10^{-1}$ down to $10^{-4}$). One power gone.
  2. That linear piece is odd in $\hat u$, while the UV residue of the transverse factor is even (it came from $\frac{u^{m}u^{n}}{u^{4}}$ structures). The angular average kills the product. A second power gone.

So the whole UV logarithm of the row is $2b$, and since $\mathcal C_{2b}$ is $z$-independent, the residue computed before carries over unchanged:

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\!\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\; \frac{\mathcal P_{gg}\!\big(\tfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\big)}{2N_{c}} \!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\frac{(x'-w')\!\cdot\!(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}} \bigg[\int_{\rm UV}\!\frac{d^{2}z}{(z-x)^{2}}\bigg] \big[U(x')-U(w')\big]N_{c}\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]\rho\rho$$

$\mathcal P_{gg}$ times the two LO Weizsäcker–Williams kernels times an LO-like colour structure: this is the piece that renormalises, and it is now cleanly isolated. That is the whole point of the split and it works.

4. But the infrared logarithm is shared

At large $|z|$ the gluon's Wilson line goes to the identity, not to $U(x)$, and
$$\mathcal C_{2a}\Big|_{U(z)\to\mathbb 1}=-f^{adc}f^{fbc}U^{db}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\;\neq\;0 \qquad(\|\cdot\|=1.29,\ \text{against } N_{c}\|U\|=2.43).$$

So $2a$ keeps a large-$|z|$ logarithm too. Numerically, $|z|^{2}\times\langle\cdot\rangle$ (constant means log divergent):

Both are non-zero constants. The practical consequence:

The split is clean for the UV and not for the IR. Treat them differently:
  • UV: subtract and renormalise on $2b$ alone. $2a$ needs nothing.
  • IR: the cancellation against the $\bar{\mathfrak B}_{2}$ (virtual) term of eq. (1) has to be done on $2a$ and $2b$ together. Do not declare $2a$ finite and set it aside — it is UV finite, not finite.
Since $\mathcal C_{2a}$ and $\mathcal C_{2b}$ have different colour structures at large $|z|$, the two IR pieces will pair with different pieces of $\bar{\mathfrak B}_{2}$. That is worth checking explicitly before you rely on the cancellation.

5. Three placement slips in the LaTeX (both equations)

  1. The phases are outside the integrals they depend on. $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)p^{+}/k^{+}}$ sits before the $\Bigg\{$, i.e. outside both $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}dp^{+}$ and $\int_{x,y,z,x'}$ — but it depends on $z$, $x$ and $p^{+}$. Move it inside, next to the transverse integrand.
  2. The colour structure and the sources are outside the transverse integral. The first $\Bigg\}$ closes right after the tensor bracket, leaving $\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\big[\cdots\big] \rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)$ outside $\int_{x,y,z,x'}$ — but they depend on $x,y,z,x'$. That closing brace belongs at the very end.
  3. Unbalanced braces. One $\Bigg\{$ is opened and two $\Bigg\}$ are closed. Fixing (2) fixes this too.

What you did fix since last time, and correctly: $\int_{w,w'}\to\int_{w'}$ in both equations. Still outstanding from before: the $U^{b'a}(w')$ versus $U^{ab'}(w')$ index order in the first bracket, and defining $\bar{\mathfrak A}$, $\bar{\mathfrak B}$, $\bar{\mathfrak C}$ — the overall sign of the row still rests on them, since the vertex product alone gives a minus.
