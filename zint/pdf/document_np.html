The $z$ integral of two WW kernels

Where it diverges, how to regulate it, the transverse logarithm, and the splitting function

$r\equiv y'-y$,  $\hat r=r/|r|$,  $d=2-2\epsilon$,  $\zeta\equiv\dfrac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$,  $\bar\zeta=1-\zeta$

The object. $$T^{mm'}(y,y')=\int d^{2}z\;\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}}\;\frac{(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y'-z)^{2}}$$

The answer. It has no ultraviolet divergence; what diverges is the infrared end, $|z|\to\infty$, logarithmically. With a disc cutoff $$T^{mm'}=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\Big)\delta^{mm'}-\pi\,\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'},$$ and in $d=2-2\epsilon$ with $\epsilon>0$ $$T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}=C(\epsilon)\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon} \Big[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\Big].$$

Because $r=y'-y$ is conjugate to the measured momentum, the resulting logarithm $\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2})$ is the collinear one, and in the two–$\mathfrak C$ row its coefficient comes out as exactly $P_{gg}(\zeta)/2N_{c}$.

2. Two master integrals

Write $z=z^{1}+iz^{2}$. For a 2D vector $a$ the combination $a^{1}-ia^{2}$ is $\bar a$, so

$$\frac{a^{m}}{a^{2}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \frac{\bar a}{|a|^{2}}=\frac{1}{a}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\bar a}.$$

Everything reduces to two integrals over the disc $|z|<R$.

2.1 The holomorphic one

$$\boxed{\ \int_{|z|<R}\frac{d^{2}z}{z-y}=-\pi\bar y\ }\qquad(|y|<R)$$

Proof. Split the disc at $|z|=|y|$. For $|z|>|y|$ expand $\frac{1}{z-y}=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{y^{n}}{z^{n+1}}$; every term has a nonzero power of $e^{i\theta}$ and its angular integral vanishes. For $|z|<|y|$ expand $\frac{1}{z-y}=-\frac{1}{y}\sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac zy\right)^{n}$; only $n=0$ survives the angular integral, leaving $-\frac{1}{y}\cdot\pi|y|^{2}=-\pi\bar y$. $\square$  (numeric $-2.51292-1.57113i$ vs exact $-2.51327-1.57080i$)

2.2 The mixed one

$$\boxed{\ L(a,b)=\int_{|z|<R}\frac{d^{2}z}{(\bar z-\bar a)(z-b)}=\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{|a-b|^{2}}\ }$$

Proof. Use $\frac{1}{\bar z-\bar a}=\partial_{\bar z}\log|z-a|^{2}$ and $\partial_{\bar z}\frac{1}{z-b}=\pi\delta^{(2)}(z-b)$, then integrate by parts:

$$L=\underbrace{\oint_{|z|=R}\log|z-a|^{2}\,\frac{dz}{2i(z-b)}}_{\to\ \pi\log R^{2}} \;-\;\pi\log|a-b|^{2}. \qquad\square$$

($R=20$: numeric $16.040$ vs exact $16.020$; $R=50$: $21.794$ vs $21.778$.)

3. The two contractions

Set $u=y-z$, $v=y'-z$, so $u-v=r$.

3.1 The trace

$$T^{11}+T^{22}=\mathrm{Re}\int d^{2}z\,\frac{1}{u\,\bar v} =\mathrm{Re}\int\frac{d^{2}z}{(z-y)(\bar z-\bar y')}=L(y',y)=\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}.$$

3.2 The traceless part

$$T^{11}-T^{22}-i\big(T^{12}+T^{21}\big)=\int d^{2}z\,\frac{1}{u\,v} =\int\frac{d^{2}z}{(z-y)(z-y')} =\frac{1}{y-y'}\int d^{2}z\left[\frac{1}{z-y}-\frac{1}{z-y'}\right],$$

and §2.1 gives

$$=\frac{-\pi\bar y+\pi\bar y'}{y-y'}=-\pi\,\frac{\bar r}{r}=-\pi e^{-2i\phi},\qquad r=|r|e^{i\phi}.$$

(numeric $0.5700-3.0851i$ vs exact $0.5665-3.0901i$.)

3.3 Solve

Parity forbids an $\epsilon^{mm'}$ piece, and $T$ is symmetric (swapping $m\leftrightarrow m'$ is $y\leftrightarrow y'$, i.e. $r\to-r$, which both structures are even under), so

$$T^{mm'}=A\,\delta^{mm'}+B\,\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}.$$

Matching: $\hat r^{1}\hat r^{1}-\hat r^{2}\hat r^{2}=\cos2\phi$ and $2\hat r^{1}\hat r^{2}=\sin2\phi$, so §3.2 gives $B=-\pi$; §3.1 gives $2A+B=\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}$. Hence

$$T^{mm'}=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\Big)\delta^{mm'}-\pi\,\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}$$

4. Dimensional regularisation

Take $d=2-2\epsilon$ with $\epsilon>0$: that is what damps the large–$|z|$ end. Feynman–parametrise, shift $z=z'+xy+\bar xy'$ so that $xu^{2}+\bar xv^{2}=z'^{2}+\Delta$ with $\Delta=x\bar x\,r^{2}$, and note $u=-z'+\bar xr$, $v=-z'-xr$:

$$u^{m}v^{m'}\ \to\ z'^{m}z'^{m'}-x\bar x\,r^{m}r^{m'}\ \to\ \frac{\delta^{mm'}}{d}z'^{2}-x\bar x\,r^{m}r^{m'}.$$

With the two standard Euclidean integrals

$$\int\frac{d^{d}l}{[l^{2}+\Delta]^{2}}=\pi^{d/2}\Gamma\big(2-\tfrac d2\big)\Delta^{\frac d2-2},\qquad \int\frac{d^{d}l\;l^{2}}{[l^{2}+\Delta]^{2}}=\pi^{d/2}\frac{d}{2}\Gamma\big(1-\tfrac d2\big)\Delta^{\frac d2-1},$$

both $x$ integrals reduce to the same Euler beta, $\int_{0}^{1}(x\bar x)^{-\epsilon}dx=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)}$, and

$$T^{mm'}_{\epsilon}=C(\epsilon)\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon} \left[\frac{\delta^{mm'}}{2\epsilon}-\hat r^{m}\hat r^{m'}\right],\qquad C(\epsilon)=\pi^{1-\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)} =\pi\,e^{-\epsilon(\log\pi+\gamma_{E}-2)} .$$

Cross-check on the trace. Contract with $\delta^{mm}=d$ and compare with the independent route through the massless bubble: write $u\cdot v=\frac12(u^{2}+v^{2}-r^{2})$, drop the two scaleless tadpoles, and use $\int\frac{d^{d}z}{(z-y)^{2a}(z-y')^{2b}}=\pi^{d/2}\frac{\Gamma(a+b-\frac d2)\Gamma(\frac d2-a)\Gamma(\frac d2-b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(d-a-b)}(r^{2})^{\frac d2-a-b}$. The two agree identically (symbolic difference $0$).

The pole is infrared. $\epsilon>0$ means $d<2$, which converges at large $|z|$ — §1. Be careful here: the bubble route does produce $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$ poles in intermediate steps (the scaleless tadpole is $\pi[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}]$, zero only because two divergences are set equal, and $G(1,1)$ is genuinely log UV divergent at $z\to y$). They cancel between the two. The surviving pole is $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}$, so it is absorbed into the gluon distribution, not into $\alpha_{s}$.

5. The transverse logarithm

Attach the $\overline{\rm MS}$ factor $\big(\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\big)^{\epsilon}$ that rides on $g^{2}\mu^{2\epsilon}$:

$$\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\Big)^{\epsilon}C(\epsilon)\big(r^{2}\mu^{2}\big)^{-\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon} =\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+2-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2}\big)\Big]\equiv\pi\big[\mathcal L+2\big],$$
$$\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2}\big),\qquad r=y'-y.$$

Matching against the cutoff form of §3.3 fixes the dictionary, $\frac{\pi}{2}(\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}+1)=\frac{\pi}{2}[\mathcal L+2]$, i.e.

$$R=\frac{e^{1/2}}{2\pi\mu}.$$
Why this is the collinear logarithm. In the row, the surviving phase is $e^{-ik\cdot r}$ — $r=y'-y$ is conjugate to the measured transverse momentum. So $\mathcal L$ is the logarithm between the factorisation scale $\mu$ and the hard transverse scale $r^{-1}$, which the remaining $y,y'$ Fourier transform ties to $k_{\perp}$. A splitting function times a logarithm of that ratio is exactly the DGLAP structure. ($R$ is the outer radius, so $\mu$ here is a factorisation scale, not a renormalisation scale — do not run $\alpha_{s}$ with it.)

6. Feeding it back: the $2/k^{+}$ term dies

In the two–$\mathfrak C$ row the two vertices contract to three structures. Writing them in the splitting variable $\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{S}$, $S=p^{+}+k^{+}$ — the $\mathfrak C$ vertex splits one gluon of $S$ into the measured $k^{+}$ and the unobserved $p^{+}$, so $a=\frac{S}{p^{+}}=\frac{1}{\bar\zeta}$ and $b=\frac{S}{k^{+}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}$ —

$$c_{1}=d_{\perp}-\frac{2}{\bar\zeta}-\frac{2}{\zeta}\ \ (\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}),\qquad c_{2}=\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar\zeta^{2}}\ \ (\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}),\qquad c_{3}=\frac{2}{\zeta\bar\zeta}\ \ (\delta_{km'}\delta_{k'm}).$$
Because $T^{mm'}$ is symmetric (§3.3), $\delta_{km'}\delta_{k'm}$ and $\delta_{km}\delta_{k'm'}$ give the same contraction, so only $c_{1}+c_{3}$ acts: $$c_{1}+c_{3}=d_{\perp}+\frac{2}{\zeta\bar\zeta}\big[1-\zeta-\bar\zeta\big]=d_{\perp}.$$

The $\frac{2}{k^{+}}$ term of the bracket cancels, and in this variable the reason is plain: it is the momentum sum rule $\zeta+\bar\zeta=1$.

Keeping $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ consistently, so that $\frac{d_{\perp}}{2\epsilon}=\frac1\epsilon-1$ and $\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,T}{C(\epsilon)(r^{2}\mu^{2})^{-\epsilon}}=\frac{d}{2\epsilon}-1=\frac1\epsilon-2$, and using $\frac{C(\epsilon)(\cdots)^{-\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\to\pi[\mathcal L+2]$ while $C(\epsilon)(\cdots)^{-\epsilon}\to\pi$, the whole $z$–integrated structure collapses to

$$\frac{\pi}{k^{+2}}\Big\{\,C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X \;+\;\zeta\bar\zeta\,\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\Big\}$$

$\mathcal X=\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x'-y')}{(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}$,   $\mathcal R=\frac{[r\cdot(x-y)][r\cdot(x'-y')]}{r^{2}(x-y)^{2}(x'-y')^{2}}$. Symbolic difference from the direct $\epsilon$–expansion: exactly $0$. The $\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}$ that appears on the way is absorbed by $S^{2}=k^{+2}/\zeta^{2}$.

7. That coefficient is the splitting function

$$C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta =\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta\bar\zeta}-2 =\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2N_{c}},\qquad P_{gg}(\zeta)=2N_{c}\Big[\frac{\zeta}{\bar\zeta}+\frac{\bar\zeta}{\zeta}+\zeta\bar\zeta\Big].$$

The colour reduction of the row supplies exactly one factor $N_{c}$ ($U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$, then $f^{c'da}f^{cda}=N_{c}\delta^{c'c}$), so the two combine into $N_{c}C_{\rm UV}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2}$ with nothing left over. And the non–logarithmic remainder carries $\zeta\bar\zeta$ — the regular piece of $P_{gg}$, the only term with no endpoint pole.

7.1 The convolution

At fixed $k^{+}$, $S=k^{+}/\zeta$, so $dp^{+}=\frac{k^{+}}{\zeta^{2}}d\zeta$ and $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}\frac{dS}{S}$ — the usual DGLAP measure:

$$\frac{d^{3}N}{d^{3}k}\bigg|_{\mathfrak C\mathfrak C} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{2k^{+}} \int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{k^{+}/(k^{+}+\Lambda)}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}} \left\{\frac{P_{gg}(\zeta)}{2}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X+N_{c}\,\zeta\bar\zeta\big[\mathcal X-2\mathcal R\big]\right\}$$ $$\times\int_{x,x',y,y'}e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}\, \big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$$

The collinear counterterm that removes the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is the matching $-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{P_{gg}}{2}\otimes(\text{LO})$.

7.2 The two endpoints

The $\zeta$ integral is elementary, $\int\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=F(\zeta)$ with

$$F(\zeta)=2\log\zeta-\log(1-\zeta)-\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta}-\frac{1}{2\zeta^{2}} \qquad\big(F'=C_{\rm UV}/\zeta^{2}\ \checkmark\big),$$
  • $\zeta\to1$ is $p^{+}\to0$: $-\log(1-\zeta)\to\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ — the rapidity logarithm, coefficient exactly $1$. This is what goes to JIMWLK, and what $\big[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\big]_{+}$ isolates.
  • $\zeta\to0$ is $p^{+}\to\vee$: $\frac{1}{\zeta^{3}}$ gives $\frac{\vee^{2}}{2k^{+2}}$ — the kinematic end, where the eikonal treatment must be cut.
  • Restricting to $p^{+}\le k^{+}$ (i.e. $\zeta\ge\frac12$) removes the second problem entirely: $$\int_{1/2}^{\zeta_{+}}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}C_{\rm UV}(\zeta)=\log\frac{2k^{+}}{\Lambda} \qquad(\text{checked: }14.508658\text{ vs }14.508658\text{ at }k^{+}=1,\Lambda=10^{-6}).$$

And the familiar sum rule sits in the same function: $\int_{0}^{1}\big[\zeta\bar\zeta+\frac{1}{\zeta\bar\zeta}-2\big]d\zeta=\frac16-2+(\text{endpoint logs}) =2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6}$, which times $2N_{c}$ is $4N_{c}\ell_{k}-\frac{11N_{c}}{3}=4N_{c}\ell_{k}-b_{0}$.
