The $\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)}\!\times\!\mathfrak A^{(3)}$ row

Checking the split into $N_{1}$ (one $\rho$ each) and $N_{2}$ (the two–$\rho$ piece)

$\ell_{k}\equiv\log\dfrac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$,  $r\equiv x-w$,  $r_{0}=\dfrac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}{\mu}$,  $b_{0}=\dfrac{11N_{c}}{3}$

Verdict. $N_{1}$ is correct — prefactor, transverse structure and both colour brackets all reproduce exactly (§1–2), and its logarithm bracket collapses to something much simpler than written (§3). $N_{2}$'s prefactor is also correct, but its ket colour bracket is index–inconsistent: $f^{abc}$ cannot be pulled out as an overall factor, because the two terms carry genuinely different colour tensors (§4). Four smaller things need fixing (§5), one of which — the symbol $\mathbf k$ doing double duty as both the observed and the loop momentum — will bite. Two good signs along the way: $-\frac{67}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}$ is the known NLO BFKL/BK constant, and the $\log\vee$ in $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$'s two–$\rho$ bracket cancels exactly.

1. Prefactors

From $-\mathfrak A^{a}_{i}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2\pi}\frac{ig}{\sqrt{k^{+}}}\int_{x}\frac{(x-z)^{i}}{(x-z)^{2}}\rho^{a}$, so $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}=-\frac{ig}{\sqrt2\pi\sqrt{k^{+}}}(\cdots)$, and the two pieces of $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$ carry $-\frac{ig^{3}N_{c}}{16\sqrt2\pi^{3}\sqrt{k^{+}}}$ and $+\frac{ig^{3}}{32\sqrt2\pi^{5}\sqrt{k^{+}}}$:

piececomputedquotedratio
$N_{1}$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{32\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ same1
$N_{2}$$-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{64\pi^{6}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ same (times $f^{abc}$, see §4)1

Both exact. The $i$'s work out: $(-i)\times(-i)=-1$ for $N_{1}$ against the explicit minus in $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$'s first line, giving a real positive $N_{1}$; and $(-i)\times(+i)=+1$ against the explicit minus you carry in front of $N_{2}$.

2. The colour brackets of $N_{1}$

$$\text{bra}=-\bar{\mathfrak A}^{\dagger(1)a}_{i}(-w')+\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)b'}_{i}(-w')U^{b'a}(w') =\frac{ig}{\sqrt2\pi\sqrt{k^{+}}}\int_{x'}\frac{(x'-w')^{i}}{(x'-w')^{2}} \Big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{b'a}(w')\Big]\rho^{b'}(x'),$$ $$\text{ket}=U^{ab}(w)\mathfrak A^{(3)b}_{i}(-w)-\bar{\mathfrak A}^{(3)a}_{i}(-w) \ \Longrightarrow\ \Big[U^{ab}(w)-U^{ab}(x)\Big]\rho^{b}(x)\quad\checkmark$$

The ket matches yours exactly. The bra matches up to one transpose: the row has $U^{b'a}(w')$, you wrote $U^{ab'}(w')$. These are different matrices ($\|U-U^{T}\|=0.998$ on a random adjoint line), and only one ordering makes $[U(x')-U(w')]$ a sensible matrix difference. Pick one and use it in both places — the same issue appeared in the two–$\mathfrak C$ row. The transverse factor $\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(x-w)}{(x'-w')^{2}(x-w)^{2}}$ and the measure $\int_{w,w',x,x'}$ are both right.

3. The logarithm bracket collapses

This is worth doing before anything else. The second factor in $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$ is

$$\frac{11}{3}+\log\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-1\Big)-\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda},$$

and the three logarithms combine (symbolic difference $0$, and to machine precision numerically):

$$\log\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}+\log\Big(\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-1\Big)-\log\frac{\vee}{\Lambda} =\log\frac{\Lambda^{2}(\vee-k^{+})}{k^{+2}\vee} =\boxed{\;2\log\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}+\log\Big(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\Big)\;}$$

3.1 And then all the $\vee$ dependence disappears

As $\vee\to\infty$: $\log(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee})\to0$, and the tail $\log^{2}(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee})+\log(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee})\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}\to0$ as well (the second because $\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\log\vee\to0$). $N_{1}$ therefore reduces to

$$\left[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-2\gamma_{E}-\log\frac{(x-w)^{2}\mu^{2}}{4}\right] \left[\frac{11}{3}-2\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}\right]-\frac{67}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}$$

Two checks fall out of this:

  • $-\frac{67}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}=-4.1545763$ is exactly the constant that appears in the NLO BFKL / BK kernel. Its presence here is a strong sign the $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$ expression is right.
  • $\frac{11}{3}=b_{0}/N_{c}$ for pure glue, so the pole term is $N_{c}\cdot(-\frac{2}{\epsilon})\cdot\frac{11}{3}=-\frac{2b_{0}}{\epsilon}$.
This $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is ultraviolet. Unlike the two–$\mathfrak C$ row and the $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ row, where the pole came from $|z|\to\infty$ and was infrared, here it multiplies $b_{0}$ and renormalises $g^{2}$. Concretely, $$-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-2\gamma_{E}-\log\frac{r^{2}\mu^{2}}{4}=-2\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right], \qquad r_{0}=\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}{\mu},$$ which is exactly the position–space $\overline{\rm MS}$ dictionary used earlier in this work. The residual $-2\ell_{k}$ multiplying the same bracket is a mixed $\frac{1}{\epsilon}\times$ rapidity–log term and belongs with the evolution counterterm, not with the coupling.

4. $N_{2}$: $f^{abc}$ cannot be pulled out front

$\mathfrak A^{(3)a}$'s two–$\rho$ piece carries $f^{abc}\{\rho^{b}(x),\rho^{c}(y)\}$, free index $a$. Building the ket bracket from that:

$$\text{term 1}=U^{ab}(w)\,\mathfrak A^{(3)b}=U^{ab}(w)\,f^{bcd}\{\rho^{c}(x),\rho^{d}(y)\},\qquad \text{term 2}=f^{abc}\,U^{bd}(x)U^{ce}(y)\{\rho^{d}(x),\rho^{e}(y)\}.$$
Term 2 is what you wrote and it is right. Term 1 is not: you wrote $f^{abc}U^{ab}(w)\{\rho^{b}(x),\rho^{c}(y)\}$, in which the index $b$ appears three times — in $f^{abc}$, in $U^{ab}$ and in $\rho^{b}$. In term 1 the $f$ carries the index contracted with the Wilson line, not the free index, so it sits inside: $$\Big[\,U^{ab}(w)f^{bcd}\{\rho^{c}(x),\rho^{d}(y)\} \;-\;f^{abc}U^{bd}(x)U^{ce}(y)\{\rho^{d}(x),\rho^{e}(y)\}\,\Big]$$

and the $f^{abc}$ must come out of your prefactor $-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}f^{abc}}{64\pi^{6}k^{+}}$, which becomes $-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{64\pi^{6}k^{+}}$.

The two tensors are genuinely different, not proportional — checked with explicit $SU(3)$ structure constants and random adjoint Wilson lines:

||U^{ab}(w) f^{bde}||           = 4.8990
||f^{abc} U^{bd}(x) U^{ce}(y)|| = 4.8990
|| difference ||                = 7.1113      -> not proportional

(They do coincide in structure when $x=y$, where adjoint invariance gives $f^{abc}U^{bd}(x)U^{ce}(x)=U^{aa'}(x)f^{a'de}$ to $2\times10^{-16}$ — but $x\neq y$ here.)

4.1 One thing that works nicely in $N_{2}$

The $\log\vee$ in $\mathfrak A^{(3)}$'s momentum bracket cancels exactly. Collecting the coefficient of $\log\vee$ from all four $\vee$–dependent terms:

$$\frac{(2p^{2}-2k\!\cdot\!p+k^{2})k^{i}+k^{2}p^{i}}{(k-p)^{2}} -\frac{p^{2}k^{i}+k^{2}p^{i}}{(k-p)^{2}}-k^{i}-2k^{i}+2k^{i} =\frac{(k-p)^{2}k^{i}}{(k-p)^{2}}-k^{i}=0 .$$

The first two combine because $2p^{2}-2k\!\cdot\!p+k^{2}-p^{2}=(k-p)^{2}$, and the two $-2k^{i}$ logs cancel because $\log\frac{k^{+}p^{2}}{\vee k^{2}+k^{+}p^{2}}\to-\log\frac{\vee k^{2}}{k^{+}p^{2}}$. Symbolically exactly zero. So that piece is $\vee$–stable, as it should be.

5. Four smaller fixes

  1. $N_{2}$'s measure omits $x'$. It reads $\int_{w,w'}\cdots\int_{x,y}$, but $x'$ appears both in $\frac{(x'-w')^{i}}{(x'-w')^{2}}$ and in $\rho^{b'}(x')$. It should be $\int_{x,y,x'}$.
  2. $\mathbf k$ means two different things in $N_{2}$. It is the observed transverse momentum in $e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot(w'-w)}$ and in $\frac{d^{3}N}{d^{3}k}$, and simultaneously the loop momentum in $\int d^{2}\mathbf k$ and $e^{i\mathbf k\cdot(w-y)}$. Rename the loop momentum (say $\mathbf q$) before you do anything with the $w$ dependence, or the two will be combined by accident — they both multiply $w$ in an exponent.
  3. The row is not real as it stands. It is $[\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)}]\times[\mathfrak A^{(3)}]$ in one ordering only, and the bracket $\mathcal G$ is attached to $(x-w)$ but not to $(x'-w')$, so the expression is complex. The physical result needs $+\,\text{c.c.}$ (equivalently $2\,{\rm Re}$), or the companion row $\mathfrak A^{(3)\dagger}\times\mathfrak A^{(1)}$ written out separately.
  4. Subscripts. The definitions write $\mathfrak A^{a}_{2i}$ and $\mathfrak A^{(3)a}_{2i}$ while the row uses $\mathfrak A^{(1)}_{i}$ and $\mathfrak A^{(3)}_{i}$. Same object — just make it uniform.

6. $N_{1}$, corrected and simplified

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{1}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}N_{c}}{32\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{w,w',x,x'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}\,\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(x-w)}{(x'-w')^{2}(x-w)^{2}}$$ $$\times\left\{-2\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{|x-w|}{r_{0}}\right] \left[\frac{11}{3}-2\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}+\log\Big(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\Big)\right] -\frac{67}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}+\log^{2}\Big(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\Big) +\log\Big(1-\frac{k^{+}}{\vee}\Big)\log\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}\right\}$$ $$\times\Big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\Big]\rho^{b'}(x')\;\Big[U^{ab}(w)-U^{ab}(x)\Big]\rho^{b}(x) \;+\;\text{c.c.}$$

$r_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}/\mu$. Everything in the second $\vee$–bracket vanishes as $\vee\to\infty$, leaving $-2\big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{|x-w|}{r_{0}}\big]\big[\frac{11}{3}-2\ell_{k}\big]-\frac{67}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}$.
