Part $2a$: no UV — but the plus prescription needs two distributions per end

Every step, and the one place where a single $[\,1/\eta\,]_{+}$ would be illegitimate

$\eta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\eta=1-\eta$,  $L=\log\dfrac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$,  $\mathcal W=\dfrac{(x'-w')\!\cdot\!(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}$,  phase $=\exp\!\big[-ik\!\cdot\!(w'-\bar\eta z-\eta x)\big]$

No, $2a$ has no ultraviolet divergence. There is nothing to regularise: keep $d_{\perp}=2$ everywhere, no $\overline{\rm MS}$, no counterterm. But the $p^{+}$ integration is not the simple two-sided plus prescription I described before. $2a$ has a $\frac{1}{z^{2}}$ tail and a mirror $\frac{1}{x^{2}}$ tail, each cut off only by its own oscillation — $e^{i\bar\eta k\cdot z}$ and $e^{i\eta k\cdot x}$. As $\eta\to1$ the first switches off, as $\eta\to0$ the second does, so the $p^{+}$ integrand behaves as $\frac{A\log(1/\text{pole})+B}{\text{pole}}$ at both ends. A single $\big[\frac{1}{\eta}\big]_{+}$ is then illegitimate, because its $g(0)$ is itself logarithmically divergent. You need four distributions, two per endpoint, and the result carries $\log^{2}\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$.

1. There is no UV divergence at $z\to x$

The reason is in the colour factor, before any numerics. Using the fusion identity at coincident points,

$$\mathcal C_{2a}^{af}=-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x) =-f^{adc}f^{fbe}\,U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]=\frac{N_{c}}{2}\big[U^{af}(x)-U^{af}(z)\big]+O\big((z-x)^{2}\big),$$

so $\mathcal C_{2a}=O(|z-x|)$. Two powers are gained, not one:

  1. the linear vanishing beats the kernel's $\frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}$ once $d^{2}z=|u|\,d|u|\,d\phi$ is counted;
  2. the linear piece is odd in $\widehat{z-x}$, while the UV residue $C_{\rm UV}(\eta)\mathcal W\frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}$ is even, so the angular average removes it too.

2. What $2a$ does have: two long-distance tails

Both $z$ and $x$ are integration variables over the whole plane (each carries a Wilson line), and the colour-stripped integrand is invariant under $(x\leftrightarrow z,\ \eta\leftrightarrow\bar\eta)$ — verified earlier to $1.6\times10^{-13}$. So a tail in one implies a tail in the other.

A diagnostic trap I fell into. With the phase kept, the angular average of a $\frac{1}{v^{2}}$ tail is $\sim\frac{J_{0}(q|v|)}{v^{2}}$, which oscillates and decays — so “$|v|^{2}\langle f\rangle\to$ constant” is the wrong test and wrongly says there is no tail. 3. Therefore the $p^{+}$ integrand is $\dfrac{A\log+B}{\text{pole}}$

Combining the tail coefficient's $\frac{1}{\bar\eta}$ with the tail integral's $\log\frac{1}{\bar\eta}$, and the same on the $x$ side, the $z$- and $x$-integrated function $G(\eta)=\int_{x,y,z,x'}(\dots)$ behaves as

$$G(\eta)\ \xrightarrow{\eta\to0}\ \frac{A_{0}\log\frac{1}{\eta}+B_{0}}{\eta}, \qquad G(\eta)\ \xrightarrow{\eta\to1}\ \frac{A_{1}\log\frac{1}{\bar\eta}+B_{1}}{\bar\eta},$$ $$A_{1}=\frac{\pi}{k^{+}}\,\mathcal W\,\Big[-f^{adc}f^{fbc}U^{db}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\Big], \qquad A_{0}=\text{the same with }x\leftrightarrow z,\ U(x)\to\mathbb 1 .$$
This is why a single plus prescription will not do. The prescription $\int\frac{d\eta}{\eta}\big[g(\eta)-g(0)\big]$ requires $g(0)$ finite, and here $g(\eta)=\eta\,G(\eta)=A_{0}\log\frac{1}{\eta}+B_{0}$ diverges at $\eta=0$. Writing $\big[\frac{1}{\eta}\big]_{+}$ alone and putting $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ on the $\delta$ would be subtracting infinity from infinity, and the finite part would be meaningless. This corrects the two-sided-but-single-distribution form I gave you earlier, which assumed $h(\eta)$ stood in for the whole $\eta$-dependence — it does for the integrand at fixed $z$, but not after the $z$ and $x$ integrals are done, which is where the extra $\log$ is generated.

4. The correct treatment: two distributions at each end

$$\frac{1}{\eta}=\Big[\frac{1}{\eta}\Big]_{+}+\delta(\eta)\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}, \qquad \frac{\log\frac{1}{\eta}}{\eta}=\Big[\frac{\log\frac{1}{\eta}}{\eta}\Big]_{+} +\frac{1}{2}\,\delta(\eta)\log^{2}\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda},$$

and the same two in $\bar\eta$ — four in all. Both identities checked against the direct cut-off integral with a smooth test function:

5. The step-by-step recipe for $2a$

  1. Set $d_{\perp}=2$ and drop all thought of dimensional regularisation. $2a$ is UV finite.
  2. Do the transverse integrals first, at fixed $\eta$: $\int_{x,y,z,x'}$ with the phase $\exp[-ik\cdot(w'-\bar\eta z-\eta x)]$ and the colour factor $-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)]$. Call the result $G(\eta)$. Both integrals converge — $z\to x$ by §1, large $|z|$ and $|x|$ by the oscillations.
  3. Extract the four endpoint constants $A_{0},B_{0},A_{1},B_{1}$. $A_{0}$ and $A_{1}$ you have in closed form above; $B_{0}$ and $B_{1}$ come out of the same transverse integrals with the tail removed.
  4. Subtract and integrate: $$\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}G =\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\bigg\{\frac{A_{0}+A_{1}}{2}L^{2}+(B_{0}+B_{1})L +\int_{0}^{1}\!d\eta\Big[G-\frac{A_{0}\log\frac1\eta+B_{0}}{\eta} -\frac{A_{1}\log\frac1{\bar\eta}+B_{1}}{\bar\eta}\Big]\bigg\}+O(\Lambda)$$ The bracket is integrable on $[0,1]$ by construction and is the numerical piece.

Step 4 is the plus prescription, written so that you can see what it does: the two $\delta$ coefficients are $L$ and $\frac{L^{2}}{2}$, and the remainder is the $[\ ]_{+}$ part.

Both halves carry a $\log^{2}\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$, which is what you should expect of a self-energy loop on the measured gluon: it is the Sudakov double logarithm. Their sum, together with the companion rows, is what has to reduce to the JIMWLK structure. Worth checking once you have the companions: the $\log^{2}$ coefficients should combine into something proportional to the LO cross section, since a double rapidity log cannot survive in a single-log evolution equation.
