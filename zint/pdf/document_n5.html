The $\overline{\rm MS}$ row in transverse position space

Trading $\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{k^{2}}$ for the dipole size, and what it costs

$r=z-x$,  $s=y-x$,  $q=\bar\xi k$,  $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$,   $M^{2}=\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$,  $D_{0}=D\big|_{r=0}=\tfrac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi}$

1. What is actually in momentum space

Only one thing: the argument of the logarithm. The row itself — the Wilson lines, the colour sources, the kernel $\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}$ — is already a transverse–position–space object. What came out of $\overline{\rm MS}$ was

$$T\big|_{\overline{\rm MS}}=\pi\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}},\qquad q=\bar\xi k,$$

i.e. the produced–gluon momentum, not the dipole size.

This is not a rewriting. You cannot turn $\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{k^{2}}$ into $\log\big(\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)$ by algebra — the two differ by $\log\big(k^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)$, which is a real function of the kinematics. The conversion needs the piece the draft you are holding left out: the finite remainder

$$\mathcal R=\int d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{D}-\frac{1}{D_{0}}\right].$$

It is ultraviolet finite and needs no regulator, so it is easy to forget — but it carries a $\log q^{2}$ of its own, of the opposite sign, and that is what cancels the $q^{2}$.

2. Regulate in position space to begin with

The cleanest route never leaves position space. Cut the transverse integral at short distance, $|r|>r_{0}$. Then

$$\int_{|r|>r_{0}}\!\!d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}} =2\pi\!\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\!\frac{dr}{r}J_{0}(q r) =\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}{q^{2}r_{0}^{2}}+O(q^{2}r_{0}^{2}).$$

The mass regulator $\frac{1}{r^{2}}\to\frac{1}{r^{2}+m^{2}}$ gives $2\pi K_{0}(m|q|)$, which is the same expression with $m=r_{0}$. Checked:

The $\overline{\rm MS}$ dictionary

$$\pi\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{q^{2}}=\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}{q^{2}r_{0}^{2}} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\;r_{0}=\frac{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}{\mu}\;}$$

($\mu$ the $\overline{\rm MS}$ scale). Exact to all digits, both signs of the log — this is the only place $\gamma_{E}$ and $\log2$ ever enter.

3. The master, entirely in position space

$$G=\int d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}D} \;=\;\frac{\pi}{D_{0}}\left[\log\frac{D_{0}}{r_{0}^{2}}+\hat I(\xi)\right], \qquad D_{0}=\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi},$$ $$\hat I(\xi)=D_{0}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!dt\left[e^{itq\cdot s}\, \frac{|q|K_{1}\big(|q|\sqrt{\Delta_{0}}\big)}{\sqrt{\Delta_{0}}}-\frac{1}{t\,D_{0}}\right], \qquad \Delta_{0}=t\big[(1-t)s^{2}+M^{2}\big].$$

No $\gamma_{E}$, no $\log2$, no $q^{2}$ — the scale in the logarithm is the dipole size divided by $\bar\xi$, and the regulator sits where it belongs, as a transverse distance. $\hat I$ carries no regulator and no $\mu$. Verified against direct two–dimensional quadrature of $\int_{|r|>r_{0}}$:

Substituting $r_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}/\mu$ recovers the $\overline{\rm MS}$ form,

$$\log\frac{D_{0}}{r_{0}^{2}}=\log\frac{\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi}+2\big(\gamma_{E}-\log2\big),$$

exact to twelve digits. So the celebrated $2(\gamma_{E}-\log2)$ is nothing but the $\overline{\rm MS}$ translation constant: in position space with a distance cutoff it is not there at all.

4. The $p^{+}$ integral, and the answer

Split $\log\frac{D_{0}}{r_{0}^{2}}=\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$; the first factor is $\xi$–independent. With $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_{c}}$ and $\ell_{k}=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$,

$$\int d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}=2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6},\qquad \int d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}=\frac{\ell_{k}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36},$$ $$\int d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\,\hat I=\frac{e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,\ell_{k}^{2}+c_{0}\ell_{k}+c_{1}.$$

The row in transverse position space. With $\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}_{2}}{d^{3}k} =\frac{\alpha_s^{2}N_{c}}{8\pi^{5}k^{+}}\int_{x,y,x',w'}e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\, \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\,\big[\mathcal P+\mathcal F\big]\times$ (Wilson lines),

$$\mathcal F=\Big(2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\,\log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}} +\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,\ell_{k}^{2} +\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}+c_{0}\ell_{k}+c_{1},$$

and if you insist on $\overline{\rm MS}$, put $r_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}/\mu$:

$$\mathcal F=\Big(2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big[\log\big(\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}\big)+2(\gamma_{E}-\log2)\Big] +\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,\ell_{k}^{2} +\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}+c_{0}\ell_{k}+c_{1}.$$

Two readings. First, $\big(2\ell_{k}-\tfrac{11}{6}\big)\times2N_{c}=4N_{c}\ell_{k}-b$ at $N_{f}=0$, so the constant riding on it enters as $2b(\gamma_{E}-\log2)$ — exactly the term in $K_{JSJ}$, Eq. (2.64) of arXiv:1610.03453 that their footnote 2 records as having been missing. Second, the $\ell_{k}^{2}$ coefficient is $\tfrac12\big(1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\big)$, one half from each endpoint of the $\xi$ integral; it is regulator–independent and agrees with the mass–regulator computation.

On the draft you attached. It is the three–page version, which kept only $T$ and therefore states that “the scale in the logarithm is $k^{2}$, not the dipole size”. That reading is superseded: once $\mathcal R$ is kept the $q^{2}$ cancels and the logarithm is of the dipole size. The current four–page dimreg_msbar.pdf has §§6–9 rewritten accordingly.
