$r=z-x$,  $s=y-x$,  $P=x'-w'$,  $\xi=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\xi=1-\xi$,  $q=\bar\xi k$,  $M^{2}=\dfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$,  $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$,  $D_{0}=D\big|_{r=0}=\dfrac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi}$,  $\lambda=\dfrac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$,  $L=\log\dfrac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$,  $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\dfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\dfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$,  $\mathcal W=\dfrac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}s^{2}}$,  $\ell=\log\dfrac{k^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}$

The answer. With $\mathcal N=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}$ and $\mathcal U_{b}=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\big[-N_{c}U^{af}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(y)\big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)$: $$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm UV}_{2b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\;\mathcal W\,\bigg\{ \underbrace{\Big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\Big]}_{\text{UV term}} +\underbrace{\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,L^{2}+c_{0}\,L+H}_{\text{other terms}}\bigg\}\,\mathcal U_{b}$$
  • There is exactly one UV pole, with coefficient $2L-\frac{11}{6}$.
  • $c_{0}$ (eq. 6.4) and $H$ (eq. 7.3) are finite, dimensionless numbers that depend only on $k\cdot(y-x)$ and $|k||y-x|$. Each is given as an explicit one-dimensional integral.
  • Warning. The $\frac{1}{2}e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}L^{2}$ is an artefact of this split. The rest of 2b carries exactly $-\frac{1}{2}e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}\mathcal WL^{2}$, so the two cancel (§9). The whole of 2b keeps only $\frac{1}{2}\mathcal WL^{2}$.

0. Your expression, checked, and rewritten

Prefactor. $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}=\frac{\mathcal N}{\pi}$. This agrees with the .tex start of part (b).

Colour. $\big[-N_{c}U^{af}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(y)\big]=N_{c}\big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\big]$, so the colour factor is $\mathcal U_{b}$. It has no $z$ and no $\xi$ dependence.

Phase. $e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}e^{iq\cdot r}$ is correct, with $q=\bar\xi k$.

$D$. Your $D_{0}=s^{2}+M^{2}=\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi}$ is the value of $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$ at $r=0$, with $M^{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$. Check: $s^{2}+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}=\frac{\bar\xi+\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}=\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi}$.

Rewrite. Since $\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}r^{2}}=\mathcal W\,\frac{s^{2}}{r^{2}}$, all the $P$ dependence sits in $\mathcal W$, and

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm UV}_{2b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\,\mathcal W\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\;\frac{1}{\pi}\,K(\xi)\;\mathcal U_{b}, \qquad K(\xi)\equiv\mu^{-2\epsilon}\!\int d^{d}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi\,D\,r^{2}}.$$

$K$ depends only on $\xi$, $k$ and $s$. The whole problem is this one scalar integral.

1. Does this piece contain all the UV divergence of 2b?

Yes. As $r\to0$ the full 2b integrand goes to $\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}}$, where

$$T_{\rm UV}=d_{\perp}\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{P^{2}r^{4}}+\Big(\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\Big)\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}r^{2}}.$$

Your $\xi\bar\xi$ term is $\xi\bar\xi\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}r^{2}}$ instead of the tensor. The pole comes from the angular average, and $\langle\hat r^{i}\hat r^{m}\rangle=\frac{\delta^{im}}{d}$. So $d_{\perp}\langle(P\cdot\hat r)(s\cdot\hat r)\rangle=\frac{d_{\perp}}{d}P\cdot s=P\cdot s$, since $d_{\perp}=d$. The two therefore have the same pole.

The difference, $\xi\bar\xi\frac{2(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)-(P\cdot s)r^{2}}{P^{2}r^{4}}\cdot\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{\bar\xi D}$, is traceless and UV finite. It belongs to the rest of 2b, where it can be computed at $d_{\perp}=d=2$. Your split is valid as long as the rest of 2b is defined as (full 2b) $-$ (this piece).

3. Exact decomposition of $K$

3.1 An algebraic identity

Expand $D=r^{2}-2r\cdot s+s^{2}+M^{2}=r^{2}-2r\cdot s+D_{0}$. Hence $D_{0}=D+2r\cdot s-r^{2}$, and with $\bar\xi D_{0}=s^{2}$:

$$\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi D r^{2}}=\frac{D_{0}}{D\,r^{2}}=\frac{D+2r\cdot s-r^{2}}{D\,r^{2}} =\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{2\,r\cdot s}{r^{2}D}-\frac{1}{D}.$$

So

$$K(\xi)=\underbrace{\mu^{-2\epsilon}\!\int d^{d}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}}}_{M_{0}\ (\text{pole})}\;+\;\underbrace{\int d^{2}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{2\,r\cdot s}{r^{2}D}}_{A\ (\text{finite})}\;-\;\underbrace{\int d^{2}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{D}}_{B\ (\text{finite})}.$$

$A$ and $B$ are finite:

  • $A$: $\frac{r\cdot s}{r^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{r}$ at small $r$, and $\sim\frac{1}{r^{3}}$ at large $r$;
  • $B$: $D\geq M^{2}$ and the phase controls the $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ tail.

So they are computed at $d=2$. All of the $\epsilon$ dependence is in $M_{0}$.

3.2 The pole term $M_{0}$

From $\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}(r^{2})^{-1}=\pi\,\Gamma(-\epsilon)\big(\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\big)^{\epsilon}$ and $\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_{E}}$:

$$M_{0}=\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{q^{2}}\Big],\qquad\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}\equiv-\frac{1}{\epsilon}.$$

In transverse coordinates (rowV_coordinate_UV.pdf, §4 and (R1)), write $M_{0}=s^{2}J_{0}+\int d^{2}r\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big[e^{iq\cdot r}-\frac{s^{2}}{r^{2}+s^{2}}\big]$. This uses

$$s^{2}J_{0}=\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\Big],\qquad \int d^{2}r\,\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big[e^{iq\cdot r}-\frac{s^{2}}{r^{2}+s^{2}}\Big]=-\pi\log\frac{q^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}.$$

The two add up to the same $M_{0}$. The second line is $2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\big[J_{0}(q|s|x)-\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\big]$.

3.3 The term $B$

Shift $r=s+u$, so that $D=u^{2}+M^{2}$ and $e^{iq\cdot r}=e^{iq\cdot s}e^{iq\cdot u}$. The standard 2D Fourier transform $\int d^{2}u\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot u}}{u^{2}+M^{2}}=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u\,du\,J_{0}(qu)}{u^{2}+M^{2}}=2\pi K_{0}(qM)$ gives

$$B=2\pi\,e^{iq\cdot s}\,K_{0}(qM),\qquad qM=\bar\xi|k|\cdot|s|\sqrt{\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}}=|k||s|\sqrt{\xi\bar\xi}.$$

3.4 The term $A$ (Feynman parameter)

Combine the denominators. Use $\frac{1}{ab}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{[ta+(1-t)b]^{2}}$ with $a=D$ and $b=r^{2}$:

$$tD+(1-t)r^{2}=r^{2}-2t\,r\cdot s+tD_{0}=(r-ts)^{2}+\Delta,\qquad \Delta=tD_{0}-t^{2}s^{2}=t\,s^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-t\Big)=t\,s^{2}\Big(1-t+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}\Big)>0.$$

Shift $r=ts+u$. Then $2r\cdot s=2u\cdot s+2ts^{2}$ and $e^{iq\cdot r}=e^{itq\cdot s}e^{iq\cdot u}$:

$$A=\int_{0}^{1}\!dt\;e^{itq\cdot s}\int d^{2}u\;e^{iq\cdot u}\,\frac{2u\cdot s+2ts^{2}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}}.$$

Two Fourier transforms, both obtained from $\int d^{2}u\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot u}}{u^{2}+\Delta}=2\pi K_{0}(q\sqrt\Delta)$:

  • Differentiate in $\Delta$, using $K_{0}'=-K_{1}$: $$\int d^{2}u\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot u}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}}=-\partial_{\Delta}\big[2\pi K_{0}(q\sqrt\Delta)\big]=\frac{\pi q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_{1}(q\sqrt\Delta).$$
  • Write $\frac{u^{j}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}\frac{1}{u^{2}+\Delta}$ and integrate by parts ($\int e^{iq\cdot u}\partial_{j}f=-iq^{j}\int e^{iq\cdot u}f$): $$\int d^{2}u\,e^{iq\cdot u}\frac{u^{j}}{(u^{2}+\Delta)^{2}}=\frac{iq^{j}}{2}\,2\pi K_{0}(q\sqrt\Delta)=i\pi q^{j}K_{0}(q\sqrt\Delta).$$

Therefore

$$A=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\!dt\;e^{itq\cdot s}\Big[2i\,(q\cdot s)\,K_{0}(q\sqrt\Delta)+\frac{2ts^{2}q}{\sqrt\Delta}K_{1}(q\sqrt\Delta)\Big].$$

3.5 Check against direct integration

In $d=2$, $\int d^{2}r\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big[e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi D}-\frac{s^{2}}{r^{2}+s^{2}}\big]$ is absolutely convergent. By 3.1–3.4 it must equal $-\pi\log\frac{q^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}+A-B$. Test case: $s=(0.7,-0.4)$, $k=(1.3,0.5)$:

4. The UV term and the other terms, separated

Put 3.2 (coordinate form), 3.3 and 3.4 into $K=M_{0}+A-B$ and divide by $\pi$:

$$\frac{K(\xi)}{\pi}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}}_{\text{UV term}}\;+\;\underbrace{G(\xi)}_{\text{other terms}},$$ $$G(\xi)=-\log\frac{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\;+\;a(\xi)\;-\;2\,e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot s}\,K_{0}\big(|k||s|\sqrt{\xi\bar\xi}\big),$$ $$a(\xi)=\frac{A}{\pi}=\int_{0}^{1}\!dt\;e^{it\bar\xi k\cdot s}\Big[2i\bar\xi(k\cdot s)\,K_{0}\big(\bar\xi|k|\sqrt\Delta\big)+\frac{2t\,\bar\xi|k|\,s^{2}}{\sqrt\Delta}K_{1}\big(\bar\xi|k|\sqrt\Delta\big)\Big],\qquad \Delta=ts^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-t\Big).$$

(If you prefer the momentum form, use $\log\frac{\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}=\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{k^{2}}+\ell$ and move $\ell$ into the other terms.) So, before the $p^{+}$ integral,

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm UV}_{2b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\,\mathcal W\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\Big\{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}+G(\xi)\Big\}\,\mathcal U_{b}.$$

5. $p^{+}$ integral of the UV term

The UV bracket does not depend on $\xi$. So we only need

$$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}=\frac{1}{6}+(L-1)+(L-1)=2L-\frac{11}{6},$$

using $\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}=\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\big(\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-1\big)=\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}-(1-2\lambda)\to L-1$, and the same for $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$.

6. The endpoints of $G(\xi)$

The plus prescription needs to know how $C_{\rm UV}G$ behaves at each end. We have $C_{\rm UV}\to\frac{1}{\xi}$ at $\xi\to0$ and $C_{\rm UV}\to\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ at $\xi\to1$. Throughout we use $K_{0}(z)=-\log\frac{ze^{\gamma_{E}}}{2}+O(z^{2}\log z)$ and $zK_{1}(z)=1+O(z^{2}\log z)$.

6.1 $\xi\to1$ ($\bar\xi\to0$)

  • $-\log\frac{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}=2\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-\ell$.
  • $qM=|k||s|\sqrt{\xi\bar\xi}\to0$, so $2K_{0}(qM)=-\log\frac{k^{2}s^{2}\xi\bar\xi e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}=\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-\ell+O(\bar\xi)$, and $e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot s}\to1$.
  • $a\to0$. Since $zK_{1}(z)\le1$, the $K_{1}$ term is bounded by $\frac{2ts^{2}}{\Delta}=\frac{2}{1/\bar\xi-t}\le\frac{2\bar\xi}{\xi}\to0$, and the $K_{0}$ term is $O(\bar\xi\log\bar\xi)$.
$$\Rightarrow\quad G\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to1}\ 2\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-\ell-\Big(\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-\ell\Big)=\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\qquad(\text{constant term exactly }0).\tag{6.1}$$

Meaning. For $\bar\xi\to0$ the integrand stays $\approx\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ from $|s|$ out to $M=\frac{|s|}{\sqrt{\bar\xi}}$, which gives $\int_{|s|}^{M}\frac{2\pi r\,dr}{r^{2}}=\pi\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$.

6.2 $\xi\to0$

  • $q\to k$, so $-\log\frac{q^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\to-\ell$.
  • $M^{2}\to\xi s^{2}$, so $2K_{0}(qM)\to\log\frac{1}{\xi}-\ell$ and $e^{iq\cdot s}\to e^{ik\cdot s}$.
  • In $a(\xi)$, $\Delta\to ts^{2}(1-t+\xi)$ becomes small near $t=1$. There the $K_{1}$ term $\to\frac{2ts^{2}}{\Delta}\to\frac{2}{1-t+\xi}$, and $\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2\,dt}{1-t+\xi}=2\log\frac{1+\xi}{\xi}$. Subtracting this and adding it back:
$$a(\xi)\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to0}\ 2e^{ik\cdot s}\log\frac{1}{\xi}+a_{\rm reg},\qquad a_{\rm reg}=\int_{0}^{1}\!dt\Big\{e^{itk\cdot s}\Big[2i(k\cdot s)K_{0}(|k|\sqrt{\Delta_{0}})+\frac{2t|k|s^{2}}{\sqrt{\Delta_{0}}}K_{1}(|k|\sqrt{\Delta_{0}})\Big]-\frac{2e^{ik\cdot s}}{1-t}\Big\},$$

with $\Delta_{0}=t(1-t)s^{2}$. The braces are integrable at $t=1$. Adding up:

$$G\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to0}\ -\ell+2e^{ik\cdot s}\log\frac{1}{\xi}+a_{\rm reg}-e^{ik\cdot s}\Big(\log\frac{1}{\xi}-\ell\Big) =e^{ik\cdot s}\log\frac{1}{\xi}+c_{0},\tag{6.3}$$ $$c_{0}=a_{\rm reg}-\big(1-e^{ik\cdot s}\big)\,\ell.\tag{6.4}$$

7. $p^{+}$ integral of the other terms: the plus prescription

Both ends carry a $\frac{\log}{\text{pole}}$, so each end needs two distributions. Subtract the endpoint behaviour and add it back:

$$C_{\rm UV}G=\underbrace{\Big[C_{\rm UV}G-\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}\log\frac{1}{\xi}+c_{0}}{\xi}-\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big]}_{\text{integrable on }[0,1]} +e^{ik\cdot s}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\xi}}{\xi}+\frac{c_{0}}{\xi}+\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}.$$

(The $O(1)$ part of $C_{\rm UV}=\frac{1}{\xi}-1+\dots$ times $\log\frac{1}{\xi}$ is integrable, so it stays inside the bracket.) The elementary integrals are

$$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{d\xi}{\xi}=\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\to L,\qquad \int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\xi}}{\xi}d\xi=\Big[-\tfrac{1}{2}\log^{2}\xi\Big]_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\to\frac{1}{2}L^{2},$$

and the same for $\bar\xi$. In distribution language:

$$\frac{1}{\xi}=\Big[\frac{1}{\xi}\Big]_{+}+\delta(\xi)L,\quad\frac{\log\frac{1}{\xi}}{\xi}=\Big[\frac{\log\frac{1}{\xi}}{\xi}\Big]_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\delta(\xi)L^{2},\quad \frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}=\Big[\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big]_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\delta(\bar\xi)L^{2}.$$
$$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,G(\xi)=\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot s}}{2}\,L^{2}+c_{0}\,L+H+O(\lambda\log^{2}\lambda),$$ $$H=\int_{0}^{1}\!d\xi\Big[C_{\rm UV}(\xi)G(\xi)-\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}\log\frac{1}{\xi}+c_{0}}{\xi}-\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big].\tag{7.3}$$

8. Final answer

Put §5 and §7 into §4:

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm UV}_{2b\,\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot x}\, \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\bigg\{\Big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\Big]$$ $$\qquad\qquad+\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}\,L^{2}+c_{0}\,L+H\bigg\}\Big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\Big]\Big[-N_{c}U^{af}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(y)\Big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)$$
  • UV term: $\big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\big)\big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\big]$. The coefficient is $\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}C_{\rm UV}=\int\frac{P_{gg}}{2N_{c}}$.
  • Other terms: $\frac{1+e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}}{2}L^{2}+c_{0}L+H$. Here $c_{0}$ is (6.4) and $H$ is (7.3); both are finite and free of $\epsilon$ and $\Lambda$.
  • Momentum form, if preferred: replace the log by $\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{k^{2}}$ and add $\big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\big)\ell$ to the other terms.

9. Important: the $\frac{1}{2}e^{ik\cdot(y-x)}L^{2}$ cancels against the rest of 2b

This double log comes from the region $r\to s$ at $\xi\to0$ (eq. 6.3). It is not in the full 2b:

  • The only block of 2b enhanced by $\frac{1}{\xi}$ is $T_{4}=\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\frac{P\cdot(s-r)}{P^{2}r^{2}}\big[1-\frac{s\cdot r}{2s^{2}}\big]$. It vanishes at $r=s$ (sympy: $T_{4}|_{r=s}=0$), so the full 2b has no $\log\frac{1}{M^{2}}$ there.
  • Your piece has $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}r^{2}}$ instead, which is $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\mathcal W\neq0$ at $r=s$.
  • So the rest of 2b (full $-$ your piece) carries $-\mathcal W e^{ik\cdot s}\frac{\log(1/\xi)}{\xi}$, which gives $-\frac{1}{2}e^{ik\cdot s}\mathcal WL^{2}$. This agrees with the earlier finding that the full 2b remainder has only a simple pole at $\xi\to0$ ($\xi\,\mathcal I_{b}\to\beta_{0}$: 2.60199, 2.61189, 2.61435, 2.61517, 2.61539).

Practical advice. Either:
  • keep this split, but do not plus-prescribe the $e^{ik\cdot s}\frac{\log(1/\xi)}{\xi}$ separately in each half — add the two halves first; or
  • subtract a UV term without $\frac{1}{D}$, such as $\frac{C_{\rm UV}\mathcal W s^{2}}{r^{2}(r^{2}+s^{2})}$ (rowV_coordinate_UV.pdf). It has no $r=s$ singularity, so no artefact appears.
Either way, the UV term, $\mathcal W(2L-\frac{11}{6})\big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}(y-x)^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\big]$, is the same.
