Two questions

Why the two–gluon rows carry no ultraviolet divergence — and which diagrams give DGLAP with a fragmentation function

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $\zeta\equiv\dfrac{k^{+}}{S}$,  $r\equiv y'-y$

Short answers. (1) Your field–theory instinct is right, and it is not violated: these rows are cut diagrams, not loops. The unobserved gluon is on shell, integrated over three–dimensional phase space, not over $\int d^{4}\ell$. That does not by itself guarantee finiteness — real–emission integrals can still diverge at large $p_{\perp}$ — but here they do not, for a structural reason given in §2. And the ultraviolet divergence that field theory demands is present in this calculation: it sits in the virtual row, $\mathfrak A^{\dagger(1)}\!\times\!\mathfrak A^{(3)}$, as exactly $-\frac{2b_{0}}{\epsilon}$ (§3). (2) Fragmentation DGLAP comes from a splitting that happens after the last interaction with the target, and it has three unmistakable kinematic signatures (§4). The two–$\mathfrak C$ row has all three — which means my earlier description of it as an initial–state, PDF–type collinear pole was wrong. It is a fragmentation contribution (§5).

1. A cut is not a loop

In the cross section the unobserved gluon appears on both sides of the cut, at the same momentum. Its integration is

$$\int\frac{dp^{+}\,d^{2}p_{\perp}}{2p^{+}(2\pi)^{3}}\qquad\text{(on shell, }p^{-}=\tfrac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{2p^{+}}\text{ fixed)},$$

not $\int\frac{d^{4}\ell}{(2\pi)^{4}}$ over an off–shell line. Ultraviolet divergences in field theory come from the deep off–shell region of a genuine loop; a cut line has no such region — one integration is removed by the mass–shell delta. So the naive counting that gives $\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{2}$ or $\log\Lambda_{\rm UV}$ for a loop simply does not apply to these rows.

But that is not the whole answer, and it would be sloppy to stop there. A real–emission integral can still diverge at large $p_{\perp}$ if the matrix element does not fall fast enough — that is exactly what a collinear–factorisation counterterm sometimes has to remove. In transverse position space, large $p_{\perp}$ is the region where points collapse, which is precisely what every scan in this work has been testing. So the finiteness is a result, not an automatic consequence. §2 says why it comes out that way.

2. Why the collapse regions converge

The rule established throughout: in two transverse dimensions a logarithmic short–distance divergence needs the integrand to go as $\rho^{-2}$, because $n$ collapsing points supply $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$. A single Weizsäcker–Williams kernel is only $\rho^{-1}$. To get $\rho^{-2}$ you need two kernels singular on the same separation.

Now look at where the unobserved gluon sits. Its transverse position $z$ is shared between amplitude and conjugate — it is one integration variable, not two. The two kernels attached to it join $z$ to different points:

$$\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}}\quad\text{(amplitude)},\qquad \frac{(y'-z)^{m'}}{(y'-z)^{2}}\quad\text{(conjugate)},$$

and $y$ and $y'$ are held apart by the measurement phase $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-y)}$. To make both kernels singular at once you must collapse $y$, $y'$ and $z$ together — three points, measure $\rho^{4}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$, against an integrand of only $\rho^{-2}$. The measure wins by $\rho^{2}$.

The one place a genuine $\rho^{-2}$ does appear is a vanishing light–cone energy denominator, e.g. $D=p^{+}(x-z)^{2}+k^{+}(x-w)^{2}\to0$ when $x,z,w$ collapse. But that too needs three points: integrand $\rho^{-2}$ against $\rho^{3}d\rho$. Checked explicitly in the $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ and $\mathfrak B_{3}$ rows — convergent, with room to spare.

4. What a fragmentation contribution looks like

Both DGLAP flavours use the same $P_{gg}$; what distinguishes them is when the splitting happens relative to the target, and that shows up in three places.

initial state → PDFfinal state → fragmentation
(A) diagramparent splits before the shockwave; both daughters cross separately, so Wilson lines appear at both daughter positions, $U(y)U(z)$ parent crosses the shockwave and splits after; the Wilson lines see only the parent's position, and the daughters enter only through the splitting kernel
(B) collinear regiondaughter collinear to the beam: small $k_{\perp}$ of a daughterthe two final–state gluons collinear to each other, $M^{2}=\frac{(p^{+}k_{\perp}-k^{+}p_{\perp})^{2}}{p^{+}k^{+}}\to0$, i.e. relative $q_{\perp}\to0$ — which in position space is large relative separation, not a collapse
(C) kinematicshard factor at the measured $k$; the convolution rides on the incoming fluxhard factor at the parent momentum $k/\xi$, with $E\frac{d\sigma^{h}}{d^{3}k}=\int\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}D(\xi)\Big[E'\frac{d\hat\sigma}{d^{3}k'}\Big]_{k'=k/\xi}$

So, concretely, the diagrams that give you a gluon fragmentation function are those in which a single gluon is produced from the source, picks up its Wilson line, and then undergoes a $g\to gg$ splitting with one daughter measured and one integrated out — the splitting vertex sitting downstream of every $U$. Diagrams where the splitting precedes the shockwave, so that both daughters scatter independently, give the initial–state (PDF–type) log instead.

A quark fragmentation function would need a $g\to q\bar q$ vertex, i.e. $P_{qg}$ rather than $P_{gg}$. None of the vertices in this calculation ($\mathfrak A$, $\mathfrak B_{2}$, $\mathfrak B_{3}$, $\mathfrak C$) are quark vertices, so at this order you can only generate the gluon FF.

5. The two–$\mathfrak C$ row is a fragmentation contribution

A correction to what I told you earlier. In the DGLAP note I wrote that $r=y'-y$ is conjugate to the measured momentum $k$, and therefore that the $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ should be absorbed into the gluon distribution. The first statement is wrong, and the second follows from it. Here is the correct kinematics.

The two $\mathfrak C$ deltas gave $w-z=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y-z)$ and $w'-z=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y'-z)$, so

$$w'-w=\frac{S}{k^{+}}\,(y'-y)=\frac{r}{\zeta} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad e^{-ik\cdot(w'-w)}=e^{-i\left(\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}\right)\cdot r}.$$
$r$ is conjugate to $\dfrac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}$, not to $k_{\perp}$. And $\frac{k_{\perp}}{\zeta}$ is the parent's transverse momentum: in a collinear splitting the daughter takes the fraction $\zeta$ of both the longitudinal and the transverse momentum, so $k_{\perp}^{\rm parent}=k_{\perp}/\zeta$. The hard factor is being evaluated at $k/\zeta$ — criterion (C).

All three signatures are then present:

  1. Coefficient $P_{gg}(\zeta)$ with $\zeta=\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}$, the fraction of the parent carried by the measured gluon — the fragmentation variable.
  2. Measure $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$, which is exactly the $\frac{dz}{z^{2}}$ of the collinear fragmentation formula for the invariant cross section — not the $\frac{d\xi}{\xi}$ of a parton–density convolution.
  3. Hard factor at $k/\zeta$, from the phase above.

And criterion (B) fits too: the divergence of that row sits at $|z|\to\infty$ — large relative separation, i.e. small relative $q_{\perp}$, the two final–state gluons becoming collinear to each other. It is not at any collapse of points, which is why it never looked ultraviolet.

So the pole should be absorbed into a gluon fragmentation function $D_{g/g}(\zeta,\mu_{F})$ by the counterterm $-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{P_{gg}}{2}\otimes(\text{LO})$ on the outgoing leg, with the hard factor kept at $k/\zeta$.

5.1 The one check that would settle it independently

The three signatures above are kinematic and unambiguous. The structural confirmation is criterion (A): in the reduced colour structure the unobserved gluon's Wilson line cancelled ($U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$), leaving one Wilson line per side, at $y$ and $y'$ — the parent positions — with $z$ surviving only inside the splitting kernel. That is the final–state pattern of (A). To make it airtight rather than suggestive, check the time ordering directly in the derivation of the $\mathfrak C$ vertex: whether its $\delta^{(2)}$ places the splitting before or after the shockwave is fixed there, and it is the one ingredient I cannot verify from the expressions alone.
