The answers. With $\mathcal W=\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}$ and $\mathcal T=\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)-2\,(x'-w')\cdot\hat k\;(y-x)\cdot\hat k}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}$: $$\frac{d^{3}N_{b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\bigg\{\mathcal W\Big[\Big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{k^{2}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}L^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{67}{18}\Big]+\frac{1}{6}\,\mathcal T+\frac{\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}}{\pi}L+\frac{\mathcal F_{b}}{\pi}\bigg\}\,\mathcal U_{b}$$ $$\frac{d^{3}N_{a}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\,L^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}}{\pi}L+\frac{\mathcal F_{a}}{\pi}\bigg\}$$
  • Part (b) has exactly one UV pole, coefficient $2L-\frac{11}{6}$. The $-\frac{11}{6}$ is $\frac{\beta_{0}}{2N_{c}}$ at $n_{f}=0$.
  • Part (a) has no UV divergence.

0. Starting point

From the .tex, the equation after the $w$ integration, split by the identity $f^{abc}f^{def}U^{be}(z)U^{cf}(z)=N_{c}U^{ad}(z)$ into (a) and (b). I move the phases and colour factors inside the integrals they depend on; in the .tex they sit outside. The colour factors are

$$\mathcal U_{b}=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\,N_{c}\big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y)\qquad(\text{no }z\text{ dependence}),$$ $$\mathcal U_{a}(z)=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\big(-f^{adc}f^{fbe}\big)U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y).$$

The last form of part (a)'s colour is identical to the .tex's $-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)$, because the identity turns $f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(x)$ into $N_{c}U^{af}(x)$ (checked, $10^{-15}$). One caveat on signs: the product of the three vertices gives an overall minus relative to the .tex; the $+$ you write depends on the $\bar{\mathfrak A}$ convention, which is not defined in the file.

1. The six blocks in $r,s,P$

Substitute $y-x=s$, $x-z=-r$, $y-z=s-r$, $z-x=r$, $x'-w'=P$ into the bracket and contract with $\frac{P^{i}r^{m}}{P^{2}r^{2}}$. Call the result $T(r)=\sum_{n}T_{n}$:

$$T_{1}=d_{\perp}\,\xi\bar\xi\Big[\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{P^{2}r^{4}}-\frac{P\cdot r}{2P^{2}r^{2}}\Big],\qquad T_{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}\,\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[1+\frac{r\cdot(s-r)}{2(s-r)^{2}}\Big],$$ $$T_{3}=\bar\xi\,\frac{P\cdot r}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[2-\frac{2\,s\cdot r}{r^{2}}-\frac{s\cdot r}{2s^{2}}\Big],\qquad T_{4}=\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\,\frac{P\cdot(s-r)}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[1-\frac{s\cdot r}{2s^{2}}\Big],$$ $$T_{5}=\xi\,\frac{P\cdot r}{P^{2}r^{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\,s\cdot r}{r^{2}}-\frac{s\cdot(s-r)}{2(s-r)^{2}}\Big],$$ $$T_{6}=\frac{(P\cdot r)(2s\cdot r-r^{2})}{P^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{(s\cdot r)\,P\cdot(s-r)}{2P^{2}r^{2}(s-r)^{2}}-\frac{(P\cdot s)\,r\cdot(s-r)}{2P^{2}r^{2}s^{2}}.$$

Each equals the corresponding .tex block to $10^{-13}$ over 400 random configurations.

2. Denominator, phase and measure

  1. Denominator. $p^{+}s^{2}+(k^{+}-p^{+})(s-r)^{2}=k^{+}\bar\xi\,D$, with $D=(r-s)^{2}+M^{2}$ and $M^{2}=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^{2}$.
  2. Phase. $-k\cdot(w'-z)-\xi\,k\cdot(z-x)=-k\cdot(w'-x)+\bar\xi\,k\cdot r$, so the phase is $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\,e^{iq\cdot r}$ with $q=\bar\xi k$.
  3. Measure. $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}=\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}d\xi$, and $\int_{z}=\int d^{2}r$ at fixed $x$.

The constants combine as $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{8\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}\cdot\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{k^{+}} =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}}$, so both parts become

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{a,b}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{k^{+}} \int_{x,y,x',w'}e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\int d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T(r)}{\bar\xi D}\;\mathcal U_{a,b}.$$

3.3 Subtract and add

4. Dimensional regularisation of the UV part

4.1 The scalar master

The general formula is $\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}(r^{2})^{-a}=\pi^{d/2}2^{d-2a}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-a)}{\Gamma(a)}(q^{2})^{a-d/2}$. With $a=1$ and $d=2-2\epsilon$:

$$\int d^{d}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}}=\pi\,\Gamma(-\epsilon)\Big(\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\Big)^{\epsilon},\qquad \Gamma(-\epsilon)=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_{E}+O(\epsilon).$$

The integral has mass dimension $2\epsilon$, so multiply by $\mu^{-2\epsilon}$. Then with $\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}=4\pi\mu^{2}e^{-\gamma_{E}}$:

$$M_{0}\equiv\mu^{-2\epsilon}\!\int d^{d}r\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{r^{2}}=-\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\gamma_{E}+\log\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\Big] =\pi\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{q^{2}}\Big],\qquad\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}\equiv-\frac{1}{\epsilon}.$$

The UV region converges only for $\epsilon<0$, so $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}$ is the positive UV pole. Two checks:

  • $\epsilon M_{0}\to-\pi$ exactly.
  • With a hard cutoff $|r|>\rho$, the same integral is $-\pi\log\frac{q^{2}\rho^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}$. The Bessel quadrature gives $56.9502112$ for $\rho=10^{-4}$, $q=1.3$, and $93.2910566$ for $\rho=10^{-6}$, $q=0.4$, both matching that formula.

4.2 The tensor master

In any dimension $\frac{r^{i}r^{m}}{r^{4}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{\delta^{im}}{r^{2}}-\partial^{i}\frac{r^{m}}{r^{2}}\Big]$. Integrating by parts, $\int e^{iq\cdot r}\partial^{i}f=-iq^{i}\int e^{iq\cdot r}f$. Differentiating the scalar master, $\int e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r^{m}}{r^{2}}=-i\partial_{q^{m}}\big[\pi\Gamma(-\epsilon)(\tfrac{q^{2}}{4\pi})^{\epsilon}\big]\to2\pi i\frac{q^{m}}{q^{2}}$. Therefore

$$M_{2}^{im}\equiv\mu^{-2\epsilon}\!\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r^{i}r^{m}}{r^{4}}=\frac{\delta^{im}}{2}\,M_{0}-\pi\,\hat q^{i}\hat q^{m}.$$

Trace check: $\frac{d}{2}M_{0}-\pi=M_{0}-\epsilon M_{0}-\pi=M_{0}$. In the cutoff scheme the $\hat q\hat q$ coefficient comes out $-3.14159265359$.

4.3 Contract, keeping $d_{\perp}$ symbolic

$$\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}} =\frac{1}{P^{2}s^{2}}\Big[d_{\perp}\xi\bar\xi\Big(\frac{P\cdot s}{2}M_{0}-\pi(P\cdot\hat q)(s\cdot\hat q)\Big)+\Big(\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}\Big)(P\cdot s)\,M_{0}\Big].$$

Now $\frac{d_{\perp}}{2}M_{0}=(1-\epsilon)M_{0}=M_{0}+\pi+O(\epsilon)$. This is the one place where setting $d_{\perp}=2$ too early would lose a finite term. With $\hat q=\hat k$:

$$\int d^{d}r\,e^{iq\cdot r}\,\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}} =\pi\,\mathcal W\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}}\Big] +\pi\,\xi\bar\xi\,\mathcal T,$$ $$\mathcal W=\frac{P\cdot s}{P^{2}s^{2}},\qquad \mathcal T=\frac{P\cdot s-2(P\cdot\hat k)(s\cdot\hat k)}{P^{2}s^{2}}.$$

(sympy: the difference from $C_{\rm UV}\,(P\cdot s)\,M_{0}$ is exactly $\pi\xi\bar\xi[P\cdot s-2(P\cdot\hat q)(s\cdot\hat q)]$.)

5. Part (b) after the $z$ integration

Pulling one $\pi$ into the prefactor turns $\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}$ into $\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{4}}$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\, \Big\{\mathcal W\,C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{\bar\xi^{2}k^{2}}\Big] +\xi\bar\xi\,\mathcal T+\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathcal I_{b}(\xi)\Big\}\,\mathcal U_{b},$$ $$\mathcal I_{b}(\xi)=\int d^{2}r\;e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot r}\Big[\frac{T(r)}{\bar\xi D}-\frac{T_{\rm UV}(r)}{s^{2}}\Big]_{d_{\perp}=2}\qquad(\text{finite, no }\epsilon).$$

How each entry is obtained:

  • $\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}\,d\xi=\int\big(\frac{1}{\bar\xi}-1\big)=\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}-(1-2\lambda)\to L-1$, and the same for $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$.
  • For the log moment of $\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}$, put $v=\bar\xi$: $-2\int_{\lambda}\frac{(1-v)\log v}{v}\,dv=-2\big[F(1)-F(\lambda)\big]$ with $F=\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}v-v\log v+v$, which gives $L^{2}-2$.
  • For $\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$: $-2\Big[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1-\xi)}{\xi}\,d\xi-\int_{0}^{1}\log(1-\xi)\,d\xi\Big]=-2\big[-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+1\big]$.

Quadrature at $\lambda=10^{-7}$ gives $30.40285817$ and $259.3606566$, against $30.40285797$ and $259.3606533$ from the formulas. Also $\int_{0}^{1}\xi\bar\xi\,d\xi=\frac{1}{6}$ for the $\mathcal T$ term. So the closed-form part of (b) is

$$\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\Big\{\mathcal W\Big[\Big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{k^{2}}\Big)+L^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{67}{18}\Big]+\frac{1}{6}\mathcal T\Big\}\,\mathcal U_{b}.$$

6.2 The remainder $\mathcal I_{b}$: can the plus prescription handle it?

The poles in $\xi$ come from $T_{4}$ ($\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$) and $T_{2}$ ($\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}$). The plus prescription $\int\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\big[g(\xi)-g(0)\big]$ is legitimate only if $g(\text{endpoint})$ is finite, so everything depends on how $\mathcal I_{b}$ behaves at each end. $$\mathcal I_{b}\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to0}\ \frac{\beta_{0}}{\xi},\qquad \mathcal I_{b}\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to1}\ \frac{-\pi\mathcal W\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}+\beta_{1}}{\bar\xi}.$$
  • At $\xi\to0$: a simple pole with finite $g(0)=\beta_{0}$. The plus prescription works.
  • At $\xi\to1$: $g(\xi)=\bar\xi\,\mathcal I_{b}$ itself diverges like $\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$. One plus distribution is not enough.

6.3 Where the $\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ comes from

As $\bar\xi\to0$ the $r$ integral has three scales: $|s|$, the mass $M=|s|\sqrt{\xi/\bar\xi}\to\infty$, and the oscillation length $\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\bar\xi k}\gg M$. At fixed $r$ the integrand is just $\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ times something finite; the logarithm is generated entirely at large $|r|$:

  • in $|s|<|r|<M$, $T_{2}$ (whose weight $\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}$ is large) has a $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ tail, giving $\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{2\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$;
  • in $M<|r|<\frac{1}{q}$, the pieces of $T_{3},T_{4},T_{6}$ that grow like $|r|$ give a second $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ tail and another $\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{2\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$.

So the true integrand carries $+\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$. The UV counterterm $\frac{T_{\rm UV}}{s^{2}}$ keeps its $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ all the way from $0$ to $\frac{1}{q}$ and so carries $\frac{2\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ — this is the $\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi^{2}}$ of §5. Their difference, $-\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$, is what $\mathcal I_{b}$ carries, as the numbers show.

6.4 The plus prescription, done correctly

You need these two distributions at $\xi\to1$ (checked against direct cut-off integration), plus the ordinary one at $\xi\to0$:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}=\Big[\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\Big]_{+}+\delta(\bar\xi)\,L,\qquad \frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}=\Big[\frac{\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big]_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta(\bar\xi)\,L^{2},\qquad \frac{1}{\xi}=\Big[\frac{1}{\xi}\Big]_{+}+\delta(\xi)\,L.$$

Applying them:

$$\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;\mathcal I_{b}(\xi)=\beta_{0}L+\beta_{1}L-\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{2}L^{2}+\mathcal F_{b}+O(\Lambda),$$ $$\mathcal F_{b}=\int_{0}^{1}\!d\xi\,\Big[\mathcal I_{b}(\xi)-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\xi}-\frac{\beta_{1}-\pi\mathcal W\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big],\qquad \beta_{0}=\lim_{\xi\to0}\xi\,\mathcal I_{b},\qquad \beta_{1}=\lim_{\xi\to1}\Big[\bar\xi\,\mathcal I_{b}+\pi\mathcal W\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\Big].$$

The bracket in $\mathcal F_{b}$ is integrable at both ends by construction.

6.5 Part (b): final answer

Add the closed-form part (6.1) to $\frac{1}{\pi}\times$(6.4). The two $L^{2}$ terms combine, $L^{2}-\frac{1}{2}L^{2}=\frac{1}{2}L^{2}$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{b}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\bigg\{\mathcal W\Big[\Big(2L-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+\log\frac{\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}}{k^{2}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}L^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{67}{18}\Big] +\frac{1}{6}\,\mathcal T+\frac{\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}}{\pi}\,L+\frac{\mathcal F_{b}}{\pi}\bigg\}\,\mathcal U_{b}$$

7. Part (a): is there a UV divergence? No.

The colour factor vanishes as $z\to x$: $\mathcal U_{a}\propto U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)=r\cdot\partial U^{ce}(x)+O(r^{2})$. Two powers are gained against the $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ of $T_{\rm UV}$:

  1. one from the linear vanishing, against $d^{2}r=|r|\,d|r|\,d\phi$;
  2. one more because the linear term is odd in $\hat r$ while $T_{\rm UV}$ is even, so the angular average kills it.

Check (with a smooth adjoint field $U$): $\rho^{2}\times\langle\text{integrand of (a)}\rangle_{\hat r}$ is $4.18\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-7}$, $10^{-9}$, $10^{-11}$ at $\rho=10^{-2},\dots,10^{-5}$ — it falls as $\rho^{2}$. There is nothing to regularise: no counterterm, no $\overline{\rm MS}$, $d_{\perp}=2$.

8. Part (a): the $p^{+}$ integration

Since there is no subtraction, the whole $r$ integral is finite at each $\xi$:

$$\frac{d^{3}N_{a}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\!d\xi\;\mathcal I_{a}(\xi),\qquad \mathcal I_{a}(\xi)=\int d^{2}r\;e^{i\bar\xi k\cdot r}\,\frac{T(r)}{\bar\xi D}\,\mathcal U_{a}(x+r).$$

The endpoint structure is the same as in (b):

  • $\xi\to0$: a simple pole $\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\xi}$. The only $\frac{1}{\xi}$ is $T_{4}$, whose $r$ integral at $\xi=0$ converges at $r\to0$ (§7), at $r\to s$ ($T_{4}$ vanishes there) and at large $r$ (it oscillates as $e^{ik\cdot r}$). The plus prescription works.
  • $\xi\to1$: the logarithm is generated at large $|r|$, exactly as in §6.3, but now with no counterterm. The whole $+\frac{\pi\mathcal W}{\bar\xi}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ therefore survives, multiplied by the colour factor at large $|r|$: $$\mathcal I_{a}\ \xrightarrow{\xi\to1}\ \frac{\pi\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}+\alpha_{1}}{\bar\xi},\qquad \mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}=\big[U^{ab'}(x')-U^{ab'}(w')\big]\big(-f^{adc}f^{fbc}U^{db}(x)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\big)\rho^{b'}(x')\rho^{f}(y).$$ This assumes $U(z)\to\mathbb 1$ outside the target (the usual finite-target convention). In the test configuration the colour matrix inside $\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}$ has norm $1.30$, so it does not vanish. Two distributions are needed, as in §6.4.
$$\frac{d^{3}N_{a}}{d^{3}k}=\mathcal N\!\int_{x,y,x',w'}\!e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\,L^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}}{\pi}\,L+\frac{\mathcal F_{a}}{\pi}\bigg\}$$ $$\mathcal F_{a}=\int_{0}^{1}\!d\xi\,\Big[\mathcal I_{a}-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\xi}-\frac{\alpha_{1}+\pi\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}}{\bar\xi}\Big],\qquad \alpha_{0}=\lim_{\xi\to0}\xi\,\mathcal I_{a},\qquad \alpha_{1}=\lim_{\xi\to1}\Big[\bar\xi\,\mathcal I_{a}-\pi\mathcal W\,\mathcal U_{a}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\Big].$$

(Here $\alpha_{0,1}$ and $\mathcal F_{a}$ already contain the colour factor, since it sits inside the $r$ integral.)

9. One caveat for the later $x$ integral

The $\delta(\xi)$ terms, $\beta_{0}L$ in (b) and $\alpha_{0}L$ in (a), are finite at fixed $x,y$. But when you later do $\int_{x}$ they are logarithmically divergent at large $|x|$. Near $r\approx s$ with $|s|$ large, $T_{4}$'s residue is $\frac{P\cdot(s-r)}{2P^{2}s^{2}}$, and $\int d^{2}u\,e^{ik\cdot u}\frac{-P\cdot u}{u^{2}}=-2\pi i\frac{P\cdot k}{k^{2}}$. So $$\beta_{0}\ \xrightarrow{|s|\to\infty}\ -\,\frac{i\pi\,(P\cdot k)}{P^{2}k^{2}}\;\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}}{s^{2}},$$ and together with $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-x)}$ the total phase is $e^{-ik\cdot w'}e^{ik\cdot y}$, with no $x$ in it. So $\int d^{2}x\,\beta_{0}\sim\int\frac{d^{2}x}{(y-x)^{2}}$ diverges. Before the $r$ integral, this is a $\frac{1}{x^{2}}$ tail measured at $-0.0457352$ against a predicted $-0.0457352$. Physically it is the $p^{+}\to0$ gluon far from everything else, and its colour factor there ($N_{c}[U(y)-\mathbb 1]$ in (b)) is not the LO one. None of the formulas above is affected, because the $x$ integral is not done here. When you do it, this piece has to be either combined with the other Group I rows, or regulated by keeping $\xi$ nonzero through the $x$ integral.
