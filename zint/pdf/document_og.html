Group II, Row IV — the $p^{+}$ integration

Can the plus prescription be used, and the calculation carried to the end

$S=p^{+}\!+k^{+}$,  $t=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $r=y'-y$,  $\kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r$,  $X=\dfrac{x-y}{(x-y)^{2}}$,  $X'=\dfrac{x'-y'}{(x'-y')^{2}}$,  $\mathcal L=\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}+2-\log\!\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big)$

Answer: yes, and cleanly. The only $p^{+}\to0$ singularity in this row is the $\frac{1}{p^{+}}\delta_{mm'}\delta_{kk'}$ term, and its companion function is the phase $F(p^{+})=e^{-i\kappa p^{+}/k^{+}}$, which is perfectly regular at $p^{+}=0$ with $F(0)=1$. That is exactly the condition the plus prescription needs, so $$\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}F(p^{+}) =\underbrace{F(0)\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}}_{\text{rapidity log}\ \to\ \text{JIMWLK}} +\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!dp^{+}\,\frac{F(p^{+})-F(0)}{p^{+}}}_{\text{finite, stays in the impact factor}}$$ works with no extra subtraction — unlike the running–coupling row, where the companion carried its own $\log\bar\xi$ and the naive $[\,]_{+}$ failed. One warning, which is new: the $\frac1\epsilon$ in $\mathcal L$ is infrared (from $|z|\to\infty$), not ultraviolet — see §5.

1. The contraction, once more

$\mathcal G^{mm'}$ is symmetric, so the $\frac{2}{k^{+}}\big(\delta_{mk'}\delta_{m'k}-\delta_{m'k'}\delta_{km}\big)$ term contracts to zero. Keeping $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ and using $\epsilon\mathcal L\to1$,

$$\delta^{mm'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=\pi(\mathcal L-2),\qquad d_{\perp}X_{m}X'_{m'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=\pi(\mathcal L-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2\pi(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}},$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad \big[\text{bracket}\big]^{kmk'm'}\mathcal G^{mm'}X^{k}X'^{k'} =\pi\bigg[\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}\,A+\Big(\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big)B\bigg],$$ $$\boxed{\;A=(\mathcal L-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}},\qquad B=(\mathcal L-2)(X\!\cdot\!X')\;}$$

Neither $A$ nor $B$ depends on $p^{+}$. That is the whole reason the $p^{+}$ integral factorises into two numbers.

2. The two $p^{+}$ masters

Use $t=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$, so $\sigma\equiv\frac{S}{k^{+}}=1+t$, $dp^{+}=k^{+}dt$, and the phase of your expression is $e^{-i\kappa}e^{-i\kappa t}$. Then $\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}=\frac{1}{k^{+}}\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}}$ and $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}=\frac{1}{k^{+}}\big(t+\frac1t\big)$, so with $\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\cdot\pi\cdot\frac{1}{k^{+}}=\frac12$:

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\,e^{-i\sigma\kappa}\big[\cdots\big] =\frac12\Big[A\,J_{1}+B\,J_{2}\Big],$$ $$J_{1}=e^{-i\kappa}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dt\,\frac{t\,e^{-i\kappa t}}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad J_{2}=e^{-i\kappa}\!\int_{\lambda}^{T}\!\!dt\,\Big(t+\frac1t\Big)e^{-i\kappa t},$$

with $\lambda=\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$ and $T=\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-1$.

$J_{1}$: convergent at both ends, no prescription needed

In the variable $\sigma=1+t$ it is $\int_{1}^{\infty}\big(\frac1\sigma-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\big)e^{-i\kappa\sigma}d\sigma=E_{1}(i\kappa)-E_{2}(i\kappa)$, and $E_{2}(z)=e^{-z}-zE_{1}(z)$ gives

$$\boxed{\;J_{1}(\kappa)=(1+i\kappa)\,E_{1}(i\kappa)-e^{-i\kappa}\;}$$

(checked against oscillatory quadrature to 10 digits at $\kappa=0.4,1.2,3.0$). Small $\kappa$: $J_{1}\to-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-1$.

3. $J_{2}$ and the plus prescription

The criterion

$\int_{\lambda}\frac{dt}{t}g(t)$ may be written as $g(0)\log\frac{1}{\lambda}+\int_{0}\big[\frac1t\big]_{+}g$ if and only if $g(0)$ exists. Here $g(t)=e^{-i\kappa t}$ and $g(0)=1$: the $p^{+}$ dependence sits only in the phase, and the phase is analytic at $p^{+}=0$. Nothing else in the integrand is singular there ($\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}\to0$, $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}\to0$, and $A,B,\mathcal L$ are $p^{+}$–independent). So the prescription applies.

$$\frac{\theta(t-\lambda)}{t}\;=\;\bigg[\frac1t\bigg]_{+}+\delta(t)\,\log\frac{1}{\lambda}+O(\lambda), \qquad \int_{0}^{T}\!dt\,\bigg[\frac1t\bigg]_{+}f(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{T}\!dt\,\frac{f(t)-f(0)}{t}$$

Doing the two pieces

Subtracted (the $[\,]_{+}$ piece), in closed form:

$$\int_{0}^{T}\!\frac{dt}{t}\Big[e^{-i\kappa t}-1\Big]=-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa T)-E_{1}(i\kappa T)$$

(verified to 10 digits). Linear piece, exact and regular at $\kappa\to0$:

$$\int_{0}^{T}\!t\,e^{-i\kappa t}dt=\frac{e^{-i\kappa T}(1+i\kappa T)-1}{\kappa^{2}}\ \xrightarrow[\kappa\to0]{}\ \frac{T^{2}}{2}$$

Assembling, and noting that $\log\frac{T}{\lambda}-\log(i\kappa T)=\log\frac{1}{\lambda}-\log(i\kappa)$ so that the longitudinal cutoff drops out of the logarithm:

$$J_{2}(\kappa)=e^{-i\kappa}\Bigg[\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\Bigg] \;+\;e^{-i\kappa}\Bigg[\frac{e^{-i\kappa T}(1+i\kappa T)}{\kappa^{2}}-E_{1}(i\kappa T)\Bigg]$$

The second bracket oscillates in $\kappa$ with period $\sim1/T$ and is annihilated by the remaining $\int_{y,y'}$ (Riemann–Lebesgue) as $\vee\to\infty$. Keeping only the first,

$$\boxed{\;J_{2}(\kappa)=e^{-i\kappa}\bigg[\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log|\kappa|-i\frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\kappa-\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\bigg]\;}$$
Why the $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+2}}$ term is not a plus–prescription problem but does need the phase. It vanishes at $p^{+}=0$, so it is invisible to $[\,]_{+}$. At the upper end it grows linearly; drop the $e^{-i\kappa p^{+}/k^{+}}$ from your phase and it gives $\frac{\vee^{2}}{2k^{+2}}$, a quadratic divergence in the longitudinal cutoff. With the phase it is the bounded oscillating term above. Your phase — $e^{-ik(y'-y)}e^{-ik(y'-y)p^{+}/k^{+}}$ — is now correct, and this is what it buys you.

4. The finished cross section

$$\frac{d^{3}N^{\rm NLO}}{d^{3}k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\,\frac{N_{c}}{2k^{+}} \int_{x,x',y,y'}\Big\{\,J_{1}(\kappa)\,A\;+\;J_{2}(\kappa)\,B\,\Big\}\, \big[U^{e'c}(y')-U^{e'c}(x')\big]\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\,\rho^{e'}(x')\rho^{e}(x)$$ $$A=(\mathcal L-1)(X\!\cdot\!X')-\frac{2(X\!\cdot\!r)(X'\!\cdot\!r)}{r^{2}},\qquad B=(\mathcal L-2)(X\!\cdot\!X'),$$ $$J_{1}=(1+i\kappa)E_{1}(i\kappa)-e^{-i\kappa},\qquad J_{2}=e^{-i\kappa}\Big[\log\tfrac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-\tfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\Big],$$ $$\mathcal L=\frac1\epsilon+2-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big),\quad r=y'-y,\quad \kappa=k_{\perp}\!\cdot\!r .$$

Written with the rapidity log separated out, which is the form you want for matching onto evolution:

$$J_{1}A+J_{2}B=\underbrace{e^{-i\kappa}(\mathcal L-2)(X\!\cdot\!X')\,\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}}_{\text{rapidity divergent}} \;+\;\underbrace{J_{1}A+e^{-i\kappa}\Big[-\gamma_{E}-\log(i\kappa)-\tfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\Big]B}_{\text{the }[\,]_{+}\text{ remainder: finite, }\Lambda\text{-independent}}$$

5. Read the $\frac1\epsilon$ correctly: it is infrared

This is the one thing that differs from the running–coupling row, and it changes what you do with the pole. The divergence of $\mathcal G^{mm'}$ is not at $z\to y$. Trace it:

$$\delta^{mm'}\mathcal G^{mm'}=\int d^{2}z\,\frac{(y-z)\!\cdot\!(y'-z)}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}$$
  • $z\to y$ (or $z\to y'$): integrand $\sim\frac{1}{|y-z|}$, integrable in two dimensions. No UV.
  • $|z|\!\to\!\infty$: integrand $\to\frac{1}{z^{2}}$, and $\int^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}z}{z^{2}}$ is log divergent. This is the pole.

With a hard cutoff $|z|<R$, the trace is $\pi\log\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}$ — verified numerically:

So in $d_{\perp}=2-2\epsilon$ it is $\epsilon>0$ that regulates it, and

$$\mathcal L=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}+2-\log\big(4\pi^{2}\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}r^{2}\big).$$

Consequences, all of which matter for what you do next:

  • Do not absorb it into the running coupling. $\overline{\rm MS}$ here is only a bookkeeping convention for the constant that rides with $\log r^{2}$; there is no charge renormalisation to do.
  • It must cancel against companion rows. $\int d^{2}z\,\frac{(y-z)\cdot(y'-z)}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}$ is precisely the "real" piece of the BK/JIMWLK dipole kernel, whose large–$z$ log cancels against the $\frac{1}{(y-z)^{2}}$ and $\frac{1}{(y'-z)^{2}}$ pieces — the combination $\frac{(y-y')^{2}}{(y-z)^{2}(y'-z)^{2}}$ is large–$z$ convergent. Those come from the rows of Group II in which $U(z)$ survives the colour reduction. Here $U(z)$ was eliminated, so this row sees the divergence bare.
  • The cross term $\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ is therefore expected, and is resolved by the same cancellation — it is the statement that the JIMWLK kernel itself must be assembled before the rapidity log is absorbed.

6. Small things still to fix in your expression

  1. $\int_{w,w'}$ should be gone — $w,w'$ were eaten by the two delta functions, and $z$ by $\mathcal G^{mm'}$. What is left is $\int_{x,x',y,y'}$.
  2. $\int_{x,y,y'}$ is missing $x'$.
  3. $U^{ce}(x)$ should be $U^{ec}(x)$ — same index order as its partner $U^{ec}(y)$, otherwise the $\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]$ dipole does not form. (Likewise check $U^{e'c}(x')$.)
  4. $\epsilon\to\epsilon_{\rm IR}$ in $\mathcal L$, per §5.

The prefactor $\frac{N_{c}}{k^{+}}$ and the phase $e^{-ik(y'-y)}e^{-ik(y'-y)p^{+}/k^{+}}$ are now both correct.
