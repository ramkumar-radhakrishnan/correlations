The ultraviolet part of the $C_1\otimes B_2$ row

The $z\to x$ divergence, its coefficient, and nothing else

Including the factor–of–2 audit of the $S_1=(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)/r^4$ term

0. Answer to the question first

No factor of 2 is missing. The coefficient of $S_1$ in the bracket is $c_1=d_\perp\xi\bar\xi=2\xi\bar\xi$, and the angular average supplies a compensating $\tfrac12$:

$$\Big\langle\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^{4}}\Big\rangle=\frac{s\cdot P}{d\,r^{2}} \;\xrightarrow{\;d=2\;}\;\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^{2}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c_1\Big\langle S_1\Big\rangle=2\xi\bar\xi\cdot\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^{2}} =\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}} ,$$

which is the $\xi(1-\xi)$ of $P_{gg}$ with coefficient exactly 1, as it must be. Dropping the $\tfrac12$ would give $2\xi(1-\xi)$ and the wrong splitting function; keeping the $2$ of $c_1$ and the $\tfrac12$ of the average is what makes it come out right.

There is a place where a spurious 2 can appear, and it is worth stating explicitly because the earlier notes were sloppy about it: $d_\perp$. The instantaneous $\delta^{im}$ vertex carries an explicit $d_\perp$, and the angular average carries $1/d$. These are the same dimension, so they cancel exactly:

$$c_1\big\langle S_1\big\rangle=\frac{d_\perp}{d}\,\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{s\cdot P}{r^2} =\xi\bar\xi\,\frac{s\cdot P}{r^2}\qquad\text{for } d=d_\perp,\ \text{any }d_\perp .$$

Writing $\tfrac{d_\perp}{2}\xi\bar\xi$ — i.e. keeping $d_\perp$ in the vertex but averaging in exactly two dimensions — is the inconsistent hybrid. It agrees at $d_\perp=2$, so nothing computed so far changes, but it wrongly suggests that the $\xi(1-\xi)$ piece of $P_{gg}$ receives an $O(\epsilon)$ correction from this term. It does not.

1. Where the divergence is

With $r=z-x$, $s=y-x$, $P=x'-w'$, $\xi=p^+/k^+$, $\bar\xi=1-\xi$, and

$$p^+(y-x)^2+(k^+-p^+)(y-z)^2=k^+\bar\xi D,\qquad D=(r-s)^2+M^2,\qquad M^2=\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}s^2 ,$$

the transverse integrand of Eq. (1.1) is singular only at $r\to0$, i.e. $z\to x$. There $D\to D_0\equiv s^2+M^2=s^2/\bar\xi$ and $(r-s)^2\to s^2$, both finite and non–zero, and $e^{iq\cdot r}\to1$. So the whole question is the small–$r$ behaviour of the bracket.

In two dimensions $d^2r=r\,dr\,d\theta$, so a term going like $r^{-n}$ is logarithmically divergent for $n=2$ and convergent for $n\le1$. The bracket contains only $1/r^2$ and $1/r^4$ denominators, so only two kinds of term can diverge:

$$S_1=\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^{4}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_2=\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}} .$$

Everything else is either odd in $r$ at leading order (the $\frac{P\cdot r}{r^2}\sim1/|r|$ structures, integrable), or bounded, or carries a numerator that vanishes at the point where its denominator does. In particular the $(s-r)^2$ structures behave as $1/|r-s|$ near $r=s$ and are integrable there, so $z\to y$ produces no divergence.

2. The angular average — where the 2 lives

In $d$ transverse dimensions $\langle\hat r^{\,i}\rangle=0$ and $\langle\hat r^{\,i}\hat r^{\,j}\rangle=\delta^{ij}/d$, so

$$\Big\langle\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^{4}}\Big\rangle=P_is_j\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d\,r^{2}} =\frac{s\cdot P}{d\,r^{2}},\qquad \Big\langle\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}}\Big\rangle=\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}},\qquad \Big\langle\frac{P\cdot r}{r^{2}}\Big\rangle=0 .$$

Numerically, $\big\langle\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^4}\big\rangle\frac{r^2}{s\cdot P} =0.500000000000$ at $d=2$ (§6A). The three facts to keep straight:

$$\text{bracket}\;\xrightarrow[\ r\to0\ ]{}\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\frac{s\cdot P}{P^{2}r^{2}}, \qquad C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi} =\xi(1-\xi)-2+\frac{1}{\xi(1-\xi)}=\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c},$$ $$P_{gg}(\xi)=2N_c\Big[\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}+\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}+\xi(1-\xi)\Big].$$

The correspondence is one to one: $\xi(1-\xi)$ from the instantaneous $\Theta_1$, $\tfrac{\xi}{1-\xi}$ from $\Theta_2$, $\tfrac{1-\xi}{\xi}$ from $\Theta_4$; $\Theta_3,\Theta_5,\Theta_6$ contribute nothing to the ultraviolet. Checked directly against the tensors of (1.1) — at $\xi=0.3$ the six coefficients come out $(0.21,\,0.4286,\,-0.7,\,2.3333,\,-0.3,\,1)$, i.e. $(\xi\bar\xi,\ \tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi},\ -\bar\xi,\ \tfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi},\ -\xi,\ 1)$ to seven digits (§6B).

4. From an asymptotic statement to an exact one

The angular argument of §2–§3 identifies the divergence but only as $r\to0$. It becomes an exact identity by splitting $S_1$ into its trace and traceless parts,

$$\frac{(P\cdot r)(s\cdot r)}{r^{4}} =\underbrace{\frac{s\cdot P}{2r^{2}}}_{\rm trace} +\underbrace{P_is_j\Big[\frac{r^ir^j}{r^{4}}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{2r^{2}}\Big]}_{\rm traceless},$$

the traceless bracket being orthogonal to every function of $r^2$ alone and hence contributing no logarithm. Since the coefficient of $S_1$ is $2\xi\bar\xi$ and that of $S_2$ is $\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\tfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi}$, and $\tfrac12(2\xi\bar\xi)+\tfrac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\tfrac{\bar\xi}{\xi} =C_{\rm UV}$,

$$P^{2}\times\text{bracket}\;=\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\,\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}}\;+\;\big(\text{integrable at }r=0\big), \qquad\text{for every }r,$$

so the ultraviolet content of the row is one term, and it is unambiguous. (The remainder is written out in the companion note; it is not needed here.)

5. The $z$ integral of the UV term, and the $p^+$ integral

Regulate $r^{2}\to r^{2}+m^{2}$. Only the UV term needs it:

$$\int d^{2}r\;\frac{e^{iq\cdot r}}{(r^{2}+m^{2})\,D} =\frac{2\pi}{D_0}K_0(m|q|)+\text{finite} =\frac{\pi}{D_0}\log\frac{D_0}{m^{2}}+\text{finite}, \qquad D_0=\frac{s^{2}}{\bar\xi}.$$

Inserting this in (1.11), the explicit $\bar\xi$ of $1/D_0=\bar\xi/s^{2}$ cancels the $1/\bar\xi$ of the longitudinal denominator exactly, and the transverse structure collapses to a product of two Weizsäcker–Williams kernels:

$$\frac{1}{\bar\xi}\,\frac{1}{P^{2}}\Big[\text{UV term}\Big] =\pi\,\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_c}\; \frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}\,(y-x)^{2}}\; \log\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\bar\xi\,m^{2}} .$$

Now the $p^+$ integral, $dp^+=k^+d\xi$, $\ell_k=\log(k^+/\Lambda)$, $L=\log\big[(y-x)^2/m^2\big]$; the logarithm above is $L+\log\tfrac{1}{1-\xi}$. All six moments are elementary:

Three readings. The double logarithm comes only from $\Theta_2$ and the $\pi^2/6$ only from $\Theta_4$; it is the instantaneous $\Theta_1$ that turns $-2$ into $-\tfrac{11}{6}$ and $-2$ into $-\tfrac{67}{36}$. And

$$-\frac{11}{6}\times2N_c=-\frac{11N_c}{3}=-b\Big|_{N_f=0},$$

so this row supplies, on its own, the $\tfrac{11N_c}{6}\delta(1-\xi)$ of the regularised splitting function — a running–coupling contribution.

The constant, in the paper's normalisation. $C_{\rm UV}=\xi(1-\xi)-2+\tfrac{1}{\xi(1-\xi)}$ is literally the weight of Eq. (C.10) of arXiv:1610.03453. Because $C_{\rm UV}$ is symmetric under $\xi\leftrightarrow1-\xi$, $\int C_{\rm UV}\log[\xi(1-\xi)]=2\int C_{\rm UV}\log(1-\xi)$, so

$$\int_\lambda^{1-\lambda}\!\!d\xi\,C_{\rm UV}\log\big[\xi(1-\xi)\big] =-\ell_k^{2}+\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3} =-2\left(\frac{\ell_k^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{67}{36}\right),$$

which is (C.10) exactly. That $\tfrac{67}{18}-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}$ is the source of the $\big(\tfrac{67}{9}-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}\big)N_c$ of $K_{JSJ}$, Eqs. (2.64), (2.66), (4.36).

6. Dimensional regularisation

The entire regulator dependence is the single factor $\pi\log(1/m^{2})$. Matching against $\int d^{d_\perp}\!r\,e^{iq\cdot r}/r^{2} =\pi^{1+\epsilon}2^{2\epsilon}\Gamma(\epsilon)(q^{2})^{-\epsilon}$ with $d_\perp=2+2\epsilon_{\rm UV}$ gives

$$\log\frac{1}{m^{2}}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}} +\log\!\big(\pi e^{\gamma_E}\mu^{2}\big),$$

i.e. the $1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}$ that cancels against the $-2/\epsilon$ of the gluon self–energy counterterm. (With $d_\perp=2-2\epsilon$ the pole is $-1/\epsilon$.) As shown in §0 and §2, the $\xi(1-\xi)$ coefficient is $d_\perp/d=1$ exactly, so continuing the transverse plane produces no $O(\epsilon)$ correction to it — the $d_\perp$ of the instantaneous vertex and the $1/d$ of the angular average cancel. (This corrects a remark in the first companion note, which used the hybrid $\tfrac{d_\perp}{2}\xi\bar\xi$ and suggested such a correction existed. Nothing computed at $d_\perp=2$ changes.)
