The four–kernel row: $z$ and $y'$ integrated

Where the divergences are, and the terms separated

$d^{3}N_{\rm NLO}/d^{3}k$ with $\big[\tfrac{\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}-\tfrac{\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}}{p^{+}}-\tfrac{\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}}{k^{+}}\big]$

0. The headline, before anything else

This row has no ultraviolet divergence in the transverse integrations. The whole transverse integrand is a product of four Weizsäcker–Williams kernels,

$$\frac{(y'-z)^{m}}{(y'-z)^{2}}\;\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}\;\frac{(y-w)^{i}}{(y-w)^{2}}\;\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{(x-z)^{2}},$$

and the bracket that contracts them contains only $\delta$'s and longitudinal denominators — no transverse denominator at all. Each kernel is singular like $1/|{\rm sep}|$ at its own coincidence, and no pair of points appears in two kernels, so nowhere is there a $1/{\rm sep}^{2}$. In two dimensions $\int d^{2}\!\rho\;\rho^{-1}\sim\int\!d\rho$ converges, so every coincidence limit — $y'\to z$, $x'\to y'$, $y\to w$, $x\to z$, and any pair of them at once — is integrable.

This is the structural difference from the row we did before. There the light–cone vertex supplied a second $\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}$ on top of the kernel $\frac{(z-x)^{m}}{(z-x)^{2}}$; contracting them gave $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$, and that is what produced the $\log(1/m^{2})$ and the $P_{gg}$ coefficient. Here there is no such doubling.

What the $z$ and $y'$ integrals do contain is a single logarithm, coming from the opposite end — large $|y'-z|$ — cut off by the oscillating phase. It is $\log\frac{1}{Q^{2}c^{2}}$ with $Q$ the momentum conjugate to the integration variable, and it turns into the rapidity logarithms after the $p^{+}$ integral. §5 separates it from the rest.

1. Notation, and the contraction

Write $\xi=p^{+}/k^{+}$, so that

$$\left[\frac{\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}}{p^{+}+k^{+}}-\frac{\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}}{p^{+}} -\frac{\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}}{k^{+}}\right] =\frac{1}{k^{+}}\left[\frac{\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ij}}{1+\xi}-\frac{\delta_{jm}\delta_{ik'}}{\xi} -\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}\right],$$

and abbreviate the four kernels

$$K_1^{m}=\frac{(y'-z)^{m}}{(y'-z)^{2}},\quad K_2^{k'}=\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}},\quad K_3^{i}=\frac{(y-w)^{i}}{(y-w)^{2}},\quad K_4^{j}=\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{(x-z)^{2}} .$$

Contracting the four indices through the three $\delta\delta$ structures collapses the whole transverse structure into three products of two scalar dipole kernels (checked symbolically, §8A):

$$K_1^{m}K_2^{k'}K_3^{i}K_4^{j}\Big[\cdots\Big] =\frac{1}{k^{+}}\Big[\underbrace{\frac{(K_1\!\cdot\!K_2)(K_3\!\cdot\!K_4)}{1+\xi}}_{T_1} \;\underbrace{-\;\frac{(K_1\!\cdot\!K_4)(K_2\!\cdot\!K_3)}{\xi}}_{T_2} \;\underbrace{-\;(K_1\!\cdot\!K_3)(K_2\!\cdot\!K_4)}_{T_3}\Big].$$

The phase is $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}$; both factors depend on $y'$, only the second on $z$.

2. Both integrations reduce to one tensor master

In $T_1,T_2,T_3$ the variable $y'$ (or $z$) appears only through $K_1$ and one of $K_2$ (or $K_4$) — the other two kernels are spectators. Shifting to the separation as integration variable, each case becomes the same object:

$$B^{mj}(Q,c)\;\equiv\;\int d^{2}r\;e^{-iQ\cdot r}\;\frac{r^{m}}{r^{2}}\;\frac{(c-r)^{j}}{(c-r)^{2}} .$$

(In the first case the two phase factors combine, $e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)} =e^{-ik\cdot(z-w)}e^{-i(1+\xi)k\cdot r}$; in the second only the second factor depends on $z$, $e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}=e^{+i\xi k\cdot\rho}$.) Explicitly:

$$\int\! d^{2}y'\,e^{-ik\cdot(y'-w)}e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}\big[T_1{+}T_2{+}T_3\big] =\frac{e^{-ik\cdot(z-w)}}{k^{+}} \left[\frac{(e\!\cdot\!d)\,\delta_{mj}}{(1{+}\xi)e^{2}d^{2}} -\frac{d^{m}e^{j}}{\xi\,d^{2}e^{2}}-\frac{e^{m}d^{j}}{e^{2}d^{2}}\right]B^{mj}\big((1{+}\xi)k,\,x'{-}z\big),$$ $$\int\! d^{2}z\,e^{-i\xi k\cdot(y'-z)}\big[T_1{+}T_2{+}T_3\big] =\frac{1}{k^{+}}\left[-\frac{b^{m}e^{j}}{(1{+}\xi)b^{2}e^{2}} +\frac{(b\!\cdot\!e)\,\delta_{mj}}{\xi\,b^{2}e^{2}}+\frac{e^{m}b^{j}}{e^{2}b^{2}}\right] B^{mj}\big(-\xi k,\,x{-}y'\big).$$

As before, this presumes the Wilson lines $U(y')$, $U(z)$ are taken outside the transverse integral; the algebraic reduction above is pointwise and holds regardless.

3. The master, derived

Feynman–parametrise the two denominators:

$$\frac{1}{r^{2}(c-r)^{2}}=\int_0^1\frac{d\alpha}{\big[\alpha r^{2}+\bar\alpha(c-r)^{2}\big]^{2}} =\int_0^1\frac{d\alpha}{\big[(r-\bar\alpha c)^{2}+\Delta\big]^{2}},\qquad \Delta=\alpha\bar\alpha\,c^{2}.$$

Shift $r=\bar\alpha c+u$, so $r^{m}=u^{m}+\bar\alpha c^{m}$, $(c-r)^{j}=\alpha c^{j}-u^{j}$ and $e^{-iQ\cdot r}=e^{-i\bar\alpha Q\cdot c}e^{-iQ\cdot u}$. The three $u$–integrals are the two–dimensional Bessel transforms, with $\lambda=|Q|\sqrt\Delta$:

$$U_2=\frac{\pi|Q|}{\sqrt\Delta}K_1(\lambda),\qquad V_2^{i}=-i\pi Q^{i}K_0(\lambda),\qquad T_2^{ij}=\pi\Big[\delta^{ij}K_0(\lambda)-\frac{Q^{i}Q^{j}\sqrt\Delta}{|Q|}K_1(\lambda)\Big],$$

and expanding the numerator $(u^{m}+\bar\alpha c^{m})(\alpha c^{j}-u^{j})$:

$$B^{mj}(Q,c)=\int_0^1\!\!d\alpha\;e^{-i\bar\alpha Q\cdot c} \Big[-T_2^{mj}+\alpha\,c^{j}V_2^{m}-\bar\alpha\,c^{m}V_2^{j}+\alpha\bar\alpha\,c^{m}c^{j}\,U_2\Big].$$

The $\alpha$–integral converges at both ends: at $\alpha\to0$ the pieces behave as $\log\alpha$ (integrable) or tend to constants — e.g. $\alpha\bar\alpha c^{m}c^{j}U_2\to\pi c^{m}c^{j}/c^{2}$ — and likewise at $\alpha\to1$. That convergence is the statement of §0: no regulator is needed.

The trace is an object already computed and checked against quadrature in the first note,

$$\delta_{mj}B^{mj}(Q,c)=-\overline{A_7(Q,c)},\qquad A_7(q,s)=\int d^{2}r\;e^{iq\cdot r}\frac{r\cdot(r-s)}{r^{2}(r-s)^{2}},$$

which agrees to $10^{-15}$ (§8C) and fixes the normalisation of the whole construction.

4. Where the logarithm is

$B^{mj}$ is finite at $r\to0$ and $r\to c$; the only large contribution comes from $|r|\gg|c|$, where the integrand tends to $-e^{-iQ\cdot r}r^{m}r^{j}/r^{4}$ and the integral is held together only by the oscillation. Its size is therefore set by $|Q|$, and it grows logarithmically as $|Q||c|\to0$. In the $\alpha$–representation this is visible as $K_0(\lambda)\to\log\frac{2}{\lambda}-\gamma_E$ with $\lambda=|Q||c|\sqrt{\alpha\bar\alpha}$; using $\int_0^1 d\alpha\,\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\bar\alpha}}=1$,

$$B^{mj}\;\longrightarrow\;-\pi\delta^{mj}\Big[\log\frac{2}{|Q||c|}-\gamma_E+1\Big] +\pi\frac{Q^{m}Q^{j}}{Q^{2}}+\pi\frac{c^{m}c^{j}}{c^{2}}\;+\;O(|Q||c|).$$

5. The separation

$$B^{mj}(Q,c)=\underbrace{-\frac{\pi}{2}\,\delta^{mj}\,\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{Q^{2}c^{2}}}_{\textbf{(I) the logarithm}} \;\underbrace{-\;\pi\delta^{mj}+\pi\frac{Q^{m}Q^{j}}{Q^{2}}+\pi\frac{c^{m}c^{j}}{c^{2}}}_{\textbf{(II) finite, }Q\text{- and }c\text{-direction terms}} \;+\;\underbrace{\mathcal B^{mj}(Q,c)}_{\textbf{(III) the rest}}$$

with $\mathcal B^{mj}$ defined as the difference — exactly, by the $\alpha$–integral of §3 minus (I)+(II) — and vanishing as $O(|Q||c|)$. The split is exact; it organises the answer by powers of $|Q||c|$, which is the produced–gluon transverse momentum times the transverse size of the dipole the kernel connects. Numerically (§8D), $\mathcal B$ falls from $1.6$ at $|Q||c|\simeq0.9$ to $1.0\times10^{-2}$ at $|Q||c|\simeq10^{-3}$, against a $B$ of size $\sim24$ there.

Two remarks. Group (I) is a multiple of $\delta^{mj}$, so in the contractions of §2 it is picked up by all three terms: by $T_2$ (or $T_1$ in the $z$–integration) through the trace, giving $-\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{Q^{2}c^{2}}$, and by the other two through $\delta_{mj}d^{m}e^{j}=d\cdot e$. And group (I) has exactly the functional form of the ultraviolet logarithm of the previous row, $\pi\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{q^{2}m^{2}}$ — but here the short–distance scale is replaced by $1/|c|$, i.e. by the dipole size, because the divergence is at large $|r|$, not small. Nothing is regularised; nothing is subtracted.

6. The $p^{+}$ integral, and where the rapidity logarithms come from

With $\xi=p^{+}/k^{+}$ the range $p^{+}\in[\Lambda,\,V-k^{+}]$ becomes $\xi\in[\lambda,\Xi]$, $\lambda=\Lambda/k^{+}$, $\Xi=V/k^{+}-1$, and the three weights are $\frac{1}{1+\xi}$, $-\frac{1}{\xi}$, $-1$. Because $Q$ depends on $\xi$, so does the logarithm:

$\int d^{2}y'$$(1+\xi)k$$-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\delta^{mj}\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{(1+\xi)^{2}k^{2}c^{2}}$ regular at $\xi\to0$; $\sim-\pi\delta^{mj}\log\Xi$ at the top
$\int d^{2}z$$-\xi k$$-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\delta^{mj}\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{\xi^{2}k^{2}c^{2}}$ $\to+\pi\delta^{mj}\log\frac1\xi$ as $\xi\to0$

In the $z$–integration the logarithm therefore hits the $\frac1\xi$ pole of $T_2$ and gives a double rapidity logarithm,

$$\int_{\lambda}^{\Xi}\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\,\log\frac{1}{\xi^{2}} =\log^{2}\lambda-\log^{2}\Xi=\ell_k^{2}-\log^{2}\Xi,\qquad \ell_k=\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda},$$

while the $\xi$–independent parts of groups (I) and (II) give the single logarithm $\int_\lambda^\Xi\frac{d\xi}{\xi}=\ell_k+\log\Xi$. The remaining weights are elementary, $\int\frac{d\xi}{1+\xi}=\log\frac{1+\Xi}{1+\lambda}\to\log\frac{V}{k^{+}}$ and $\int d\xi=\Xi-\lambda$, the last being linear in the upper cutoff — a feature of the $-\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}/k^{+}$ term as written, worth checking against the source before it is used.

Summary of the three groups, as asked.
“UV” terms: there are none. The transverse integrations are ultraviolet finite (§0), so no regulator, no $1/\epsilon$, no $\overline{\rm MS}$ subtraction is called for in this row.
Logarithmic terms: group (I), $-\frac{\pi}{2}\delta^{mj}\log\frac{4e^{-2\gamma_E}}{Q^{2}c^{2}}$, from large $|r|$ — these become the rapidity logarithms $\ell_k$, $\ell_k^{2}$ after the $p^{+}$ integral.
Finite terms: group (II), $-\pi\delta^{mj}+\pi\frac{Q^{m}Q^{j}}{Q^{2}}+\pi\frac{c^{m}c^{j}}{c^{2}}$.
Other terms: group (III), $\mathcal B^{mj}$, exact as the one–parameter integral of §3 and $O(|Q||c|)$.

7. What to check before going further

  • The upper limit $\int_{\Lambda}^{V-k^{+}}dp^{+}$ together with the $-\delta_{im}\delta_{jk'}/k^{+}$ term gives $\int d\xi\cdot1=\Xi-\lambda$, linearly divergent in $V$. Either $V$ is a genuine physical cutoff here or that term pairs with something not shown.
  • The colour structure carries $U(y')$ and $U(z)$, so as written the transverse integrals of §2 are the ones with the Wilson lines taken outside. The reduction to $B^{mj}$ (§1–§2) is pointwise and unaffected.
  • If a $\overline{\rm MS}$ logarithm is expected from this row, it has to come from somewhere other than these four kernels — the power counting of §0 leaves no room for it.
