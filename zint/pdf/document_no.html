The $\mathfrak A^{\dagger}\bar{\mathfrak C}^{\dagger}\times\mathfrak B_{2}$ row

Transverse divergences, the $p^{+}$ integral, and whether the $+$ prescription may be used

$S\equiv p^{+}+k^{+}$,  $A\equiv(x-z)^{2}$,  $B\equiv(x-w)^{2}$,  $C\equiv(z-w)^{2}$,  $\beta\equiv\dfrac{k^{+}B}{A}$,  $\kappa\equiv\dfrac{k\cdot(y'-z)}{k^{+}}$

Verdict. The prefactor, the $\mathfrak C$ delta, the phase split and the tensor contraction are all correct except one term: the second half of your $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ bracket carries the wrong vector (§2). On divergences: no ultraviolet divergence anywhere — all 26 collapsing regions converge (§3) — and, unlike the previous rows, no infrared one either: the $z$ integral converges as it stands, because the phase $e^{-ik\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}(y'-z)}$ damps it (§4). The $p^{+}$ integral is clean, six exponential–integral masters, no pole on the contour (§5). But the answer to your last question is no. The $+$ prescription is precisely what must not be applied naively here: the $p^{+}\to0$ subtraction deletes that phase and creates an infrared divergence the row does not have (§6).

2. The contraction — one term is wrong

With $M^{ij}\equiv\frac{\delta^{ij}(A-B)}{2SC}+\frac{\mathcal K^{ij}}{k^{+}}+\frac{\mathcal P^{ij}}{p^{+}}$,

$$\mathcal K^{ij}=\frac{(x-z)^{j}(z-w)^{i}}{C}-\frac{(x-z)^{j}(x-w)^{i}}{2B},\qquad \mathcal P^{ij}=\frac{(x-w)^{i}(z-w)^{j}}{C}+\frac{(x-w)^{i}(x-z)^{j}}{2A},$$

contracting the $\mathfrak C$ bracket $\big[\delta_{k'm}\delta_{ji}-\frac{S}{p^{+}}\delta_{jm}\delta_{k'i}-\frac{S}{k^{+}}\delta_{im}\delta_{k'j}\big]$ over $i,j$ and multiplying by the $\frac{p^{+}}{S}$ of §1 gives

$$\frac{p^{+}}{S}\,\delta_{k'm}\,\mathrm{tr}\,M\;-\;M^{k'm}\;-\;\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}M^{mk'} .$$

Expanded, this is your six–term bracket. Five terms match exactly, including the two $-\frac{(A-B)}{2C}$ pieces inside your $\frac{p^{+}\delta_{k'm}}{k^{+}S}$ and $\frac{\delta_{k'm}}{S}$ brackets, which come from the $\delta^{ij}$ part of $M$ feeding into the $-\frac{S}{p^{+}}$ and $-\frac{S}{k^{+}}$ structures of $\mathfrak C$. The exception:

$$\text{you wrote}\quad -\frac{1}{p^{+}}\left[\frac{(x-w)^{k'}(z-w)^{m}}{(z-w)^{2}} +\frac{(x-z)^{m}\,\boldsymbol{(z-w)^{k'}}}{2(x-z)^{2}}\right]$$ $$\text{it should be}\quad -\frac{1}{p^{+}}\left[\frac{(x-w)^{k'}(z-w)^{m}}{(z-w)^{2}} +\frac{(x-z)^{m}\,\boldsymbol{(x-w)^{k'}}}{2(x-z)^{2}}\right]$$

because this term is $-\frac{\mathcal P^{k'm}}{p^{+}}$, and both halves of $\mathcal P^{ij}=(x-w)^{i}\big[\frac{(z-w)^{j}}{C}+\frac{(x-z)^{j}}{2A}\big]$ carry the same $(x-w)^{i}$. The error is exactly

$$\Delta=+\frac{1}{p^{+}}\,\frac{(x-z)^{k'}(x-z)^{m}}{2(x-z)^{2}},\qquad \|\Delta\|=\frac{1}{2p^{+}}\ \text{(configuration independent)},$$

which is why the measured discrepancy was the same number, $1.3514=\frac{1}{2\times0.37}$, at every test configuration. With the fix the contraction matches to $3\times10^{-16}$.

2.1 Two smaller fixes

  • The measure. You write $\int_{w,w',y',z,y,x}$. But $w'$ has been eaten by the delta, $y$ does not appear in the integrand, and $x'$ is missing (it is in both $\frac{(x'-y')^{k'}}{(x'-y')^{2}}$ and $\rho^{e'}(x')$). It should be $\int_{x,x',y',z,w}$.
  • Transposes. The bra bracket is the one from the two–$\mathfrak C$ row and carries the same $U^{d'b}(z)$ / $U^{bd'}(z)$ and $U^{e'c'}(y')$ / $U^{c'e'}(x')$ mismatch. The ket bracket $[f^{cbd}U^{ac}(w)\rho^{d}(x)-f^{acd}U^{bc}(z)U^{de}(x)\rho^{e}(x)]$ is right, with $f$ correctly not factored out (the two carry $f^{cbd}$ and $f^{acd}$).

3. No ultraviolet divergence

All 26 subsets of $\{x,x',y',z,w\}$ collapsed at scale $\rho$ against $\rho^{2(n-1)}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$: every one converges. The region you had in mind, $x,z,w\to$ a point where $D=p^{+}A+k^{+}B$ vanishes like $\rho^{2}$, gives integrand $\rho^{-2}$ against a three–point measure $\rho^{3}d\rho$.

Why the loop has no transverse UV. The loop gluon carries $p^{+}$ and is emitted at $z$ in the ket ($\mathfrak B_{2}$) and absorbed at $z$ in the bra — the $\mathfrak C$ delta puts the bra's emission point $w'$ at $\frac{Sy'-p^{+}z}{k^{+}}$, so the loop closes at the same transverse point on both sides. There is no separation to shrink, hence no short–distance singularity. The loop's large–momentum behaviour is carried entirely by the $p^{+}$ integration instead (§5), where the $h=p^{+}$ master grows like $\vee$.

5. The $p^{+}$ integral

Writing $D=A(p^{+}+\beta)$ with $\beta=\frac{k^{+}B}{A}$, and noting $A,B>0$ so $\beta>0$ and there is no pole on the contour, the $p^{+}$ dependence reduces to six masters. Because of the phase they are exponential integrals, not logarithms:

$$\mathcal M[h]=\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!\!dp^{+}\,\frac{h(p^{+})\,e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}}{p^{+}A+k^{+}B}, \qquad h\in\Big\{\tfrac{p^{+}}{S^{2}},\tfrac{p^{+}}{S},\tfrac{1}{S},1,\tfrac{1}{p^{+}},p^{+}\Big\},$$ $$\int\frac{e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}}{p^{+}+a}\,dp^{+}=e^{i\kappa a}\,\mathrm{Ei}\big(-i\kappa(p^{+}+a)\big),$$ $$\mathcal M[1]=\frac{e^{i\kappa\beta}}{A}\mathrm{Ei}\big(-i\kappa(p^{+}+\beta)\big),\qquad \mathcal M\Big[\frac{1}{p^{+}}\Big]=\frac{1}{A\beta}\Big[\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa p^{+}) -e^{i\kappa\beta}\mathrm{Ei}\big(-i\kappa(p^{+}+\beta)\big)\Big],$$ $$\mathcal M[p^{+}]=\frac{1}{A}\Big[\frac{e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}}{-i\kappa}-\beta\,e^{i\kappa\beta}\mathrm{Ei}\big(-i\kappa(p^{+}+\beta)\big)\Big],\qquad \mathcal M\Big[\frac{1}{S}\Big]=\frac{e^{i\kappa k^{+}}\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa(p^{+}\!+\!k^{+}))-e^{i\kappa\beta}\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa(p^{+}\!+\!\beta))}{A(\beta-k^{+})},$$

and $\mathcal M[\frac{p^{+}}{S}]$, $\mathcal M[\frac{p^{+}}{S^{2}}]$ follow from the same residues (the latter with a double pole at $p^{+}=-k^{+}$). All six against quadrature at $A=0.62$, $B=1.45$, $\kappa=0.83$, $\Lambda=10^{-7}$, $\vee=40$:

Only $h=\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ is singular as $\Lambda\to0$, and what it produces is not $\log\Lambda$:

$$\mathrm{Ei}(-i\kappa\Lambda)\ \longrightarrow\ \gamma_{E}+\log\big(|\kappa|\Lambda\big) -\frac{i\pi}{2}\mathrm{sgn}\,\kappa =\gamma_{E}+\log\Lambda+\log\frac{|k\cdot(y'-z)|}{k^{+}}-\frac{i\pi}{2}\mathrm{sgn}\,\kappa$$

(checked to six digits at $\kappa=0.83,2.4$ and $\Lambda=10^{-6},10^{-8}$). The rapidity logarithm arrives welded to a transverse logarithm.

6. Can you use the $+$ prescription? — No, not naively

At fixed transverse points there is nothing wrong with the identity. With $g(p^{+})=\frac{e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}}{p^{+}A+k^{+}B}$ and $g(0)=\frac{1}{k^{+}B}$,

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{P}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}\,g(p^{+}) =g(0)\log\frac{P}{\Lambda}+\int_{0}^{P}\frac{dp^{+}}{p^{+}}\big[g(p^{+})-g(0)\big]$$

is exact. The damage is done by what happens next.

$g(0)$ deletes the $z$–phase. The only thing damping the $z$ integral is $e^{-i\kappa p^{+}}$ with $\kappa=\frac{k\cdot(y'-z)}{k^{+}}$; setting $p^{+}=0$ removes it. Measured at large $|z|$:

The subtraction term sits on a flat plateau, equal to nine digits to

$$-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(x'-y')\cdot(x-w)}{(x'-y')^{2}\,k^{+}(x-w)^{2}}=-0.212517467,$$

so its $z$ integral is logarithmically divergent. The unsubtracted term at any $p^{+}\neq0$ falls away. The prescription manufactures an infrared divergence that the row does not have.

The mechanism is that the damping scale depends on $p^{+}$:

$$|z|_{\rm max}\sim\frac{k^{+}}{|k|\,p^{+}}:\qquad p^{+}=10^{-1}\to12,\quad 10^{-2}\to124,\quad 10^{-3}\to1240,\quad 10^{-4}\to12400,$$

visible in the table above as the $p^{+}=0.01$ column decaying later than the $p^{+}=0.37$ one. The $z$ and $p^{+}$ integrations do not commute.

6.1 What to do instead

  1. There is no UV step. Nothing to regulate before the $p^{+}$ integral.
  2. Do not split off $\log\frac{\vee-k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ at fixed $z$. Either keep the $z$ and $p^{+}$ integrals together, or introduce $d=2-2\epsilon$ on $z$ first — not because the row needs it, but because it is what makes the subtraction term finite and the split legitimate.
  3. Then the prescription is safe, but the logarithm is not the usual one. By §5 it comes as $\log(|\kappa|\Lambda)$, so the $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ multiplies a $z$ integral that is itself log divergent, while its partner $\log|\kappa|\sim\log|z|$ against $\frac{d^{2}z}{|z|^{2}}$ supplies a second logarithm. The row therefore produces a mixed $\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ and a $\log^{2}$, not a clean single rapidity logarithm riding on a leading–order structure. Plan the absorption into evolution accordingly.
