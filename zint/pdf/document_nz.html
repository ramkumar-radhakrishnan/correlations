1. Prefactor — exact

With $\mathfrak A^{(1)}$ evaluated at $p^{+}\!+k^{+}$ and $\mathfrak C_{1}$ at $k^{+}_{\rm arg}=S$ (so $\sqrt{p^{+}(S-p^{+})}/S=\sqrt{p^{+}k^{+}}/S$):

$$|\mathfrak A^{(1)}|^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi^{2}S},\qquad |\mathfrak C_{1}|^{2}=\frac{g^{2}p^{+}k^{+}}{8\pi^{2}S^{3}},\qquad \frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}|\mathfrak A|^{2}|\mathfrak C|^{2}=\frac{g^{4}}{32\pi^{7}}\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{4}}$$

= your $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g^{4}}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{p^{+}k^{+}}{S^{4}}$, ratio $1$. The four phases $(-i)(-1)(-1)(+i)=1$, so the row is real.

The $\Theta(k^{+}_{\rm arg}-p^{+}-\Lambda)$ becomes $\Theta(k^{+}-\Lambda)$ here, so it imposes nothing on $p^{+}$ — the limits are the ones you wrote.

2. The $w,w'$ integration — and the cancellation that makes it work

$\delta^{(2)}\big[y-w+\frac{p^{+}}{S}(w-z)\big]$ has $w$–coefficient $-\frac{k^{+}}{S}$, so

$$w=\frac{Sy-p^{+}z}{k^{+}},\qquad \int d^{2}w\,\delta^{(2)}=\Big(\frac{S}{k^{+}}\Big)^{2}\ \text{per delta} \;\Rightarrow\;\Big(\frac{S}{k^{+}}\Big)^{4}\ \text{for both.}$$

But the $\mathfrak C$ kernel is not inert under the substitution: $w-z=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y-z)$, so

$$\frac{(w-z)^{m}}{(w-z)^{2}}=\frac{k^{+}}{S}\,\frac{(y-z)^{m}}{(y-z)^{2}},\qquad \text{two of them}\;\Rightarrow\;\Big(\frac{k^{+}}{S}\Big)^{2}.$$
net factor $=\Big(\dfrac{S}{k^{+}}\Big)^{4}\!\times\!\Big(\dfrac{k^{+}}{S}\Big)^{2}=\dfrac{S^{2}}{k^{+2}}$  —  which is what you used.

(Worth flagging because using $\frac{S^{4}}{k^{+4}}$ alone, or forgetting the Jacobian entirely, both give the wrong answer; only the combination is right.) And

$$w'-w=\frac{S}{k^{+}}(y'-y)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{-ik(w'-w)}=e^{-i\frac{k}{\zeta}\cdot(y'-y)}=e^{-ik\cdot r}\,e^{-i\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}k\cdot r},$$

exactly your split. This is the fragmentation signature: the hard factor is evaluated at the parent's transverse momentum $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, not at $k_{\perp}$.

4. The Wilson–line reduction — right answer, wrong index orders

With uniform orderings, i.e.

$$\text{ket}=f^{cda}\,U^{db}(z)\big[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ec}(x)\big]\rho^{e}(x),\qquad \text{bra}=f^{c'd'a}\,U^{d'b}(z)\big[U^{e'c'}(y')-U^{e'c'}(x')\big]\rho^{e'}(x'),$$

the reduction runs $\sum_{b}U^{d'b}(z)U^{db}(z)=\delta^{d'd}$, then $\sum_{a,d}f^{c'da}f^{cda}=N_{c}\delta^{c'c}$, giving $N_{c}\big[U(y')-U(x')\big]\big[U(y)-U(x)\big]$ — verified to $3\cdot10^{-15}$. Your quoted answer is correct.

But as literally written it does not follow — the discrepancy is $5.11$ in norm. Two problems, both the same family:
  1. The $z$ lines are transposed between the two terms: $U^{db}(z)$ in term 1 versus $U^{bd}(z)$ in term 2 (and $U^{d'b}$ vs $U^{bd'}$ in the bra). Then the cross terms give $\sum_{b}U^{d'b}U^{bd}=[U(z)U(z)]^{d'd}$, which is not a delta, so no $N_{c}$. Your top–line expression has $U^{bd}(z)$ in both ket terms, which is consistent — the expanded version is where it broke.
  2. The $y$ and $x$ lines are transposed: $U^{ec}(y)$ versus $U^{ce}(x)$. This one matters more than bookkeeping: $[U(y)-U(x)^{T}]$ does not vanish at $y=x$ ($\|U(x)-U(x)^{T}\|=1.24$), and that vanishing is exactly what tames the $y\to x$ end of the $\mathfrak A$ kernel. Your final line still reads $[U^{ec}(y)-U^{ce}(x)]$; the second must be $U^{ec}(x)$.

The $z$–dependence after the Wilson–line reduction is only in the two $\mathfrak C$ kernels ($U(z)$ has cancelled), so the $z$ integral is the master

7. The fragmentation variables

$$p^{+}=k^{+}\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta},\qquad dp^{+}=-\frac{k^{+}}{\zeta^{2}}\,d\zeta,\qquad \int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}\!dp^{+}=k^{+}\!\!\int_{k^{+}/\vee}^{k^{+}/(k^{+}+\Lambda)}\!\!\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$$

The $\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}$ measure, the hard factor at $k_{\perp}/\zeta$, and $P_{gg}(\zeta)\log\mu^{2}$ are the three hallmarks of a fragmentation function — all three present. The lower limit $\zeta_{\min}=k^{+}/\vee$ is kinematic; the upper limit $\zeta\to1$ is $p^{+}\to\Lambda$.

8. Can you use the $+$ prescription? Yes — here, unlike in the $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ row

The only $\zeta\to1$ singularity is $\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}$ inside $C_{\rm UV}$. Write the $\zeta$–integral as $\int d\zeta\,\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}F(\zeta)$ with everything else in $F$. The $+$ prescription is legitimate iff $F$ is smooth at $\zeta=1$ with $F(1)$ finite.

After the $z$ integral, the only remaining $\zeta$–dependence in $F$ is the phase $e^{-ik\cdot r/\zeta}$:

Analytic at $\zeta=1$, and $F(1)=e^{-ik\cdot r}\,\mathcal L\,\mathcal X$ — the LO structure. Nothing is deleted and nothing becomes singular.

Contrast with the $|\mathfrak B_{2}|^{2}$ row, where I told you not to apply it naively. There the $z$ integral had not been done, so setting the subtraction point killed the $z$–phase and manufactured an IR divergence the row did not have. Here the $z$ integral is already done, $z$ is gone, and the subtraction point is harmless. The difference is not the prescription — it is what is left in $F$ when you evaluate it at the endpoint.

The identity, verified numerically (agreement $1.6\cdot10^{-7}$ at $\delta=10^{-7}$):

$$\int_{a}^{1-\delta}\!\!d\zeta\,\frac{\zeta F(\zeta)}{1-\zeta} =\int_{a}^{1}\!d\zeta\,\frac{\zeta F(\zeta)-F(1)}{1-\zeta}\;+\;F(1)\,\log\frac{1-a}{\delta}, \qquad a=\frac{k^{+}}{\vee},\ \ \delta=\frac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad \text{isolated log}\;=\;F(1)\,\log\frac{k^{+}(\vee-k^{+})}{\vee\,\Lambda}.$$

So the $+$ prescription does exactly the right job: it separates a finite, $\Lambda$–independent integral from one term proportional to the LO structure times $\log\frac{1}{\Lambda}$ — the piece that goes into the evolution.

Three conditions.
  1. Do the $z$ integral first. The coefficient of $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is $\zeta$–dependent and blows up at $\zeta=1$; $+$–subtracting in $p^{+}$ before renormalising mixes an endpoint subtraction into an unrenormalised pole.
  2. Do not use $\Lambda$ and the $+$ prescription for the same singularity. Once you $+$–subtract, the $\Lambda$–dependence lives only in the explicit $F(1)\log\frac{k^{+}(\vee-k^{+})}{\vee\Lambda}$ above. Keeping $\Lambda$ in the subtracted integral as well double counts.
  3. Your range is $[\zeta_{\min},1]$, not $[0,1]$. The textbook $\int_{0}^{1}\big[\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\big]_{+}d\zeta=0$ does not hold here; the compensating term is the $\log\frac{1-a}{\delta}$ written above. Either use that form, or extend to $[0,1]$ and add the $\log(1-\zeta_{\min})$ back by hand.
