Part $2a$: the $p^{+}$ integration by plus prescription

Yes — but you need it at both endpoints, and this row has no DGLAP in it

$\eta=\dfrac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}$,  $\bar\eta=1-\eta$,  $A=(y-x)^{2}$,  $B=(y-z)^{2}$,  $\kappa=k\!\cdot\!(z-x)$,  $\lambda=\dfrac{\Lambda}{k^{+}}$,  $h(\eta)=\dfrac{e^{-i\eta\kappa}}{\eta A+\bar\eta B}$

Yes, and the answer is cleaner than Row IV's in one way and messier in another. The plus prescription is legitimate at both ends — the companion function $h(\eta)$ is smooth on the whole of $[0,1]$, with $h(0)=\frac{1}{B}$ and $h(1)=\frac{e^{-i\kappa}}{A}$ both finite, and there is no pinch because $\eta A+\bar\eta B\geq\min(A,B)>0$. But this row has two endpoint poles, not one: $\mathcal T_{4}$ goes as $-\frac{1}{\eta}$ at $p^{+}\to0$ and $\mathcal T_{2}$ as $-\frac{1}{\bar\eta}$ at $p^{+}\to k^{+}$. So you get two $\delta$-type terms and $2\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ worth of rapidity logarithm. And do not change variables to $\xi=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+p^{+}}$: there is no fragmentation structure here at all.

1. Where the $p^{+}$ integrand is singular

The six tensors of eq. (3) are $p^{+}$-independent. All the $p^{+}$ dependence is in three places: the six weights, the denominator $k^{+}\big[\eta A+\bar\eta B\big]$, and the phase $e^{-i\eta\kappa}$.

Why two, when Row IV had one. This is a loop topology: $\mathfrak C_{1}$ splits the measured $k^{+}$ into $p^{+}$ and $k^{+}\!-p^{+}$, and $\mathfrak B_{2}$ re-merges them. Both daughters can go soft, so both ends are singular and $p^{+}$ is bounded, $0\leq p^{+}\leq k^{+}$ — which is exactly why your limits are $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}$ and not $\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}$. In Row IV the measured gluon kept all of $k^{+}$ and the emitted one ran up to $\vee-k^{+}$, so there was one soft end only.

2. The prescription is legitimate at both ends

The test is the one from before: $\int\frac{d\eta}{\eta}\big[g(\eta)-g(0)\big]$ needs $g(0)$ finite. Here the residue of each pole is a ($p^{+}$-independent) tensor times $h(\eta)$, and

$$\eta A+\bar\eta B\;\geq\;\min(A,B)\;>\;0\quad\text{for all }\eta\in[0,1] \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad h\ \text{smooth on }[0,1],$$ $$h(0)=\frac{1}{(y-z)^{2}}\quad\text{finite},\qquad h(1)=\frac{e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)}}{(y-x)^{2}}\quad\text{finite}.$$

The denominator is linear in $\eta$ and both $A$ and $B$ are positive, so there is no pinch anywhere in the interval — that is the whole content of the check, and it passes.

3. The result

$$\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}\!\!\frac{dp^{+}}{2\pi}\;(\cdots) =\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\!d\eta\bigg\{ -\Big[\tfrac{1}{\eta}\Big]_{+}\bar\eta\,h(\eta)\,\mathcal T_{4} -\Big[\tfrac{1}{1-\eta}\Big]_{+}\eta\,h(\eta)\,\mathcal T_{2} +h(\eta)\Big[\eta\bar\eta\,\mathcal T_{1}+\bar\eta\,\mathcal T_{3}+\eta\,\mathcal T_{5}-\mathcal T_{6}\Big]\bigg\}$$ $$-\;\frac{k^{+}}{2\pi}\,\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}\, \bigg[\frac{\mathcal T_{4}}{(y-z)^{2}}+\frac{e^{-ik\cdot(z-x)}\,\mathcal T_{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}\bigg]$$

Both $\delta$ terms carry the same logarithm, because $\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{d\eta}{\eta}=\int_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\frac{d\eta}{1-\eta} =\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\to\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$. Checked against direct numerical integration:

4. The two poles are mirror images — but you cannot fold them

Write the total phase as $\exp\!\big[-ik\cdot(w'-\bar\eta z-\eta x)\big]$ (which is what $e^{-ik\cdot(w'-z)}e^{-i\eta k\cdot(z-x)}$ is). Then the whole transverse-times-phase factor is invariant under $x\leftrightarrow z$ together with $p^{+}\to k^{+}-p^{+}$:

$$\max\big|F(x,y,z;p^{+})-F(z,y,x;k^{+}\!-p^{+})\big|=1.6\times10^{-13} \qquad(\text{200 configurations, }\max|F|=586).$$
But the colour factors are not symmetric under $x\leftrightarrow z$ — not the original $f^{adc}f^{ebf}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(y)$, and not $2a$'s $-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)\big[U^{ce}(z)-U^{ce}(x)\big]$ or $2b$'s $N_{c}\big[U^{af}(y)-U^{af}(x)\big]$. So you may not use the symmetry to fold one pole onto the other and double it. Keep both terms explicitly.

For $2b$, regulate the transverse UV first, then do $p^{+}$.

6. One thing not to do: no $\xi$ change of variable here

In Row IV the substitution $\xi=\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}+p^{+}}$ produced the fragmentation measure $\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}$, a hard factor at $\frac{k}{\xi}$, and $\mathcal P_{gg}(\xi)$. None of that happens here. In this row $p^{+}\leq k^{+}$, so $\xi$ would only cover $[\tfrac12,1]$, and the measured momentum never leaves $k$: the phase is $\exp[-ik\cdot(w'-\bar\eta z-\eta x)]$, with no $\frac{k}{\xi}$ anywhere. There is no $\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{2}}$ and no convolution with a hard factor at a shifted momentum, so this row carries no DGLAP. It is a virtual correction: everything it produces is $\delta(1-\xi)$-like in the factorisation sense. $\mathcal P_{gg}$ did appear in it, but as the transverse UV residue of part $2b$, not as a splitting convolution in $p^{+}$.

So the natural variable is $\eta=\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\in[0,1]$, the natural form is the two-sided plus prescription above, and the output is $2\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ times two $z$- and $x$-dependent coefficients plus a finite remainder. The $\log\frac{k^{+}}{\Lambda}$ terms are the rapidity divergence of the loop — they belong with the JIMWLK evolution of the target, alongside Row IV's, and their $\Lambda$ must cancel there.
