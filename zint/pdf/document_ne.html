The third row — this one does have a UV divergence

And its coefficient is exactly $P_{gg}$

$\xi\equiv p^{+}/k^{+}$,  $\bar\xi\equiv1-\xi$,   $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\equiv\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi}=\frac{P_{gg}(\xi)}{2N_{c}}$

0. Verdict

Yes — there is a transverse ultraviolet divergence, and this is the first row in the series that has one. It is logarithmic, at $z\to x$, and its coefficient is

$$\frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\;\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}\,(y-x)^{2}},$$

verified to $10^{-12}$ on random geometries. So this row contributes to DGLAP, unlike the three before it — and you must not apply the $+$ prescription to it (§4).

The expression itself checks out: prefactor, $k^{+}$ powers, Feynman denominator, delta function and limits all reproduce from the definitions (§1).

The decisive structural difference. Here $\mathfrak C$ is used with first argument $k^{+}$ and second $p^{+}$, so its index bracket keeps both $\frac{k^{+}}{p^{+}}$ and $\frac{k^{+}}{k^{+}-p^{+}}$. In the earlier rows the first argument was $p^{+}+k^{+}$, which turned the second into the finite $\frac{p^{+}+k^{+}}{k^{+}}$ and killed the collinear endpoint. Consistently, the limits here are the symmetric $\int_{\Lambda}^{k^{+}-\Lambda}$ — both endpoints cut — rather than $\int_{\Lambda}^{\vee-k^{+}}$.

2. The ultraviolet divergence

Where it comes from. The new $\mathfrak B_{2}$ is the gluon vertex: it carries $\frac{1}{(x-z)^{2}}$, $\frac{1}{(x-w)^{2}}$ and $\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}$ — squared denominators, on separations that $\mathfrak C$'s $\frac{(z-x)^{m}}{(z-x)^{2}}$ already acts on. Contracting, for instance,

$$\frac{(z-x)^{m}}{(z-x)^{2}}\cdot\frac{(y-x)^{i}(x-z)^{m}}{(x-z)^{2}} =-\frac{(y-x)^{i}}{(z-x)^{2}},$$

a true $1/\rho^{2}$. The earlier rows had only single kernels and could never build one.

3. The coefficient is exactly $P_{gg}$

The bracket carries the $\xi$–structures $1,\;\frac{1}{\bar\xi^{2}},\;\frac{1}{\xi},\; \frac{1}{\xi^{2}},\;\frac{1}{\bar\xi},\;\frac{1}{\xi\bar\xi}$, and the prefactor supplies an overall $\xi\bar\xi$. Multiplying:

$$\xi\bar\xi,\quad\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi},\quad\bar\xi,\quad\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi},\quad\xi,\quad1$$

— precisely the ingredients of $C_{\rm UV}=\xi\bar\xi+\frac{\xi}{\bar\xi}+\frac{\bar\xi}{\xi} =\xi\bar\xi+\frac{1}{\xi\bar\xi}-2$, the leftovers $\xi+\bar\xi+1=2$ supplying the $-2$. And they assemble into exactly that combination:

The ratio is constant to six digits over four decades at both endpoints, and symmetric under $\xi\leftrightarrow\bar\xi$ as $P_{gg}$ must be. The constant is the transverse structure:

$$\text{integrand}\;\xrightarrow[\;z\to x\;]{}\; \frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\;\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}\,(y-x)^{2}}$$

checked against the full expression to $10^{-12}$ on five random geometries at two values of $\xi$.

This is the same shape as the very first row we did, $C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\frac{s\cdot P}{r^{2}}$: a product of two Weizsäcker–Williams kernels dotted together, times the splitting function, over the squared separation that is going to zero.

4. So: do not use the $+$ prescription here

The objection is different from before, and more fundamental. In the earlier rows the $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ was a standalone soft pole, and the obstacle to splitting was that the subtraction term was infrared divergent. Here the $\frac{1}{\xi}$ is one half of $P_{gg}$, tied by the $\xi\leftrightarrow\bar\xi$ symmetry to the $\frac{1}{\bar\xi}$ pole at the other endpoint. A $+$ prescription applied to $\frac{1}{p^{+}}$ alone would tear $C_{\rm UV}$ apart and destroy the DGLAP structure.

What to do instead — the treatment we already built

  1. Split off the UV piece exactly, as in the first note: $T=C_{\rm UV}(\xi)\frac{(x'-w')\cdot(y-x)}{(x'-w')^{2}(y-x)^{2}}\frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}+T_{\rm fin}$, with $T_{\rm fin}$ free of the $1/\rho^{2}$.
  2. Regulate the $\frac{1}{(z-x)^{2}}$, either in momentum space with dimensional regularisation and $\overline{\rm MS}$, or — equivalently and more simply — with the position–space cutoff $|z-x|>r_{0}$, $r_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}/\mu$.
  3. Do the $\xi$ integral over the symmetric range, which is what the $\Theta$ function gives you: $$\int_{\Lambda/k^{+}}^{1-\Lambda/k^{+}}\!d\xi\;C_{\rm UV}(\xi)=2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6},$$ and $\big(2\ell_{k}-\frac{11}{6}\big)\times2N_{c}=4N_{c}\ell_{k}-\frac{11N_{c}}{3}=4N_{c}\ell_{k}-b$ at $N_{f}=0$: the constant is the beta–function coefficient, i.e. coupling renormalisation. That is the DGLAP / running–coupling structure, and it is exactly what the earlier rows lacked.
