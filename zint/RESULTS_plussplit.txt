A. THE SPLIT, TERM BY TERM
   bracket middle term is  -delta_jm delta_ik' / p+ , and
     -int_L^P dp+/p+ g  =  -int_L^P dp+ [g(p+)-g(0)]/p+  -  g(0) log(P/L)
   with the overall minus in front of the row, the log piece comes out with a PLUS:
     (-1) x (-1) x g(0) log(P/L)  =  + g(0) log(P/L)   <- matches your second term's sign
   prefactor: (1/4pi^4) x (1/2pi) = 1/(8 pi^5)  ->  1/(8*pi**5)
   so the 1/(8 pi^5) and the + sign of your second term are CORRECT.

   delta_jm delta_ik' contracted on (y'-z)^m (x'-y')^k' (y-w)^i (x-z)^j :
      m=j : (y'-z).(x-z)     i=k' : (x'-y').(y-w)
   which is exactly the numerator you wrote in the second term -> CORRECT.

   ==> BUT the second term still carries the whole bracket
       [ d_k'm d_ij/(p++k+) - d_jm d_ik'/p+ - d_im d_jk'/k+ ].
       That is a leftover: the contraction has ALREADY been done (it is in the
       numerator), the 1/p+ has ALREADY been integrated (it is the log), and there
       is no dp+ left for a free p+ to live in.  DELETE the bracket.

D. SO: DOES THE FIRST TERM DIVERGE?
   [1/p+]_+ acts as  [g(p+) - g(0)]/p+ .  g(0) is the integrand with phase = 1.
   Its z-integrand is S2 = (y'-z).(x-z)/[(y'-z)^2(x-z)^2], the BK REAL kernel,
   with NO phase left to cut it.  int rho drho (-1/rho^2) = -log : DIVERGENT,
   for every value of p+.  The same divergence sits in your second term
   (it IS g(0)).  They cancel only in the sum, i.e. only if you never split.
