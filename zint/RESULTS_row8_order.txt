============================================================================
2.  WHY -- the p+/transverse coupling
============================================================================
   the ONLY place p+ meets a transverse variable is the phase
       e^{-i k.(y'-y) p+/k+}      kappa = k.(y'-y)/k+ = -1.192000
   it involves y and y' ONLY.  z, x, x' never see p+ at all, so the whole
   z, x, x' structure is a SPECTATOR factor that pulls out of the p+ integral.
   contrast the earlier rows, where p+ sat inside the z phase and the
   p+ -> 0 subtraction stripped it, exposing a bare 1/z^2 tail.

============================================================================
3.  IS THE g(0) SUBTRACTION TERM STILL TRANSVERSE-SAFE?
============================================================================
   at p+ = 0 the phase becomes e^{-i k.(y'-y)}, NOT 1: the LO oscillation survives.
   large-|y| angular average x R^2, subtraction term (p+ = 0):
      R = 1e+02      |A| R^2 = 2.41391e-02
      R = 1e+03      |A| R^2 = 1.03785e-02
      R = 1e+04      |A| R^2 = 8.79211e-03
   falls as R^{-1/2} (Bessel) -> convergent.  The subtraction is safe.

============================================================================
4.  THE PRICE OF LEAVING z UN-INTEGRATED
============================================================================
   the 2/k+ term, pointwise in z:  max |integrand| = 173.73851  (NOT zero)
   after int d^2z                                  = 1.489e-03  (zero)
   so if you keep z un-integrated you must carry a term that is only
   zero under the integral -- harmless, but do not drop or approximate it
   before integrating.

   and the z integral is still IR log divergent:  tr T = pi log(R^2/r^2) = 33.2415 at R = 300
   -> the + prescription does NOT remove the need to regulate z.
