\section*{Two-$\rho$ terms, limiting case ($\Lambda\to0$, $\vee\to\infty$): all groups added}
\noindent Starting point: \texttt{Two\_rho\_limiting.tex}. The $\rho$'s are operators and their order is kept: $\rho(x^{\prime})$ always stands to the left. The Wilson lines commute with the $\rho$'s. Nothing is averaged over the projectile; all colour indices are explicit. Groups I and III come with ``$+$c.c.'' (hermitian conjugate); Group II does not. The upper limits $\vee-k^{+}$ and $\frac{\vee}{k^{+}}-1$ are kept as in the file; in the limit they go to $\infty$.

\noindent\textbf{Checks.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The two colour identities above were checked with exact SU(3) adjoint Wilson lines and non-commuting random matrices for $\rho$; the residuals are $5\times10^{-13}$ and $1\times10^{-13}$.

No two groups cancel against each other: they differ in their kernels, their phases, or both.

\section{How the c.c.\ terms are formed}
In every Group I and Group III term the coefficient is real, and so are the kernels (including the $[\cdots]_{+}$ distributions), the adjoint Wilson lines $U$ and the structure constants $f$. The $U$'s commute with the $\rho$'s, so each colour factor can be written with all $U$'s first and the two $\rho$'s last. The hermitian conjugate therefore does only two things: it flips the sign of the phase, and it reverses the order of the two $\rho$'s ($\rho^{a\dagger}=\rho^{a}$):
\begin{equation}
\Big(e^{-i\varphi}\,\mathcal{C}^{ab}[U]\,\rho^{a}(u)\rho^{b}(v)\Big)^{\dagger}=e^{+i\varphi}\,\mathcal{C}^{ab}[U]\,\rho^{b}(v)\rho^{a}(u).
\end{equation}
Adding a term to its conjugate:
\begin{align}
e^{-i\varphi}\rho^{a}(u)\rho^{b}(v)+e^{+i\varphi}\rho^{b}(v)\rho^{a}(u)
&=\cos\varphi\,\big(\rho^{a}(u)\rho^{b}(v)+\rho^{b}(v)\rho^{a}(u)\big)-i\sin\varphi\,\big(\rho^{a}(u)\rho^{b}(v)-\rho^{b}(v)\rho^{a}(u)\big)\notag\\
&=\cos\varphi\,\big\{\rho^{a}(u),\rho^{b}(v)\big\}-i\sin\varphi\,\big[\rho^{a}(u),\rho^{b}(v)\big].\label{eq:comb}
\end{align}
Both pieces are hermitian: $\{\rho,\rho\}$ is hermitian, and $[\rho,\rho]$ is anti-hermitian, so $i[\rho,\rho]$ is hermitian. With $[\rho^{a}(u),\rho^{b}(v)]=i\kappa f^{abc}\rho^{c}(u)\delta^{(2)}(u-v)$ the second piece becomes $-i\sin\varphi\,[\rho^{a}(u),\rho^{b}(v)]=\kappa\sin\varphi\,f^{abc}\rho^{c}(u)\,\delta^{(2)}(u-v)$, a real one-$\rho$ term. It is kept as a commutator below. For commuting $\rho$'s only the $\cos\varphi$ term remains.

The phases $\varphi$ of the five Group I and Group III structures are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Group I Row II, and the third part of Group III: $\varphi=k\cdot(w^{\prime}-w)$;
\item Group I Row V, main term: $\varphi=k\cdot(w^{\prime}-z)+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\,k\cdot(z-x)$;
\item Group I Row V, subtraction line: $\varphi=k\cdot(w^{\prime}-x)$;
\item Group III, first and second parts: $\varphi=k\cdot(y^{\prime}-w)+\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\,k\cdot(y^{\prime}-z)$.
\end{itemize}
In the $\xi$ versions, $\frac{p^{+}}{k^{+}}\to\xi$.
