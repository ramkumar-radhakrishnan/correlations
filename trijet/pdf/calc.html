Step 0. The result, before the work

Skip to §13 for the two answer tables. In summary, the LO trijet integrated over the gluon phase space against a dijet measurement function produces exactly nine divergent structures. Six of them cancel (three $1/\varepsilon$ poles against the virtual, three $\ln^2 z_0$ double logs against each other). The three that survive are all single $\ln(1/z_0)$ and they assemble into one object, $\ln(z_f/z_0)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$. Everything else is finite and is listed explicitly in §13.2.

Step 1. The starting point

1.1 Kinematics and measure

Dipole frame; $q^\mu=(-Q^2/2q^-,q^-,\boldsymbol 0_\perp)$, $P^\mu=(P^+,M^2/2P^+,\boldsymbol 0_\perp)$; $z_i=k_i^-/q^-$; $\delta_z^{(3)}=\delta(1-z_1-z_2-z_g)$; transverse positions $\boldsymbol x_\perp,\boldsymbol y_\perp,\boldsymbol z_\perp$ for $q,\bar q,g$ (primed in the conjugate amplitude); $\boldsymbol r_{xy}=\boldsymbol x_\perp-\boldsymbol y_\perp$; $\bar Q^2=z_1z_2Q^2$; $c_0=2e^{-\gamma_E}$; $\tilde\mu^2=4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\mu^2$.

The gluon phase space, which is where all the divergences live:

$$\int\!\mathrm d\Phi_g=\int\!\frac{\mathrm dk_g^-\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}}{(2\pi)^3 2k_g^-} =\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^3}\int_{z_0}^{1-z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\int\!\mathrm d^{2-\varepsilon}\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}, \qquad z_0=\frac{\Lambda^-}{q^-}.$$

Two regulators are in place from the start: the longitudinal cut-off $\Lambda^-$ (rapidity), and $d=2-\varepsilon$ transverse dimensions (collinear/UV). Note CSSV use $d=2-\varepsilon$; papers using $2-2\varepsilon$ are recovered by $\varepsilon\to2\varepsilon$.

1.2 The trijet cross-section

Split the amplitude by whether the gluon is emitted before or after the shockwave:

$$\mathcal M=\mathcal M_{\rm bef}+\mathcal M_{\rm aft}\,,$$

so that the cross-section has three classes of interference:

$$\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\;=\;\underbrace{|\mathcal M_{\rm aft}|^2}_{\text{Channels A, B}} \;+\;\underbrace{2\,{\rm Re}\,\mathcal M_{\rm bef}\mathcal M_{\rm aft}^*+|\mathcal M_{\rm bef}|^2}_{\text{Channel C}} .$$

Explicitly, for the pieces that matter:

Channel A — $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2$ (gluon emitted off the quark after the shockwave, in amplitude and conjugate). Colour structure $C_F\,\Xi_{\rm LO}$. CSSV (B.1):

$$\mathrm d\sigma^{\gamma^*_{\rm L}+A\to q\bar qg+X}_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2} =\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2N_c\delta_z^{(3)}}{(2\pi)^8}\!\int\!\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp\, e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}-i\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{yy'}}\,\Xi_{\rm LO}$$ $$\times\ \alpha_sC_F\left\{32z_1z_2^3(1-z_2)^2Q^2\Big(1+\frac{z_g}{z_1}+\frac{z_g^2}{2z_1^2}\Big) K_0(\bar Q_{\rm R2}r_{xy})K_0(\bar Q_{\rm R2}r_{x'y'})\, \frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}}{\big(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\big)^2}\right\},$$

$\bar Q^2_{\rm R2}=z_2(1-z_2)Q^2$. The $\mathrm R_2'\times\mathrm R_2'$ mirror follows by $q\leftrightarrow\bar q$.

Channel B — $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2'$ (gluon off the quark in the amplitude, off the antiquark in the conjugate). Colour structure $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$. CSSV (B.11):

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2'}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2N_c\delta_z^{(3)}}{(2\pi)^8} \!\int\!\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp\,e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}-i\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{yy'}}\,\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$$ $$\times(-\alpha_s)32z_1^3z_2^3(1-z_2)(1-z_1)Q^2K_0(\bar Q_{\rm R2}r_{xy})K_0(\bar Q_{\rm R2'}r_{x'y'}) \Big[1+\frac{z_g}{2z_1}+\frac{z_g}{2z_2}\Big]$$ $$\times\ e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xy'}}\, \frac{(z_1\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-z_g\boldsymbol k_{1\perp})\cdot(z_2\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-z_g\boldsymbol k_{2\perp})} {(z_1\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-z_g\boldsymbol k_{1\perp})^2(z_2\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-z_g\boldsymbol k_{2\perp})^2}\,, \qquad \bar Q^2_{\rm R2'}=z_1(1-z_1)Q^2 .$$

Channel C — gluon crosses the shockwave. Colour structures $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},2},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},4}$; explicit $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integration; wave function $K_0(QX_{\rm R})$ with

$$X_{\rm R}^2=z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2+z_1z_g\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2+z_2z_g\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2\,, \qquad \boldsymbol w_\perp=\frac{z_1\boldsymbol x_\perp+z_g\boldsymbol z_\perp}{z_1+z_g}\,. $$

Full expression: CSSV (3.23).

1.3 What we compute

$$\Sigma\;\equiv\;\int\!\mathrm d\Phi_g\;\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\;\mathcal M(k_1,k_2,k_g)\;+\;\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm V}\,,$$

with $\mathcal M$ the IRC-safe dijet measurement function. The task is to write $\Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm div}+\Sigma_{\rm fin}$ and identify $\Sigma_{\rm div}$.

Step 2. Channel A: the eikonal decomposition

2.1 Change of variable

Define the gluon momentum relative to the emitter,

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}\;\equiv\;\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\;,$$

which for $z_g\ll z_1$ is (minus) the collinearity variable $\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}_{gq\perp}$ of the jet algorithm. Then the eikonal factor in Channel A is $e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}/\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2$, and taking $z_g\to0$ inside the curly bracket of (B.1) collapses the wave function onto the LO one,

$$\Big(1+\frac{z_g}{z_1}+\frac{z_g^2}{2z_1^2}\Big)\to1,\qquad \bar Q_{\rm R2}\to\bar Q,\qquad 32z_1z_2^3(1-z_2)^2Q^2K_0K_0\ \to\ \frac{4}{\alpha_s}\times\mathcal R^{\rm L}_{\rm LO}\ \ (\text{schematically}),$$

leaving the pure emission factor CSSV (B.2):

$$\frac{4\alpha_sC_F}{(2\pi)^2}\times\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}} {\big(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\big)^2}\,.$$

2.2 The three-way split

Write the numerator as an exact identity, using $e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}} =e^{-i\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}e^{-i\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}$:

$$\underbrace{\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2}}_{\text{exact}} =\underbrace{\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2}}_{\textbf{(div)}} \;-\;\underbrace{\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}\big(1-e^{-i\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}\big)}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2}}_{\textbf{(sud2)}}\,,$$

and define (no-Sud) $\equiv$ (full Channel A, all $z_g$ dependence kept) $-$ (div) $-$ (sud2). This is CSSV (B.3)–(B.5). The point of the split:

  • (div) has no $z_g$ anywhere except the $1/z_g$ of the measure → carries the whole $\ln z_0$ and $\ln^2 z_0$ divergence;
  • (sud2) vanishes as $\xi\equiv z_g/z_1\to0$ like $\xi$ → its $\mathrm d\xi/\xi$ integral converges, no $z_0$ needed. It is the piece that correctly retains the $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\sim z_g\boldsymbol P_\perp$ phase space;
  • (no-Sud) is finite by construction.

Why not just set $z_g=0$ in the denominator too? Because in back-to-back kinematics $|\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}|\sim|\boldsymbol q_\perp|$ while $\frac{z_g}{z_1}|\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}|\sim z_g|\boldsymbol P_\perp|$, and these are the same order even for small $z_g$. Dropping it is legitimate only if you want the leading $z_g\to0$ divergence and nothing else [CSSV footnote 31].

2.3 ▣ Integral 1 — the eikonal transverse integral

The out-of-cone condition of the jet algorithm is, in the small-$R$ limit, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2\gt R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\min(1,\xi^2)$ [CSSV (3.4)]; take $\xi\le1$ so the constraint is $\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2\gt\Lambda^2$ with $\Lambda=R|\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}|\xi$. We need

$$I(\Lambda,\boldsymbol\Delta)=\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}}{(2\pi)^2}\, \frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2}\, \Theta(\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2-\Lambda^2)\,,\qquad \boldsymbol\Delta=\boldsymbol r_{xx'} .$$

Do the angle first: $\int_0^{2\pi}\!\mathrm d\phi\,e^{-i\mathcal C\Delta\cos\phi}=2\pi J_0(\mathcal C\Delta)$, so

$$I=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm d\mathcal C}{\mathcal C}\,J_0(\mathcal C\Delta) =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda\Delta}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm dx}{x}J_0(x).$$

Use $\displaystyle\int_a^\infty\!\frac{\mathrm dx}{x}J_0(x)=\ln\frac2a-\gamma_E+\mathcal O(a^2) =\ln\frac{c_0}{a}+\mathcal O(a^2)$:

$$I(\Lambda,\boldsymbol\Delta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\!\frac{c_0}{\Lambda\Delta}+\mathcal O(\Lambda^2\Delta^2) \;=\;-\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol\Delta^2R^2\xi^2}{c_0^2}\Big)+\mathcal O(R^2)$$

which is CSSV (B.9). [Checked numerically, checks1.py: agreement to $\mathcal O(\Lambda^2\Delta^2)$, the advertised power correction.]

2.4 ▣ Integral 2 — the $\xi$ integral, and the divergence appears

Assemble (div) with the out-of-cone constraint. With $4\alpha_sC_F\times I(\Lambda,\boldsymbol r_{xx'})=-\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\ln\big(\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2R^2\xi^2/c_0^2\big)$, and writing $A\equiv\ln\big(\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2R^2/c_0^2\big)$ so that the bracket is $A+2\ln\xi$,

$$\int_{z_0}^{z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}(\cdots) =-\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\int_{z_0/z_1}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi}\big(A+2\ln\xi\big) =-\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\Big[A\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-\ln^2\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big],$$

using $\int_\epsilon^1\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi}=\ln\frac1\epsilon$ and $\int_\epsilon^1\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi}2\ln\xi=-\ln^2\epsilon$. Hence

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2,\,\rm out,\,soft-div} =[\,\cdot\,]\;\Xi_{\rm LO}\;\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi} \left\{\ \underbrace{\ln^2\!\Big(\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)}_{\textbf{D2: soft double log}} -\ \underbrace{\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)}_{\textbf{D1: rapidity log}} \ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2R^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\right\}+\mathcal O(R^2)$$

which is CSSV (B.7), derived. Here and below $[\,\cdot\,]\equiv\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2N_c\delta_z^{(2)}}{(2\pi)^6}\int\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}-i\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{yy'}}\mathcal R^\lambda_{\rm LO}$.

This is the first place a divergence is visible. Two of them, in fact, with completely different fates: the $\ln^2(z_1/z_0)$ is D2 and will cancel; the $\ln(z_1/z_0)\times(\text{transverse log})$ is D1 and will become JIMWLK.

2.5 The (sud2) piece — finite, no $z_0$

Identical algebra with the extra factor $(1-e^{-i\xi\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}})$, which supplies the convergence at $\xi\to0$:

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2,\rm sud2}=[\,\cdot\,]\,C_F\Xi_{\rm LO}\,\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi} \int_0^1\!\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi}\Big[1-e^{-i\xi\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}\Big] \ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2R^2\xi^2}{c_0^2}\Big)+\mathcal O(R^2),$$

= CSSV (B.10) = (3.8). Finite. Note the lower limit is $0$, not $z_0$: no rapidity regulator is needed once (div) has been removed.

2.6 In-cone — where the $1/\varepsilon$ lives

For $\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2\lt R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\min(1,\xi^2)$ the quark and gluon form one jet. The transverse integral is now over the inside of the cone, where $1/\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2$ is unintegrable in $d=2$; in $d=2-\varepsilon$ it gives $1/\varepsilon$. Keeping the exact $\xi$-dependent numerator of (B.1) reproduces the $q\to qg$ splitting function:

$$\Big(1+\frac{z_g}{z_1}+\frac{z_g^2}{2z_1^2}\Big)\frac{1}{\xi}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{2C_F}P_{gq}(\xi) =\frac{1+(1-\xi)^2}{2\xi}\quad\text{up to }\mathcal O(\xi)\ \text{and }\varepsilon\text{-terms}.$$

The coefficient of the pole is therefore the $\xi$-integrated splitting function:

$$\int_{z_0/z_1}^{1}\!\mathrm d\xi\;\frac{1+(1-\xi)^2}{\xi} =\int_{z_0/z_1}^{1}\!\mathrm d\xi\Big(\frac2\xi-2+\xi\Big) =2\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-2+\frac12=2\Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-\frac34\Big).$$

▣ Integral 3. The $3/4$ is exactly the finite part of the $z$-integrated $P_{qq}$, and it is what will become $-3\ln R$ in the final answer. The full in-cone result, with the $\varepsilon$-expansion and the $\mathcal O(\xi)$ terms kept, is CSSV (B.24) — quoted input:

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2,\rm in}=\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi} \left\{\Big(\frac34-\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+\ln^2z_1-\ln^2z_0-\frac{\pi^2}{6} +\Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-\frac34\Big)\ln\frac{R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2}{\tilde\mu^2z_1^2} +\frac14+\frac32\Big(1-\ln\frac{z_1}{2}\Big)\right\}$$

Observe that the $1/\varepsilon$ coefficient is precisely $-\frac1{\varepsilon}\int_{z_0/z_1}^1\mathrm d\xi\,P_{gq}(\xi)/C_F$ from the box above. That is the Harris–Owens HC weight, HO (2.33)–(2.36), in CGC clothing.

Step 3. Channel B: $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2'$

3.1 ▣ Integral 4 — the interference transverse integral

The transverse structure of (B.11) is, after $\boldsymbol l_\perp\equiv\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}$ and $\boldsymbol K_\perp\equiv\frac{z_g}{z_1z_2}\boldsymbol P_\perp$,

$$\mathcal J_R(\boldsymbol r_\perp,\boldsymbol K_\perp)\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol l_\perp}{(2\pi)} \,e^{-i\boldsymbol l_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol r_\perp}\, \frac{4\,\boldsymbol l_\perp\!\cdot\!(\boldsymbol l_\perp+\boldsymbol K_\perp)}{\boldsymbol l_\perp^2(\boldsymbol l_\perp+\boldsymbol K_\perp)^2} \qquad\text{[CSSV (B.14)]}.$$

As $\boldsymbol K_\perp\to0$ (i.e. $z_g\to0$) the two poles pinch and $\mathcal J_R$ diverges logarithmically. Its singular part is obtained by replacing the numerator by $\boldsymbol l_\perp^2$ and cutting the integral between $|\boldsymbol K_\perp|$ and $c_0/|\boldsymbol r_\perp|$ (the Fourier transform provides the upper cut):

$$\mathcal J_R=4\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol l_\perp}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol l_\perp^2} \Theta\Big(\frac{c_0^2}{\boldsymbol r_\perp^2}\ge\boldsymbol l_\perp^2\ge\boldsymbol K_\perp^2\Big)-i =\frac{4}{4\pi}\ln\!\frac{c_0^2}{\boldsymbol r_\perp^2\boldsymbol K_\perp^2}-i =-\frac1\pi\Big[\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol K_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_\perp^2}{c_0^2}\Big)+i\pi\Big],$$

reproducing CSSV (B.15). (The $i\pi$ cancels against the conjugate diagram $\mathrm R_2'\times\mathrm R_2$.)

3.2 ▣ Integral 5 — the $z_g$ integral

Substituting $\boldsymbol K_\perp^2=z_g^2\boldsymbol P_\perp^2/(z_1z_2)^2$ into the $\Theta$-regulated form gives the soft-divergent piece CSSV (B.17); integrating it over the gluon phase space,

$$\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol l_\perp}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol l_\perp^2} \Theta\Big(\frac{c_0^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2}\ge\boldsymbol l_\perp^2\ge\frac{z_g^2\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}{z_1^2z_2^2}\Big) =\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\!\frac{c_0^2z_1^2z_2^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2z_g^2\boldsymbol P_\perp^2},$$

and multiplying by $(-4\alpha_s)$,

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2',\rm soft-div}=[\,\cdot\,]\,\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}\, \frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\int_{z_0}^{z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g} \left[\ \underbrace{2\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_g}{z_1z_2}\Big)}_{\to\ \textbf{D2}} +\ \underbrace{\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)}_{\to\ \textbf{D1}}\right]$$

= CSSV (B.20), derived. The first term gives $\int_{z_0}^{z_1}\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}2\ln\frac{z_g}{z_1z_2} =\ln^2z_1-\ln^2z_0-2\ln(z_1z_2)\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}$ — another $\ln^2z_0$ (D2). The second gives $\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2}{c_0^2}$ — another rapidity log (D1).

3.3 The (sud2) piece

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2',\rm sud2}=[\,\cdot\,]\,\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}\, \frac{-\alpha_s}{\pi}\int_0^1\!\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi} \Big[1-e^{-i\xi\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xy'}}\Big] \ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2\xi^2}{z_2^2c_0^2}\Big)$$

= CSSV (B.22) = (3.9). Finite. Since this diagram has no collinear divergence (the in-cone phase space is $\mathcal O(R^2)$ suppressed), the full gluon phase space may be used.

Step 4. Channel C: gluon crossing the shockwave

These diagrams have no soft (D2) and no collinear (D3) divergence — the internal propagator does not go on shell, and the $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integration is regulated at large distance by colour neutrality of the target and at short distance by the UV cancellation with $\mathrm{SE}_1$. They carry only the rapidity divergence D1, from $\mathrm dz_g/z_g$:

$$X_{\rm R}^2\xrightarrow{z_g\to0}z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2,\qquad K_0(QX_{\rm R})\to K_0(\bar Qr_{xy}),\qquad \Xi_{{\rm NLO},1},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},2},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},4}\ \text{survive with}\ \boldsymbol z_\perp\ \text{kept}.$$

Accordingly CSSV (3.23) carries its own explicit counterterm, the last line,

$$-\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2N_c\delta_z^{(2)}}{(2\pi)^8}\,\alpha_s\,\Theta(z_f-z_g)\times\text{“slow”},$$

with “slow” $=$ the $z_g\to0$ limit of the formula itself. Subtracting it makes the gluon phase-space integral of Channel C finite as $\Lambda^-\to0$; the subtracted piece is moved into the $\ln(k_f^-/\Lambda^-)$ term. This is the trijet's own rapidity subtraction. The same happens in the virtual, CSSV (3.17), with $\Theta(z_f-z_g)$ subtractions.

Step 5. The virtual inputs (quoted)

Two virtual objects are needed. Both are one-loop CGC amplitudes computed in CSV (arXiv:2108.06347) and are inputs, not part of the divergence extraction:

(V1) The infrared-divergent virtual, $C_F\Xi_{\rm LO}$ channel — the sum of $\mathrm{SE}_2\!\times\!$LO, $\mathrm{SE}_3\!\times\!$LO, $\mathrm V_2\!\times\!$LO and the UV-divergent part of $\mathrm{SE}_1\!\times\!$LO. CSSV (B.23):

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm IR\times LO}=[\,\cdot\,]\,\Xi_{\rm LO}\,\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{2\pi} \left\{\Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}+\ln\frac{z_2}{z_0}-\frac32\Big) \Big(\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+\ln\big(e^{\gamma_E}\pi\mu^2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\big)\Big) +\frac12\ln^2\frac{z_2}{z_1}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\frac52-\frac12\right\},$$

plus its complex conjugate $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO\times IR}$ ($\boldsymbol r_{xy}\to\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}$).

(V2) The soft-divergent part of $\mathrm V_3\times$LO, $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$ channel. CSSV (B.30):

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm V_3\times\rm LO,soft-div}+c.c.=[\,\cdot\,]\,\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}\, \frac{-\alpha_s}{\pi}\int_{z_0}^{z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g} \left[2\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_g}{z_1z_2}\Big)+\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|}{c_0^2}\Big)\right]+(1\leftrightarrow2).$$
Note the structure. (V2) is identical to the real Channel-B result (B.20) except for (i) an overall minus sign and (ii) $\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2\to|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|$. That is real–virtual cancellation made manifest: the longitudinal structures match exactly and only the transverse argument differs. The mismatch of transverse arguments is what survives as the physical logarithm.

This is the Kinoshita–Lee–Nauenberg cancellation, and structurally it is exactly HO (3.110), $A_1^v+A_1^s+A_1^{\rm coll}=0$. Note that it works only because the same rapidity regulator $z_0$ appears in both: the collinear and rapidity regulators are entangled, which is the hallmark of light-cone gauge calculations.

The $\overline{\rm MS}$ scale cancels with it. Using $\tilde\mu^2=4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\mu^2$, so that $\ln(e^{\gamma_E}\pi\mu^2)-\ln\tilde\mu^2=-\ln c_0^2$:

$$\text{coefficient of }\ln\mu^2:\quad \underbrace{\Big(\ln\tfrac{z_1}{z_0}+\ln\tfrac{z_2}{z_0}-\tfrac32\Big)}_{\rm virtual} \underbrace{-\Big(\ln\tfrac{z_1}{z_0}-\tfrac34\Big)-\Big(\ln\tfrac{z_2}{z_0}-\tfrac34\Big)}_{\rm real\ in-cone}=0 .$$

Step 7. ▣ Cancellation of the soft double logarithms

7.1 Channel A ($C_F\Xi_{\rm LO}$)

Step 9. ▣ Rapidity factorization: splitting off the divergence

Introduce the factorization scale $z_f=k_f^-/q^-$ and use the identity $\ln\frac{z_i}{z_0}=\ln\frac{z_i}{z_f}+\ln\frac{z_f}{z_0}$ in $\Sigma_A$ and in (B.31).

9.1 Channel A

The $\ln(z_f/z_0)$ part of the first two terms of $\Sigma_A$ is

$$-\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_f}{z_0}\Big)\left[\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|} +\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|}\right] =-\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_f}{z_0}\Big)\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}\Big),$$

because the two half-powers of $|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|$ add up to one full power. Hence

Divergent (Channel A) — CSSV (B.26):

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO,slow}=[\,\cdot\,]\;\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\, \ln\!\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\, \ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}\Big)\;\Xi_{\rm LO}$$

Finite remainder (Channel A) — CSSV (B.27): $\Sigma_A$ with $z_0\to z_f$ in the two longitudinal logarithms,

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm no-pole}=[\,\cdot\,]\Xi_{\rm LO}\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi} \left\{-\ln\frac{z_1}{z_f}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|} -\ln\frac{z_2}{z_f}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|}\right.$$ $$\left.-\frac34\ln\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}{c_0^4} -3\ln R+\frac12\ln^2\frac{z_1}{z_2}+\frac{11}{2}+3\ln2-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\right\}$$

Consistency: $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO,slow}+\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm no-pole}=\Sigma_A$ exactly, since $\ln\frac{z_f}{z_0}+\ln\frac{z_i}{z_f}=\ln\frac{z_i}{z_0}$ term by term.

Step 10. ▣ The divergence is the JIMWLK kernel

The divergent terms (B.26) and (B.32) do not look like an evolution kernel until the transverse logarithms are undone. Use the identity ▣ Integral 6 [CSSV (3.27)]

$$\frac1\pi\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\left[ \frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zx'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2} -\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2}\right] =\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2}\Big),$$

and, for the full four-term dipole kernel appearing in (3.28),

$$\mathcal K_{\rm dip}=\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zx'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2} -\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2} +\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zy}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy'}^2} -\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx'}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy'}^2},$$
$$\frac1\pi\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\;\mathcal K_{\rm dip} \;=\;\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}\Big)$$

[Both identities verified numerically to $10^{-7}$ or better, checks4.py.] Running this backwards turns (B.26) into an explicit $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integral — a gluon emitted at $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ — i.e. CSSV (3.28):

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO,slow}=[\,\cdot\,]\,\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi^2}\ln\!\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big) \int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\;\mathcal K_{\rm dip}\;\Xi_{\rm LO}.$$

Doing the same for every colour channel (the $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1,2,4}$ channels already come with their $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integral explicit, from Channel C and from the virtual (3.17)) assembles the complete rapidity divergence CSSV (3.29), which is

$$\boxed{\ \mathrm d\sigma_{\rm slow}\;=\;\ln\!\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\ }$$ $$\mathcal H_{\rm LL}=\frac12\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol u_\perp\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol v_\perp \frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+,a}_{\rm cl}(\boldsymbol u_\perp)}\eta^{ab}(\boldsymbol u_\perp,\boldsymbol v_\perp) \frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+,b}_{\rm cl}(\boldsymbol v_\perp)},$$ $$\eta^{ab}=\frac1\pi\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp}{(2\pi)^2} \frac{(\boldsymbol u_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)\!\cdot\!(\boldsymbol v_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)} {(\boldsymbol u_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)^2(\boldsymbol v_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)^2} \big[1+U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u)U(\boldsymbol v)-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u)U(\boldsymbol z)-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol z)U(\boldsymbol v)\big]^{ab}$$

with the RG equation $\partial_{Y_f}W^{\rm LL}_{Y_f}[\rho_A]=\mathcal H_{\rm LL}W^{\rm LL}_{Y_f}[\rho_A]$, $Y_f=\ln(1/z_f)$, and initial condition at $z_0=\frac{Q_0^2}{Q^2}\frac{x_{\rm Bj}}{x_0}$ [CSSV (3.38)].

Where each of the four terms in $\eta^{ab}$ came from. The $-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u)U(\boldsymbol z)$ and $-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol z)U(\boldsymbol v)$ terms are the real trijet (Channel C: the gluon crossed the shockwave at $\boldsymbol z_\perp$, so $U(\boldsymbol z)$ appears). The $1$ and $U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u)U(\boldsymbol v)$ terms are the virtual $q\bar q$ diagrams, where no gluon crosses. You cannot obtain B-JIMWLK from the trijet alone: without the virtual terms the $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integral of $\eta^{ab}$ diverges at large $|\boldsymbol z_\perp|$ and the evolution is not probability conserving.

Large $N_c$ (BK). For the dipole $S_{xy}=\langle D_{xy}\rangle$ the hierarchy closes:

$$\partial_YS_{xy}=\frac{\bar\alpha_s}{2\pi}\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp \frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xz}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2}\big[S_{xz}S_{zy}-S_{xy}\big], \qquad\bar\alpha_s=\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{\pi},$$

where the three terms $S_{xz}S_{zy}$ (real) and $-S_{xy}$ (virtual) map onto the four terms of $\eta^{ab}$ above.

Step 11. ▣ The lifetime-ordering correction

Steps 9–10 used a naive slice, $z_g\lt z_f$ at all $\boldsymbol z_\perp$. That is too much phase space. In Channel C and in the virtual (3.17), taking $z_g\to0$ meant $K_0(QX_{\rm R/V})\to K_0(\bar Qr_{xy})$ with

$$X_{\rm R}^2\approx X_{\rm V}^2\approx z_1z_2\boldsymbol u_\perp^2+z_g\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\quad\text{(back-to-back)},$$

which is legitimate only where $z_g\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\le z_1z_2\boldsymbol u_\perp^2$ [CSSV (4.43)]. With $\boldsymbol u_\perp^2\sim1/Q^2$ and $\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\sim1/\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2$ this is

$$\frac{z_g}{\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2}\le\frac{z_1z_2}{Q^2} \iff k_g^+\ge k_f^+\iff\frac{1}{k_g^+}\le\frac{1}{k_f^+}:\quad \textbf{the gluon must be shorter-lived than the parent dipole.}$$

In coordinate space, $k_g^-\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\le k_f^-/Q_f^2$ [CSSV (4.47)], $Q_f^2=2k_f^+k_f^-\sim\boldsymbol P_\perp^2$. In the plane of $t=\ln(z_f/z_g)$ and $\rho=\ln\!\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2\big)$ the allowed slow region is the wedge $\{t\gt0\}\cap\{\rho\lt t\}$, not the quadrant $\{t\gt0\}$. The excess is the triangle $0\lt t\lt\rho$.

11.1 ▣ Integral 7 — the excess, evaluated

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}\!\int_{z_0}^{z_f}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g} \!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\, \Theta\Big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2-\frac{z_f}{z_g}\Big) \frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\qquad\text{[CSSV (4.50)]}$$

Longitudinal: with $t=\ln(z_f/z_g)$ the $\Theta$ is simply $\Theta(\rho-t)$, so $\int_0^{T}\!\mathrm dt\,\Theta(\rho-t)\to\rho\,\Theta(\rho)$ as $T=\ln(z_f/z_0)\to\infty$. $z_0$ drops out — the constraint supplies its own cutoff, so $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ is a genuine finite $\mathcal O(\alpha_s)$ term, not a redefinition of the divergence.

Transverse: the kernel is log-divergent only at $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b_\perp$ and $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b'_\perp$, where $\Theta$ cuts it at $1/Q_f$. Near $\boldsymbol b_\perp$, with $r=|\boldsymbol r_{zb}|$: kernel $\to1/r^2$, $\rho\to\ln(r^2Q_f^2)$, so

$$\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\frac{\ln(r^2Q_f^2)}{r^2} =2\pi\!\int_{1/Q_f}^{|\boldsymbol r_{bb'}|}\!\frac{\mathrm dr}{r}\ln(r^2Q_f^2) \overset{s=\ln(r^2Q_f^2)}{=}\pi\!\int_0^{\rho_{\max}}\!\!s\,\mathrm ds=\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{\max}^2, \quad\rho_{\max}=\ln\!\big(\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2\big).$$

Doubling for the $\boldsymbol b'_\perp$ endpoint:

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}\times\pi\rho_{\max}^2 =\;-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\big(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2\big)\,\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f} \;+\;\mathcal O\!\Big(\frac{1}{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2}\Big)$$

= CSSV (4.51). [Verified numerically, checks2.py: for $\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2$ from $400$ to $1.6\times10^5$ the ratio to $\pi\ln^2$ is $1.0000$ and the residual, divided by $\ln(\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2)$, does not grow — so there is indeed no leftover single logarithm.]

Split into Sudakov pieces, CSSV (4.52):

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}=\underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}}_{\text{double log, }<0} \underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{\pi}\ln\frac{Q_f^2c_0^2}{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}}_{\text{single log, scheme dependent}} \underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\frac{Q_f^2c_0^2}{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}}_{\text{constant}}\ \ \times\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}$$

The double log is negative — Sudakov suppression, as physics requires. Had you stopped at Step 10 with the naive slice, this term would be absent and the double log extracted from the remaining impact factor would come out with the wrong (positive) sign.

13.3 One-line summary

Of the trijet's divergences, only $\ \ln(z_f/z_0)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\ $ survives.
The $1/\varepsilon$ poles cancel against the virtual by KLN; the $\ln^2z_0$ soft double logs cancel in-cone against out-of-cone and real against virtual;
everything else is finite, and the lifetime-ordering wedge moves one extra $-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2)$ from the evolution to the finite part.
