0. The answer

(a) The divergence structure is unchanged. The cross-section still has a single simple pole $1/\xi$ as the gluon becomes slow; mass does not regulate it. The separation of §5 is term-by-term identical.

(b) The divergent coefficient changes by exactly one substitution — the massless leading-order transverse-photon factor is replaced by the massive one:

$$\big[\zeta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\big(R_0\!\cdot\!\bar R_0\big) \ \longrightarrow\ \big[\zeta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\big(R_0\!\cdot\!\bar R_0\big)\;+\;m^2 ,$$

with everything else — the universal weight $16\zeta^2\bar\zeta^{\,2}$, the three eikonal kernels $\mathcal K_{\bar q\bar q},\mathcal K_{qq},\mathcal K_{q\bar q}$, the Wilson-line structures $\mathbb W^{(0)}$, and hence the B‑JIMWLK/BK kernel — completely unchanged.

(c) Bessel argument. All mass dependence of the energy denominators is the single replacement $$Q^2\ \longrightarrow\ Q^2+m^2\Big(\frac{1}{\zeta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\Big)\quad\text{(3-body)}, \qquad Q^2\ \longrightarrow\ Q^2+\frac{m^2}{z_q(1-z_q)}\quad\text{(2-body)} .$$

The reason (b) is so simple: the mass term in the gluon-emission vertex is $\mathcal O(\xi^2)$, so it drops out of the soft limit entirely; only the mass term in the photon vertex survives, and it is precisely the $m^2K_0K_0$ piece of the massive $\gamma^*_{\rm T}\to q\bar q$ wave function. The full $\Phi\Phi^*$ (§4) is much richer — four independent transverse structures instead of one, with $m^0,m^2,m^4$ terms — but all the new pieces are power-suppressed in $\xi$.

1. The dictionary: your vertex factors, recovered

Write $\bar\zeta\equiv1-\zeta$, and recall $\zeta+\eta+\xi=1$ with $\zeta,\eta,\xi$ the $+$-momentum fractions of quark, antiquark, gluon. The ansatz is the standard LCPT one: a photon vertex carrying the photon polarization index $l$ and a "momentum" index $i$, and an emission vertex carrying the gluon polarization index $r$ and a momentum index $m$,

$$\mathcal V^{li}_\gamma(\lambda_1)=a\,\delta^{li}+i\lambda_1\epsilon^{li},\qquad \mathcal V^{rm}_g(\lambda_{\rm em})=b\,\delta^{rm}+i\lambda_{\rm em}c\,\epsilon^{rm},$$

so that $\Phi^{lirm}_{\lambda_1\lambda}=\mathcal V^{li}_\gamma\mathcal V^{rm}_g$, with $l$ and $r$ summed between $\Phi$ and $\Phi^*$ and $i,m$ contracted with $R^i,Y^m$. Summing over $\lambda_1=\pm1$ and over $l,r$,

$$\Phi\Phi^*=2\big(a_{\rm amp}a_{\rm conj}+1\big)\big(b_{\rm amp}b_{\rm conj}+c_{\rm amp}c_{\rm conj}\big)\,\delta^{ij}\delta^{mn} -2\big(a_{\rm amp}+a_{\rm conj}\big)\big(c_{\rm amp}b_{\rm conj}+b_{\rm amp}c_{\rm conj}\big)\,\epsilon^{ij}\epsilon^{mn}.$$

With $z_q$ the quark's fraction of the photon momentum and the emitter's parent fraction $P$:

$$a=2z_q-1,\qquad b=2(P-\xi)+\xi=P(2-w),\qquad c=\xi=Pw,\qquad w\equiv\frac{\xi}{P},$$

i.e. $\mathcal V_g^{rm}=P\big[(2-w)\delta^{rm}+i\lambda_{\rm em}w\,\epsilon^{rm}\big]$ — the textbook massless $q\to qg$ light-cone vertex. Channel by channel:

Channel$z_q$$a$$P$$b$$c$

Verified. Substituting these into the formula above reproduces (1.2), (1.10) and (1.18) identically once $\eta=1-\zeta-\xi$ is imposed — all six coefficients ($\delta\delta$ and $\epsilon\epsilon$ in three channels), with no free parameter left. The relative sign of $c$ between the two channels is fixed by (1.18) and is the statement that the emitting line's helicity is $\mp\lambda_1$ for the antiquark/quark.

2. The massive vertices

Both are computed from explicit Lepage–Brodsky light-cone spinors,

$$u(k,\lambda)=\frac{k^++\beta m+\boldsymbol\alpha_\perp\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol k_\perp}{\sqrt{k^+}}\chi_\lambda, \qquad v(k,\lambda)=\frac{k^+-\beta m+\boldsymbol\alpha_\perp\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol k_\perp}{\sqrt{k^+}}\chi_{-\lambda},$$

checked against $\bar uu=2m$, $\bar u\gamma^+u=2k^+$, $\bar vv=-2m$ and $\sum_\lambda u\bar u=\slashed k+m$.

2.1 Photon vertex $\gamma^*_{\rm T}\to q\bar q$

$$\bar u(k_1,\lambda_1)\gamma^l v(k_2,\lambda_2)\ \propto\ \underbrace{\delta_{\lambda_2,-\lambda_1}\big[(2z_q-1)\delta^{li}+i\lambda_1\epsilon^{li}\big]\boldsymbol P^i}_{\text{helicity conserving}} \;-\;\underbrace{\delta_{\lambda_2,+\lambda_1}\,\lambda_1\,m\,\big(\delta^{l1}-i\lambda_1\delta^{l2}\big)}_{\textbf{mass}}$$

The mass term has no transverse-momentum factor and requires $\lambda_2=+\lambda_1$; since $(\delta^{l1}-i\lambda_1\delta^{l2})=\sqrt2\,\epsilon^{*l}_{\lambda_1}$ it locks the photon helicity to the quark helicity. Because it lives in a different helicity channel, it does not interfere with the first term at leading order — which is why the LO massive dijet is the familiar $\big[z_q^2+(1-z_q)^2\big]\varepsilon^2K_1K_1+m^2K_0K_0$.

2.2 Emission vertex $q\to qg$ in light-cone gauge

$$\bar u(p',\lambda')\slashed\epsilon^{*}_{(r)}u(p,\lambda)\ \propto\ \frac{P}{\xi}\left\{ \underbrace{\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}\big[(2P-\xi)\delta^{rm}+i\lambda\,\xi\,\epsilon^{rm}\big]\boldsymbol\kappa^m}_{\text{helicity conserving}} \;-\;\underbrace{\delta_{\lambda',-\lambda}\,\lambda\,m\,\frac{\xi^2}{P}\,\big(\delta^{r1}+i\lambda\delta^{r2}\big)}_{\textbf{mass}}\right\}$$

The helicity-conserving piece is exactly the $b,c$ of §1 — an independent confirmation of the dictionary. The mass term carries an explicit $\xi^2$. That single fact is what makes the answer in §0(b) so simple.

2.3 Energy denominators

For an $n$-parton Fock state of a photon of virtuality $Q^2$ the light-cone energy denominator is $\sum_i(\boldsymbol k_{i\perp}^2+m_i^2)/z_i+Q^2$. The transverse part is unchanged by the mass, so all the mass dependence is an additive shift of $Q^2$:

$$\underbrace{Q^2+\frac{m^2}{z_q(1-z_q)}}_{q\bar q}\ ,\qquad \underbrace{Q^2+m^2\Big(\frac1\zeta+\frac1\eta\Big)}_{q\bar qg}\quad(\text{gluon massless}).$$

Hence every $K_{0,1}(Q\mathcal D)$ in your expressions becomes $K_{0,1}(\mathcal E\,\mathcal D)$ with

$$\boxed{\ \mathcal E^2=Q^2+m^2\Big(\frac{1}{\zeta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\Big)\ }$$

Consistency check. As $\xi\to0$, $\eta\to\bar\zeta$ and $\mathcal E^2\to Q^2+m^2\big(\tfrac1\zeta+\tfrac1{\bar\zeta}\big)=Q^2+\tfrac{m^2}{\zeta\bar\zeta}$, while $\mathcal D^2\to\zeta\bar\zeta\,\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2$. So $\mathcal E^2\mathcal D^2\to\big[\zeta\bar\zeta Q^2+m^2\big]\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2=\varepsilon^2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2$ — exactly the massive LO dijet Bessel argument, with $\varepsilon^2=\zeta\bar\zeta Q^2+m^2$. The 3-body and 2-body mass shifts match automatically.

4. The massive $\Phi\Phi^*$

Below, all four transverse vectors are written explicitly ($R,Y$ from the amplitude; $\bar R,\bar Y$ from the conjugate) since the new structures are not all of the amplitude×conjugate form. Recall $\bar\zeta=1-\zeta$, $\eta=1-\zeta-\xi$.

4.1 Antiquark-emission channel (replaces your 1.2)

$m^0$ — your (1.2), unchanged:

$$8\big[\eta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\big[\zeta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\delta^{ij}\delta^{mn} -8\xi(2\zeta-1)\big(2\bar\zeta-\xi\big)\epsilon^{ij}\epsilon^{mn} \qquad\big[\times R^i\bar R^jY^m\bar Y^n\big]$$

$m^2$ — diagonal (sign-unambiguous):

$$\mathrm{P2}\times\mathrm{P2}:\quad 8m^2\big[\eta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\,\delta^{mn}\qquad\big[\times Y^m\bar Y^n\big]$$ $$\mathrm{P3}\times\mathrm{P3}:\quad 8m^2\,\frac{\xi^4}{\bar\zeta^{\,2}}\big[\zeta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\,\delta^{ij}\qquad\big[\times R^i\bar R^j\big]$$

$m^4$:

$$\mathrm{P4}\times\mathrm{P4}:\quad 8m^4\,\frac{\xi^4}{\bar\zeta^{\,2}}\qquad\big[\times 1\big]$$

$m^2$ — massless×mass interference:

$$\mathrm{P1}\times\mathrm{P4}+c.c.:\ \ +8m^2\frac{\xi^2\zeta\eta}{\bar\zeta}\Big[(R\!\cdot\!Y)+(\bar R\!\cdot\!\bar Y)\Big], \qquad \mathrm{P2}\times\mathrm{P3}+c.c.:\ \ -8m^2\frac{\xi^2\zeta\eta}{\bar\zeta}\Big[(\bar R\!\cdot\!Y)+(R\!\cdot\!\bar Y)\Big]$$

Note the interference terms pair two amplitude-side vectors, $(R\!\cdot\!Y)$ — a structure absent at $m=0$. This is because P4 carries no free index, so the amplitude's $R$ and $Y$ contract with each other.

4.2 Quark-emission channel (replaces your 1.10)

By $\zeta\leftrightarrow\eta$, $x\leftrightarrow y$, $R\to\tilde R$, $Y\to X$, parent $P=1-\eta=\zeta+\xi$:

$$m^0:\ \text{your (1.10)};\qquad \mathrm{P2}^2:\ 8m^2\big[\zeta^2+(1-\eta)^2\big]\delta^{mn};\qquad \mathrm{P3}^2:\ 8m^2\frac{\xi^4}{(1-\eta)^2}\big[\eta^2+(1-\eta)^2\big]\delta^{ij};\qquad \mathrm{P4}^2:\ 8m^4\frac{\xi^4}{(1-\eta)^2}.$$

4.3 Interference channel (replaces your 1.18)

$$m^0:\ \text{your (1.18)};\qquad \mathrm{P2}\times\mathrm{P2}:\ \ 8m^2\big[\xi(1-\xi)+2\zeta\eta\big]\,\delta^{mn};\qquad \mathrm{P4}\times\mathrm{P4}:\ \ 8m^4\frac{\xi^4}{\bar\zeta\,(\zeta+\xi)} .$$

The $\mathrm{P2}\times\mathrm{P2}$ coefficient is the same second bracket that appears in your (1.18) — as it must be, since P2 differs from P1 only at the photon vertex.

The mass terms have no such freedom, so the relative sign between the $m^0$ path and the mass paths — i.e. the sign of the $\mathrm{P1}\!\times\!\mathrm{P4}$ and $\mathrm{P2}\!\times\!\mathrm{P3}$ interference terms — cannot be determined from your PDF alone. It does not affect anything below: every interference term is $\mathcal O(\xi^2)$ and drops out of the divergence (§5). The diagonal terms P2×P2, P3×P3, P4×P4 are sign-unambiguous.

4.4 Instantaneous terms

The instantaneous quark propagator is $\gamma^+/(2k^+)$, which is mass-independent, so the vertex structures (1.3), (1.4), (1.11), (1.12), (1.19), (1.20) are not modified. What changes is (i) the spinors they are contracted with, which adds mass terms of the same four-path type, and (ii) the Bessel argument $Q\to\mathcal E$. Since (1.3)-type terms are $\mathcal O(\xi)$ and (1.4)-type are $\mathcal O(\xi^2)$ already, their mass corrections are $\mathcal O(\xi)$ or smaller and likewise do not touch the divergence.

4.5 Wilson lines

Unchanged. $\mathcal W$, $S$, $Q$ and all of (1.5)–(1.8), (1.13)–(1.16), (1.21)–(1.24) are eikonal: the quark crosses the shockwave at fixed transverse position and picks up a Wilson line, independently of its mass, up to corrections suppressed by $m/(\text{large }k^+)$. Mass enters the trijet only through the wave functions.

5. The divergence separation with $m\neq0$

5.1 Nothing changes structurally

The three statements of the massless analysis survive verbatim:

  • The only singularity is the $1/\xi$ pole. Mass does not regulate soft gluon emission: the emission vertex's helicity-conserving piece is mass-independent, so $\mathrm d^3N\sim\mathcal G(0)/\xi$ exactly as before.
  • The transverse integrals stay ultraviolet-finite. The four-term $\mathcal W$ combination still vanishes at $\boldsymbol z\to\boldsymbol y$ (because $y'\to y$ there), and that argument never referred to the wave function.
  • The collinear region is still a boundary, not a pole — and is now additionally screened by the mass (see §5.4).

So the separation is the same subtraction:

$$\frac{\mathrm d^3N}{\mathrm d^3k_1\mathrm d^3k_2\mathrm d^3k_3} =\underbrace{\frac{\mathcal G_m(0)}{\xi}}_{\textbf{divergent}} +\underbrace{\frac{\mathcal G_m(\xi)-\mathcal G_m(0)}{\xi}}_{\textbf{finite}}, \qquad \mathcal G_m(\xi)-\mathcal G_m(0)=\mathcal O(\xi).$$

with $\ \mathcal B_1=\dfrac{K_1(\varepsilon\,\mathcal D_0)K_1(\varepsilon\,\bar{\mathcal D}_0)}{\mathcal D_0\bar{\mathcal D}_0}$, $\ \mathcal B_0=K_0(\varepsilon\,\mathcal D_0)K_0(\varepsilon\,\bar{\mathcal D}_0)$, $\ \varepsilon^2=\zeta(1-\zeta)Q^2+m^2$, and $\mathcal K$, $\mathbb W^{(0)}$ exactly as in the massless case.

The curly bracket in the first line is nothing but the massive leading-order transverse-photon $q\bar q$ wave function squared, $\big[\zeta^2+\bar\zeta^{\,2}\big]\varepsilon^2K_1K_1\,\hat{\boldsymbol r}\!\cdot\!\hat{\bar{\boldsymbol r}}+m^2K_0K_0$. So the whole effect of the quark mass on the divergence is to replace the LO dijet by its massive version.

5.4 Consequences

  • B‑JIMWLK / BK is untouched. The kernel is eikonal and mass-independent; the identity $\mathcal K_{qq}+\mathcal K_{\bar q\bar q}-\mathcal K_{q\bar q}-\mathcal K_{\bar qq} =\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}\!\cdot\!\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}}$, and its coincidence limit $(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol y)^2/[(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol x)^2(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol y)^2]$, hold verbatim. Heavy-quark trijets evolve with the same small-$x$ equation; the mass sits entirely in the impact factor.
  • No new divergence, and none removed. Mass cannot cut off $\int\mathrm d\xi/\xi$, because soft-gluon emission from a massive quark is still eikonal.
  • The finite part is genuinely richer: it now contains the three extra transverse structures of §3 and the $m^4$ term, all of which are $\mathcal O(\xi)$ or smaller relative to the pole.
  • Dead-cone screening. The quasi-collinear region $\boldsymbol z\to\boldsymbol y$ is softened: the emission mass term is $\mathcal O(\xi^2)$ and the Bessel argument $\mathcal E\mathcal D$ never vanishes. This matters only if you subsequently integrate the gluon out (there the mass regulates what the jet definition had to regulate at $m=0$), not for the fully differential trijet.
  • Two mass scales, not one. $\mathcal E^2=Q^2+m^2(1/\zeta+1/\eta)$ depends on $\eta$, i.e. on $\xi$; expanding it in $\xi$ generates additional $\mathcal O(\xi)$ terms in the finite part that have no massless counterpart.
