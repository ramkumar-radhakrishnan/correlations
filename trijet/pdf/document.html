0. The answer, in one page

You have the leading-order trijet (three-parton, $q\bar qg$) cross-section in the CGC. Integrated over the gluon phase space it is not finite: it carries one logarithmic rapidity (slow-gluon) divergence, one soft double logarithm, and one final-state collinear pole. The goal is to peel these off with a phase-space slice and be left with (i) a finite, fully differential, three-parton weight you can integrate by Monte Carlo, (ii) a finite two-parton (dijet) weight that you add to the virtual corrections, and (iii) one single-logarithmic piece — and only that piece — which is absorbed into B‑JIMWLK (large $N_c$: BK) evolution of the target.

Master decomposition. With $\delta$ the soft slicing parameter, $R$ the jet radius (collinear slicing parameter), $z_f=k_f^-/q^-$ the rapidity factorization scale, $Q_f^2=2k_f^+k_f^-$ its conjugate hard scale, and $z_0=\Lambda^-/q^-$ the rapidity regulator:

$$ \underbrace{\int_{\Phi_g}\!\mathrm d\sigma^{\rm LO}_{3}\,\mathcal M_2}_{\text{trijet, divergent}} \;+\;\underbrace{\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm V}}_{\text{dijet, divergent}} \;=\; \underbrace{\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_f}{z_0}\Big)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes \mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}}_{\textbf{(I) B-JIMWLK / BK}} \;+\;\underbrace{\Delta_{\rm lt}(Q_f)}_{\textbf{(II) lifetime add-back}} \;+\;\underbrace{\mathrm d\sigma^{(2)}_{\rm fin}(\delta,R,z_f)}_{\textbf{(III) finite dijet}} \;+\;\underbrace{\int_{\bar{\rm H}\bar{\rm C}}\!\mathrm d\sigma^{\rm LO}_{3}\,\mathcal M_3}_{\textbf{(IV) finite trijet}} $$

Every one of (II), (III), (IV) is finite in $d=2$ transverse dimensions and free of $\ln(1/z_0)$. Piece (I) is the entire divergence and is the only thing that renormalizes the target. The sum of (III) + (IV) is independent of the arbitrary slicing parameter $\delta$; the sum of (I) + (II) + (III) + (IV) is independent of $z_f$ and $Q_f$ up to $\mathcal O(\alpha_s^2)$.

The one non-obvious ingredient is piece (II). If you define the "slow" region simply as $z_g\lt z_f$ — a straight cut in the gluon's minus-momentum fraction — you over-subtract. The naive slow limit is only legitimate where the emitted gluon's lifetime is shorter than the parent $q\bar q$ dipole's lifetime,

$$\frac{1}{k_g^{+}}\;\le\;\frac{1}{k_f^{+}}\ ,\qquad k_g^{+}=\frac{\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2}{2z_g q^-}\ ,$$

and the region you wrongly removed must be given back. Adding it back produces exactly

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}\;=\;-\,\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\,\ln^2\!\big(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2\big)\,\times(\text{LO})\,,$$

a negative Sudakov double logarithm. Getting the sign of that double log right is the sharpest diagnostic that your divergence extraction is correct: a naive $z_g\lt z_f$ slice gives it with the wrong (positive) sign, which is unphysical. This is the content of §4.3 of CSSV and it is the reason the lifetime-ordering paper is relevant to your problem.

1. Setup, frame and notation

1.1 Kinematics

Work in the dipole (projectile) frame. The virtual photon carries a large minus light-cone momentum and no transverse momentum; the nucleus carries a large plus momentum:

$$q^\mu=\Big(-\frac{Q^2}{2q^-},\,q^-,\,\boldsymbol 0_\perp\Big),\qquad P^\mu=\Big(P^+,\,\frac{M^2}{2P^+},\,\boldsymbol 0_\perp\Big),\qquad a\!\cdot\! b=a^+b^-+a^-b^+-\boldsymbol a_\perp\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol b_\perp .$$

Photon polarization $\lambda=0\,({\rm L}),\pm1\,({\rm T})$; $x_{\rm Bj}=Q^2/(2q^-P^+)$. The quark, antiquark and gluon four-momenta are $k_1^\mu,k_2^\mu,k_g^\mu$ with longitudinal momentum fractions

$$z_1=\frac{k_1^-}{q^-},\qquad z_2=\frac{k_2^-}{q^-},\qquad z_g=\frac{k_g^-}{q^-} .$$

At LO (dijet) $\delta_z^{(2)}=\delta(1-z_1-z_2)$; for the trijet $\delta_z^{(3)}=\delta(1-z_1-z_2-z_g)$. Transverse positions at which the partons cross the shockwave: $\boldsymbol x_\perp$ (quark), $\boldsymbol y_\perp$ (antiquark), $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ (gluon); primed coordinates in the conjugate amplitude. Differences are written $\boldsymbol r_{xy}\equiv \boldsymbol x_\perp-\boldsymbol y_\perp$, and

$$\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp=\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol x_\perp \mathrm d^2\boldsymbol x'_\perp \mathrm d^2\boldsymbol y_\perp \mathrm d^2\boldsymbol y'_\perp,\qquad \bar Q^2=z_1z_2Q^2 .$$

Back-to-back variables (needed only for the Sudakov discussion):

$$\boldsymbol q_\perp=\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol k_{2\perp},\quad \boldsymbol P_\perp=z_2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}-z_1\boldsymbol k_{2\perp},\quad \boldsymbol u_\perp=\boldsymbol x_\perp-\boldsymbol y_\perp,\quad \boldsymbol b_\perp=z_1\boldsymbol x_\perp+z_2\boldsymbol y_\perp,$$

with $|\boldsymbol u_\perp|\sim 1/|\boldsymbol P_\perp|$ and $|\boldsymbol b_\perp|\sim 1/|\boldsymbol q_\perp|$ conjugate pairs. The back-to-back limit is $|\boldsymbol q_\perp|\ll|\boldsymbol P_\perp|$.

The single most common error in this calculation is to conflate $z_0$, $z_f$ and $\delta$. $\ln(1/z_0)$ is a divergence and must leave the impact factor. $\ln(1/z_f)$ is a finite logarithm that stays in the impact factor and is what makes the impact factor factorization-scheme dependent. $\ln(1/\delta)$ must cancel identically.

1.3 Wilson lines and the colour correlators

Eikonal propagation through the shockwave is a Wilson line in the fundamental (quark) or adjoint (gluon) representation,

$$V(\boldsymbol z_\perp)=\mathcal P\exp\Big(ig\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\mathrm dz^-A^{+,c}_{\rm cl}(z^-,\boldsymbol z_\perp)t^c\Big),\qquad U(\boldsymbol z_\perp)=\mathcal P\exp\Big(ig\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\mathrm dz^-A^{+,c}_{\rm cl}(z^-,\boldsymbol z_\perp)T^c\Big),$$

[CSSV (A.5)–(A.6)], with dipole and quadrupole operators

$$D_{xy}=\tfrac{1}{N_c}{\rm Tr}\big(V(\boldsymbol x_\perp)V^\dagger(\boldsymbol y_\perp)\big),\qquad Q_{xy,y'x'}=\tfrac{1}{N_c}{\rm Tr}\big(V(\boldsymbol x_\perp)V^\dagger(\boldsymbol y_\perp)V(\boldsymbol y'_\perp)V^\dagger(\boldsymbol x'_\perp)\big).$$

All observables are CGC averages $\langle\mathcal O\rangle_Y=\int\mathcal D[\rho_A]\,W_Y[\rho_A]\,\mathcal O[\rho_A]$ over the colour-source weight functional at projectile rapidity $Y$. The five correlators that appear at NLO are CSSV Table 1:

$$\Xi_{\rm LO}=\big\langle 1-D_{xy}-D_{y'x'}+Q_{xy,y'x'}\big\rangle,\qquad \Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}=\tfrac{N_c}{2}\big\langle 1-D_{xy}-D_{y'x'}+D_{xy}D_{y'x'}\big\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2N_c}\Xi_{\rm LO},$$ $$\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1}=\tfrac{N_c}{2}\big\langle 1-D_{y'x'}+Q_{zy,y'x'}D_{xz}-D_{xz}D_{zy}\big\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2N_c}\Xi_{\rm LO},$$ $$\Xi_{{\rm NLO},2}=\tfrac{N_c}{2}\big\langle 1-D_{y'x'}+Q_{xz,y'x'}D_{zy}-D_{xz}D_{zy}\big\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2N_c}\Xi_{\rm LO},$$ $$\Xi_{{\rm NLO},4}=\tfrac{N_c}{2}\big\langle Q_{z'x',xz}Q_{zy,y'z'}-D_{xz}D_{zy}-D_{y'z'}D_{z'x'}+1\big\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2N_c}\Xi_{\rm LO}.$$

$\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1,2,4}$ depend on the gluon transverse coordinate $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ (the gluon crossed the shockwave); $\Xi_{\rm LO}$ and $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$ do not. That distinction is what organizes the divergence structure, as we now show.

1.4 The LO dijet cross-section

$$\frac{\mathrm d\sigma^{\gamma^*_\lambda+A\to q\bar q+X}}{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\mathrm d\eta_1\mathrm d\eta_2}\bigg|_{\rm LO} =\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2N_c\delta_z^{(2)}}{(2\pi)^6}\!\int\!\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp\, e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{yy'}}\, \Xi_{\rm LO}\,\mathcal R^{\lambda}_{\rm LO}(\boldsymbol r_{xy},\boldsymbol r_{x'y'})$$

[CSSV (2.6)], with the QED splitting factors

$$\mathcal R^{\rm L}_{\rm LO}=8z_1^3z_2^3Q^2K_0(\bar Qr_{xy})K_0(\bar Qr_{x'y'}),\qquad \mathcal R^{\rm T}_{\rm LO}=2z_1z_2\big[z_1^2+z_2^2\big]\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}}{r_{xy}r_{x'y'}}\bar Q^2K_1(\bar Qr_{xy})K_1(\bar Qr_{x'y'}).$$

This is completely finite. Every divergence discussed below is a property of the trijet (and of the one-loop dijet), not of this object.

1.5 The LO trijet: structure that matters for the divergences

For $\gamma^*_\lambda+A\to q(k_1)\,\bar q(k_2)\,g(k_g)+X$ the amplitude splits, in the shockwave approximation, into two classes according to whether the gluon is emitted before or after the shockwave:

$$\mathcal M \;=\; \mathcal M_{\rm bef}\;+\;\mathcal M_{\rm aft}\,. $$
  • $\mathcal M_{\rm bef}$ (gluon crosses the shockwave). Three Wilson lines, $V(\boldsymbol x_\perp)\otimes V^\dagger(\boldsymbol y_\perp)\otimes U(\boldsymbol z_\perp)$; these are the contributions that generate $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},2},\Xi_{{\rm NLO},4}$ and the $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integration. The light-cone wave function involves $K_0(QX_{\rm R})$ with the effective $q\bar qg$ dipole size $$X_{\rm R}^2=z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2+z_1z_g\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2+z_2z_g\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2\qquad\text{[CSSV (3.25)]}.$$
  • $\mathcal M_{\rm aft}$ (gluon emitted after the shockwave; diagrams $\mathrm R_2,\mathrm R_2'$ of CSSV Fig. 1). Only the $q\bar q$ dipole $V(\boldsymbol x_\perp)V^\dagger(\boldsymbol y_\perp)$ appears, so these terms carry $\Xi_{\rm LO}$ or $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$, i.e. no $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ dependence. The wave function is the LO one at shifted virtuality, $K_0(\bar Q_{\rm R2}r_{xy})$ with $\bar Q^2_{\rm R2}=z_2(1-z_2)Q^2$, multiplied by the eikonal emission factor $$\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}}{\big(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\tfrac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\big)^2}\qquad\text{[CSSV (B.1)]}.$$
Why this split is the right organizing principle. Only $\mathcal M_{\rm aft}$ has an on-shell internal quark propagator as $k_g^\mu\to0$; hence only $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2$, $\mathrm R_2'\times\mathrm R_2'$ and $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2'$ carry the genuine soft ($\ln^2$) divergence and the final-state collinear pole. The rapidity ($\ln$) divergence, on the other hand, is carried by all diagrams, because $\int\mathrm dz_g/z_g$ comes from the phase-space measure. Keeping these two facts apart is the whole game.

The explicit trijet expressions are long; the pieces we actually need are CSSV (B.1) [$\mathrm R_2\!\times\!\mathrm R_2$], (B.11) [$\mathrm R_2\!\times\!\mathrm R_2'$], and (3.23) [gluon crossing the shockwave]. They are reproduced in §7.

2. Complete divergence inventory of the LO trijet

The gluon phase space measure carries an explicit $1/z_g$:

$$\int\!\mathrm d\Phi_g=\int\!\frac{\mathrm dk_g^-\,\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}}{(2\pi)^3\,2k_g^-} =\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^3}\int_{z_0}^{1-z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\,. $$

Divergences arise wherever the squared amplitude fails to vanish fast enough at the boundaries of this integration. There are exactly three such places.

2.1 (D1) Rapidity ("slow-gluon") divergence — the one that goes into evolution

Limit: $z_g\to0$ at fixed $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$ (equivalently, fixed $\boldsymbol z_\perp$). Regulator: $z_g\gt z_0=\Lambda^-/q^-$. Strength: single logarithm $\ln(1/z_0)$.

In this limit the $q\bar qg$ light-cone wave functions collapse onto the LO one,

$$X_{\rm R}^2\ \xrightarrow{\ z_g\to0\ }\ z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2,\qquad X_{\rm V}^2\ \xrightarrow{\ z_g\to0\ }\ z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2,\qquad K_0(QX_{\rm R/V})\ \to\ K_0(\bar Q r_{xy}),$$

and what is left multiplying $\mathrm dz_g/z_g$ is a purely transverse emission kernel of dipole type. Using CSSV's identity (3.27),

$$\frac1\pi\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\Big[\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zx'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2}-\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2}\Big]=\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2}\Big),$$

the slow limit of the LO colour structure organizes into [CSSV (3.28)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO,slow}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}e_f^2\delta_z^{(2)}}{(2\pi)^6}\!\int\!\mathrm d^8\boldsymbol X_\perp\, \mathcal R^\lambda_{\rm LO}\,e^{-i\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}-i\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol r_{yy'}} \frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi^2}\ln\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\,\mathcal K_{\rm dip}\;\Xi_{\rm LO},$$ $$\mathcal K_{\rm dip}=\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zx'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2}-\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2}+\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zy}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy'}^2}-\frac{\boldsymbol r_{zx'}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol r_{zy'}}{\boldsymbol r_{zx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy'}^2}\,. $$

Collecting all colour channels (there are six independent transverse structures once the quadrupoles are included) gives the complete rapidity divergence, CSSV (3.29), which is exactly the leading-log JIMWLK Hamiltonian acting on the LO cross-section:

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm slow}=\ln\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\,,\qquad\text{[CSSV (3.32)]}$$ $$\mathcal H_{\rm LL}=\frac12\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol u_\perp\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol v_\perp\, \frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+,a}_{\rm cl}(\boldsymbol u_\perp)}\,\eta^{ab}(\boldsymbol u_\perp,\boldsymbol v_\perp)\, \frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+,b}_{\rm cl}(\boldsymbol v_\perp)}\,,$$ $$\eta^{ab}(\boldsymbol u_\perp,\boldsymbol v_\perp)=\frac1\pi\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp}{(2\pi)^2} \frac{(\boldsymbol u_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)\!\cdot\!(\boldsymbol v_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)}{(\boldsymbol u_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)^2(\boldsymbol v_\perp-\boldsymbol z_\perp)^2} \big[1+U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u_\perp)U(\boldsymbol v_\perp)-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u_\perp)U(\boldsymbol z_\perp)-U^\dagger(\boldsymbol z_\perp)U(\boldsymbol v_\perp)\big]^{ab}.$$
Crucial bookkeeping point. The four terms in the bracket of $\eta^{ab}$ are not all supplied by the real trijet. The "$1$" and the $U^\dagger U$ terms come from the virtual $q\bar q$ diagrams in their own $z_g\to0$ limit; the $U^\dagger(\boldsymbol u)U(\boldsymbol z)$ and $U^\dagger(\boldsymbol z)U(\boldsymbol v)$ terms come from the real trijet. You cannot build B‑JIMWLK (nor BK) from the trijet alone: without the virtual slow pieces the kernel is not real-virtual complete, has no probabilistic interpretation, and the $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integral does not converge. So "take the divergence out of the trijet" in practice means "take the divergence out of the trijet and out of the one-loop dijet, and recognize the sum as $\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$".

2.2 (D2) Soft divergence — a double log, and it must cancel, not evolve

Limit: $k_g^\mu\to0$, i.e. $z_g\to0$ and $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\to0$ together. Origin: only $\mathcal M_{\rm aft}$ ($\mathrm R_2,\mathrm R_2'$), where the internal quark propagator goes on shell. Strength: $\ln^2(1/z_0)$.

Concretely, isolate from $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2$ the strict eikonal piece [CSSV (B.4)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R2\times\mathrm R2,\,\rm soft-div}\;\propto\; \Xi_{\rm LO}\,\mathcal R^\lambda_{\rm LO}\times 4\alpha_sC_F\, \frac{e^{-i(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol r_{xx'}}}{\big(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\tfrac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\big)^2}\,,$$

and integrate it over the out-of-cone gluon phase space. With $\xi=z_g/z_1$ and the transverse integral cut by the jet condition [CSSV (B.9)],

$$\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}_{gq\perp}}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{e^{-i\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}_{gq\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta}}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}_{gq\perp}^2}\Theta\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2_{gq\perp}-R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\xi^2\big) =-\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol\Delta^2R^2\xi^2}{c_0^2}\Big)+\mathcal O(R^2),\quad c_0=2e^{-\gamma_E},$$

one obtains [CSSV (B.7)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R2\times\mathrm R2,\,\rm out,\,soft-div}=\ \cdots\ \times\Xi_{\rm LO}\, \frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\left\{\ln^2\!\Big(\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)-\ln\!\Big(\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2R^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\right\}+\mathcal O(R^2).$$
Read the structure. The $\ln^2(z_0)$ is (rapidity log) × (angular log): the $\xi^2$ inside the transverse logarithm is angular ordering, and $\int_{z_0/z_1}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\xi}\,2\ln\xi=-\ln^2(z_0/z_1)$. This double log is not part of the JIMWLK kernel — leading-log rapidity evolution is single-logarithmic. It cancels, at the level of the IRC-safe dijet observable, against the in-cone real contribution and the virtual $\mathrm V_3$ diagram. If a $\ln^2(1/z_0)$ survives in your impact factor, you have made an error.

The corresponding statements for the other colour channel: the interference $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2'$ soft-divergent piece is CSSV (B.17), and integrated, CSSV (B.20),

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R2\times\mathrm R2',\rm soft-div}=\ \cdots\ \times\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\!\int_{z_0}^{z_1}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\left[2\ln\Big(\frac{z_g}{z_1z_2}\Big)+\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\right]\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}\,,$$

which again produces $\ln^2 z_0$, cancelling against the $z_g\to0$ singularity of $\mathrm V_3\times$LO [CSSV (B.30)].

2.3 (D3) Final-state collinear divergence

Limit: $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol x_\perp$ (or $\boldsymbol y_\perp$) at fixed $z_g$; in momentum space $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}/z_g\to\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}/z_1$. Regulator: dimensional regularization in the transverse plane, $d=2\to d=2-\varepsilon$ (CSSV's convention; other papers use $2-2\varepsilon$). Strength: single $1/\varepsilon$.

Because $\int\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp/\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2$ is logarithmically divergent at short distance, the real trijet has a $1/\varepsilon$ pole whenever the gluon becomes collinear to the quark or the antiquark. By Kinoshita–Lee–Nauenberg this cancels once the final state is defined inclusively over degenerate configurations — i.e. once a jet algorithm is applied. Explicitly, the virtual pole [CSSV (B.23)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm IR\times LO}\ \propto\ \Xi_{\rm LO}\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{2\pi} \left\{\Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}+\ln\frac{z_2}{z_0}-\frac32\Big)\Big(\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+\ln\big(e^{\gamma_E}\pi\mu^2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\big)\Big)+\frac12\ln^2\frac{z_2}{z_1}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\frac52-\frac12\right\}$$

is cancelled by the in-cone real emission [CSSV (B.24)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R2\times\mathrm R2,\rm in}=\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\left\{\Big(\frac34-\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big)\frac{2}{\varepsilon} +\ln^2 z_1-\ln^2 z_0-\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-\frac34\Big)\ln\Big(\frac{R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2}{\tilde\mu^2z_1^2}\Big)+\frac14+\frac32\Big(1-\ln\frac{z_1}{2}\Big)\right\}$$

plus its $q\leftrightarrow\bar q$ mirror. The physical remnant is the jet-radius logarithm. After the dust settles the coefficient of $\ln R$ in the finite dijet weight is $-3\ln R=-\tfrac32\ln R^2$ [CSSV (3.14)], which is precisely the finite part of the $z$-integrated DGLAP $P_{qq}$ splitting function — the collinear-factorization fingerprint.

2.4 (D4) Ultraviolet divergence — a virtual-only statement

The transverse UV region $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol x_\perp$ with the gluon reabsorbed produces UV $1/\varepsilon$ poles in $\mathrm{SE}_1$, $\mathrm{SE}_2$, $\mathrm{SE}_3$ and $\mathrm V_2$; these cancel among themselves and against the in-cone real [CSSV §3.2, footnote 13]. A trijet with three resolved jets has no UV divergence. It is listed here only so that the inventory is complete and so that you do not double-count $\mathrm{SE}_1$, whose UV-divergent part belongs to the $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1}$ structure and whose UV-finite part belongs to $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm V,no-sud,other}$.

2.5 What is not divergent (and why that is a feature of the CGC)

  • No initial-state collinear singularity. The projectile is a photon; the target is a shockwave, resummed to all orders in $g^2A^{1/3}$. There is no PDF to factorize into. The role played by $\mu_f$ and DGLAP in collinear factorization is played here by $z_f$ and JIMWLK. This is the single biggest structural difference from Harris–Owens.
  • No infrared divergence at large $|\boldsymbol z_\perp|$. Colour transparency / colour neutrality: the correlators $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},i}$ vanish when the emitted gluon is placed far from the $q\bar q$ pair, so $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\to0$ at fixed $z_g$ is regulated by the target itself (physically, by $Q_s$). Do not introduce a transverse-momentum cutoff for it.
  • No divergence at $z_g\to1$ or at $z_1,z_2\to0$ for fixed jet kinematics.

3. Two-cutoff phase-space slicing, translated to the CGC

3.1 The method in its original setting

Harris–Owens organize an NLO calculation with a $2\to2$ Born, $2\to3$ real emission and one-loop virtual corrections by slicing the real three-body phase space, hierarchically, with two small dimensionless cutoffs:

$$\sigma=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\Phi}\!\int_{\rm S}\!\overline{\textstyle\sum}|M_3|^2\mathrm d\Gamma_3}_{\text{soft}} +\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\Phi}\!\int_{\rm HC}\!\overline{\textstyle\sum}|M_3|^2\mathrm d\Gamma_3}_{\text{hard-collinear}} +\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\Phi}\!\int_{\overline{\rm HC}}\!\overline{\textstyle\sum}|M_3|^2\mathrm d\Gamma_3}_{\text{hard, non-collinear}} \qquad\text{HO (2.1),(2.2),(2.24)}$$
  • S: $0\le E_5\le\delta_s\sqrt{s_{12}}/2$ (gluon 5 soft, in the partonic CM frame). In S the matrix element is replaced by the eikonal (double-pole) approximation, $|M_3|^2\big|_{\rm soft}\simeq -g^2\mu_r^{2\epsilon}\sum_{f,f'}\frac{p_f\cdot p_{f'}}{(p_f\cdot p_5)(p_{f'}\cdot p_5)}M^0_{ff'}$ [HO (2.20)], and the gluon is integrated analytically over all of its angular phase space in $n=4-2\epsilon$ dimensions. Result: a two-body weight containing $1/\epsilon^2$, $1/\epsilon$, $\ln^2\delta_s$, $\ln\delta_s$.
  • HC: $E_5\gt\delta_s\sqrt{s_{12}}/2$ but some invariant $s_{ij}\le\delta_c s_{12}$. Here the matrix element is replaced by the leading-pole (splitting-function) approximation, $\overline{\sum}|M_3|^2\simeq\overline{\sum}|M_2|^2\,P_{ij}(z,\epsilon)\,g^2\mu_r^{2\epsilon}\frac{2}{s_{45}}$ [HO (2.32)], and integrated over the collinear slice. Result: a second two-body weight, with $1/\epsilon$, $\ln\delta_c$, $\ln\delta_s$ and the DGLAP kernels.
  • $\overline{\rm HC}$: everything else. Finite, computed numerically in exactly four dimensions with a Monte Carlo, keeping full three-body kinematics.

Adding the virtual corrections, all $\epsilon$ poles cancel [HO (3.109)–(3.110): $A_2^v+A_2^s=0$, $A_1^v+A_1^s+A_1^{\rm coll}=0$], and the answer is two finite pieces — a two-body weight depending explicitly on $\ln\delta_s,\ln^2\delta_s,\ln\delta_c$ and a three-body weight depending on them through the boundary of its integration region. When histogrammed together for an infrared-safe observable, all cutoff dependence cancels, provided $\delta_c\ll\delta_s\ll1$.

Why slicing and not subtraction, here. Subtraction (dipole, FKS) needs a local counterterm that reproduces the full singular behaviour of the matrix element point-by-point in phase space. In the CGC the "matrix element" is a multi-dimensional transverse-coordinate integral of products of Wilson-line correlators, and a local counterterm would have to be constructed in the eight-dimensional $\boldsymbol X_\perp$ space simultaneously with the $z_g$ integration. Slicing needs only the integrated approximants in the singular regions, which are exactly the objects one already computes analytically (the eikonal limit, the collinear limit, the slow limit). That is why every existing small-$x$ NLO dijet calculation is, in effect, a slicing calculation — including CSSV, whose "in-cone / out-of-cone" and "soft-div / sud2 / no-Sud" split is a two-cutoff slice, written in a different language.

3.3 The genuinely new feature: the slice is two-dimensional

In Harris–Owens the factorization scale $\mu_f$ enters through a one-dimensional renormalization group: you cut the collinear region at $\delta_c$, subtract the $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterm, and what remains depends on $\ln\mu_f$ linearly. Nothing about the soft region interferes with that.

Here it does. The rapidity divergence (D1) and the soft divergence (D2) live in overlapping corners of the gluon phase space: D1 is $z_g\to0$ at any $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$, D2 is $z_g\to0$ with $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\to0$. Consequently:

A cut of the form "$z_g\lt z_f$, all $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$" is the wrong slicing surface. It assigns to the evolution part of the phase space where the soft (Sudakov) approximation, not the slow (eikonal-in-$z_g$) approximation, is the valid one. The correct surface is fixed by lifetime ordering and is a wedge, not a half-plane. This is §5, and it is the reason the second uploaded paper is needed.

4. Slicing the trijet, region by region

4.1 The regions

Let $\mathcal M_2$ / $\mathcal M_3$ be the (IRC-safe) measurement functions selecting a two-jet / three-jet configuration. Define, hierarchically:

S $\;=\;\{\,z_0\lt z_g\lt\delta\,\}$, all $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$.
H $\;=\;\{\,z_g\gt\delta\,\}$, and within it
  HC $\;=\;\mathrm H\cap\{\text{gluon in-cone with }q\text{ or }\bar q\} =\mathrm H\cap\big\{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2_{gq\perp}\le R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\min(1,z_g^2/z_1^2)\ \text{or}\ (q\to\bar q)\big\}$,
  $\overline{\rm HC}$ $\;=\;\mathrm H\setminus\mathrm{HC}$ — three resolved jets.

Then, identically,

$$\int_{\Phi_g}\!\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\,\mathcal M =\underbrace{W_{\rm S}(\delta,\varepsilon,z_0)}_{\text{2-body, analytic}} +\underbrace{W_{\rm HC}(\delta,R,\varepsilon)}_{\text{2-body, analytic}} +\underbrace{\int_{\overline{\rm HC}}\!\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\,\mathcal M_3}_{\text{3-body, finite}}\;+\;\mathcal O(\delta,R^2).$$

4.2 Region S — the soft weight

In S the gluon is unresolved: up to $\mathcal O(\delta)$ it does not change the jet momenta, so the measurement function is the LO one, $\mathcal M\to\mathcal M_2(k_1,k_2)$, whether the gluon happens to lie inside or outside a cone. Replace the trijet by its $z_g\to0$ limit and integrate over all $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$ in $d=2-\varepsilon$:

$$W_{\rm S}=\int_{z_0}^{\delta}\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\int\!\frac{\mathrm d^{2-\varepsilon}\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2-\varepsilon}}\; \Big[\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\Big]_{\rm eik}\,\mathcal M_2 .$$

Two structurally different things come out of this single integral, and they must be separated by hand:

  1. The single-log (D1) piece, from the whole $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}$ range with the $z_g$-independent dipole kernels — this is $\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\ln(\delta/z_0)$.
  2. The double-log (D2) piece and the $1/\varepsilon$ collinear pole (D3), from the region where the transverse integral is itself logarithmic with a $z_g$-dependent boundary.

For the $\Xi_{\rm LO}$ colour channel the out-of-cone part of this integral is CSSV (B.7), quoted in §2.2; for the $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$ channel it is CSSV (B.20). Both carry $\ln^2 z_0$, and both carry $\ln z_0$.

4.3 Region HC — the in-cone collinear weight

Here $z_g\gt\delta$ but the gluon is clustered with the quark (or antiquark) into a single jet of momentum $p_J^\mu=k_1^\mu+k_g^\mu$, $z_J=z_1+z_g$. The trijet matrix element is replaced by its leading collinear behaviour,

$$\mathrm d\sigma_3^{\rm LO}\Big|_{\rm coll}\;\simeq\;\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}(p_J,k_2)\otimes \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\,\frac{P_{qq}(z,\varepsilon)}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2_{gq\perp}}\,,\qquad P_{qq}(z,\varepsilon)=C_F\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}+\varepsilon P'_{qq}(z),\quad P'_{qq}=-C_F(1-z),$$

[HO (2.32),(2.37)–(2.38)], with $z=z_1/z_J$, and integrated over the cone $\boldsymbol{\mathcal C}^2_{gq\perp}\le R^2\boldsymbol p_J^2\min(z^2,(1-z)^2)$ in $d=2-\varepsilon$. The result is CSSV (B.24) (plus its $q\leftrightarrow\bar q$ mirror), quoted in §2.3. It contains:

$$W_{\rm HC}\;\supset\;\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac34-\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}\Big),\qquad \ln^2 z_1-\ln^2z_0,\qquad \Big(\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}-\frac34\Big)\ln\frac{R^2\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2}{\tilde\mu^2z_1^2}\,,$$

with $\tilde\mu^2=4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\mu^2$ the $\overline{\rm MS}$ scale. Note the $3/4$: that is $\tfrac{3}{4}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\int_0^1\!\mathrm dz\,\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}\big|_{\rm reg}$, the finite part of the $z$-integrated $P_{qq}$, and it is what becomes the $-3\ln R$ of the final answer.

4.4 Region $\overline{\rm HC}$ — the finite trijet you integrate numerically

No approximation: the exact $q\bar qg$ cross-section, in exactly four dimensions, with the constraints $z_g\gt\delta$ and both out-of-cone conditions imposed. This is the object CSSV call the "no-Sud" real cross-section: Eqs. (3.21), (3.22) (gluon not crossing the shockwave) and (3.23) (gluon crossing). CSSV state explicitly that this piece "is free of any divergence, as well as large back-to-back logarithms, for all kinematic regions of the final state" — which is exactly the property you want from piece (IV).

Two practical remarks:

  • In (3.23) the $z_g\to0$ limit has already been subtracted by the term labelled "slow" (the last line of that equation), with a $\Theta(z_f-z_g)$. That subtraction is the same object as the one you are performing here; do not do it twice.
  • The upper limit of the $z_g$ integral in the analytic weights is arbitrary (momentum conservation gives $z_g\le1-z_1$, but any choice differs by finite terms which are compensated in $\overline{\rm HC}$). CSSV take $z_1$ for $\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2$ and $z_2$ for $\mathrm R_2'\times\mathrm R_2'$ to preserve $q\leftrightarrow\bar q$ symmetry [discussion after CSSV (B.6) and (B.21)].

4.5 The virtual dijet

The one-loop $q\bar q$ contribution supplies (i) the UV poles that cancel among $\mathrm{SE}_{1,2,3},\mathrm V_2$; (ii) the IR $1/\varepsilon$ pole, CSSV (B.23), that cancels against $W_{\rm HC}$; (iii) its own $\mathrm dz_g/z_g$ rapidity divergence — the $\mathrm V_1,\mathrm V_3,\mathrm{SE}_1$ diagrams have a loop $z_g$ integral running down to zero — which supplies the virtual half of the JIMWLK kernel; and (iv) a $\ln^2 z_0$ from $\mathrm V_3$, CSSV (B.30), that cancels against (B.20).

4.6 Assembling: the two cancellations

(a) $\varepsilon$-poles and $\ln^2z_0$ cancel. For the $\Xi_{\rm LO}$ channel [CSSV (B.25)]:

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm IR\times LO}+\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO\times IR} +\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2,\rm in}+\mathrm d\sigma_{\mathrm R_2\times\mathrm R_2,\rm out,soft-div}+(\mathrm R_2\to\mathrm R_2')$$ $$=\ \cdots\Xi_{\rm LO}\,\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\left\{-\ln\frac{z_1}{z_0}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|} -\ln\frac{z_2}{z_0}\ln\frac{\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}{|\boldsymbol r_{xy}||\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}|} -\frac34\ln\frac{\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}^2\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}^2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}{c_0^4}\right.$$ $$\left.-3\ln R+\frac12\ln^2\frac{z_1}{z_2}+\frac{11}{2}+3\ln2-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\right\}+\mathcal O(R^2).$$

No $1/\varepsilon$; no $\ln^2z_0$. Only single $\ln(1/z_0)$ survives — which is the statement that rapidity factorization holds at leading logarithmic accuracy. For the $\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3}$ channel the same happens, CSSV (B.31).

(b) Isolating the surviving single log. Introduce $z_f=k_f^-/q^-$ and split $\ln(1/z_0)=\ln(z_f/z_0)+\ln(1/z_f)$. The $\ln(z_f/z_0)$ part is [CSSV (B.26), (B.32)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO,slow}=\ \cdots\ \frac{\alpha_sC_F}{\pi}\ln\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xx'}^2\boldsymbol r_{yy'}^2}\Big)\Xi_{\rm LO},$$ $$\mathrm d\sigma_{{\rm NLO}_3,\rm slow}=\ \cdots\ \frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\ln\Big(\frac{k_f^-}{\Lambda^-}\Big)\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy'}^2\boldsymbol r_{yx'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2\boldsymbol r_{x'y'}^2}\Big)\Xi_{{\rm NLO},3},$$

and together with the analogous $\boldsymbol z_\perp$-dependent channels they reconstruct CSSV (3.29) $=\ln(k_f^-/\Lambda^-)\,\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$. The $\ln(1/z_f)$ part stays behind as a finite, $z_f$-dependent contribution to the impact factor — CSSV (B.27), (B.33), (B.34).

At this point you have achieved what you asked for, except that the split in (b) was made with a naive $z_g\lt z_f$ cut. Correcting that is §5.

5. The rapidity divergence done properly: lifetime ordering

5.1 Where the naive slow limit breaks down

The naive prescription is: in the term $\ln(k_f^-/\Lambda^-)\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$, "set $z_g=0$ everywhere except in the $1/z_g$ divergence" [CSSV, above (4.39)]. Look at where $z_g$ actually sits. The trijet wave functions contain

$$K_0(QX_{\rm R}),\quad K_0(QX_{\rm V}),\qquad X_{\rm R}^2=z_1z_2\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2+z_1z_g\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2+z_2z_g\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2,$$ $$X_{\rm V}^2=z_2(1-z_g)\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2+z_g(z_1-z_g)\boldsymbol r_{zx}^2+z_2z_g\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2 \quad\text{[CSSV (4.40),(4.41)]}.$$

Setting $z_g=0$ in $X_{\rm R/V}$ recovers the LO wave function $K_0(\bar Qr_{xy})$. But $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ is integrated: no matter how small $z_g$ is, there is always a region of large $|\boldsymbol r_{zx}|,|\boldsymbol r_{zy}|$ in which the transverse size compensates the smallness of $z_g$ and the $z_g$-suppressed terms are not negligible. Setting $z_g=0$ there is simply wrong, and the phase space you assign to the evolution is too large.

In back-to-back kinematics the two effective sizes simplify to a common form [CSSV (4.42)],

$$X_{\rm R}^2\;\approx\;X_{\rm V}^2\;\approx\;z_1z_2\boldsymbol u_\perp^2+z_g\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2 ,$$

with $\boldsymbol u_\perp=\boldsymbol x_\perp-\boldsymbol y_\perp$ and $\boldsymbol b_\perp=z_1\boldsymbol x_\perp+z_2\boldsymbol y_\perp$. The approximation $K_0(QX_{\rm R/V})\to K_0(\bar Qu_\perp)$ is therefore legitimate only when

$$z_g\,\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\;\le\;z_1z_2\,\boldsymbol u_\perp^2\qquad\text{[CSSV (4.43)]}$$

5.2 The constraint is lifetime ordering

Read the constraint in momentum space. Parametrically $\boldsymbol u_\perp^2\sim1/Q^2$ and $\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\sim1/\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2$, so (4.43) becomes [CSSV (4.44)]

$$\frac{z_g}{\boldsymbol k^2_{g\perp}}\;\le\;\frac{z_1z_2}{Q^2} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{1}{k_g^+}\;\le\;z_1z_2\,\frac{2q^-}{Q^2}\;\sim\;\frac{1}{q^+}\,, \qquad k_g^+=\frac{\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2}{2z_gq^-}\,. $$

Since $1/k^+$ is the lifetime (coherence time) of a light-cone fluctuation of plus-momentum $k^+$, this says:

the emitted gluon fluctuation must be shorter-lived than the parent $q\bar q$ dipole: $$\tau_g=\frac{1}{k_g^+}\;\le\;\frac{1}{k_f^+}=\tau_{q\bar q}\,,\qquad 2k_f^+k_f^-\equiv Q_f^2,\quad Q_f^2\sim Q^2\sim\boldsymbol P_\perp^2 .$$

This is the small-$x$ analogue of angular ordering / the BFKL "consistency condition": successive emissions in the projectile cascade are ordered both in $k^-$ (decreasing, the usual rapidity ordering $k_g^-\le k_f^-$) and in $k^+$ (increasing, i.e. decreasing lifetime, $k_g^+\ge k_f^+$).

In coordinate space, using $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2\sim1/\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\sim1/\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2$ and symmetrizing between amplitude and conjugate amplitude, the condition $1/k_g^+\le1/k_f^+$ reads [CSSV (4.47)]

$$k_g^-\,\min\big(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2\big)\;\le\;\frac{k_f^-}{Q_f^2}\,, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac{z_g}{z_f}\,\min\big(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2\big)\,Q_f^2\;\le\;1 .$$

5.3 The correct slicing surface: a wedge, not a half-plane

Introduce the two logarithmic variables that span the emission ("Lund") plane of the slow gluon:

$$t\;\equiv\;\ln\frac{z_f}{z_g}\ \ (\ge0\ \text{for a slow gluon}),\qquad \rho\;\equiv\;\ln\!\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\,Q_f^2\big)\;\simeq\;\ln\frac{Q_f^2}{\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2}\,. $$

Then:

  • rapidity ordering $z_g\lt z_f$  $\Longleftrightarrow$  $t\gt0$;
  • lifetime ordering $k_g^+\ge k_f^+$  $\Longleftrightarrow$  $\rho\lt t$.

5.4 The over-subtraction, computed

Quantitatively: the unconstrained ("naive") leading-log rapidity evolution of the Weizsäcker–Williams gluon TMD, obtained as the back-to-back/correlation limit of CSSV (3.29), is [CSSV (4.45)]

$$\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}(\boldsymbol b_\perp,\boldsymbol b'_\perp)=\hat G^{ij}_{Y_0} +\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\!\int_{Y_0}^{Y_f}\!\mathrm dY\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp \left\{-\frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\hat G^{ij}_{Y}+\text{other correlators}\right\},$$

with $\hat G^{ij}_Y(\boldsymbol b_\perp,\boldsymbol b'_\perp)$ the coordinate-space WW distribution, CSSV (2.22)–(2.23). Imposing the lifetime constraint of §5.2 gives the kinematically improved equation [CSSV (4.48)]

$$\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}=\hat G^{ij}_{Y_0}-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\!\int_{Y_0}^{Y_f}\!\mathrm dY\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\, \Theta\Big(Y_f-Y-\ln\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2\big)\Big) \frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\hat G^{ij}_Y+\dots$$

Using this instead of the unconstrained equation amounts to subtracting from the evolution, and therefore adding back to the impact factor, exactly the hatched triangle of Figure 2 [CSSV (4.50)]:

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}\;=\;-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}(\boldsymbol b_\perp,\boldsymbol b'_\perp) \int_{z_0}^{z_f}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\, \Theta\Big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2-\frac{z_f}{z_g}\Big) \frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\,. $$

This integral is elementary (it is done in full in Appendix A); the result is convergent as $z_0\to0$ — the $\Theta$ function itself cuts the $z_g$ integral — and equals [CSSV (4.51)]

$$\boxed{\ \Delta_{\rm lt}\;=\;-\,\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\,\ln^2\!\big(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2\big)\;\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}(\boldsymbol b_\perp,\boldsymbol b'_\perp)\;+\;\mathcal O\!\Big(\frac{1}{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2}\Big)\ }$$

and, split into its Sudakov double-log, single-log and constant pieces [CSSV (4.52)], with $c_0=2e^{-\gamma_E}$:

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}= \underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}}_{\text{Sudakov double log}} \underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{\pi}\ln\!\Big(\frac{Q_f^2c_0^2}{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}\Big)\ln\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}}_{\text{Sudakov single log}} \underbrace{-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\Big(\frac{Q_f^2c_0^2}{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}\Big)}_{\text{finite}} .$$

5.5 The sign of the double logarithm is your check

Use this as a hard test of your extraction. Soft gluon radiation must suppress the back-to-back peak: emitted soft gluons contribute $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\sim\boldsymbol q_\perp$ to the imbalance, so the coefficient of $\alpha_s\ln^2(\boldsymbol P_\perp/\boldsymbol q_\perp)$ has to be negative.

If you slice with a naive $z_g\lt z_f$ cut and hand the whole quadrant to JIMWLK, the Sudakov double logarithm you extract from the remaining impact factor comes out positive — the wrong sign; see the discussion below CSSV (4.32)–(4.33). Restoring the triangle $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ flips it. So: compute the coefficient of $\alpha_s\ln^2$; if it is positive, you have over-subtracted the rapidity divergence.

With $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ restored, the complete double + single Sudakov result for a longitudinal photon reads [CSSV (4.53)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma^{(0),\lambda={\rm L}}=\alpha_{\rm em}\alpha_se_f^2\delta_z^{(2)}\frac{8(z_1z_2)^3Q^2\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}{(\boldsymbol P_\perp^2+Q^2)^4} \int\!\frac{\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}}{(2\pi)^4}e^{-i\boldsymbol q_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol r_{bb'}}\hat G^0_{Y_f}(\boldsymbol r_{bb'}) \left\{1-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{4\pi}\ln^2\!\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\right.$$ $$\left.-\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\left[C_F\ln\Big(\frac{1}{z_1z_2R^2}\Big)-N_c\ln\Big(\frac{z_f\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}{z_1z_2c_0^2Q_f^2}\Big)\right]\ln\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{c_0^2}\Big)\right\}+\mathcal O(\alpha_s),$$

where the $\hat G^0_{Y_f}$ appearing here must be evolved with the constrained equation (4.48). The double-log coefficient is now $-\alpha_sN_c/4\pi\lt0$, as required.

5.6 The constrained evolution equations you actually feed the divergence into

(i) B‑JIMWLK, unconstrained (leading log). The divergence of §2.1 renormalizes the CGC weight functional:

$$\frac{\partial W^{\rm LL}_{Y_f}[\rho_A]}{\partial Y_f}=\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\,W^{\rm LL}_{Y_f}[\rho_A], \qquad Y_f=\ln(1/z_f),\qquad\text{[CSSV (3.34),(3.35)]}$$

equivalently, for the LO colour structure, $\Xi_{\rm LO}(\boldsymbol X_\perp|Y_f)=\Xi^{(0)}_{\rm LO}(\boldsymbol X_\perp|Y_0)+\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\Xi^{(0)}_{\rm LO}\ln(z_f/z_0)+\mathcal O(\alpha_s^2)$ [CSSV (3.33)]. The initial condition is set at $$z_0\;=\;\frac{Q_0^2}{Q^2}\frac{x_{\rm Bj}}{x_0}\qquad\text{[CSSV (3.38)]},$$ which follows from $k_g^+\le x_0P^+$ together with the on-shell relation $2k_g^+k_g^-=\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}^2$; typically $x_0=0.01$ and the MV model is used for $W_{Y_0}$.

(ii) The whole LO+NLO cross-section then reads [CSSV (3.36),(3.37)]

$$\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO+NLO}=\int\!\mathcal D[\rho_A]\,W^{\rm LL}_{Y_f}[\rho_A]\,\big(\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}+\alpha_s\,\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm NLO,i.f.}\big),$$

with the impact factor $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm NLO,i.f.}$ defined as the finite $\mathcal O(\alpha_s)$ remainder after the rapidity divergence has been factorized out. Note that the colour correlators inside $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$ and $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm NLO,i.f.}$ must be defined without the $\langle\dots\rangle_Y$ average, which is done once, explicitly, in this formula [CSSV footnote 16].

(iii) Kinematically improved WW evolution — the version you must use if you want the Sudakov sign right — is CSSV (4.48), reproduced in §5.4.

(iv) BK. At large $N_c$, and for observables controlled by the dipole operator, the JIMWLK hierarchy closes onto the Balitsky–Kovchegov equation for $S_{xy}=\langle D_{xy}\rangle$,

$$\frac{\partial S_{xy}(Y)}{\partial Y}=\frac{\bar\alpha_s}{2\pi}\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\, \frac{\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{xz}^2\boldsymbol r_{zy}^2}\Big[S_{xz}(Y)S_{zy}(Y)-S_{xy}(Y)\Big], \qquad \bar\alpha_s=\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{\pi},$$

which is just $\mathcal H_{\rm LL}$ acting on a dipole with the quadrupoles factorized. The corresponding kinematic constraint for the dipole operator is [CSSV (4.49)]

$$\Theta\Big(Y_f-Y-\ln\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\big/\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2\big)\Big),$$

i.e. the familiar time-ordering / "kinematically constrained BK" restriction, in which the scale in the logarithm is the internal parent-dipole size $1/\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2$.

5.7 A subtlety worth flagging: WW is not BFKL after the improvement

Compare the two constraints:

$$\text{WW TMD:}\quad\Theta\big(Y_f-Y-\ln(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\,Q_f^2)\big),\qquad \text{dipole:}\quad\Theta\big(Y_f-Y-\ln(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)/\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2)\big).$$

For the WW distribution the constraint involves the external kinematic scale $Q_f^2\sim\boldsymbol P_\perp^2\sim1/\boldsymbol r_{xy}^2$ — the hard scale of the measurement — and not $1/\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2$. CSSV emphasize the consequence: since the unconstrained rapidity evolution of the WW TMD is BFKL in the dilute limit, after kinematic improvement the dilute limit of the WW evolution is not given by the kinematically improved BFKL equation. If you are building a numerical solver, do not recycle a kcBK/kcBFKL kernel for the WW distribution.

The practical reason is easy to see from §5.1: the constraint came from requiring $K_0(QX_{\rm R/V})\approx K_0(\bar Qu_\perp)$, and the scale in that Bessel function is set by the photon virtuality and the jet transverse momentum, i.e. by $\boldsymbol u_\perp$, not by the impact-parameter separation $\boldsymbol r_{bb'}$.

One more remark, since it is easy to worry about it: because $k_f^+$ is arbitrary, $Q_f^2$ is arbitrary too, as long as it is parametrically of order $\boldsymbol P_\perp^2$. Different choices shuffle terms between the double log and the single log; the double-log coefficient is scheme independent, the single-log coefficient is not. CSSV note that there is a choice, $x_f=\frac{Q^2}{c_0^2\boldsymbol P_\perp^2}\frac{x_1x_2}{x_{\rm Bj}}$ [CSSV (4.54)], for which the scheme-dependent part of the single-log coefficient vanishes.

6. The final decomposition and a working algorithm

6.1 Master formula

$$\mathrm d\sigma^{\gamma^*_\lambda+A\to\,\text{2 jets}+X} =\int\!\mathcal D[\rho_A]\;W^{\rm LL,\,kc}_{Y_f}[\rho_A]\; \Big\{\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}\;+\;\alpha_s\,\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm NLO,i.f.}\Big\}$$ $$\alpha_s\,\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm NLO,i.f.}\;=\; \underbrace{\Big[W_{\rm S}+W_{\rm HC}+\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm V}\Big]_{\rm finite}(\delta,R,z_f)}_{\textbf{(III) finite dijet weight}} \;+\;\underbrace{\Delta_{\rm lt}(Q_f)}_{\textbf{(II)}} \;+\;\underbrace{\int_{\overline{\rm HC}(\delta,R)}\!\!\mathrm d\sigma^{\rm LO}_{3}\,\mathcal M_3}_{\textbf{(IV) finite trijet}}$$

$W^{\rm LL,kc}_{Y_f}$ is the weight functional evolved from $Y_0=\ln(1/z_0)$ to $Y_f=\ln(1/z_f)$ with the kinematically constrained (lifetime-ordered) kernel; that evolution is piece (I). All $1/\varepsilon$, all $\ln^2(1/z_0)$ and all $\ln(1/z_0)$ have been removed.

In the language of CSSV's Eq. (3.6)–(3.7) the same content is written

6.2 Algorithm

  1. Fix the scales. Choose $z_f$ (e.g. $\min(z_1,z_2)$ or $z_1z_2$), $Q_f^2\sim\boldsymbol P_\perp^2$ (e.g. $Q_f=\boldsymbol P_\perp/c_0$), the jet radius $R$, and a soft slice $\delta$ with $z_0\ll z_f\lesssim\delta\ll1$. Require $\alpha_s\ln(z_{1,2}/z_f)\ll1$.
  2. Solve the evolution. Evolve $W_{Y}[\rho_A]$ (or, in the back-to-back limit, $\hat G^{ij}_Y$) from $Y_0=\ln(1/z_0)$, Eq. (3.38), to $Y_f$, using the kinematically constrained kernel (4.48). Initial condition: MV model at $x_0\simeq0.01$. This step consumes the divergence and produces nothing else.
  3. Trijet Monte Carlo (IV). Generate $q\bar qg$ events with the exact CGC matrix element, impose $z_g\gt\delta$ and both out-of-cone conditions, cluster into three jets, integrate in four dimensions. Nothing here is divergent.
  4. Two-body weight (III). Add the analytic soft weight $W_{\rm S}$, the in-cone weight $W_{\rm HC}$ and the virtual $\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm V}$; check that $1/\varepsilon$ and $\ln^2 z_0$ cancel, then subtract the slow term $\ln(z_f/z_0)\mathcal H_{\rm LL}\otimes\mathrm d\sigma_{\rm LO}$ that you already gave to step 2. The explicit results are (B.25)→(B.27) and (B.31)→(B.33).
  5. Lifetime add-back (II). Add $\Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2)\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}$. Equivalently and more precisely: the difference between evolving with the constrained kernel (step 2) and the unconstrained one. Do not do both — if your evolution code already implements (4.48), $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ is already accounted for and adding it again double counts.
  6. Histogram (III) + (IV) together for your infrared-safe observable.
Step 5 is the one place where it is easy to double count. Piece (II) is defined as (unconstrained evolution) $-$ (constrained evolution), moved from the evolution side to the impact-factor side. You either (a) evolve with the naive kernel and add $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ to the impact factor, or (b) evolve with the constrained kernel (4.48) and add nothing. Option (b) is preferable because it resums the double transverse logarithms to all orders and stabilizes the evolution beyond leading-log accuracy; option (a) is only correct to $\mathcal O(\alpha_s)$.

6.3 Checks to run, in order of usefulness

  1. $\delta$-independence. Vary $\delta$ over a decade (e.g. $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$). (III) and (IV) each move by $\ln\delta,\ln^2\delta$; their sum must be flat within Monte Carlo errors. This is the standard slicing plateau test (HO §III, Appendix D).
  2. Sign of the Sudakov double log. Must be negative; see §5.5.
  3. $\varepsilon$-pole cancellation, analytically. The residues must cancel before any numerical work: $A^{\rm virt}_{1/\varepsilon}+A^{\rm in-cone}_{1/\varepsilon}=0$, the analogue of HO (3.109)–(3.110).
  4. Absence of $\ln^2 z_0$. Independently of the pole cancellation. If it survives, you have mistaken part of the soft region for the slow region.
  5. $z_f$- and $Q_f$-independence. Vary $z_f$ between $z_1z_2$ and $\min(z_1,z_2)$, and $Q_f$ by a factor of two. The full LO+NLO answer must move only by $\mathcal O(\alpha_s^2)$; the size of that motion is your theory-uncertainty band.
  6. $R\to$ small. The $\ln R$ dependence of (III) must be $-3\ln R$ per $q\bar q$ pair [CSSV (3.14)] and must be compensated by (IV) up to $\mathcal O(R^2)$.
  7. Dilute limit. Expanding the Wilson lines to lowest order must reproduce the known collinear-factorization NLO $\gamma^*g\to q\bar q$ result for the same observable.

8. Pitfalls, in decreasing order of how often they bite

  1. Over-subtracting the rapidity divergence. Slicing at $z_g\lt z_f$ over the whole transverse plane hands the Sudakov region to the evolution. Symptom: wrong-sign Sudakov double log. Cure: §5.
  2. Double counting the lifetime correction. $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ is the difference between constrained and unconstrained evolution. Add it to the impact factor or use the constrained kernel — never both (§6.2, step 5).
  3. Trying to build BK/JIMWLK from the real trijet alone. The virtual $q\bar q$ diagrams supply the $U^\dagger U$ (virtual) terms of $\eta^{ab}$. Without them the $\boldsymbol z_\perp$ integral of the kernel does not converge and the evolution is not probability conserving (§2.1).
  4. Confusing $\ln(1/z_0)$ with $\ln^2(1/z_0)$. Only the single log is factorized into the target. A $\ln^2$ that refuses to cancel means the in-cone/out-of-cone or the real/virtual bookkeeping is inconsistent.
  5. Dropping $z_g$ in the eikonal denominator. In $1/(\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}-\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp})^2$ it is tempting to set $z_g=0$. In back-to-back kinematics $\boldsymbol k_{g\perp}\sim\boldsymbol q_\perp$ and $\frac{z_g}{z_1}\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}\sim z_g\boldsymbol P_\perp$ are the same order even for small $z_g$; the term must be kept [CSSV footnote 31]. It is only droppable if you want the leading $z_g\to0$ divergence and nothing else.
  6. Choosing the jet algorithm after the fact. The in-cone weight, the $\ln R$ coefficient and the finite constants all depend on the clustering criterion. CSSV use the cone/E-scheme criterion (3.2)–(3.4); the generalized-$k_t$ family gives (3.5) and different finite terms (CSSV Appendix D). In the small-$R$ limit they differ only by $\mathcal O(\alpha_s)$ constants — but those constants matter at NLO.
  7. Forgetting that $R$-dependence does not cancel. Unlike the slicing parameter $\delta$, $R$ is part of the observable definition. Only $\mathcal O(R^2)$ is dropped.
  8. Setting the running-coupling scale by hand and claiming NLO accuracy. The one-loop correction to the classical shockwave background contains the $\beta$-function and turns $\alpha_s$ into a running coupling, but the scale can only be fixed at two loops, i.e. by the NNLO impact factor [CSSV footnote 10, and Eqs. (4.58)–(4.59)].
  9. Large-$N_c$ too early. The kernel structure at finite $N_c$ requires quadrupoles ($\Xi_{{\rm NLO},1,2,4}$). Take $N_c\to\infty$ only after the divergence extraction, not before.
  10. Assuming TMD factorization survives. CSSV argue it holds only in the window $Q_s\ll q_\perp\ll P_\perp$; when $Q_s\sim q_\perp\ll P_\perp$ it is violated at NLO by higher twists. That is a statement about the finite pieces, not about the divergence extraction, but it limits what you may do with the answer.

Appendix A. The over-subtraction integral, in full

We evaluate

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\,\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}(\boldsymbol b_\perp,\boldsymbol b'_\perp) \int_{z_0}^{z_f}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\!\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\; \Theta\!\Big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\,Q_f^2-\frac{z_f}{z_g}\Big)\, \frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\;,$$

i.e. CSSV (4.50): the piece of phase space that a naive $z_g\lt z_f$ slice would hand to the evolution but that lifetime ordering forbids.

Step 1: the longitudinal integral

Substitute $t=\ln(z_f/z_g)$, so that $\mathrm dz_g/z_g=\mathrm dt$ and $z_g\in(z_0,z_f)\ \Leftrightarrow\ t\in(0,T)$ with $T=\ln(z_f/z_0)$. Writing

$$\rho\;\equiv\;\ln\!\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)\,Q_f^2\big),$$

the step function is simply $\Theta(\rho-t)$, so

$$\int_{z_0}^{z_f}\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\,\Theta\Big(\dots\Big)=\int_0^{T}\!\mathrm dt\,\Theta(\rho-t)=\min(\rho,T)\ \xrightarrow{\ T\to\infty\ }\ \rho\,\Theta(\rho).$$
Note that $z_0$ has dropped out. The lifetime constraint provides its own cutoff on the $z_g$ integration: a gluon can only violate lifetime ordering if $\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2\gt1$, and then only down to $z_g\gt z_f/(\min(\ldots)Q_f^2)$. This is why $\Delta_{\rm lt}$ is a genuinely finite $\mathcal O(\alpha_s)$ correction to the impact factor, not a redefinition of the divergence.
$$\Rightarrow\qquad \Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f} \int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\;\frac{\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2}{\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2}\; \ln\!\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2\big)\, \Theta\!\big(\min(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2,\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)Q_f^2-1\big).$$

Step 2: the transverse integral

The dipole kernel $\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2/(\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2\boldsymbol r_{zb'}^2)$ is logarithmically divergent only at the two endpoints $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b_\perp$ and $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b'_\perp$; at large $|\boldsymbol z_\perp|$ it falls as $\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2/|\boldsymbol z_\perp|^4$ and, even multiplied by $\ln|\boldsymbol z_\perp|^2$, converges. The double logarithm therefore comes entirely from the two endpoint regions.

Take $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b_\perp$ and set $r\equiv|\boldsymbol r_{zb}|\ll|\boldsymbol r_{bb'}|$. Then $|\boldsymbol r_{zb'}|\simeq|\boldsymbol r_{bb'}|$, $\min(\ldots)=r^2$, and the kernel reduces to $1/r^2$. The two $\Theta$-conditions become $1/Q_f\lt r$, while the endpoint approximation holds for $r\lt|\boldsymbol r_{bb'}|$:

$$\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\frac{\ln(r^2Q_f^2)}{r^2} =2\pi\!\int_{1/Q_f}^{|\boldsymbol r_{bb'}|}\!\frac{\mathrm dr}{r}\,\ln(r^2Q_f^2) \;\overset{s=\ln(r^2Q_f^2)}{=}\;2\pi\!\int_0^{\rho_{\max}}\!\frac{\mathrm ds}{2}\,s \;=\;\frac{\pi}{2}\,\rho_{\max}^2,\qquad \rho_{\max}\equiv\ln\!\big(\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2\big).$$

The endpoint $\boldsymbol z_\perp\to\boldsymbol b'_\perp$ gives an identical contribution, so the total is $\pi\rho_{\max}^2$. Hence

$$\Delta_{\rm lt}=-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi^2}\,\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}\times\pi\,\rho_{\max}^2 =-\frac{\alpha_sN_c}{2\pi}\ln^2\!\big(Q_f^2\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2\big)\,\hat G^{ij}_{Y_f}\,,$$

which is CSSV (4.51). (CSSV evaluate the integral exactly in their Appendix F and find that the remainder is a power correction, $\mathcal O(1/(\boldsymbol r_{bb'}^2Q_f^2))$, with no leftover single logarithm.)

Geometric reading. Since $\int\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp/\boldsymbol r_{zb}^2=\pi\!\int\!\mathrm d\rho$ and the longitudinal integral contributed the factor $\rho$ (the height of the triangle at abscissa $\rho$),

$$\int\!\mathrm d^2\boldsymbol z_\perp\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm dz_g}{z_g}\Big|_{\rm over-sub} \;=\;\pi\!\int_0^{\rho_{\max}}\!\!\rho\,\mathrm d\rho\;=\;\pi\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac12\rho_{\max}^2}_{\text{area of the triangle, Fig. 2}}$$

The Sudakov double logarithm is the area of the region between the naive rapidity-slicing boundary and the lifetime-ordering boundary. That is the cleanest one-line summary of why the two uploaded papers belong together.
