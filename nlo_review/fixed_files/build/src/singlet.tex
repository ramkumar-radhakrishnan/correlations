\section{Colour-singlet averaged form of the finite, DGLAP and RC terms (P6)}\label{sec:singlet}
\noindent[\textbf{New section; fix P6} (\texttt{fix\_singlet.tex}, taken over with the kernel definitions adapted to this file). \textbf{Correction of a statement of the brief and of the reviews:} the cubic (three-$\rho$) terms do \emph{not} vanish after the average over a Gaussian (or any colour-singlet) projectile; only their symmetrised part does (it is odd in $k$). Reordering the operator product into the symmetrised one leaves two-point contact terms $\propto\delta^{(2)}(p_{i}-p_{j})\,\langle\rho^{f}\rho^{f}\rangle$, even in $k$, of the same order as the quadratic terms, which are written out below.]\par
\noindent The finite terms, the DGLAP term and the running-coupling term are written here, like the BFKL and JIMWLK terms, averaged over the projectile, with traces of adjoint Wilson lines. Labels, kernels and conventions are those of the sections above. For the soft-remainder rows (Group I Rows III, V and Group III Rows III, V, VI) the singlet form is written row by row with the \emph{full} kernel of the row, i.e.\ the regular term written in the row plus the row's own member of the block $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:fx-soft}); $\mathcal{S}$ only regroups these members, so the sum is the same, and the colour structures are those of the rows (the pairing of $\mathcal{S}$ is not visible in this form). Only the colour factor is replaced, so that the replacement can be repeated mechanically if a kernel changes. The common prefactor is
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{g^{4}}{16\pi^{5}}\,\frac{1}{k^{+}}\,\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\,,\notag
\end{align}
the numerical factors of the rows are written inside the braces. ``c.c.'' is implied for Groups I and III and is written explicitly.

\subsection{Projectile average: what is assumed}
\noindent The projectile enters every term as an ordered product of two or three colour-charge operators, $C^{a_{1}\dots a_{n}}\,\rho^{a_{1}}(p_{1})\cdots\rho^{a_{n}}(p_{n})$, with a $c$-number tensor $C$ made of adjoint Wilson lines, $f^{abc}$ and $N_{c}$, and $[\rho^{a}(x),\rho^{b}(y)]=if^{abc}\rho^{c}(x)\,\delta^{(2)}(x-y)$. We assume
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A1)] \emph{Global colour invariance} (colour-singlet projectile): the projectile density matrix commutes with the total charge $Q^{a}=\int_{x}\rho^{a}(x)$. Then $\langle\rho^{a}(x)\rangle=0$ (there is no invariant vector in the adjoint representation) and
\begin{align}
\langle\rho^{a}(x)\,\rho^{b}(y)\rangle=\frac{\delta^{ab}}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\,n(x,y)\,,\qquad n(x,y)\equiv\langle\rho^{f}(x)\rho^{f}(y)\rangle=n(y,x)=n(x,y)^{*}\,;\label{eq:s-A1}
\end{align}
the symmetry follows from $\langle[\rho^{a}(x),\rho^{b}(y)]\rangle=if^{abc}\langle\rho^{c}\rangle\delta^{(2)}(x-y)=0$, the reality from the hermiticity of $\rho^{f}$.
\item[(A2)] \emph{Symmetrised three-point function of $d$-type} [\textbf{changed}; author's answer (date 2026-11-20), verbatim: ``This is symmetrized form: $\langle\rho^{a}\rho^{b}\rho^{c}\rangle_{\rm sym}=d^{abc}\langle d^{efg}\rho^{e}\rho^{f}\rho^{g}\rangle_{\rm sym}$'', normalisation confirmed (``Yes thats my indended''); \texttt{notes/fix\_dabc.tex}]: with $S^{abc}(x,y,z)\equiv\langle\{\rho^{a}(x)\rho^{b}(y)\rho^{c}(z)\}_{\rm sym}\rangle$, $\{\dots\}_{\rm sym}$ the average over the six orderings,
\begin{align}
S^{abc}(x,y,z)=d^{abc}\,\frac{N_{c}}{(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\,o(x,y,z)\,,\qquad o(x,y,z)\equiv\langle d^{efg}\{\rho^{e}(x)\rho^{f}(y)\rho^{g}(z)\}_{\rm sym}\rangle\,.\label{eq:s-A2d}
\end{align}
\end{itemize}
The normalisation follows from $d^{abc}d^{abc}=(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)/N_{c}$; $o$ is real and totally symmetric. By (A1), $S^{abc}=d^{abc}g_{d}(x,y,z)+f^{abc}g_{f}(x,y,z)$ with real $g_{d}$ totally symmetric and real $g_{f}$ totally antisymmetric in the three points; (A2) sets $g_{f}=0$ and $g_{d}=\frac{N_{c}}{(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}o$. $o$ is C-odd (the projectile odderon): under $\rho^{a}t^{a}\to-(\rho^{a}t^{a})^{T}$ the cubic Casimir density $d^{efg}\rho^{e}\rho^{f}\rho^{g}$ changes sign. It vanishes for a Gaussian projectile (Gaussian Weyl symbol of the density matrix, $W[\rho]\propto e^{-\frac12\int\rho\,\mu^{-1}\rho}$ as in the MV model: the symmetrised products are the classical moments of $W$, and its odd moments vanish) and for any ensemble invariant under an antiunitary map $\rho^{a}\to-\rho^{a}$. A non-zero $g_{f}=\langle f^{efg}\{\rho^{e}\rho^{f}\rho^{g}\}_{\rm sym}\rangle/(N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1))$ (C-evenness does not remove it) would add $t\,\Phi\,g_{f}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})$ per cubic structure, $\Phi$ at separate points, $t=1$ (ord), $2$ (A12, A23). For $SU(2)$, $d^{abc}=0$ and (A2) means $S^{abc}=0$. No model for $o$ is assumed. [Author's answers (date 2026-11-20) to the questions of \texttt{fix\_dabc}: $g_{f}=0$ intended: ``yes''; $SU(2)$: $S^{abc}=0$: ``yes''; model for $o$: ``No model keep it general''; odd part: ``configuration by configuration''; odd part in the main text: ``yes''.] No assumption about the four-point function is needed (four-$\rho$ rows contribute only to the evolution terms).

\noindent\textbf{Quadratic terms.} By \eqref{eq:s-A1} the order of the two operators does not matter (the commutator is a one-$\rho$ operator), and
\begin{align}
\langle C^{ab}\rho^{a}(p)\rho^{b}(q)\rangle=\frac{C^{aa}}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\,n(p,q)\,.\label{eq:s-quad}
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Cubic terms.} Reordering an ordered product into the symmetrised one produces single commutators, which are \emph{two}-$\rho$ operators, and double commutators, which are one-$\rho$ operators (zero average by (A1)). Exactly, for $A,B,C$ standing for $\rho^{a}(p_{1}),\rho^{b}(p_{2}),\rho^{c}(p_{3})$: $ABC=\{ABC\}_{\rm sym}+\frac12\big([A,B]C+A[B,C]+[A,C]B\big)+(\text{double commutators})$. With $\Phi\equiv C^{abc}f^{abc}$ (the Wilson lines evaluated at the coincident points where the $\delta$-function puts them) this gives
\begin{align}
\langle C^{abc}\rho^{a}(p_{1})\rho^{b}(p_{2})\rho^{c}(p_{3})\rangle&=C^{abc}S^{abc}+\frac{i\,\Phi}{2(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\Big[\delta^{(2)}(p_{1}-p_{2})\,n(p_{1},p_{3})+\delta^{(2)}(p_{2}-p_{3})\,n(p_{1},p_{2})-\delta^{(2)}(p_{1}-p_{3})\,n(p_{1},p_{2})\Big],\label{eq:s-ord}\\
\langle C^{abc}\rho^{a}(p_{1})\{\rho^{b}(p_{2}),\rho^{c}(p_{3})\}\rangle&=2C^{abc}S^{abc}+\frac{i\,\Phi}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\Big[\delta^{(2)}(p_{1}-p_{2})\,n(p_{1},p_{3})-\delta^{(2)}(p_{1}-p_{3})\,n(p_{1},p_{2})\Big],\label{eq:s-a23}\\
\langle C^{abc}\{\rho^{a}(p_{1}),\rho^{b}(p_{2})\}\rho^{c}(p_{3})\rangle&=2C^{abc}S^{abc}+\frac{i\,\Phi}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\Big[\delta^{(2)}(p_{2}-p_{3})\,n(p_{1},p_{2})-\delta^{(2)}(p_{1}-p_{3})\,n(p_{1},p_{2})\Big],\label{eq:s-a12}
\end{align}
($\Phi$ of the ordering written first). Under (A2) the first term is $\frac{N_{c}}{(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\Delta\,o(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})$, $\Delta\equiv\sum_{\rm orderings}C^{abc}d^{abc}$ ($=C^{abc}d^{abc}$ for ord, $2C^{abc}d^{abc}$ for A23, A12; Sec.~\ref{sec:s-dabc}); it vanishes for a Gaussian projectile, \emph{but the contact terms do not}: they are two-point terms $\propto\delta^{(2)}(p_{i}-p_{j})\,n$, of the same order as the quadratic terms, and a colour-neutral projectile does not remove them. The cubic rows therefore do \emph{not} vanish after the average; what vanishes is their symmetrised part. The contact terms are written below as quadratic terms at coincident points: $\mathfrak{K}\big|_{q\to p}$ denotes the kernel of the row with the point $q$ replaced by $p$ (the integral over $q$ is removed by the $\delta$-function); $n$ is written as $\langle\rho^{f}\rho^{f}\rangle$.

\noindent\textbf{Parity in $k$.} Write a term as $A(k)\langle O\rangle$, $A$ the $c$-number factor (prefactor and kernel), $O$ the colour operator; its c.c.\ is $A(k)^{*}\langle O^{\dagger}\rangle=A(k)^{*}\langle O\rangle^{*}$, so the singlet form of [term + c.c.] is [singlet form of the term] + its complex conjugate. All kernels depend on $k$ through $e^{-ik\cdot r}$, $\mathcal{E}(s)$, $\mathcal{J}(s)$, $\widehat{\mathcal{E}}(s)$, $\Psi(s)$, $\pi\delta(s)-i{\rm P}\frac1s$, all of the form $f(-s)=f(s)^{*}$; the quadratic rows have real prefactors, the cubic rows an explicit $i$ (in Group I Row II, $N_{2b}$, the $i$ is in the Fourier transform of the odd function $\mathcal{R}^{i}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})$). Hence $A(-k)=A(k)^{*}$ for the quadratic and $A(-k)=-A(k)^{*}$ for the cubic rows (for Group II Row I, first part, which has no c.c., $N_{1b}$ is $N_{1a}$ with $w\leftrightarrow w'$ and the colour factors in the reverse order, which gives the same statement for $N_{1a}+N_{1b}$). With $\langle O\rangle=\mathsf{S}+i\mathsf{C}$ ($\mathsf{S}=C^{abc}S^{abc}$ and $\mathsf{C}$ real; $\mathsf{C}$ = contact part): term $+$ c.c.\ $=\mathsf{S}\,[A(k)-A(-k)]+i\mathsf{C}\,[A(k)+A(-k)]$ for the cubic rows. \emph{The symmetrised part of the cubic terms is odd under $k\to-k$, their contact part is even}, and the quadratic terms are even. The part of the finite terms odd in $k$ is exactly the $S^{abc}$ part; under (A2) it is given in Sec.~\ref{sec:s-dabc}, configuration by configuration of the target, and it vanishes for $o=0$.

\noindent\textbf{Rows not listed.} Group I Row I and Group II Rows II, III and Group III Row IV contain only four-$\rho$ terms (evolution only); Group I Row IV and Group III Row I cancel identically (operator identity, hence also in singlet form).

\subsection{Colour structures and their singlet factors}
\noindent Notation ($U$ adjoint, real, $U^{T}U=1$, $U^{\dagger}=U^{T}$; $(T^{e})_{bc}=-if^{ebc}$, ${\rm Tr}\,T^{a}T^{b}=N_{c}\delta^{ab}$; at one point $f^{abc}U^{bd}U^{ce}=U^{ah}f^{hde}$, equivalently $U\,T^{b}U^{\dagger}=T^{a}U^{ab}$):
\begin{align}
\mf{D}(a,b)&\equiv{\rm Tr}\{U^{\dagger}(a)U(b)\}=U^{ij}(a)U^{ij}(b)\,,\notag\\
\mf{F}(a,b,c)&\equiv{\rm Tr}\{T^{e}U^{\dagger}(a)U(b)T^{e}U^{\dagger}(b)U(c)\}=f^{ijk}f^{lmn}\,U^{il}(a)\,U^{jm}(b)\,U^{kn}(c)\,,\notag\\
\mf{Q}(a,b;c,d)&\equiv{\rm Tr}\{T^{e}U^{\dagger}(a)U(b)T^{e}U^{\dagger}(c)U(d)\}\,.\notag
\end{align}
$\mf{D}$ is symmetric, $\mf{F}$ totally symmetric; $\mf{Q}(a,b;c,d)=\mf{Q}(c,d;a,b)=\mf{Q}(d,c;b,a)$. At coincident points: $\mf{D}(a,a)=N_{c}^{2}-1$, $\mf{F}(a,a,c)=N_{c}\mf{D}(a,c)$, $\mf{F}(a,a,a)=N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)$, $\mf{Q}(a,a;c,d)=N_{c}\mf{D}(c,d)$, $\mf{Q}(a,b;b,d)=\mf{F}(a,b,d)$, $\mf{Q}(a,b;c,a)=\mf{F}(a,b,c)$. [The second form of $\mf{F}$: $U(b)T^{e}U^{\dagger}(b)=T^{h}U^{he}(b)$ and $(T^{e})_{ij}=-if^{eij}$.] A trace with one $U$ at each of two $\rho$ points comes from $U^{ab}(p)U^{ab'}(q)\delta^{bb'}$; a product $f\,f\,UUU$ with the $\rho$ indices contracted by $\delta$ (quadratic) or by $f^{abc}$ (cubic, $\Phi$) is an $\mf{F}$; the structures with four Wilson lines of Group II Row I and Group III Row II give $\mf{Q}$. Example (Group I Row V, $N_{2a}$): $[U(x')-U(w')]^{ab'}\big[-f^{adc}f^{fbe}U^{db}(x)U^{ce}(z)+N_{c}U^{af}(x)\big]\delta^{b'f}=-\mf{F}(x',x,z)+\mf{F}(w',x,z)+N_{c}[\mf{D}(x',x)-\mf{D}(w',x)]$. The Wilson-line arguments are ordered by the fixed list $x,y,x',y',z,w,w'$.

\subsection{Symmetrised part of the cubic rows ($d^{abc}$)}\label{sec:s-dabc}
\noindent[\textbf{New} (\texttt{notes/fix\_dabc.tex}), following from the author's (A2).] Contracting the three $\rho$ indices of a cubic structure with $d^{abc}$ gives a network with one $f$, one $d$ and Wilson-line chains; chains joining two legs of $f$ to two legs of $d$ give $f^{abe}d^{abe'}=0$, the others reduce, with $U^{T}U=1$ and $(D^{e})_{bc}\equiv d^{ebc}$, to
\begin{align}

\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;c,e)\equiv i\,{\rm Tr}\{T^{h}U^{\dagger}(a)U(b)D^{h}U^{\dagger}(c)U(e)\}\,.\label{eq:s-GQ}
\end{align}
$\mf{G}$ is real and totally antisymmetric ($\mf{G}(a,a,c)=0$); $\mf{Q}_{d}$ is real, $\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;c,e)=-\mf{Q}_{d}(e,c;b,a)$, $\mf{Q}_{d}(a,a;c,e)=\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;c,c)=0$, $\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;c,a)=-\mf{G}(a,b,c)$, $\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;b,c)=\mf{G}(a,b,c)|_{U\to U^{T}}$. Both are invariant under $U(p)\to RU(p)R'$, C-odd ($V\to V^{*}$ changes their sign; $\mf{D},\mf{F},\mf{Q}$ are C-even), $O(\epsilon^{3})$ for a weak target $V=e^{i\epsilon\alpha^{a}t^{a}}$, and zero for $SU(2)$. With $U^{ab}=2{\rm tr}(t^{a}Vt^{b}V^{\dagger})$ (Fierz; exact)
\begin{align}
\mf{G}(a,b,c)&={\rm Im}\big[{\rm tr}(V_{a}^{\dagger}V_{b})\,{\rm tr}(V_{b}^{\dagger}V_{c})\,{\rm tr}(V_{c}^{\dagger}V_{a})\big]+{\rm Im}\,{\rm tr}\big(V_{a}^{\dagger}V_{b}V_{c}^{\dagger}V_{a}V_{b}^{\dagger}V_{c}\big)\,,\notag\\
\mf{Q}_{d}(a,b;c,e)&=-{\rm Im}\big[{\rm tr}A\,{\rm tr}B\,{\rm tr}(A^{\dagger}B^{\dagger})\big]-{\rm Im}\,{\rm tr}\big(ABA^{\dagger}B^{\dagger}\big)\,,\qquad A=V_{a}^{\dagger}V_{b},\ B=V_{c}^{\dagger}V_{e}\,.\label{eq:s-Gfund}
\end{align}

\subsection{What is not settled (NOTE)}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] \textbf{NOTE:} \emph{The cubic rows do not vanish.} Contrary to the statement that the cubic terms vanish for a Gaussian projectile, only their symmetrised part does; the reordering commutators leave two-point contact terms (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:s-ord}--\eqref{eq:s-a12}), of the same order as the quadratic finite terms, even in $k$, and not removed by colour neutrality. They are written above. Whether they cancel among the rows (or against the corresponding contact terms of the three-$\rho$ evolution terms) was not checked: that needs the kernels integrated, and the kernels differ from row to row.
\item[(2)] \textbf{NOTE:} \emph{Integrability at the contact configurations} was checked only for the two kernels above. The other contact kernels put two $\rho$ points together in kernels that were checked for integrability only at separate points; no kernel has a factor singular at the contact itself (checked by inspection: no kernel depends on the difference of two $\rho$ points that are merged), but the new coincidences of the merged point with $z$, $w$, $w'$, $y'$ were not power-counted row by row.
\item[(3)] \emph{Large-distance tails in singlet form.} The tail sums of \texttt{Finite\_terms\_final} (OPEN I-2, II/III-2, II/III-3; corrected in fix\_soft) were computed for the symmetrised two-$\rho$ parts of the rows; for the cubic rows these are the operator form of the contact terms written here, so the cancellations found there, with their restrictions, should carry over to the singlet form. They were not recomputed in singlet form.
\item[(4)] The kernels of the soft-remainder rows are the full row kernels (rows $+$ their members of $\mathcal{S}$), as in Finite\_terms\_final.tex; if they are replaced, the colour factors and coefficients above stay valid as long as the colour structures listed in the table are kept (the coefficients are the row prefactor times $c\,i$).
\item[(5)] \textbf{Answered} (author's answers (date 2026-11-20)): (A2) is the $d^{abc}$ form~\eqref{eq:s-A2d}, with $g_{f}=0$, $S^{abc}=0$ for $SU(2)$, $o$ general; the odd part is stated configuration by configuration, Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-odd}. Open: for a local $o$, integrability at the triple coincidence of the $\rho$ points.
\end{itemize}
