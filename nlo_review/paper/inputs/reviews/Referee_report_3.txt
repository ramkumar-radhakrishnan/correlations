 Referee report
 Single inclusive gluon production at mid rapidity at next-to-leading order in the Color Glass Condensate (manuscript, 100 pp.,
 prepared for JHEP)



 Recommendation: major revision. The result cannot yet be called correct. The parts that could be checked by
 hand are mostly right, but the manuscript itself leaves open the checks that would establish that the full NLO
 result is finite and correct, and several of its physics claims go beyond what the calculation shows. The
 leading-logarithmic structure is credible; the genuinely new NLO content (finite terms, constant,
 running-coupling scale, cubic and contact terms) is not validated against any independent result.

 What was checked and found consistent
 •   Leading-order normalisation. The light-cone wave function (B.3), the normalisation N2 (2.14), the
     amplitude A(1) (2.15), the LO amplitude and spectrum (3.6)–(3.7) with prefactor αs/(4π4), and the
     kT-factorised limit (3.12) are mutually consistent. For a single quark the formula gives the Gunion–Bertsch
     spectrum αsCF/(π2k2).
 •   One-loop wave function. The ξ-integrals (B.12)/(E.6) give −11/6, 67/18 and −ln2δ − π2/3; combining them
     reproduces (2.16), including the cancellation of the cut-off double logarithms. The second integral of (B.15)
     agrees numerically up to O(Λ/k+). The constant is (αs/2π)·Nc(67/18 − π2/6) relative to LO, and the Fourier
     transform (2.16) → (2.17) is consistent.
 •   Pole table. Table 2 adds up as stated: UV poles sum to ℓ∨/2, IR poles to −ℓ∨. The coefficients agree with
     (2.16), (5.7) and the counterterm, and Z3 − 1 = (αsβ0/4π)(2/ε) is consistent with ZgZ31/2 = 1 in light-cone
     gauge.
 •   Collinear sector. ∫dξ ξPgg = 0, the double-plus integral (E.3) = −11/6, and (5.12) = −14.3225 for Nc = 3 all
     check numerically. The μ-dependence of the DGLAP, running-coupling and LO terms combines consistently.
 •   Charge reordering. The decomposition (4.3) and the projectile averages (6.25) are correct (commutator
     coefficients re-derived independently).
 •   Commuting-charge limit. The coherent-state argument (3.15)/(4.2) is right: (UA − Ā)(N2 + N̄2) gives no
     g4ρ4 correction.
 •   Evolution operators. The prefactors of the BFKL and JIMWLK terms agree with (4.7) and (4.10), and the
     adjoint BK limit is correct at large Nc.

 Major issues
 1. The manuscript states that the result is unfinished.
     Sections 6.7, 6.8, 7.6 and 8 list open items:
 •   cancellation of the large-distance tail in the transition region (C10);
 •   integrability of the contact-term kernels at the new coincident points, checked for only two kernels;
 •   whether the contact terms cancel between rows;
 •   the inclusive check of the constant 67/9 − π2/3;
 •   any comparison with the known dilute limit.
     Until the contact terms are power-counted, the result is not even shown to be finite: a contact term at x = y
     produces K(x,x,z) = 1/(x−z)2, which is not integrable at z → x. A paper announcing “the NLO spectrum”
     cannot leave its own finiteness open.

 2. No comparison with any independent NLO result.
     The dilute–dilute limit must reproduce the one-loop central-emission (Lipatov) vertex plus real gg production
     in kT factorisation (Fadin–Lipatov; Bartels–Sabio Vera–Schwennsen). This is the decisive test. Without it
     only the leading-log terms (BFKL and JIMWLK) are validated, and those depend on the LO structure, not on

 3. The running-coupling conclusion is not established.
 •   In pure Yang–Mills, β0/(2Nc) = 11/6 is numerically identical to integrals of the splitting function: the “2L −
     11/6” of Row I.V, the 11/6 term inside KV2b (6.29)/(F.5), and the −11/6 tail coefficient (5.24). “β0 terms”
     cannot be separated from these without an Nf (quark-loop, BLM-type) calculation.
 •   The claimed exact cancellation of the B(x−w) logarithms between Rows I.II and I.V assumes a point-like pair
     (f f U U → NcU). The region where the pair resolves the target is left in the I.V finite terms, which still carry
     an explicit 11/6. “The emission coupling does not run with x−w” is therefore a choice of grouping, not a
     result.
 •   Horowitz–Kovchegov are described as running “with the collinear resolution scale”. As recalled, their scale
     Λcoll depends on q and k−q; this description should be checked against that paper.
 •   Showing that the residual μ-dependence comes only from the δ(1−ξ) term of Pgg is an add-and-subtract that
     amounts to setting μR = μF, which the manuscript never separates.

 4. The scheme of the finite constants is not specified.
 •   State whether gluon polarisation indices are continued to d⊥ = 2−ε (conventional dimensional regularisation)
     or kept two-dimensional. The MS-bar coupling, the MS-bar fragmentation function and the constant 67/9 −
     π2/3 are consistent only in one scheme; otherwise the constant shifts by a term of order Nc/6.
 •   The claim that Chirilli–Xiao–Yuan and Altinoluk et al. use a convention that violates the momentum sum rule
     (5.12) is strong. The remedy is the check the red note on p. 31 says was “not done”: reproduce a standard
     NLO pp → gX (or e+e− → gX) coefficient with the same conventions.

 5. Prescription dependence.
 •   Vanishing energy denominators in B3 are taken as principal values. The physical (adiabatic, iε) prescription
     for the in-state differs, as Section 6.4 notes. Show that the iπθ terms also cancel in the combined kernel
     (6.19), not only the pole.
 •   The instantaneous Group III terms converge only as distributions, πδ(s) − iP(1/s), as ∨ → ∞. They come from
     p+ near ∨, where the eikonal approximation k+soft ≪ k+valence fails, so their finite parts are not under control.

 6. The finite terms are only conditionally convergent.
     Their values depend on the order of integration and on angular prescriptions: conditions (C3), (C7), (F.12),
     and the two integrals in (6.15), each finite only “angular integral first”. A physical cross section must be
     absolutely convergent once terms are grouped correctly. Present such a grouping, or prove that the
     prescriptions cannot shift the result by a finite amount.

 7. The physics claims about the cubic terms need support.
 •   The split of the cubic terms into an odd-in-k part ∝ o (symmetrised three-point function) and even “contact
     terms of the same order as the quadratic terms” depends on operator ordering.
 •   Its physical content should be shown on a case with a unique answer — a single quark or dipole computed
     exactly in charge space, as Section 7.3 does for BFKL — and compared with Li–Skokov [50] and
     McLerran–Skokov [51]; the manuscript says this was not done.


 Errors and inconsistencies
 •   p. 64, Section 7.3: the dipole emission probability is written as (g2/4π4)CFKD. It must be g2CF/(4π3) =
     αsCF/π2 to give the αsNc/(2π2) of (7.2) — off by a factor π, probably a typo since (7.2) is right.
 •   The 11/6 term: Section 5.2 says the 11/6 part of Row I.V cancels Row I.II completely, yet (6.29)/(F.5) still
     contain an Nc·(11/6) finite kernel. This needs explanation.
 •   Section 5.2: a leftover pole ∝ ℓ∨/2 is removed only by taking ∨ → ∞ before ε → 0. State this as part of the
     eikonal approximation rather than as a cancellation.

 Presentation (would block submission as is)
 •   Placeholders (AUTHORS, AFFILIATIONS, ACKNOWLEDGMENTS) and red internal notes (“[Not
     done…]”, “[Not verified…]”) on pp. 31, 60–61, 65 and 67.
 •   Mixed log/ln (4.4, 4.5, 4.8 vs elsewhere); the ∨ notation for the cut-off is unusual and hard to read.
 •   No compact final formula: the result spans about 40 pages of row-by-row expressions, with no plots, no
     numerical evaluation, and no momentum-space form of the finite terms (needed for the dilute-limit check).
 •   Dozens of “verified to 10−15” statements cannot be reproduced; the code should be published as ancillary
     files.

 Required before resubmission
 The leading-log structure (BFKL in ln(∨/k+), JIMWLK in ln(k+/Λ), cancellation of the ρ4 terms) and the pole
 bookkeeping are credible and internally consistent; that part is essentially LO evolution plus counting. Before
 resubmitting, the authors should provide:
 •   the dilute-limit comparison with the one-loop Lipatov vertex;
 •   the Nf (quark-loop) terms, to identify β0 unambiguously;
 •   power-counting of all contact kernels;
 •   a standard collinear cross-check in a stated MS-bar scheme;
 •   a demonstration that the principal-value, distributional and integration-order prescriptions do not affect the
     result.
