\begin{comment}{1}
It seems that in Sec 4.3.1 one is neglecting a ``t-channel gluon exchange'' contribution, where one passes through a three-gluon intermediate state. This is a significant part of the result. It is also curious that the authors do not notice the absence of this contribution in the unitarity relations, which should not hold when it is neglected.
\end{comment}

\reply I agree. Eq.~(4.88) contains, in each of the three colour structures, the four-gluon contact interaction and the \emph{instantaneous} gluon exchange in the $t$-, $u$- and $s$-channel. The \emph{propagating} exchange, which is second order in $H_{ggg}$ and passes through an intermediate state, was included only in the $s$-channel, Eq.~(4.90). The propagating $t$- and $u$-channel exchanges were missing.

In the new version they are computed explicitly in Sec.~4.3.1, Eqs.~(4.91)--(4.94) and Fig.~2. Each channel has two light-cone time orderings, depending on which incoming gluon emits the exchanged gluon. For given external momenta exactly one of them contributes, which is made explicit by $\Theta$-functions. The intermediate energy denominators vanish only for collinear splitting, where the vertex factors vanish linearly, so the transverse integrals are finite. The four corresponding terms are added to the coefficient $\mathbb{D}_{2}$ in Eq.~(5.7).

I also agree about unitarity. For the intermediate state $|g(r)g(r')g(p)\rangle$ the unitarity identity, Eq.~(4.15), produces the products $-\mathbb{C}^{\dagger}(q,r',p)\,\mathbb{C}(k,r',r)$, in which the internal momentum connects an incoming and an outgoing gluon on different lines. In the old version these products had no counterpart in $\mathbb{D}_{2}$. They are reproduced exactly by the new terms. The coefficients $\mathbb{B}_{3}$ and $\mathbb{D}_{2}$ are fixed completely by the matching to the light-cone wave function, so their unitarity relations are non-trivial checks of the calculation. This is now stated in the text and shown explicitly in Eq.~(4.15).

While redoing this part I also corrected the $s$-channel result, Eq.~(4.90): a factor $1/r'^{+}$ and factors of 2 in the intermediate energy denominator were missing, and the colour factor and one Kronecker delta now agree with the vertex, Eq.~(D.3).

\changes Paragraph after Eq.~(4.88); corrected Eq.~(4.90); new Eqs.~(4.91)--(4.94) and Fig.~2; four new terms in Eq.~(5.7); new paragraph with Eq.~(4.15) and remark after Eq.~(5.12).

\begin{comment}{2}
This is related to both the ``t-channel gluon exchange'' contribution mentioned above and the contribution already included in (4.82). The $2g\to2g$ energy denominator can go to zero and thus needs to be regularized. How this is done should be discussed in the manuscript.
\end{comment}

\reply I agree this must be stated. In the new version this is done in Sec.~3.2 and Appendix~E. Even after the momentum-conserving $\delta$-function is used, the denominator $E_{i}-E_{f}$ of Eq.~(4.88) and of Eqs.~(4.91)--(4.94) vanishes for specific kinematics, i.e.\ for $r=p,\ q=k$. The wave functions are computed using non-degenerate perturbation theory. The free soft gluon states form a continuum, so the degenerate configurations $E_{i}=E_{f}$ make a set of measure zero. The perturbative series is then defined in the usual way by the $i\epsilon$ prescription,
\begin{equation*}
E_{i}-E_{f}\;\to\;E_{i}-E_{f}+in\epsilon \quad\text{at $n$-th order},\qquad \epsilon\to0^{+}.
\end{equation*}
This prescription comes from the adiabatic definition of $\Omega$ in Eq.~(3.7). Here $\Omega$ is the M{\o}ller operator of scattering theory, and $\Omega|n\rangle$ are the corresponding ``in'' states. With this, the unitarity relations, Eq.~(3.9), hold exactly. A pole at $E_{i}=E_{f}$ remains in only two coefficients, viz.\ $\mathbb{B}_{3}$ and the $\rho$-independent term of $\mathbb{D}_{2}$. There its residue is the on-shell scattering amplitude, and all the other coefficients are free of this pole. Since the pole is simple, $1/(x+i\epsilon)=\mathrm{P}(1/x)-i\pi\delta(x)$ has an integrable principal value, and the $\delta$-function term is an on-shell, anti-Hermitian contribution that does not enter the unitarity relations.

Now consider the transformed Hamiltonian $\Omega^{\dagger}H\,\Omega$. The off-diagonal elements between states of different energy cancel, as before. What is left is multiplied by the factor $in\epsilon/(E_{i}-E_{f}+in\epsilon)$. This factor equals $1$ only on the energy shell, and it goes to zero as $\epsilon\to0$ everywhere else. Since the energy shell is a set of measure zero, this leftover part does not contribute to matrix elements between normalizable states. Hence the diagonalization of Sec.~6 holds as stated.

\changes New paragraphs in Sec.~3.2; Eq.~(3.7) rewritten; new Eqs.~(3.8)--(3.9); a sentence after Eq.~(4.88); Appendix~E rewritten in the standard Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger form.

\begin{comment}{3}
At this order in perturbation theory the QCD coupling should run and the NLO result should depend on a scale associated with the coupling constant renormalization. Indeed one has here a $\beta$-function coefficient in (4.78), but somehow no running of the coupling constant. This should be addressed in the text.
\end{comment}

\reply First, the result itself does not change. The transverse integral $\int d^{2}\tilde p/\tilde p^{2}$ in Eq.~(4.82) is scaleless, so in dimensional regularization it vanishes,
\begin{equation*}
\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{2-2\epsilon}\tilde p}{\tilde p^{2}}=0 ,
\end{equation*}
and the purely gluonic overlap does not contribute to the normalization coefficient. The referee's point concerns what this zero means, and I agree it must be stated in the text. The integral vanishes because its UV and IR divergences cancel, not because it is finite:
\begin{equation*}
0=\mu^{2\epsilon}\!\int\!\frac{d^{2-2\epsilon}\tilde p}{\tilde p^{2}}=\pi\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}\Big).
\end{equation*}
The $\beta$-function coefficient enters only through the prefactor of this integral, which contains the constant $11/6$. Its UV part is
\begin{equation*}
\frac{g^{2}N_{c}}{8\pi^{3}}\Big(-\frac{11}{6}\Big)\frac{\pi}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}=-\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}},\qquad \beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}N_{c}\quad(N_{f}=0).
\end{equation*}
This is the one-loop gluon field-strength renormalization. In light-cone gauge it also fixes the renormalization of the coupling, $Z_{g}=Z_{3}^{-1/2}$ [Leibbrandt, Rev.\ Mod.\ Phys.\ 59 (1987) 1067], which is why the $\beta$-function coefficient appears. The $1/\epsilon_{\rm IR}$ pole is of collinear origin. So the vanishing of Eq.~(4.82) is the familiar statement that on-shell massless self energies vanish in dimensional regularization. The UV divergence has not disappeared; it is absorbed into the renormalized coupling $g(\mu)$. The IR (collinear) divergence cancels against real emission in infrared-safe quantities. Hence no renormalization scale appears in the normalization constant at $\mathcal{O}(g^{2})$.

Second, we are computing wave functions, and the running of the coupling is not a feature of the order $\mathcal{O}(g^{2})$ considered here. The leading emission amplitude is $\mathcal{O}(g)$, and its running, $g\to g(\mu)\,[1+\mathcal{O}(g^{2}\beta_{0}\log\mu^{2})]$, is a one-loop correction to this amplitude. It therefore first appears in the wave function at order $g^{3}$ [Lublinsky and Mulian, JHEP 05 (2017) 097; Balitsky, Phys.\ Rev.\ D 75 (2007) 014001; Kovchegov and Weigert, Nucl.\ Phys.\ A 784 (2007) 188], which is beyond the order of this paper. At $\mathcal{O}(g^{2})$ the coupling is the renormalized coupling $g(\mu)$, and the only place where $\beta_{0}$ appears is the prefactor of the scaleless integral above, which is zero.

\changes Text after Eq.~(4.82) and new Eqs.~(4.83)--(4.85); a sentence after Eq.~(5.2); the corresponding remark after Eq.~(6.8) and in the Conclusions; three new references.

\begin{comment}{4}
In Sec 3.1 it is stated that the ``coefficients are not ordinary c-number functions but operator quantities acting in the valence Hilbert space''. [\ldots] The statement about noncommuting operator coefficients only applies to the soft gluon part of the Hilbert space, not the full one. The fact that the states in e.g.\ eq (3.2) are states in only the soft subspace should be emphasized more in the text.
\end{comment}

\reply I agree. In the new version this is emphasized in Sec.~3.1. The expansion in Eq.~(3.2) is an expansion in the Fock basis of the soft subspace $\mathcal{H}_{\rm soft}$ only. The full Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\rm soft}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\rm val}$ is an ordinary complex vector space, and in a complete basis $|n\rangle_{\rm soft}\otimes|v\rangle_{\rm val}$ all coefficients are $c$-numbers. Since the valence part is not expanded, the coefficients of the soft Fock states are functionals of the valence charge density, i.e.\ operators on $\mathcal{H}_{\rm val}$, and their ordering must be kept because of Eq.~(2.19). All states of the form (3.2), and the vacuum $|0\rangle\equiv|0\rangle_{\rm soft}\otimes|v\rangle$, should be understood in this sense. The same remark is repeated where $\Omega$ is expanded in Sec.~4.
