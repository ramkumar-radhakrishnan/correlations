\subsection*{Comment 1: the $t$-channel gluon exchange in Sec.~4.3.1}

\begin{referee}{1}
It seems that in Sec 4.3.1.\ one is neglecting a ``$t$-channel gluon exchange'' contribution, where one passes through a three-gluon intermediate state. This is a significant part of the result. It is also curious that the authors do not notice the absence of this contribution in the unitarity relations, which should not hold when it is neglected.
\end{referee}

\textbf{Reply.} The referee is correct. Eq.~(4.82) contains all three colour structures, $f^{a'ad}f^{a'cb}$ ($t$-channel, $k\to r$), $f^{a'ac}f^{a'db}$ ($u$-channel, $k\to q$) and $f^{a'ab}f^{a'dc}$ ($s$-channel), and each of them carries two pieces:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=18pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item the four-gluon contact interaction (the $\delta\delta$ terms, from $F_{ij}F_{ij}$), a pointlike vertex in which no gluon is exchanged;
\item the \emph{instantaneous} gluon exchange in that channel (the terms with plus-momentum ratios, from $\Pi^2$), with denominators $-(k^+-r^+)^2$, $-(k^+-q^+)^2$ and $(k^++p^+)^2$, respectively.
\end{itemize}
In light-cone perturbation theory a gluon exchange consists of this instantaneous part and a \emph{propagating} part, which is second order in $\Hggg$ and passes through an intermediate state. This follows from the light-cone-gauge propagator,
\[
\frac{d^{\mu\nu}(k)}{k^2+i0}=\sum_{\lambda=1,2}\frac{\epsilon^\mu_\lambda\,\epsilon^{\nu*}_\lambda}{k^2+i0}+\frac{n^\mu n^\nu}{(k^+)^2}\,,
\]
whose first term propagates in light-cone time and whose second term is instantaneous. For the $s$-channel the paper includes both parts: the instantaneous part in Eq.~(4.82) and the propagating part, with a one-gluon intermediate state, in Eq.~(4.84). For the $t$- and $u$-channels only the instantaneous part was included. The propagating part was missed: one incoming gluon emits a gluon through $\Hggg$ and the other incoming gluon absorbs it through a second $\Hggg$, so the intermediate state has three gluons. This is the contribution the referee refers to. It is evaluated below and added to the paper.

\emph{The missing diagram.} Label the incoming gluons $g^a_i(k)$, $g^b_j(p)$, the outgoing gluons $g^d_l(r)$, $g^c_n(q)$ (the paper writes the polarisation of $q$ as $k$; we use $n$ to avoid confusion with the momentum $k$), and the exchanged gluon $g^e_m(r')$, with $k+p=r+q$. A gluon with positive plus momentum always propagates forward in light-cone time, so two time orderings are possible, and for given external momenta exactly one of them contributes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=18pt,itemsep=2pt]
\item $k^+>r^+$: $k\to r+r'$ first, then $r'+p\to q$. The intermediate state is $|g(r)\,g(r')\,g(p)\rangle$ with $r'=k-r$.
\item $k^+<r^+$: $p\to q+r''$ first, then $r''+k\to r$. The intermediate state is $|g(k)\,g(r'')\,g(q)\rangle$ with $r''=p-q$.
\end{itemize}
The two orderings are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:tchannel}(a),(b), together with the $s$-channel and instantaneous exchanges already in the paper, (c),(d).

\FigTchannel

In LCPT the $t$- and $u$-channel exchanges give
\begin{equation}
|(\Psi^{ba}_{ji}(k,p))^{t}_{gg}\rangle=\int d^3r\,d^3q\;|g^d_l(r)\,g^c_n(q)\rangle\,\big[T^{(a)}(r,q)+T^{(b)}(r,q)\big],
\label{eq:tch}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
T^{(a)}&=\frac{\Theta(k^+-r^+)}{E_{gg}(k,p)-E_{gg}(r,q)}\int d^3r'\;\frac{\langle g^c_n(q)|\Hggg|g^e_m(r')\,g^b_j(p)\rangle\,\langle g^d_l(r)\,g^e_m(r')|\Hggg|g^a_i(k)\rangle}{E_g(k)-E_{gg}(r,r')}\,,\nonumber\\
T^{(b)}&=T^{(a)}\Big|_{(k,a,i)\leftrightarrow(p,b,j),\;(r,d,l)\leftrightarrow(q,c,n)}\,.
\label{eq:Tab}
\end{align}
The $u$-channel is included through the symmetry of the two-gluon state: with $T=T^{(a)}+T^{(b)}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tch} equals
\[
\tfrac12\int d^3r\,d^3q\;|g(r)g(q)\rangle\,\big[T(r,q)+T(q,r)\big],
\]
where $T(q,r)$ is the $u$-channel. The spectator's energy cancels in the intermediate denominator:
\[
E_{gg}(k,p)-E_{ggg}(r,r',p)=E_g(k)-E_{gg}(r,r').
\]
Inserting the vertices \eq{D.3} and doing the $r'$ integral gives
\begin{equation}
T^{(a)}=-\frac{g^2}{32\pi^3}\,f^{ead}f^{ecb}\,
\frac{\delta^{(3)}(k+p-r-q)\,\Theta(k^+-r^+)}{\sqrt{k^+p^+q^+r^+}}\;
\frac{\xi\,P_{ilnj}}{\boldsymbol\kappa^2\,\big[E_{gg}(k,p)-E_{gg}(r,q)\big]}\,,
\label{eq:Ta}
\end{equation}
where $\xi=r^+/k^+$, $\boldsymbol\kappa=\br-\xi\bk$ is the relative transverse momentum of the splitting $k\to r+r'$, and $P_{ilnj}=\sum_m V_{iml}(k;r',r)\,V_{nmj}(q;r',p)$ is the product of the two polarisation brackets of \eq{D.3} (its explicit form is given in the revised manuscript). The colour factor $f^{ead}f^{ecb}$ is the $t$-channel structure $f^{a'ad}f^{a'cb}$ of (4.82). The intermediate denominator, $E_g(k)-E_{gg}(r,r')=-\boldsymbol\kappa^2/[2k^+\xi(1-\xi)]$, vanishes only at the collinear endpoint, where $T^{(a)}\sim1/|\boldsymbol\kappa|$ is integrable.

\emph{Expression in terms of the Ω coefficients.} Write $\omega_1(f\!\leftarrow\! i)=V_{fi}/(E_i-E_f)$ for the first-order LCPT amplitude and use $V_{fm}=\omega_1(f\!\leftarrow\! m)(E_m-E_f)$. Every second-order amplitude with a single intermediate state $m$ can then be written as
\begin{equation}
\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)=\sum_m \omega_1(f\!\leftarrow\! m)\,\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! i)\,\frac{E_m-E_f}{E_i-E_f}\,.
\label{eq:w2}
\end{equation}
From \eq{5.5}, $\langle gg|\Hggg|g\rangle=2(2\pi)^{-9/2}(E_{\rm parent}-E_{\rm daughters})\,\mathbb C$, so the new contribution to $\mathbb D_2$ is
\begin{align}
\mathbb D^{(t)dcba}_{2\,lnji}(k,p,q,r)={}&2\int\!\frac{d^3r'}{(2\pi)^3}\;\mathbb C^{bec\dagger}_{jmn}(q,r',p)\;\mathbb C^{dea}_{lmi}(k,r',r)\;
\frac{E_g(q)-E_g(r')-E_g(p)}{E_{gg}(k,p)-E_{gg}(r,q)}\nonumber\\
&+\text{(ordering (b))}\,,
\label{eq:D2t}
\end{align}
where ordering (b) follows from the relabelling in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tab}, and only one of the two terms is non-zero for given momenta. The $u$-channel enters through the term $\mathbb D_2^{dcab}(p,k,q,r)$ of \eq{4.17}. The factor 2, compared with the $s$-channel term of (5.7), is bookkeeping: the $s$-channel amplitude is symmetric under $r\leftrightarrow q$ and is shared between $\mathbb D_2^{dcba}(k,p,q,r)$ and $\mathbb D_2^{dcab}(p,k,q,r)$, whereas the $t$- and $u$-channel amplitudes are separate functions. This term is ρ-independent, so the ordering of $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb C^\dagger$ does not matter here.

\emph{Why unitarity signals the omission.} The referee is right that the unitarity relation \eq{4.11} cannot hold without this term. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:w2} and its Hermitian conjugate with $i\leftrightarrow f$, for each intermediate state $m$ one finds
\begin{align}
\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+\big[\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)\big]^\dagger
&=\frac{V_{fm}V_{mi}}{E_i-E_f}\left[\frac{1}{E_i-E_m}-\frac{1}{E_f-E_m}\right]
=-\frac{V_{fm}V_{mi}}{(E_i-E_m)(E_f-E_m)}\nonumber\\
&=-\big[\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! f)\big]^\dagger\,\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! i)\,.
\label{eq:unit}
\end{align}
This is the $\mathcal O(g^2)$ unitarity condition \eq{4.5}, $\Omega^{(2)}+\Omega^{(2)\dagger}=-\Omega^{(1)\dagger}\Omega^{(1)}$, and it holds \emph{intermediate state by intermediate state}. The ordering $V_{fm}V_{mi}$ is kept throughout, so the identity also holds for the non-commuting, ρ-dependent coefficients. For the intermediate state $|g(r)g(r')g(p)\rangle$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:tchannel}(a), $\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! i)$ is the splitting $\mathbb C(k;r',r)$ and $\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! f)$ is the splitting $\mathbb C(q;r',p)$, so the right-hand side is the product $-\mathbb C^\dagger(q;r',p)\,\mathbb C(k;r',r)$, in which the internal momentum $r'$ joins the incoming line $k$ to the outgoing line $q$. Products of this ``$t$-type'' appear on the right-hand side of \eq{4.11}. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:unit} they can only be matched by the Hermitian part of a second-order diagram with the same intermediate state, which is exactly the $t$-channel diagram. The $\mathbb D_2$ of \eq{5.7} contains no such term, so it cannot satisfy \eq{4.11}.

In the submitted version \eq{4.11} was not explicitly checked against \eq{5.7}; this was my oversight. In the revised version the check is done term by term using Eq.~\eqref{eq:unit}, and it is satisfied once the $t$- and $u$-channel terms are included.

\emph{Consequences elsewhere.} The same exchange appears as a disconnected piece (third gluon as spectator) in the three-gluon sector \eq{4.18}. It is accounted for automatically because the normal-ordered operator $\mathbb D_2\,a^\dagger a^\dagger a a$ acts on every pair. In Sec.~6, the new term in $\mathbb D_2$ is needed to cancel the part of $\tfrac12[\Hggg,\Omega^{(1)}]$ with $t$-channel structure in the $2\to2$ sector (see also Part~II, item A3).

\paragraph{Common part (for both versions).}

\textbf{Reply.} I thank the referee. The overall energy denominator can indeed vanish.

\emph{Why the denominator can vanish.} The $\delta$-function in Eq.~(4.82) conserves the plus and transverse momenta, but not the light-cone energy; non-conservation of $p^-$ at the vertices is what produces energy denominators in LCPT. After using it, $q=k+p-r$, the denominator is still a function of the free outgoing momentum $r$:
\[
D(r)=\frac{\bk^2}{2k^+}+\frac{\bp^2}{2p^+}-\frac{\br^2}{2r^+}-\frac{(\bk+\bp-\br)^2}{2(k^++p^+-r^+)}\,.
\]
This vanishes at $r=p$ (so $q=k$), which lies inside the integration region, while the numerator of Eq.~(4.82) stays finite there. More generally, writing $E_{gg}(k,p)=\mathbf P^2/2P^+ + \boldsymbol\kappa^2/[2P^+\xi(1-\xi)]$ with $\xi=k^+/P^+$ and $\boldsymbol\kappa=(1-\xi)\bk-\xi\bp$, the denominator vanishes on the whole surface
\[
\frac{\boldsymbol\kappa^2}{\xi(1-\xi)}=\frac{\boldsymbol\kappa'^2}{\xi'(1-\xi')}\,,
\]
see Fig.~\ref{fig:shell}(a),(c). This cannot happen for the vacuum incoming state of Sec.~4.1, where $E_i=0<E_f$, which is why the issue first appears with incoming soft gluons.

\FigShell

\emph{Which coefficients keep the pole.} When a transition consists of two independent sub-processes (for instance, one gluon splits while another is absorbed by the valence), both time orderings contribute. If the sub-processes change the free energy by $a$ and $b$, so that $E_i-E_f=a+b$, then
\[
\frac{1}{(a+b)\,a}+\frac{1}{(a+b)\,b}=\frac{1}{a\,b}\,,
\]
and the overall denominator cancels in the sum. A pole therefore remains only for connected transitions:

Intermediate-state denominators, e.g.\ $E_g(k)-E_{gg}(r,r')$ in the $t$-channel or $E_{gg}(k,p)-E_g(r')$ in Eq.~(4.84), involve a single splitting or merging and vanish at most at collinear endpoints, where they are integrable. On the energy shell, the residue of the remaining pole is the on-shell (Born) scattering amplitude,
\[
T_{fi}=V^{(2)}_{fi}+\sum_m\frac{V_{fm}V_{mi}}{E_i-E_m}\,,
\]
where $V^{(2)}$ is the $\mathcal O(g^2)$ part of the interaction ($H_{gggg}$ and the instantaneous terms) and $V$ the $\mathcal O(g)$ part. For $\mathbb D_2$ this is the tree-level $gg\to gg$ amplitude, and for $\mathbb B_3$ the amplitude for a soft gluon to scatter off the colour field of the valence charges. These amplitudes are non-zero, so the pole is genuine and cannot cancel.

\paragraph{Version A --- with the prescription.}

The revised manuscript specifies how the pole is treated. The following paragraph is added to Sec.~3.2:
\begin{changes}
\textit{``We use non-degenerate (Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger) perturbation theory. For the continuum of free soft-gluon states, the degenerate configurations $E_i=E_f$ form a set of measure zero, and the perturbative series is defined in the standard way by the prescription $E_i-E_f\to E_i-E_f+in\varepsilon$ at $n$-th order, $\varepsilon\to0^+$. This prescription follows from the adiabatic definition of Ω, Eq.~(3.7): with it, Ω is the M\o ller operator of scattering theory and $\Omega|n\rangle$ are the corresponding in-states. The unitarity relations (4.4)--(4.5) then hold identically. A pole at $E_i=E_f$ survives only in $\mathbb B_3$ and in the ρ-independent part of $\mathbb D_2$, where its residue is the on-shell scattering amplitude; all other coefficients are free of it. In the transformed Hamiltonian $\Omega^\dagger H\Omega$, the off-diagonal elements between states of different free energy cancel as before. What remains is multiplied by a factor $in\varepsilon/(E_i-E_f+in\varepsilon)$, which equals 1 only on the energy shell and vanishes as $\varepsilon\to0$ everywhere else. Since the energy shell is a set of measure zero, this remainder does not contribute to matrix elements between normalisable states, and the diagonalisation of Sec.~6 holds as stated.''}
\end{changes}

Explicitly, with the interaction switched on adiabatically, $H_I(x^+)\,e^{\varepsilon x^+}$, the first- and second-order amplitudes are
\begin{equation}
\omega_1(f\!\leftarrow\! i)=\frac{V_{fi}}{E_i-E_f+i\varepsilon}\,,\qquad
\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)=\sum_m\frac{V_{fm}V_{mi}}{(E_i-E_f+2i\varepsilon)(E_i-E_m+i\varepsilon)}\,,
\label{eq:ieps}
\end{equation}
and a short calculation, analogous to Eq.~\eqref{eq:unit}, gives for every $\varepsilon>0$
\begin{align*}
\omega_1(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger&=-\omega_1(f\!\leftarrow\! i),\\
\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger&=-\sum_m\frac{V_{fm}V_{mi}}{(E_i-E_m+i\varepsilon)(E_f-E_m-i\varepsilon)}=-\sum_m\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger\,\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! i).
\end{align*}
These are the unitarity conditions (4.4), (4.5); the $2i\varepsilon$ at second order is what makes the second identity exact. As $\varepsilon\to0$, $1/(x+2i\varepsilon)\to\mathrm P(1/x)-i\pi\delta(x)$ with $x=E_i-E_f$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:shell}(d)). The principal value is integrable, since the pole is simple. The $\delta$-function term is an on-shell, anti-Hermitian contribution, which does not enter the unitarity relations. The adiabatic construction of unitary light-cone perturbation theory was discussed recently in Ref.~[41], which I now cite here.

\emph{Consequence for Sec.~6.} At $\mathcal O(g)$ nothing changes: $\mathbb A$ and $\mathbb C$ have no vanishing denominators, and Eq.~(6.5) holds as stated. At $\mathcal O(g^2)$, with the prescription, the off-diagonal elements of $\Omega^\dagger H\Omega$ between states of different free energy cancel as before. What remains is multiplied by a factor of the form $in\varepsilon/(E_i-E_f+in\varepsilon)$. This factor equals 1 on the energy shell, where the surviving element is the on-shell amplitude $T_{fi}$ (the coupling between degenerate states that no unitary transformation could remove), and it vanishes as $\varepsilon\to0$ for every other pair of states. Since the energy shell is a set of measure zero, this remainder gives no contribution to matrix elements between normalisable states (wave packets). The diagonalisation of Sec.~6, Eq.~(6.10), therefore holds as stated. No coefficient computed in the paper changes, apart from the specification of the prescription.

\item Sec.~3.3: write Eq.~(3.7) in the interaction picture, with light-cone time $x^+$ and the switching factor $e^{\varepsilon x^+}$, and give Eq.~\eqref{eq:ieps} with the unitarity identities.
\item After Eqs.~(4.43) and (4.82): one sentence noting that the overall denominator vanishes for degenerate configurations (e.g.\ $r=p$, $q=k$ in (4.82)) and is understood with the prescription of Sec.~3.2.

\paragraph{Version B --- without introducing a prescription.}

We have checked that none of the results derived in the paper depends on how these zeros are treated, and the revised manuscript explains why:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=22pt,itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Unitarity.} For $f\neq i$, the Hermitian part of the $\mathcal O(g^2)$ coefficients is pole-free:
\[
\tfrac12\big[\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger\big]=-\tfrac12\sum_m\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger\,\omega_1(m\!\leftarrow\! i)\,.
\]
The pole sits entirely in the anti-Hermitian part,
\[
\tfrac12\big[\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)-\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger\big]=\frac{1}{E_i-E_f}\Big[V^{(2)}_{fi}+\tfrac12\sum_m V_{fm}V_{mi}\Big(\frac{1}{E_i-E_m}+\frac{1}{E_f-E_m}\Big)\Big],
\]
i.e.\ in the generator $\mathbb G$ of \eq{5.11}. The normalisation conditions and the unitarity relations (4.9)--(4.14) involve only the Hermitian parts.
\item \emph{Diagonalisation.} In Sec.~6 the $\mathcal O(g^2)$ coefficients enter $\Omega^\dagger H\Omega$ only through $H_0\Omega^{(2)}+\Omega^{(2)\dagger}H_0$, whose matrix elements are
\[
E_f\,\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+E_i\,\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger=-(E_i-E_f)\,\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+E_i\big[\omega_2(f\!\leftarrow\! i)+\omega_2(i\!\leftarrow\! f)^\dagger\big].
\]
The pole is multiplied by its own zero, and the remainder is the Hermitian part. The cancellation of the off-diagonal elements leading to Eq.~(6.10) therefore holds for all pairs of states with $E_i\neq E_f$ without any regularisation; the exactly degenerate pairs form a set of measure zero.
\item \emph{The coefficients themselves.} $\mathbb B_3$ and $\mathbb D_2$ are therefore given as functions of the momenta for $E_i\neq E_f$. Their extension to the energy shell, where the residue is the on-shell amplitude $T_{fi}$, requires a boundary condition (a choice of $i\varepsilon$) only when they are integrated across the energy shell in an observable that does not commute with $H_0$. This first happens in the computation of single-inclusive gluon production at NLO~[44], where the appropriate boundary condition will be specified.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Comment 3: running coupling and the β-function coefficient in (4.78)}

\begin{referee}{3}
At this order in perturbation theory the QCD coupling should run and the NLO result should depend on a scale associated with the coupling constant renormalization. Indeed one has here a β-function coefficient in (4.78), but somehow no running of the coupling constant. This should be addressed in the text.
\end{referee}

\textbf{Reply.} I thank the referee for this remark. First, the result itself does not change. The transverse integral $\int d^2\tilde p/\tilde{\mathbf p}^2$ in \eq{4.79} is scaleless, so in dimensional regularisation it vanishes,
\[
\mu^{2\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{2-2\epsilon}\tilde p}{\tilde{\mathbf p}^2}=0 .
\]
The purely gluonic overlap \eq{4.79} therefore does not contribute to the normalisation coefficient, and \eq{4.80} is correct as it stands.

The referee's point concerns what this zero means, and I agree it must be stated in the text. The integral vanishes because its UV and IR divergences cancel, not because it is finite:
\begin{equation}
0=\mu^{2\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{2-2\epsilon}\tilde p}{\tilde{\mathbf p}^2}
=\pi\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}\right)\Big|_{\epsilon_{\rm UV}=\epsilon_{\rm IR}} .
\label{eq:scaleless}
\end{equation}
The prefactor that multiplies this integral in \eq{4.79} comes from \eq{4.78} and contains the constant $11/6$. Its UV part is therefore
\[
\frac{g^2N_c}{8\pi^3}\left(-\frac{11}{6}\right)\frac{\pi}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}=-\frac{g^2}{16\pi^2}\,\frac{\beta_0}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}},\qquad \beta_0=\frac{11}{3}N_c\quad(N_f=0).
\]
This is the one-loop gluon field-strength renormalisation. In light-cone (axial-type) gauge it also fixes the renormalisation of the coupling ($Z_g=Z_3^{-1/2}$), which is why the β-function coefficient appears in \eq{4.78}. The additional $2\log(k^+/\Lambda)$ term is the light-cone-gauge rapidity (small-$x$) divergence. The $1/\epsilon_{\rm IR}$ pole has collinear origin.

So the vanishing of \eq{4.79} is the familiar statement that on-shell massless self-energies vanish in dimensional regularisation. The UV divergence has not disappeared: it is absorbed into the renormalised coupling $g(\mu)$ ($\overline{\rm MS}$). The IR (collinear) divergence cancels against real emission in infrared-safe quantities. The renormalisation scale $\mu$ therefore does not show up in $\mathbb N$ at $\mathcal O(g^2)$; it enters through the coupling. The $\mathcal O(g)$ coefficients $\mathbb A$ and $\mathbb C$ are tree level and are to be read as functions of $g(\mu)$. Their one-loop, $\mathcal O(g^3)$, corrections (Ref.~[17]) carry the compensating $\log\mu^2$ with coefficient $\propto\beta_0$. Together these turn the fixed coupling of the Weizs\"acker--Williams amplitude into a running coupling evaluated at a scale set by the gluon transverse momentum, as in the running-coupling BK/JIMWLK kernels (Balitsky; Kovchegov and Weigert). The running thus becomes explicit in the physical observable, single inclusive gluon production at NLO~[44], which combines the present $\mathcal O(g^2)$ coefficients with the $\mathcal O(g^3)$ ones. The same remarks apply to \eq{6.8}, which is the one-loop gluon self-energy and likewise vanishes as a scaleless integral.

\begin{changes}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=16pt,itemsep=2pt]
\item Keep \eq{4.80} unchanged. After \eq{4.79}, add a paragraph stating that the scaleless integral vanishes because of the UV/IR cancellation \eqref{eq:scaleless}. Identify the $11/6$ in \eq{4.78} with $\beta_0/(2N_c)$, and state that the UV part is the coupling (field-strength) renormalisation, absorbed into $g(\mu)$ in $\overline{\rm MS}$.
\item Below \eq{5.2}, state that $g\equiv g(\mu)$, and that the running coupling appears in physical observables once the $\mathcal O(g^3)$ coefficients of Ref.~[17] are included. Add a sentence to the conclusions.
\item After \eq{6.8}, add the same remark (scaleless one-loop self-energy).
\item Add references: I.~Balitsky, Phys.\ Rev.\ D 75 (2007) 014001 [hep-ph/0609105]; Yu.~V.~Kovchegov and H.~Weigert, Nucl.\ Phys.\ A 784 (2007) 188 [hep-ph/0609090]; G.~Leibbrandt, Rev.\ Mod.\ Phys.\ 59 (1987) 1067 (non-covariant gauges).
\end{enumerate}
\end{changes}

\begin{authornote}
Your treatment is kept: the integral is zero and $\mathbb N$ in \eq{4.80} does not change. Equation~\eqref{eq:scaleless} is not a new, non-zero contribution. It only splits that zero into its UV and IR parts, to show the referee where the β-function coefficient and the coupling renormalisation sit. If you prefer not to write the $1/\epsilon$ poles at all, a sentence of the form ``this zero reflects the cancellation of a UV divergence, which renormalises the coupling (coefficient $\beta_0$), against a collinear IR divergence'' is enough to answer the referee.
